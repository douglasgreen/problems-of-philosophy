# Chapter 17 — Time and Persistence

> “The past and future are not in reality. … The past exists only in memory, the future only in anticipation.”
> — Augustine, *Confessions* XI.28 (paraphrased from the argument)

> “Time is the moving image of eternity.”
> — Plato, *Timaeus* 37d

> “If time is a dimension, then ‘now’ is a word like ‘here’ — perfectly meaningful, and perfectly parochial.”
> — the B-theory, in a sentence

---

Chapter 16 supplied the framework category of modality. This chapter supplies the framework category of **time** — and the two are more closely bound than they first appear. The modal claim that the future is *open* and the past *fixed* is a claim about time; the temporal claim that *the present* is privileged is a claim about modality (what *can* still happen). To understand modality without time is to understand possibility without change; to understand time without modality is to understand change without possibility.

The chapter has an unusual structure, because the topic divides into two debates that are rarely treated together and that *should* be.

- **The nature of time** (§§1–3): is the A-series (past, present, future) real, or is it an illusion? What is the *present*, and what does *passage* consist in? What does temporal *experience* tell us about the metaphysics of time?
- **The persistence of objects** (§§4–5): given whatever time turns out to be, *how do things persist through it*? Are objects *wholes* that endure, or *worms* that perdure, or *stages* that succeed one another?

The *first* debate is about *time*; the *second* is about *things in time*. The two are connected by a single question: **what is the relation between an object and the times at which it exists?** And the connection is not merely formal: the *endurantist* has a natural ally in the A-theorist (both *privilege* the present), and the *perdurantist* has a natural ally in the B-theorist (both *spatialize* the temporal order). The alliances are not necessary, but they are *revealing*.

The chapter proceeds in seven movements. §1 states the A-theory/B-theory debate. §2 examines McTaggart’s paradox and the reality of tense. §3 examines temporal experience and the specious present. §4 examines the three theories of persistence. §5 examines the problem of temporary intrinsics, which is the *engine* of the debate. §6 examines the arrow of time and the physics–metaphysics interface. §7 previews personal identity across time. §8 gives an honest verdict.

## 1. The A-Theory and the B-Theory

### 1.1 McTaggart's two series

The modern debate begins with J. M. E. McTaggart’s “The Unreality of Time” (1908), which introduced the distinction that still organizes the field.

**The B-series.** Events are ordered by the *tenseless* relations **earlier than**, **later than**, and **simultaneous with**. The B-series is a *static* ordering: every event has a *fixed* position in it, and the relations do not change. On the B-series, the Battle of Hastings is *earlier than* the Battle of Waterloo, and it is so *once and for all*.

**The A-series.** Events are ordered by the *tensed* determinations **past**, **present**, and **future**. The A-series is *dynamic*: events *move* through it — the future becomes present, and the present becomes past. On the A-series, the Battle of Hastings *was* future, then *was* present, and *is now* past.

**The formal notation.**

Let $E(x, y)$ mean “$x$ is earlier than $y$.” The B-series is the structure $\langle T, E \rangle$ where $T$ is the set of times (or events) and $E$ is a strict linear order.

The A-series is given by the *tense operators* or the *A-predicates*:

$$
F(x) \quad \text{("}x\text{ is future")}, \qquad N(x) \quad \text{("}x\text{ is present")}, \qquad P(x) \quad \text{("}x\text{ is past")}
$$

or, in the *operator* idiom (Prior 1957, 1967):

$$
\mathcal{F}p \quad \text{("it will be that } p\text{")}, \qquad \mathcal{P}p \quad \text{("it was that } p\text{")}, \qquad \mathcal{N}p \quad \text{("it is now that } p\text{")}
$$

**The question.** Is the A-series *real* — is tense an *objective* feature of the world — or is it a *projection* of our experience onto a tenseless reality?

### 1.2 The two theories

**The A-theory (tensed).** Tense is *real* and *irreducible*. The A-series is *fundamental*, and the B-series is *derived* from it (or is a *mere* *abstraction*).

**The B-theory (tenseless).** Tense is *not* real. The B-series is *fundamental*, and the A-determinations are *indexical*: “present” means “simultaneous with *this* utterance,” “past” means “earlier than *this* utterance.” The *apparent* passage of time is a *feature of experience*, not of the world.

**The *formal* *difference*.**

| Feature | A-theory | B-theory |
|---|---|---|
| Tense is real | Yes | No |
| The present is privileged | Yes | No |
| The future is open | Yes (some versions) | No |
| Passage is real | Yes | No (it is a “myth”) |
| “Now” is | A *fundamental* *determination* | An *indexical* |
| The *truthmakers* of tensed claims | *Tensed* *facts* | *Tenseless* *facts* + *context* |

**The *paradigm* *statements*.**

- **A-theory**: Prior, *Time and Modality* (1957), *Past, Present and Future* (1967); Broad, *Scientific Thought* (1923); Tooley, *Time, Tense, and Causation* (1997); Bigelow, “Presentism and Properties” (1996); Markosian, “A Defence of Presentism” (2004); Zimmerman, “Presentism and Temporary Intrinsics” (2008).
- **B-theory**: Smart, “The River of Time” (1949); Mellor, *Real Time* (1981), *Real Time II* (1998); Lewis, “The Paradoxes of Time Travel” (1976); Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001); Dyke, *Time and the Metaphysics of Relativity* (2001); Oaklander, *The Ontology of Time* (2004).

### 1.3 Four versions of the A-theory

The A-theory is *not* a *single* *position*. The *contemporary* *debate* *distinguishes* *four* *versions*, each with a *different* *commitment* about *which* *times* *exist*.

| Version | What exists | The present is | Paradigm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Presentism** | Only the present | The *only* time that exists | Prior; Bigelow; Markosian; Zimmerman |
| **Growing block** | The past and the present | The *edge* of existence; the future does not yet exist | Broad; Tooley; Forrest; Smith |
| **Moving spotlight** | All times | The *privileged* time; all times exist, but one is “lit up” | Broad; Skow; Deasy |
| **The A-theory without a privileged present** | All times | Tense is real but *not* located at a single instant | Fine’s “fragmentalism”; some versions of “tense realism” |

**Presentism** is the most *intuitive* and the most *difficult*. It holds that *only* the present exists — that the past and the future are *not* — and it faces the *truthmaker objection*: if *only* the present exists, what *makes* *true* the claim that “the Battle of Hastings occurred in 1066”?

**Growing block** is the *compromise*: the past *exists* (it is *real*), the future *does not*. The *universe* *grows* as the present *advances*. The *advantage*: it *provides* *truthmakers* for claims about the past (the past *exists*). The *disadvantage*: it *lacks* *truthmakers* for claims about the future (which are *false*, on the *view*, not *indeterminate*).

**Moving spotlight** is the *most* *ontologically* *generous*: *all* times exist (the *block* *universe* is *real*), but *one* of them — the present — is *privileged*. The *view* *combines* the *B-theoretic* *ontology* (all times exist) with the *A-theoretic* *ideology* (one time is *present*). The *disadvantage*: the *relation* *between* the *privileged* time and the *other* times is *mysterious*.

**Fine's fragmentalism** (2005, 2006) is the *most* *radical*: reality is *fragmented* into *incompatible* *perspectives*, each of which is *real* but *cannot* be *combined* into a *single* *description*. The *view* is *deeply* *contested*, and it is *one* of the *most* *interesting* *developments* in the *contemporary* *debate*.

### 1.4 The B-theory and the block universe

The B-theory is *associated* with the **block universe** — the *view* that *all* *times* are *equally* *real*, and that the *universe* is a *four-dimensional* *whole* with *time* as a *dimension*.

**The *argument*.** The *B-theory* *requires* that *all* times *exist*. If only the present existed, the B-series would have no *relata* (the *past* and *future* events would not *exist*). So the *B-theory* *implies* *eternalism*: all times are real.

**The *block*.** The *universe* is a *four-dimensional* *manifold*, with *events* *located* at *coordinates* $\langle x, y, z, t \rangle$. The *passage* of time is an *illusion*: the *“now”* is a *slice* of the *manifold*, and the *“flow”* is a *feature* of *our* *conscious* *navigation* through it (or of our *language*).

**The *slogan*.** “The *block* *universe* *does* not *flow*; it *simply* *is*.”

**The *objection*.** The *view* *denies* the *most* *obvious* *feature* of *experience*: the *sense* that *the future* is *open* and the *past* is *closed*, and that *time* *passes*. (The *B-theorist* *reply*: the *sense* is an *illusion*, and the *illusion* is *explained* by the *asymmetry* of *memory* and *anticipation*.)

**The *connection* to *physics*.** The *special* *theory* of *relativity* *implies* that *simultaneity* is *relative* to a *frame*. If *simultaneity* is *relative*, then the *present* is *relative*, and the *A-theory* is *in* *tension* with *relativity* (see §6.4).

## 2. McTaggart's Paradox and the Reality of Tense

### 2.1 The argument

McTaggart’s argument is the *most* *famous* *argument* *against* the *reality* of *time*, and it is *surprisingly* *difficult* to *state* *precisely*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Time *essentially* involves change.} \\
2. & \text{Change *essentially* involves the *A-series* (things *become* past, *become* present, *become* future).} \\
3. & \text{The *A-series* is *self-contradictory*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Time is *unreal*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *contradiction*** (step 3). The *A-series* *assigns* *three* *incompatible* *determinations* to *every* event: it is *past*, *present*, and *future*. The *determinations* are *incompatible* (an event cannot be *both* past and present *in* the *same* *respect*). But *every* event has *all three* (its *past*, *present*, and *future* *character* are *all* *real* *features* of it). So the *A-series* *involves* a *contradiction*.

**McTaggart's *regress***. The *reply* — that an event is *future* at $t_1$, *present* at $t_2$, and *past* at $t_3$ — *does* not *avoid* the *contradiction*. It *invokes* a *further* *A-series*: $t_1$ is *future* *at* $t_0$, $t_2$ is *present* *at* $t_2$, $t_3$ is *past* *at* $t_4$. The *further* *A-series* *has* the *same* *problem*, and the *regress* is *infinite*.

**The *conclusion*.** The *A-series* *cannot* be *coherently* *described*. Since time *requires* the *A-series*, time is *unreal*.

### 2.2 The replies

The *argument* has *two* *main* *replies*, and the *choice* *between* them *determines* the *A/B* *debate*.

**(i) The B-theoretic reply: indexical analysis.** The *A-determinations* are *not* *properties* of *events*; they are *relations* *between* events and *utterances* (or *subjects*). “Event $E$ is present” means “$E$ is *simultaneous* with *this* utterance.” “$E$ is past” means “$E$ is *earlier* than *this* utterance.” The *analysis* *makes* the *A-determinations* *indexical*, like “here” and “now,” and the *contradiction* *disappears*: $E$ is *not* *both* past and present; $E$ is *past* *relative* to *one* utterance and *present* *relative* to *another*.

**The *statement*.** Smart (1949), Mellor (1981, 1998), Dyke (2001).

**The *virtue*.** The *reply* *dissolves* the *contradiction*, and it *explains* why the *A-series* *seems* *real*: we *experience* the *indexical* *relation* to *events*, and we *project* it onto the *events* *themselves*.

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *loses* the *passage*. If “present” is *indexical*, then *nothing* *moves* *through* *time*; the *“now”* is a *perspective*, not a *feature* of the *world*. The *A-theorist* *argues* that *passage* is *essential* to *time*, and the *B-theory* *cannot* *explain* it.

**(ii) The A-theoretic reply: primitive tense.** The *A-determinations* are *primitive* *properties* of *events*, and the *contradiction* is *avoided* by *distinguishing* the *times* at which the *determinations* *hold*. The *distinction* is *not* a *further* *A-series* (as McTaggart *claims*); it is a *primitive* *fact* about the *way* *tense* *works*. The *regress* is *not* *vicious* because the *A-determinations* are *primitive*, and the *primitive* *is* *not* *explained* *by* a *further* *level*.

**The *statement*.** Prior (1957, 1967), Broad (1923), Tooley (1997), Markosian (2004).

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *asserts* the *primitive* *without* *explaining* it. (The *reply* to the *objection* is the *criterion* *problem* again: the *demand* for an *explanation* of the *primitive* *cannot* be *met*, and the *demand* is *itself* *question-begging*; Ch. 13, §1.)

### 2.3 The truthmaker objection to presentism

The *most* *serious* *objection* to *presentism* is the **truthmaker objection**.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Presentism: only the present exists.} \\
2. & \text{The claim "the Battle of Hastings occurred in 1066" is *true*.} \\
3. & \text{Every *true* *claim* *requires* a *truthmaker* (Ch. 22, §2).} \\
4. & \text{The *truthmaker* would be the *event* of the *Battle* of *Hastings*.} \\
5. & \text{But the *event* does *not* *exist* (it is *past*).} \\
\hline
6. & \therefore \text{Presentism *cannot* *provide* a *truthmaker* for *true* *claims* *about* the *past*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** Either *presentism* is *false*, or the *truthmaker* *principle* is *false*, or there is a *truthmaker* *that* the *presentist* *can* *identify*.

**The *responses*.**

**(i) Ersatz presentism** (Bigelow 1996). The *truthmakers* of claims about the past are *abstract* *entities* — *“Lucretian”* *properties* *of* the *world* — that *encode* the *past*. The *world* *has* the *property* *of* *having* *contained* the *Battle* of *Hastings*, and the *property* is *present* (it is a *property* of the *present* *world*). The *truthmaker* is *abstract*, not *concrete*.

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *posits* *abstract* *entities* *that* *are* *not* *otherwise* *motivated*, and the *positing* is *ad hoc*.

**(ii) The rejection of the truthmaker principle.** The *presentist* can *deny* that *every* *truth* *requires* a *truthmaker*. Some *truths* are *true* *without* a *truthmaker* — *negative* *existentials*, *analytic* *truths*, *modal* *truths* — and *claims* *about* the *past* are *of* the *exceptional* *kind*.

**The *objection*.** The *rejection* *undermines* the *truthmaker* *program* (Ch. 22, §2), and the *program* is *one* of the *most* *important* *tools* of *contemporary* *metaphysics*.

**(iii) The *tensed* *truthmaker*.** The *truthmaker* is a *tensed* *fact* — the *fact* *that* *the* *Battle* *was* *fought* — and the *fact* *is* *present* (it is a *fact* *about* the *past*, *had* *by* the *present*). The *view* *is* *close* to *ersatz* *presentism*, and it *faces* the *same* *objection*.

**(iv) The *growing* *block* *alternative*.** The *growing* *block* *avoids* the *objection* by *making* the *past* *exist*. The *truthmaker* of the *claim* *about* *Hastings* is the *event* *itself*, which *still* *exists* (it is *past*, but *real*). The *cost* is the *loss* of *presentism*.

**The *state* of the *debate*.** The *truthmaker* *objection* is *the* *central* *problem* *for* *presentism*, and it is *unresolved*. The *presentist* *must* *either* *provide* a *truthmaker* (ersatz, tensed) or *reject* the *truthmaker* *principle* (and *pay* the *price*).

### 2.4 The rate-of-passage objection

A *related* *objection* *concerns* the *rate* of *passage*.

**The *argument* (Smart 1949).**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If time *passes*, it *passes* *at* a *rate*.} \\
2. & \text{The *rate* would be *seconds* *per* *second* — a *trivial* *identity*.} \\
3. & \text{If the *rate* is *seconds* *per* *second*, the *passage* is *not* *a* *change*.} \\
4. & \text{If the *rate* is *something* *else*, we need a *second* *time* *dimension* *in* *which* the *rate* is *measured*, and *that* *dimension* *requires* its *own* *passage*, and so on.} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The *passage* of *time* is *unintelligible*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *category* *mistake*.** The *passage* *of* *time* is *not* a *process* *that* *takes* *time*. It is the *form* of *temporal* *becoming*, and the *demand* for a *rate* is a *category* *mistake* (Lowe 1987; Maudlin 2002).

**(ii) The *no-rate* *reply*.** The *A-theorist* *can* *deny* that *passage* *has* a *rate*: passage is *primitive*, and the *rate* *question* is *misguided*. (The *reply* is *contested*; the *B-theorist* *argues* that the *denial* *makes* *passage* *unintelligible*.)

**The *state* of the *debate*.** The *objection* is *serious*, and it is *not* *decisively* *answered*. The *A-theorist* *either* *accepts* *a* *second* *time* *dimension* (a *radical* *option*; see the *optional* *thread*) or *denies* the *need* for a *rate* (the *standard* *reply*).

### 2.5 The reality of the past and the future

**The *question*.** Are the past and the future *real*?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Presentism** | No | Yes | No |
| **Growing block** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Moving spotlight** | Yes | Yes (privileged) | Yes |
| **Eternalism (B-theory)** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **No-future (open future)** | Yes | Yes | No (but different from growing block: the future is *indeterminate*) |

**The *argument* *for* *the* *reality* *of* *the* *past*.** Memories are *about* the *past*, and *true* *memories* *require* the *past* to *exist*. If the *past* *does* not *exist*, *memory* *is* *not* *about* *anything*, and the *truth* of *memory* *claims* *cannot* be *explained*. (The *presentist* *reply*: memory is *about* the *present* *trace* of the *past*, not the *past* itself.)

**The *argument* *for* *the* *openness* *of* *the* *future*.** If the *future* *is* *real*, then *future* *events* are *as* *fixed* as *past* *events*, and *fatalism* *follows*. (The *B-theorist* *reply*: the *argument* *confuses* *semantic* *determinacy* with *causal* *necessity*; the *future* *is* *determinate* but *not* *necessitated*.)

**The *state* of the *debate*.** The *reality* of *the* *past* and *future* is *contested*, and the *debate* is *connected* to the *theory* of *persistence* (§4) and to the *theory* of *personal* *identity* (Ch. 26).

## 3. Temporal Experience and the Specious Present

### 3.1 The datum

**The *datum*.** We *experience* *time*: we *hear* *melodies*, *see* *motion*, *feel* *duration*, and *sense* *passage*. The *experience* is *not* *of* *an* *instant*; it is *of* a *stretch* — a *duration* *that* *includes* the *past*, the *present*, and the *anticipated* *future*.

**The *specious* *present*.** William James (*The Principles of Psychology*, 1890, Ch. XV) *introduced* the *term*: “the *specious* *present*” is the *interval* *of* *time* *that* is *experienced* *as* *present* — *roughly* 2–3 *seconds* (James *suggested* 12 *seconds*; the *contemporary* *estimate* is *shorter*).

**The *structure*.** The *specious* *present* *contains* a *“fringe”* — the *retention* of the *immediate* *past* and the *protention* of the *immediate* *future* — and the *fringe* *is* *not* *a* *memory* *or* *an* *anticipation*; it is *part* of the *present* *experience*. The *phenomenology* is *Husserl’s* (*Lectures on the Phenomenology of Internal Time-Consciousness*, 1905/1928): the *experience* of a *melody* *involves* *retention* (the *just-past* *note*), *primal* *impression* (the *now* *note*), and *protention* (the *coming* *note*).

**The *problem*.** The *specious* *present* *raises* the *question* of *how* the *brain* *constructs* the *experience* of *duration* from *instantaneous* *inputs*, and the *answer* is *not* *obvious*.

### 3.2 The psychology

The *empirical* *literature* *is* *rich*, and it is *relevant* to the *metaphysics*.

**The *findings*.**

| Phenomenon | What it is | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **The flash-lag effect** | A *flash* *aligned* with a *moving* *object* *appears* *behind* the *object* | Nijhawan 1994 |
| **Chronostasis** | A *saccade* *makes* a *brief* *stimulus* *appear* *longer* | Yarrow et al. 2001 |
| **The oddball effect** | A *novel* *stimulus* *appears* *longer* than a *familiar* one | Tse et al. 2004 |
| **Libet's timing** | The *awareness* of a *decision* *comes* *after* the *neural* *onset* (the *readiness* *potential*) | Libet et al. 1983 |
| **Postdiction** | *Perception* *is* *retrospectively* *edited* — the *brain* *changes* what *was* *seen* | Eagleman & Sejnowski 2000 |

**The *interpretation*.** The *findings* *suggest* that *temporal* *experience* is *constructed*: the *brain* *integrates* *information* *over* a *window* (the *specious* *present*) and *assigns* *timestamps* *retrospectively*. The *experience* of *“now”* is *not* a *direct* *readout* of *the* *present*; it is a *construction* *that* *lags* the *world* *by* *~80–200 ms*.

**The *philosophical* *significance*.** If *temporal* *experience* *is* *constructed*, then the *A-theorist’s* *argument* *from* *experience* — that we *experience* *passage* — is *weakened*. The *experience* *of* *passage* may be a *construction* *of* the *brain*, *not* a *readout* of *a* *real* *feature* of the *world*.

### 3.3 Does experience support the A-theory?

**The *argument* *from* *experience*** (Craig 2000; Paul 2010; Prosser 2016). The *experience* of *passage* is *phenomenologically* *irreducible*: the *“flow”* of *time* *is* *given* *in* *experience*, and the *B-theory* *cannot* *account* for it. The *experience* is *evidence* for the *A-theory*.

**The *B-theoretic* *reply*** (Deng 2013; Hoerl 2018; Prosser 2016; Skow 2015). The *experience* of *passage* is *compatible* with the *B-theory*: the *experience* is of *succession* (we *remember* the *past* and *anticipate* the *future*), and the *“flow”* is a *feature* of the *way* *we* *represent* *succession*, not of the *world*. The *B-theory* *can* *explain* *why* the *experience* *seems* *to* *be* *of* *passage* *without* *positing* *passage*.

**The *argument* *from* *illusion*** (Paul 2010; Duncan 2015). If the *experience* of *passage* is *illusory*, the *illusion* *must* be *explained*. The *B-theory* *owes* an *account* of *why* the *illusion* *is* *systematic* and *universal*. (The *reply*: the *illusion* is *explained* by the *asymmetry* of *memory* and *anticipation*, which is *itself* *explained* by the *thermodynamic* *arrow*; §6.)

**The *state* of the *debate*.** The *argument* *from* *experience* is *the* *strongest* *argument* *for* the *A-theory*, and it is *not* *decisive*. The *B-theoretic* *reply* is *promising*, but it is *not* *complete*.

### 3.4 The specious present and the ontology of time

**The *question*.** Does the *specious* *present* *support* the *A-theory* or the *B-theory*?

**The *A-theoretic* *argument*** (Dainton 2000, 2008; Gallagher 2011). The *specious* *present* *is* a *duration* that is *experienced* *as* *present*, and the *experience* *is* *not* *a* *sequence* *of* *instants*; it is a *unified* *whole*. The *unity* *suggests* that the *present* *is* *not* *a* *point* but an *interval*, and the *interval* *supports* the *A-theoretic* *view* of *time* *as* *dynamic*.

**The *B-theoretic* *argument*** (Dainton 2000; Hoerl 2018). The *specious* *present* *is* *compatible* with the *B-theory*: the *experience* *of* *duration* is a *representation* *of* a *temporal* *extension*, and the *extension* *is* a *B-series* *structure*. The *unity* *of* the *specious* *present* *is* a *feature* of *representation*, not of *time*.

**The *verdict*.** The *specious* *present* *is* *evidence* *for* *neither* *view* *decisively*. It *constrains* the *theories*, but it *does* not *settle* the *debate*.

## 4. Endurantism, Perdurantism, Stage Theory

### 4.1 The problem of persistence

**The *problem*.** Objects *persist* through time — they *exist* *at* *different* *times* — and the *persistence* *raises* a *question*: what *is* the *relation* *between* an *object* and the *times* at which it *exists*?

**The *three* *theories*.**

| Theory | What an object is | How it persists | Proponents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Endurantism** | A *three-dimensional* *whole* that is *wholly* *present* at *each* *time* | It *endures*: it is *wholly* *present* at every moment of its existence | Aristotle; Chisholm; van Inwagen; Wiggins; Lowe |
| **Perdurantism** | A *four-dimensional* *whole* with *temporal* *parts* | It *perdures*: it is *extended* in time, and its *temporal* *parts* are *present* at different times | Lewis; Sider; Heller; Quine |
| **Stage theory** | A *momentary* *stage* (a *three-dimensional* *slice*) | It *persists* by *having* *counterparts* *at* *other* *times* | Hawley; Sider (early); Varzi |

**The *distinction*.**

```
        ENDURANTISM                    PERDURANTISM
        ────────────                   ─────────────
        t1   [ object ]                 t1 ─ [ part ]
        t2   [ object ]                 t2 ─ [ part ]   ← one four-dimensional whole
        t3   [ object ]                 t3 ─ [ part ]

        The object is wholly           The object is the whole;
        present at each time.          the parts are present at each time.
```

### 4.2 Endurantism

**The *thesis*.** An *object* is *wholly* *present* at *every* *time* at which it *exists*. It has *no* *temporal* *parts*; it is *three-dimensional*, and its *relation* to *time* is *that* of *location*, not of *composition*.

**The *argument*.**

**(i) Common sense.** We *say* that the *table* is *the* *same* *table* *today* *as* *yesterday*, and the *“same”* is *numerical* *identity*, not *mere* *similarity*. The *endurantist* *takes* the *ordinary* *language* *at* *face* *value*.

**(ii) The *unity* *of* *the* *object*.** An *object* is a *unity* — a *single* *thing* — and the *unity* is *not* *a* *collection* *of* *parts*. The *perdurantist* *makes* the *object* *a* *collection* *of* *temporal* *parts*, and the *collection* *is* *not* *a* *unity* *in* *the* *same* *sense*.

**(iii) The *no* *temporal* *parts* *intuition*.** We *do* not *experience* *ourselves* *as* *four-dimensional* *worms*; we *experience* *ourselves* *as* *three-dimensional* *beings* *that* *persist*. The *endurantist* *takes* the *phenomenology* *seriously*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *problem* of *temporary* *intrinsics* (§5). The *most* *serious* *objection*, and the *main* *argument* *for* *perdurantism*.

**(ii) The *problem* of *change*.** If the *object* is *wholly* *present* at *both* $t_1$ and $t_2$, and it has *different* *properties* at the *two* *times*, then it has *contradictory* *properties*. The *endurantist* *must* *explain* *how* the *contradiction* is *avoided*.

**(iii) The *problem* of *coincidence*.** If a *statue* and a *lump* of *clay* *occupy* the *same* *space* at the *same* *time*, the *endurantist* *must* *say* *either* that they are *identical* (which *seems* *false*, since they have *different* *persistence* *conditions*) or that they are *distinct* *objects* *that* *coincide* (the *“coincident* *objects”* *view*, which is *controversial*).

### 4.3 Perdurantism

**The *thesis*.** An *object* is a *four-dimensional* *whole* — a *“space-time* *worm”* — that is *extended* in *time*. It has *temporal* *parts* (the *stages*), and each *stage* is *present* at a *different* *time*. The *object* *as* *a* *whole* *is* *not* *present* at *any* *single* *time*; it is *present* *“at”* a *time* *by* *having* a *part* *present* at that *time*.

**The *argument*.**

**(i) The *solution* to the *problem* of *temporary* *intrinsics*.** The *poker* is *straight* at $t_1$ and *bent* at $t_2$ *because* its *$t_1$-stage* is *straight* and its *$t_2$-stage* is *bent*. The *contradiction* *disappears*.

**(ii) The *analogy* with *space*.** We *accept* that an *object* *extends* *across* *space* (it has *spatial* *parts*), and the *extension* *across* *time* is *structurally* *similar*. The *perdurantist* *argues* that the *asymmetry* *between* *space* and *time* is *not* *metaphysically* *fundamental*.

**(iii) The *unity* of *science*.** The *four-dimensional* *picture* is *natural* for *relativity* (Ch. 19, §2; §6.4), and the *perdurantist* *argues* that *metaphysics* *should* *follow* *physics*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *common* *sense* *objection*.** The *perdurantist* *denies* that the *table* is *wholly* *present* *now* — only a *slice* is *present* — and the *denial* *conflicts* with *ordinary* *experience*.

**(ii) The *problem* of *temporal* *parts*.** What *are* *temporal* *parts*? Are they *arbitrary* *slices*, or *natural* *divisions*? The *answer* is *contested*, and the *controversy* *mirrors* the *problem* of *spatial* *parts* (Ch. 15, §4.3).

**(iii) The *problem* of *the* *many*.** If the *object* *is* a *four-dimensional* *whole*, then there are *many* *overlapping* *four-dimensional* *wholes* that *differ* by *tiny* *temporal* *extensions*, and the *question* of *which* is *the* *object* is *underdetermined*.

### 4.4 Stage theory

**The *thesis*.** The *fundamental* *entities* are *momentary* *stages*, not *four-dimensional* *wholes*. The *object* *persists* *by* *having* *counterparts* *at* *other* *times*, and the *counterpart* *relation* is a *similarity* *relation* (Lewis’s *counterpart* *theory* applied to *time*; Ch. 16, §4.2).

**The *argument*.**

**(i) The *solution* to the *problem* of *temporary* *intrinsics*.** The *poker* *is* a *$t_1$-stage* that is *straight*; the *$t_2$-stage* is *bent*. The *stages* are *distinct*, and the *contradiction* *disappears*.

**(ii) The *solution* to the *problem* of *the* *many*.** There is *one* *stage* *now*, and the *question* of *which* *four-dimensional* *whole* *I* am *is* *misplaced*: *I* am the *stage*, and the *stages* *at* *other* *times* are *counterparts*, not *parts*.

**(iii) The *parsimony*.** The *stage* *theory* *avoids* the *four-dimensional* *wholes* (it *posits* *only* *stages*), and it *avoids* the *endurantist’s* *mysterious* *“wholly* *present”* *relation*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *Humphrey* *objection*** (Ch. 16, §4.3). The *stage* *theory* *denies* that *I* *was* *a* *child*; *I* *was* *only* a *counterpart* of *the* *child*. The *denial* *conflicts* with the *intuition* of *identity* *across* *time*.

**(ii) The *problem* of *the* *many* *thinkers*.** If *I* am a *stage*, and the *stage* *thinks*, then the *counterparts* *also* *think*. Are *they* *also* *me*? The *stage* *theory* *must* *explain* *why* *only* *the* *present* *stage* *is* *“me.”*

**(iii) The *problem* of *responsibility*.** If *I* am *not* *identical* to *the* *person* *who* *committed* *the* *crime*, *why* *am* *I* *responsible*? (The *reply*: *responsibility* *tracks* *counterpart* *relations*, not *identity*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 4.5 The state of the debate

| Theory | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Endurantism** | Wholly present at each time | Common sense; unity | Temporary intrinsics |
| **Perdurantism** | Four-dimensional whole | Solves temporary intrinsics | Denies common sense |
| **Stage theory** | Momentary stages | Solves temporary intrinsics; parsimony | Humphrey objection; many thinkers |

The *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *connected* to the *A/B* *debate*: the *endurantist* is *naturally* an *A-theorist* (both *privilege* the *present*), and the *perdurantist* is *naturally* a *B-theorist* (both *spatialize* the *temporal* *order*). The *connections* are *not* *logical* *entailments*, but they are *revealing* of the *structure* of the *debate*.

## 5. Temporary Intrinsics, Fission, and Recombination

### 5.1 The problem of temporary intrinsics

**The *problem*.** A *poker* is *straight* at $t_1$ and *bent* at $t_2$. *Straightness* and *bentness* are *intrinsic* *properties* (they do not *depend* on the *object’s* *relations* to *other* *things*). If the *poker* is *wholly* *present* at *both* $t_1$ and $t_2$, then it has *both* *properties* *simpliciter*, and the *properties* are *incompatible*.

**The *argument* (Lewis 1986, pp. 202–204).**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Endurantism: the *poker* is *wholly* *present* at } t_1 \text{ and at } t_2. \\
2. & \text{The *poker* is *straight* at } t_1 \text{ and *bent* at } t_2. \\
3. & \text{Straightness and bentness are *intrinsic* *properties*.} \\
4. & \text{If the *poker* has *both* *properties* *simpliciter*, it is *both* *straight* and *bent* — *contradiction*.} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{Endurantism is *false* (or *intrinsic* *properties* *are* *relational* — which *changes* the *subject*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.**

**(i) Relationalism.** The *properties* are *relational*: the *poker* *is* *straight* *at* $t_1$ *in* the *sense* *of* *bearing* the *relation* *being-straight-at* to $t_1$. The *properties* are *not* *intrinsic* in the *Lewisian* *sense*; they are *time-relative*.

**The *objection*.** The *response* *makes* *all* *properties* *relational*, and the *intuition* is that *shape* is *intrinsic*. The *relationalist* *reply*: the *intuition* is a *pre-theoretic* *prejudice*, and the *relational* *analysis* *is* the *correct* *analysis*.

**(ii) Adverbialism.** The *poker* *is* *straightly-at-$t_1$* and *bently-at-$t_2$* — the *properties* are *adverbial* *modifications* of the *way* the *poker* *has* the *property*. The *response* *is* *obscure*, and it is *widely* *rejected*.

**(iii) The *index* *theory* (Haslanger 1989). The *properties* are *time-indexed*: the *poker* *has* the *property* *being-straight-at-$t_1$* and the *property* *being-bent-at-$t_2$*. The *properties* are *distinct*, and the *contradiction* *disappears*. The *theory* *accepts* that *properties* are *temporalized*, and it *argues* that the *temporalization* *is* *not* *a* *defect*.

**The *objection*.** The *index* *theory* *is* *relationalism* *in* *disguise*: the *property* *being-straight-at-$t_1$* *is* a *relation* *to* $t_1$.

**(iv) The *denial* *of* *the* *incompatibility*.** The *properties* *are* *had* *at* *different* *times*, and the *having* *is* *temporal*: the *poker* *is* *straight* *at* $t_1$ *and* *bent* *at* $t_2$, and the *“and”* *is* *not* *a* *conjunction* *of* *simultaneous* *properties*. The *response* *is* *the* *endurantist* *orthodoxy*, and the *question* is *whether* it *is* *a* *theory* or a *restatement* *of* the *data*.

**The *perdurantist* *response*.** The *poker* *has* *temporal* *parts*; the *$t_1$-part* *is* *straight*, and the *$t_2$-part* *is* *bent*. The *contradiction* *disappears* *because* the *properties* *are* *had* *by* *different* *parts*.

**The *stage* *theorist* *response*.** The *$t_1$-stage* *is* *straight*, and the *$t_2$-stage* *is* *bent*. The *contradiction* *disappears* *because* the *stages* *are* *distinct*.

### 5.2 The problem of fission

**The *case*.** A *person* — *call* *her* *Alice* — *undergoes* a *procedure* that *splits* her *brain* into *two* *hemispheres*, each *transplanted* into a *new* *body*. The *two* *resulting* *persons* — *Alice-Left* and *Alice-Right* — *both* *remember* *Alice’s* *life*, *both* *have* her *personality*, and *both* *regard* *themselves* as *Alice*.

**The *question*.** Is *Alice* *identical* to *Alice-Left*? To *Alice-Right*? To *both*? To *neither*?

**The *problem*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Identity is *transitive*: if } a = b \text{ and } b = c, \text{ then } a = c. \\
2. & \text{If *Alice* = *Alice-Left* and *Alice* = *Alice-Right*, then *Alice-Left* = *Alice-Right*.} \\
3. & \text{But *Alice-Left* and *Alice-Right* are *distinct* persons (they are *spatially* *separated* and *psychologically* *divergent*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Alice cannot be *identical* to *both*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *endurantist* *response*.** *Alice* is *identical* to *one* of them (the *one* that *preserves* the *relevant* *continuity*), and *not* to the *other*. The *choice* is *underdetermined*, and the *underdetermination* is a *problem*.

**(ii) The *perdurantist* *response*.** *Alice* is a *four-dimensional* *whole*; *Alice-Left* and *Alice-Right* are *temporal* *parts* of *different* *wholes* that *share* the *pre-fission* *stages*. Neither is *identical* to *Alice* (because *identity* is *transitive* and the *wholes* are *distinct*), but both are *continuous* with her. The *perdurantist* *accepts* that *identity* is *not* *preserved*, and *argues* that *identity* is *not* *what* *matters* (Ch. 26, §4).

**(iii) The *stage* *theorist* *response*.** *Alice* is a *stage*; *Alice-Left* and *Alice-Right* are *counterparts* of the *stage* at *later* *times*. The *question* of *identity* is *replaced* by the *question* of *counterpart* *relations*, and the *relations* are *many*.

**The *significance*.** The *fission* *case* is *the* *strongest* *argument* *against* the *endurantist* *view* *of* *personal* *identity* (Ch. 26, §4), and it is *the* *strongest* *argument* *for* the *perdurantist* *or* *stage* *view*.

### 5.3 The problem of recombination

**The *case*.** A *table* is *composed* of *four* *legs* and a *top*. *Can* the *same* *legs* and *top* *exist* *without* the *table*? *Can* the *table* *exist* *without* *one* of its *legs*?

**The *problem*.** The *table* *supervenes* on its *parts*, but the *supervenience* *is* *not* *identity*: the *parts* *could* *exist* *without* the *table*, and the *table* *could* *exist* *without* *one* of the *parts* (if the *part* is *replaced*). The *recombination* *raises* the *question* of *which* *modal* *profile* *belongs* to the *table* and which to the *parts*.

**The *significance*.** The *problem* is *connected* to the *essentialism* of Ch. 16, §3, and to the *mereology* of Ch. 15, §4.3. The *recombination* *cases* *test* the *essentialist* *theories*, and they *generate* the *“problem* *of* *the* *many”* (Lewis 1993): *many* *overlapping* *objects* *satisfy* the *criteria* *for* *being* *the* *table*, and the *question* of *which* is *the* *table* is *underdetermined*.

### 5.4 The state of the debate

The *problem* of *temporary* *intrinsics* is the *engine* of the *persistence* *debate*, and it is *unresolved*. The *three* *theories* *each* *solve* the *problem*, and *each* *pays* a *cost*:

| Theory | Solution to temporary intrinsics | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Endurantism** | Relationalism / index theory | Properties become relational; or the theory is a restatement |
| **Perdurantism** | Temporal parts | Denies that the object is wholly present |
| **Stage theory** | Distinct stages | Denies that I was a child |

The *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to *common sense*, *parsimony*, and *explanatory* power (Ch. 4, §3). The *debate* is *one* of the *most* *technical* in *contemporary* *metaphysics*, and it is *one* of the *most* *consequential* for *personal* *identity* (Ch. 26) and *ethics* (Ch. 45).

## 6. The Arrow of Time, Entropy, and Physics

### 6.1 The arrows

**The *observation*.** Time has a *direction*: the *past* is *fixed* and the *future* is *open*; *causes* *precede* *effects*; *memories* *are* of the *past*; *entropy* *increases*. The *direction* is *not* a *feature* of the *fundamental* *laws* (they are *time-reversal* *symmetric*); it is a *feature* of the *initial* *conditions* and the *statistical* *behavior* of *large* *systems*.

**The *arrows*.**

| Arrow | Direction | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermodynamic** | Entropy increases | The second law; statistical mechanics |
| **Cosmological** | The universe expands | The Big Bang; the scale factor |
| **Radiative** | Radiation is retarded | The electromagnetic arrow; the Sommerfeld radiation condition |
| **Causal** | Causes precede effects | The causal arrow; the interventionist account (Ch. 18) |
| **Psychological** | Memory is of the past; anticipation of the future | The neural arrow; the thermodynamic arrow |

**The *question*.** Are the *arrows* *independent*, or is there a *fundamental* *arrow* from which the others *derive*?

### 6.2 Entropy and the past hypothesis

**The *statistical* *mechanics***. Boltzmann *argued* that the *second* *law* is *statistical*: entropy *increases* *because* high-entropy *macrostates* are *overwhelmingly* *more* *numerous* than low-entropy *ones*. The *arrow* is a *consequence* of the *statistics*, not of the *dynamics*.

**The *problem*.** The *statistics* *predicts* that entropy *increases* *in* the *future*, but it *also* *predicts* that entropy *was* *higher* in the *past* (the *statistical* *reasoning* is *time-symmetric*). The *observed* *arrow* — entropy *increases* *from* a *low-entropy* *past* — *requires* a *low-entropy* *initial* *condition*.

**The *past* *hypothesis*** (Albert 2000). The *universe* *began* in a *low-entropy* *state*, and the *hypothesis* is a *law-like* *posit* that *explains* the *arrow*. The *hypothesis* is *not* *derived* from the *dynamics*; it is *added* to the *theory*.

**The *double* *standard* *objection* (Price 1996; Earman 2006). The *past* *hypothesis* *explains* the *arrow* *by* *positing* a *special* *initial* *condition*, but the *specialness* is *itself* *unexplained*. Why is the *past* *special* and the *future* not? The *reply*: the *past* *hypothesis* is a *brute* *fact*, and the *bruteness* is *acceptable* (it is the *only* *option*).

### 6.3 The arrow and the metaphysics of time

**The *connection*.** The *B-theory* *fits* the *statistical* *picture*: the *block* *universe* *has* an *asymmetric* *distribution* of *entropy*, and the *arrow* is a *statistical* *feature* of the *distribution*. The *A-theory* *needs* a *further* *explanation* of *why* the *passage* *aligns* with the *entropy* *gradient*.

**The *argument*.** The *A-theorist* *can* *accept* the *statistical* *picture* and *add* that *passage* is the *form* in which the *statistical* *asymmetry* is *experienced*. The *B-theorist* *can* *accept* the *picture* and *deny* that *passage* is *anything* *over* and *above* the *asymmetry*. The *two* *views* are *not* *distinguished* *by* the *physics*; they are *distinguished* by the *metaphysics*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *physics* *constrains* the *metaphysics*, but it does *not* *settle* the *A/B* *debate*. The *philosophical* *question* — *what* *is* *passage*? — is *not* *answered* by the *thermodynamic* *arrow*.

### 6.4 Relativity and the present

**The *argument* *from* *relativity***. In *special* *relativity*, *simultaneity* is *relative* to a *frame*: two events that are *simultaneous* in *one* *frame* are *not* *simultaneous* in *another*. If the *present* is the *set* of *events* *simultaneous* with *now*, then the *present* is *relative* to a *frame* — and the *A-theory*’s *privileged* *present* is *not* *well-defined* (Rietdijk 1966; Putnam 1967).

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *B-theory* *accepts* the *conclusion***. The *block* *universe* is *the* *correct* *picture*, and *relativity* *refutes* the *A-theory*.

**(ii) The *neo-Lorentzian* *response***. There is a *privileged* *frame* (the *ether* *frame*), and *relativity* is a *phenomenological* *theory*; the *privileged* *frame* is *hidden* (Bell 1976, 1987; Maudlin 2012). The *response* *is* *contested*, and it *conflicts* with the *spirit* of *relativity*.

**(iii) The *presentism* *compatible* *response*** (Skow 2009, 2016). *Presentism* can be *reconciled* with *relativity* if *the* *present* is *defined* *by* a *frame* *relative* *to* the *subject*, or if *the* *present* is *not* a *global* *simultaneity* *slice*. The *response* is *promising*, and it is *contested*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *relativity* *argument* is *the* *strongest* *argument* *against* the *A-theory*, and it is *not* *decisive*. The *responses* are *available*, but they *cost*.

### 6.5 The block universe

**The *view*.** The *universe* is a *four-dimensional* *block*; *all* *times* are *equally* *real*; the *passage* is *an* *illusion*.

**The *virtues*.** (i) It *fits* *relativity*. (ii) It *fits* the *B-theory*. (iii) It *provides* a *unified* *account* of *time* and *space*.

**The *costs*.** (i) It *denies* *passage*. (ii) It *makes* the *future* *as* *fixed* as the *past* (the *fatalism* *worry*). (iii) It *denies* *the* *openness* *of* the *future*, which is *central* to *agency* (Ch. 27).

**The *state* of the debate.** The *block* *universe* is the *dominant* *physics*-inspired *view*, and it is *contested* by *philosophers* *who* *take* *passage* *seriously*. The *debate* is *not* *settled*.

## 7. Personal Identity Across Time (Preview of Ch. 26)

### 7.1 The connection

**The *question*.** Is *personal* *identity* *across* *time* *a* *special* *case* of *persistence*, or is it *different* in *kind*?

**The *connection*.** The *theory* of *persistence* (§4) *determines* the *theory* of *personal* *identity*. The *endurantist* *holds* that the *person* *endures* — the *same* *person* is *wholly* *present* at *each* *time* — and the *perdurantist* *holds* that the *person* *perdures* — the *person* is a *four-dimensional* *whole* with *temporal* *parts*. The *stage* *theorist* *holds* that the *person* is a *momentary* *stage*.

### 7.2 The psychological and the biological

**The *two* *families*.**

| Family | What *matters* for identity | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychological** | Continuity of *memory*, *character*, *intention* | Locke; Parfit; Shoemaker |
| **Biological** | Continuity of *organism* (the *animal*) | Olson; van Inwagen; Snowdon |

**The *debate*.** The *psychological* *view* *fits* the *fission* *case* (both *successors* have the *psychological* *continuity*, but *neither* is *identical*), and the *biological* *view* *fits* the *ordinary* *cases* (the *organism* *persists* through *unconsciousness*).

**The *preview*.** Ch. 26 *examines* the *theories* in *detail*, and it *connects* them to the *ethics* of *survival*, *advance* *directives*, and *punishment*.

### 7.3 The *moral* *stakes*

**The *question*.** Does *personal* *identity* *across* *time* *matter* for *morality*?

**The *answer* (Parfit 1984).** *Personal* *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*; *psychological* *continuity* is. The *view* *licenses* a *revised* *ethics*: *responsibility* *for* *future* *selves* is *not* *based* on *identity*, but on *continuity*; the *practices* of *promising*, *punishment*, and *compensation* *are* *reconceived*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *undermines* the *special* *concern* we have for *our* *future* *selves*, and it *licenses* *a* *form* of *self-*indifference. (Parfit’s *reply*: the *concern* is *real*, but it is *based* on *continuity*, not on *identity*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 7.4 The *bridge* to Ch. 26

The *chapter* *ends* with a *preview*: the *theory* of *persistence* *determines* the *theory* of *personal* *identity*, and the *theory* of *personal* *identity* *determines* the *ethics* of *survival*. The *next* *chapter* *takes* *up* the *questions* of *causation* and *laws* — the *modal* *structure* of *the* *world* — and *then* the *book* *turns* to *mind* (Part IV), where the *questions* of *personal* *identity* and *free* *will* are *treated* in *full*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The A/B distinction is real.** The *distinction* between *tensed* and *tenseless* *discourse* is *fundamental*, and the *two* *theories* are *the* *main* *options*.
- **McTaggart's paradox is a serious challenge.** The *paradox* is *not* *dissolved* by *any* *simple* *reply*, and the *debate* *between* the *indexical* *analysis* and the *primitive* *tense* *view* is *unresolved*.
- **The truthmaker objection to presentism is serious.** The *presentist* *must* *either* *provide* a *truthmaker* (ersatz) or *reject* the *truthmaker* *principle*.
- **The problem of temporary intrinsics is the engine of the persistence debate.** The *perdurantist* *and* the *stage* *theorist* *solve* it; the *endurantist* *must* *relationalize* or *index* the *properties*.
- **The arrow of time is a statistical and cosmological phenomenon.** The *past* *hypothesis* is the *most* *influential* *account*, and it is *contested*.
- **Relativity challenges the A-theory.** The *Rietdijk–Putnam* *argument* is *the* *strongest* *physics*-based *objection*, and the *responses* are *available* but *costly*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *reality* of *tense*.** Is tense *fundamental* (A-theory) or *derived* (B-theory)?
- **The *version* of the A-theory.** Presentism, growing block, moving spotlight, or fragmentalism?
- **The *solution* to *McTaggart*.** Is the *indexical* *analysis* *adequate*, or is *primitive* *tense* *required*?
- **The *theory* of *persistence*.** Endurantism, perdurantism, or stage theory?
- **The *connection* between *time* and *modality*.** Is the *future* *open*? Is *fatalism* *true*?
- **The *arrow* *of* *time*.** Is the *entropy* *gradient* *fundamental*, or is it *derived* from the *past* *hypothesis*?

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a *case* *study* in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *A/B* *distinction*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *fission* case, the *poker* case — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *tense* *operators*, the *B-series* *structure* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *time* is a *field* in *transition*. The *A-theory* is *intuitive* but *faces* *relativity*; the *B-theory* is *physics-friendly* but *denies* *passage*; the *persistence* *debate* is *unresolved*; the *arrow* *debate* is *technical* and *contested*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled answer*, but a *sharper understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Presentism** | Only the present exists | Intuitive | Truthmaker objection |
| **Growing block** | Past and present exist | Solves truthmaker | Future truthmakers |
| **Moving spotlight** | All times exist; one is privileged | Combines A and B | The privileged present is mysterious |
| **Eternalism (B-theory)** | All times exist; tense is indexical | Fits relativity | Denies passage |
| **Endurantism** | Wholly present at each time | Common sense | Temporary intrinsics |
| **Perdurantism** | Four-dimensional whole | Solves temporary intrinsics | Denies wholly present |
| **Stage theory** | Momentary stages | Solves temporary intrinsics; parsimony | Humphrey objection |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *two* *time* *dimensions*.** If the *A-theory* *requires* a *rate* of *passage*, it *may* *require* a *second* *time* *dimension* in which the *rate* is *measured*. The *view* (Broad 1923; Thomson 1965; Schlesinger 1980) is *radical*, and it is *contested* (the *regress* argument; the *parsimony* argument). The *two-dimensional* *view* *fits* some *versions* of the *A-theory*, and it is *one* of the *most* *interesting* *technical* *issues* in the *field*.

**2. The *relativity* *of* *simultaneity*.** The *Rietdijk–Putnam* *argument* is *influential* and *contested*. The *neo-Lorentzian* *response* (Bell, Maudlin) *postulates* a *privileged* *frame* that is *empirically* *undetectable* (the *“hidden* *variable”* *of* *time*). The *debate* is *connected* to the *interpretation* of *quantum* *mechanics* (Ch. 19, §5; Ch. 41, §4).

**3. The *physics* of *the* *arrow*.** The *past* *hypothesis* (Albert 2000) is the *most* *influential* *account* of the *arrow*, and it is *contested* (Earman 2006; Price 1996). The *debate* is *connected* to the *interpretation* of *statistical* *mechanics* and to the *direction* of *causation* (Ch. 18, §2).

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *time*.** The *Buddhist* *tradition* *developed* a *radical* *momentariness* (*kṣaṇika-vāda*) that *anticipates* the *stage* *theory*, and the *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* *tradition* *developed* a *realist* *theory* of *time* as a *substance* that *anticipates* the *A-theory*. The *Abhidharma* *debates* on *temporal* *identity* (the *“same”* *person* *across* *moments*) are *sophisticated*, and they *are* *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *temporal* *experience* and *artificial* *intelligence*.** The *AI* *systems* *process* *sequences* (the *transformer* *architectures*, the *temporal* *models*), and the *philosophical* *questions* — *do* *they* *experience* *duration*? *is* *their* *“now”* *a* *construction*? — are *new* and *urgent* (Ch. 29, §3). The *answers* *depend* on the *theory* of *temporal* *experience* of §3, and the *theory* *needs* to *be* *general* *enough* to *cover* *both* *biological* and *artificial* *systems*.

## Summary

- **The A-theory** holds that tense is *real* and *irreducible*; the **B-theory** holds that tense is *indexical* and the B-series is *fundamental*. The *diagram* of the *positions* is the *backbone* of the debate.
- **McTaggart’s paradox** argues that the A-series is *contradictory* (every event is past, present, and future). The *B-theoretic* *reply* is the *indexical* *analysis*; the *A-theoretic* *reply* is *primitive* *tense*. The *regress* is the *central* *difficulty*.
- **Presentism** (only the present exists) *faces* the *truthmaker* *objection*: *true* *claims* *about* the *past* *need* *truthmakers*, and the *past* *does* not *exist*. The *responses* are *ersatz* *presentism*, the *rejection* of the *truthmaker* *principle*, and the *tensed* *truthmaker*.
- **Temporal experience** *supports* *neither* the *A-theory* nor the *B-theory* *decisively*. The *specious* *present* is a *construction* of the *brain*, and the *experience* of *passage* is *compatible* with the *B-theory*.
- **Endurantism**, **perdurantism**, and **stage theory** are the *three* *theories* of *persistence*. The *problem* of *temporary* *intrinsics* is the *engine* of the debate: the *perdurantist* and *stage* *theorist* *solve* it by *positing* *distinct* *entities*; the *endurantist* *must* *relationalize* the *properties*.
- **The problem of fission** is the *strongest* *argument* *against* the *endurantist* *view* of *personal* *identity*, and it *supports* the *perdurantist* or *stage* *view*.
- **The arrow of time** is a *statistical* and *cosmological* phenomenon. The *past* *hypothesis* (Albert) is the *most* *influential* *account*, and it is *contested*. The *arrow* is *connected* to *memory*, *causation*, and *agency*.
- **Relativity** *challenges* the *A-theory* via the *relativity* of *simultaneity*. The *responses* are *neo-Lorentzian* (a *privileged* *frame*), *compatibility* (the *present* is *frame-relative*), and *acceptance* of the *B-theory*.
- **Personal identity** *across* *time* is *connected* to the *theory* of *persistence*, and it is the *subject* of Ch. 26. The *psychological* and *biological* *views* are the *two* *families*, and the *fission* *case* is the *test*.
