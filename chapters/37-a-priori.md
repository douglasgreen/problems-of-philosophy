# Chapter 36 — The A Priori, Analyticity, and the Philosophy of Logic

> “How are synthetic a priori judgments possible?”
> — Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), B19, the question that organised the modern theory of knowledge

> “Logic is not a science of how we think, but of how we must think if we are to think at all.”
> — a formulation of Frege’s anti-psychologism, in the spirit of *Grundgesetze* (1893), Introduction

> “If logic is true by convention, we need logic to derive the consequences of the conventions; the convention cannot precede the logic.”
> — W. V. O. Quine, “Truth by Convention” (1936), in effect

---

Chapter 35 examined the *apparatus* of formal logic and ended with a *question* it could not answer: **is logic revisable?** The question is the *point* at which the *philosophy* of logic meets the *theory* of knowledge. If logic is *a priori* — known independently of experience, and necessary — then the *revision* is impossible in principle, and the *deviant* logics are *mistaken*. If logic is *revisable* — a *theory* like any other, *chosen* by the *theoretical* *virtues* — then the *a priori* is *thinner* than the tradition *supposed*, and the *deviant* logics are *live* *options*.

This chapter examines the *foundations* of logic: the *a priori*, *analyticity*, *conventionalism*, *applicability*, and *logical knowledge*. It is the *metaphysics* and the *epistemology* of the *apparatus* that Chapter 35 *deployed*. The chapter's *thesis* is that logic is the *best* *test case* for the theory of the *a priori*, because it is the *most* *secure* case (the *laws* of logic seem *undeniable*) and the *most* *contested* (the *revisability* is a live debate). The *resolution* is not a *verdict* but a *diagnosis*: logic is *a priori* in the *thin* sense (Ch. 9, §3.4) — its *warrant* is not *empirical* — and the *thinness* is *compatible* with the *revisability*, because the *warrant* is *structural* (the *meaning* of the *connectives*) and the *structure* is *contestable* (the *logical* pluralism).

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 states Kant's question as the *background*. §2 examines the *analyticity* of logic. §3 examines *conventionalism* and its *failure*. §4 examines the *applicability* of logic. §5 examines *conceptual* truths and *explication*. §6 examines *logical* *knowledge* and the *normativity* of logic. §7 applies the framework to the *a priori* in *ethics* and *modality*. §8 gives an honest verdict.

## 1. Kant's Question and the Status of Logic

### 1.1 The peculiarity of logic

**The *datum***. The *laws* of logic are *the most certain* *propositions* we *possess* (the *identity*, the *non-contradiction*, the *excluded middle*), and their *certainty* is *not* *empirical* (no *experiment* could *refute* them *without* *presupposing* them), and their *certainty* is *not* *analytic* in the *trivial* sense (the *laws* are *not* *mere* *tautologies*; they *govern* the *inferences*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* of logic are *certain* (we *cannot* *coherently* *doubt* them).} \\
2. & \text{The *certainty* is *not* *empirical* (the *empirical* *evidence* *presupposes* the *laws*).} \\
3. & \text{The *certainty* is *not* *trivial* (the *laws* *do* *substantive* *work* in the *inference*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *laws* of logic are *a* *priori*, *necessary*, and *substantive* (the *Kantian* *characterisation*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. Logic is the *paradigm* case of the *a* *priori*: if any *knowledge* is *a* *priori*, logical *knowledge* is. And if the *a* *priori* is *problematic*, logic is the *place* where the *problem* is *most* *acute*.

### 1.2 Kant's question

**The *question***. *How* is *a* *priori* *knowledge* *possible*? — the *question* that *organises* the *Critique* *of* *Pure* *Reason* (Ch. 9, §2.1).

**Kant's *answer***. The *a* *priori* *knowledge* is *possible* because the *mind* *imposes* its *forms* (the *space*, the *time*, the *categories*) on the *manifold* of *intuition*, and the *forms* are *the* *conditions* of *experience*. The *synthetic* *a* *priori* (the *mathematics*, the *physics*) is *possible* because the *objects* of *experience* *conform* to the *forms* (Ch. 20, §2.1).

**The *application* to *logic***. The *laws* of logic are *the* *forms* of *thought* (the *categories*), and their *necessity* is the *necessity* of the *conditions* of *experience*. The *view* *grounds* the *a* *priori* *status* of logic in the *transcendental* *structure* of the *mind*.

**The *objections*** (Ch. 20, §2.4, §2.5).

**(i) The *neglected* *alternative***. The *a* *priori* *knowledge* *may* be *about* the *world* *in* *itself* (the *realist* *alternative*), and the *Kantian* *argument* *does* *not* *refute* the *alternative*.

**(ii) The *psychologism* *objection*** (Frege, *Foundations of Arithmetic*, 1884, §26; *The Basic Laws of Arithmetic*, 1893, Introduction). The *Kantian* *account* *makes* the *logical* *laws* *depend* on the *mind*, and the *dependence* is *false* (the *laws* are *objective*, and the *mind* *conforms* to them, not *vice* *versa*).

**The *significance***. The *Kantian* *answer* is *influential*, and it is *contested* (the *realist* *alternative*, the *Fregean* *anti-psychologism*), and the *contention* is the *subject* of the *chapter*.

### 1.3 The modern collapse of the analytic

**The *thesis*** (the *logical* *positivists*; Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, Ch. 1; Carnap, *The Logical Syntax of Language*, 1934). The *laws* of logic are *analytic* (they are *true* in *virtue* of *meaning*), and the *analytic* *is* the *a* *priori* (the *two* *notions* *coincide*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Analytic} \ = \ \text{A priori} \ = \ \text{Necessary}
$$

**The *argument*. The *laws* of logic are *true* in *virtue* of the *meanings* of the *logical* *constants* (the *definition* of “and”, “or”, “not”), and the *truth* in *virtue* of *meaning* is *analytic*, and the *analytic* *is* *known* *a* *priori* (the *disquotation* of the *meanings*).

**The *virtue***. The *framework* *unifies* the *a* *priori*, the *analytic*, and the *necessary*, and it *grounds* the *certainty* of logic in the *meanings* of the *connectives* (the *conventions*).

**The *collapse*** (Quine, “Two Dogmas of Empiricism,” 1951; Ch. 9, §2.4). The *framework* *collapses* because the *analytic*/*synthetic* *distinction* *fails* (the *circularity* of the *definition*, the *holism* of the *web*), and the *collapse* *leaves* the *a* *priori* *without* a *foundation*. The *laws* of logic *remain* *central* in the *web*, and the *centrality* is *not* a *justification* (the *centrality* is *a* *degree* of *entrenchment*, not a *kind* of *warrant*).

**The *significance***. The *collapse* is the *starting* *point* of the *contemporary* *debate*: the *a* *priori* is *thin* (Ch. 9, §3.4), and the *thinness* *raises* the *question* of the *status* of the *logical* *laws*.

### 1.4 What survives

**The *survivors***.

| The *notion* | The *status* | The *source* |
|---|---|---|
| **A priori** | *Thin* (a *kind* of *warrant*, not a *faculty*) | Ch. 9, §3.4 |
| **Analytic** | *Epistemic* (the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge*) | Boghossian (1996); Ch. 9, §2.5 |
| **Necessary** | *Metaphysical* (a *feature* of the *world*, not of the *language*) | Kripke (1980); Ch. 16, §3.3 |
| **Logical** | *Formal* (the *validity* in *virtue* of the *form*) | Ch. 35, §2 |

**The *consequence***. The *classical* *identification* (the *a* *priori* = the *necessary* = the *analytic*) is *broken* (Ch. 9, §3.3), and the *broken* *identification* *leaves* the *three* *notions* *independent*, and the *independence* is the *framework* of the *chapter*.

## 2. The Analyticity of Logic

### 2.1 The classical conception

**The *thesis***. The *laws* of logic are *analytic*: they are *true* in *virtue* of the *meanings* of the *logical* *constants*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *law* "if } p \text{ and } q, \text{ then } p" \text{ is *true* in *virtue* of the *meaning* of "and".} \\
2. & \text{The *truth* in *virtue* of *meaning* is *analytic* (the *Kantian*/*Fregean* *definition*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *law* is *analytic*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *conception* *explains* the *certainty* of the *laws* (the *meaning* is *transparent* to the *competent* *speaker*), and it *avoids* the *appeal* to a *mysterious* *faculty*.

**The *objections*** (Quine, “Carnap and Logical Truth,” 1960; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(i) The *circularity* *objection***. The *law* is *derived* from the *meaning* of “and”, and the *meaning* is *specified* by the *law* (the *inference* *rules*), and the *specification* is *circular* (the *meaning* *presupposes* the *law*, and the *law* *presupposes* the *meaning*).

**The *reply***(Boghossian, “Analyticity Reconsidered,” 1996; Ch. 9, §2.5). The *epistemic* *conception* of the *analytic*: the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge* of the *law*, and the *sufficiency* is *not* *circular* (the *grasp* is a *cognitive* *state*, and the *knowledge* is its *consequence*).

**The *objection* to the *reply***. The *reply* *assumes* that the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *transparent* (the *competent* *speaker* *knows* the *meaning*), and the *transparency* is *contested* (the *semantic* *externalism*; Ch. 25, §4; the *Twin* *Earth* *cases*).

**(ii) The *meaning*-*variance* *objection*** (Putnam, “Is Logic Empirical?,” 1968; Ch. 35, §7.2). The *laws* are *true* in *virtue* of the *meanings*, and the *meanings* can be *changed* (the *quantum* *logic*), and the *change* *shows* that the *laws* are *not* *necessary* (the *necessity* *depends* on the *meanings*).

**The *reply***. The *change* of *meaning* is *not* a *revision* of the *logic* (it is a *different* *logic*), and the *reply* *preserves* the *necessity* *relative* to the *meanings*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *holism* *objection***. The *meaning* of a *logical* *constant* *depends* on the *whole* *theory* (the *inferential* *role*; Ch. 31, §3.4), and the *dependence* *undermines* the *local* *analyticity* (the *law* is *not* *derivable* from the *local* *meaning*).

**The *reply**. The *meaning* *includes* the *structural* *features* (the *introduction* and *elimination* *rules*; the *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics*; §2.3), and the *reply* *grounds* the *analyticity* in the *local* *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.2 The status of the logical constants

**The *question***. What *determines* the *meaning* of a *logical* *constant* (the “and”, the “or”, the “not”, the “if”)?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *meaning* is *determined* by | The *proponent* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Referential** | The *truth* *table* (the *truth* *conditions*) | the *classical* *semantics* | The *connectives* *are* *not* *objects* |
| **Proof*-*theoretic** | The *introduction* and *elimination* *rules* | Prawitz (1965); Dummett (1991) | The *tonk* *problem* |
| **Inferential** | The *inferential* *role* (the *norms*) | Brandom (1994; 2000) | The *holism* |
| **Operational** | The *rules* of *use* (the *practice*) | the *contemporary* *literature* | The *circularity* |

**The *tonk* *problem*** (Prior, “The Runabout Inference Ticket,” 1960). Let “tonk” be a *connective* *defined* by two *rules*: from $p$ *infer* $p$ *tonk* $q$; from $p$ *tonk* $q$ *infer* $q$. The *rules* *validate* the *inference* from *any* $p$ to *any* $q$, and the *connective* *trivialises* the *logic*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* of a *logical* *constant* is *determined* by its *inference* *rules* (the *inferentialist* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{"tonk" is *defined* by the *rules* from } p \text{ to } p \text{ tonk } q \text{ and from } p \text{ tonk } q \text{ to } q. \\
3. & \text{The *rules* *license* the *inference* from } p \text{ to } q \text{ (for *any* } p, q). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *inferentialism* *is* *false* (the *rules* *do* *not* *determine* a *coherent* *meaning*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (Belnap, “Tonk, Plonk and Plink,” 1962). The *meaning* *requires* the *conservative* *extension* (the *rules* *must* *not* *license* *new* *inferences* *among* the *old* *vocabulary*) and the *harmony* (the *introduction* and the *elimination* *rules* *must* *balance*), and “tonk” *violates* the *conditions*, and the *conditions* *ground* the *meaning*.

**The *significance***. The *tonk* *problem* *shows* that the *meaning* of a *logical* *constant* is *not* *arbitrary* (the *rules* *must* *satisfy* the *conservativeness* and the *harmony*), and the *constraint* *grounds* the *analyticity* in the *structure* of the *rules*. The *reply* is *influential* and *contested*.

### 2.3 Proof-theoretic semantics

**The *thesis*** (Prawitz, *Natural Deduction*, 1965; Dummett, “The Justification of Deduction,” 1973; *The Logical Basis of Metaphysics*, 1991). The *meaning* of a *logical* *constant* is *determined* by its *introduction* *rules* (the *canonical* *proofs*), and the *elimination* *rules* are *justified* by the *introduction* *rules* (the *inversion* *principle*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Meaning}(\wedge) = \text{the *introduction* *rule*} \quad \left(\frac{p \quad q}{p \wedge q}\right)
$$

**The *virtue***. The *semantics* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *proof* *structure*, and it *fits* the *intuitionistic* *logic* (Ch. 35, §5.1) and the *anti*-*realist* *semantics* (Ch. 22, §1.3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *classical* *logic* *objection*. The *classical* *logic* *cannot* be *captured* by the *introduction* and *elimination* *rules* *without* the *appeal* to the *classical* *principles* (the *excluded* *middle*, the *double*-*negation* *elimination*), and the *appeal* *undermines* the *semantics* (the *classical* *logic* *requires* a *different* *semantics*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *classical* *logic* *has* a *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics* (the *multiple*-*conclusion* *sequent* *calculus*), and the *reply* *extends* the *semantics*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *harmony* *objection*. The *harmony* *of* the *rules* is *not* *always* *guaranteed* (the *tonk*), and the *harmony* *requires* a *restriction* (the *conservativeness*, the *inversion*), and the *restriction* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The state of the analyticity debate

**The *assessment***. The *analyticity* of *logic* is *contested*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *epistemic* *analyticity*: the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge* of the *law* (the *Boghossian* *insight*), and the *sufficiency* is *constrained* by the *harmony* and the *conservativeness* (the *Belnap* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *classical* *logic* *may* *require* a *semantic* *foundation* *different* from the *proof*-*theoretic* *one*.

## 3. Conventionalism and Its Failure

### 3.1 The conventionalist thesis

**The *thesis*** (Poincaré, *Science and Hypothesis*, 1902; Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, Ch. 1; Carnap, *The Logical Syntax of Language*, 1934). The *laws* of *logic* (and the *axioms* of *geometry*, the *principles* of *mathematics*) are *true* *by* *convention*: they are *not* *descriptions* of the *world*, and they are *not* *synthetic*; they are the *rules* of the *language*-*game*, and the *choice* of the *rules* is *pragmatic*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Logical law} = \text{the *convention* of the *language*} \ (\text{the *rule* of the *framework})
$$

**The *argument*. The *logical* *laws* are *not* *refuted* by *experience* (the *experience* *presupposes* them), and the *immunity* *shows* that the *laws* are *conventions* (the *rules* of the *description*, not the *content*).

**The *virtue***. The *view* *explains* the *immunity* of *logic* to empirical *refutation*, and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *a* *priori* (the *a* *priori* is the *conventional*).

**The *tension***. The *conventionalism* *slips* into the *relativism* (any *convention* is as *good* as any *other*; Ch. 13, §3), and the *slip* *undermines* the *objectivity* of logic.

### 3.2 Quine's "Truth by Convention"

**The *argument*** (Quine, “Truth by Convention,” 1936; “Carnap and Logical Truth,” 1960). The *conventionalism* is *incoherent*, because the *derivation* of the *consequences* of the *conventions* *requires* logic.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *conventions* *determine* the *truth* of the *logical* *laws* (the *conventionalist* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *determination* *requires* the *derivation* of the *consequences* of the *conventions*.} \\
3. & \text{The *derivation* *requires* logic (the *inference* from the *conventions* to their *consequences*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *logic* *is* *presupposed* by the *conventions* (the *conventionalism* is *circular*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *conventions* *cannot* *ground* logic, because the *conventions* *themselves* *presuppose* logic; the *conventionalism* *fails* at the *first* *step*.

**The *significance***. The *argument* is *decisive* against the *naive* *conventionalism*, and it is the *ancestor* of the *Quinean* *holism* (the *web* of *belief*; Ch. 9, §2.4).

### 3.3 Carnap's tolerance

**The *thesis*** (Carnap, *The Logical Syntax of Language*, 1934, §17; “Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology,” 1950). The *choice* of a *logic* is a *convention*, and the *principle* of *tolerance* *permits* *any* *consistent* *framework*.

**The *formula***.

> “In logic, there are no morals. Everyone is at liberty to build his own logic, i.e. his own form of language, as he wishes.”

**The *argument*. The *logical* *laws* are *internal* to the *framework* (the *rules* of the *language*), and the *choice* of the *framework* is *external* (the *pragmatic* *choice*; Ch. 22, §3.1), and the *tolerance* is the *consequence*.

**The *virtue***. The *principle* *grounds* the *logical* *pluralism* (Ch. 35, §5.5), and it *avoids* the *metaphysical* *dispute* about the *correct* *logic* (the *dispute* is *pragmatic*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *criterion*’* *objection***. The *choice* of the *framework* *requires* a *criterion* (the *fruitfulness*, the *simplicity*), and the *criterion* *presupposes* a *logic* (the *criterion* *problem*; Ch. 13, §1).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *truth*’* *objection***. The *tolerance* *makes* the *logic* *a* *mere* *tool* (there is *no* *fact* of the *matter* about the *correct* *logic*), and the *consequence* *undermines* the *realism* (the *logic* is *not* *about* the *world*).

**The *reply*** (Carnap 1950). The *internal* *questions* have *definite* *answers* (the *framework* *determines* the *truth*), and the *external* *questions* are *pragmatic*, and the *reply* *preserves* the *truth* *within* the *framework*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *framework* *internal* *truth* is *not* the *truth* *simpliciter*; Ch. 22, §3.3).

### 3.4 The lessons

**The *lessons***.

| The *lesson* | The *content* | The *consequence* |
|---|---|---|
| **The circularity** | The conventions presuppose logic | The conventionalism fails (Quine) |
| **The pragmatics** | The choice of the framework is pragmatic | The tolerance (Carnap) |
| **The residue** | The logic is not fully conventional | The realism is not refuted |

**The *significance***. The *conventionalism* *fails* *as* a *foundation*, and it *succeeds* *as* a *description* of the *practice* (the *choice* of the *framework* is *pragmatic*); the *combination* is the *most* *defensible* *position* (the *qualified* *conventionalism*).

## 4. The Applicability of Logic

### 4.1 The puzzle

**The *puzzle*** (Wigner, “The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences,” 1960; the *application* to *logic*; the *contemporary* *literature*). Why does the *world* *conform* to the *laws* of *logic*? The *laws* are *a* *priori* (they are *known* *independently* of *experience*), and the *world* *conforms* to them, and the *conformity* is a *brute* *fact* (there is *no* *explanation*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* of logic are *a* *priori* (the *epistemic* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *world* *conforms* to the *laws* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
3. & \text{The *conformity* *requires* an *explanation* (the *why* *question*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *mysterious* (the *Wigner* *puzzle*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *puzzle* is the *problem* of the *fit* between the *a* *priori* and the *world*, and the *fit* is the *deepest* *question* in the *philosophy* of logic.

### 4.2 The triviality response

**The *thesis***. The *applicability* is *trivial*: the *laws* of logic are *topic*-*neutral* (they *apply* to *any* *subject* *matter*), and the *neutrality* *explains* the *fit* (the *fit* is *not* a *substantive* *fact*; it is the *meaning* of the *neutrality*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* of logic are *topic*-*neutral* (they *do* not *describe* a *particular* *domain*).} \\
2. & \text{The *neutrality* *entails* that the *laws* *apply* to *any* *domain*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *trivial* (no *explanation* *is* *required*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *response* *dissolves* the *puzzle* (the *fit* is *not* a *fact* to be *explained*), and it *fits* the *Fregean* *conception* of *logic* as the *general* *science* (the *logic* *describes* the *structure* of any *thought*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *neutrality*’* *objection*** (Maddy, “The Philosophy of Logic,” 2012). The *laws* of logic are *not* *neutral* (they are *extrapolated* from the *reasoning* about the *medium*-*sized* *objects*), and the *application* *requires* the *empirical* *grounding* (the *logic* is *not* *a* *priori*).

**The *reply***. The *neutrality* is *formal* (the *logic* is *indifferent* to the *content*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *formal* *neutrality* from the *cognitive* *origin*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection***. The *triviality* *does* not *explain* *why* the *world* *has* a *logical* *structure* (the *structure* *may* be *imposed* by the *mind*, not *found* in the *world*).

**The *reply***. The *structure* is *both* (the *mind* *imposes* the *structure*, and the *world* *conforms*), and the *reply* *is* the *Kantian* *answer* (Ch. 20, §2.1). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.3 The realist response

**The *thesis***. The *world* has a *logical* *structure* (the *facts* *are* *structured*; the *propositions* *are* *truth*-*evaluable*), and the *structure* *grounds* the *applicability* (the *logic* *describes* the *structure*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *world* *has* a *factual* *structure* (the *facts*, the *states* of *affairs*; Ch. 15, §4.2).} \\
2. & \text{The *logic* *describes* the *structure* (the *connectives* *mirror* the *relations* among the *facts*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *grounded* in the *worldly* *structure*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *response* *grounds* the *applicability* in the *world*, and it *fits* the *truthmaker* *theory* (Ch. 32, §6) and the *correspondence* *theory* (Ch. 32, §2).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *logical* *facts*’* *objection* (the *deflationary* *response*; Ch. 32, §5). The *logic* does *not* *describe* a *special* *domain* (the *logical* *facts*), and the *realist* *response* *multiplies* the *entities*.

**The *reply***. The *logical* *facts* are *structural* (the *facts* *about* the *relations* among the *facts*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *proliferation*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *fit*’* *objection*. The *world* *could* *have* been *illogical* (the *quantum* *mechanics* *suggests* it *is*), and the *possibility* *undermines* the *realist* *response*.

**The *reply*. The *quantum* *logic* (Ch. 35, §5.4) *revises* the *logic*, and the *revision* *supports* the *empiricism* (the *logic* *is* *empirical*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.4 The structural explanation

**The *thesis*** (Steiner, “The Application of Mathematics to Natural Science,” 1989; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *applicability* is *explained* by the *structure* (the *mathematics* *and* the *logic* *describe* the *structure* of the *world*, and the *structure* is *real*; Ch. 19, §6.3).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *world* *has* a *structure* (the *relations* among the *entities*; Ch. 19, §6.3).} \\
2. & \text{The *logic* *describes* the *structure* (the *connectives* *capture* the *relations*).} \\
3. & \text{The *description* *is* the *applicability* (the *fit* is the *structural* *correspondence*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *explained* by the *structural* *realism*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *explanation* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *fit* (the *fit* is the *structural* *correspondence*, not a *brute* *fact*), and it *fits* the *structural* *realism* (Ch. 19, §6.3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *structure*’* *objection* (the *deflationary* *response*; Ch. 22, §3). The *structure* *is* *not* a *real* *feature* of the *world* (it is *a* *projection* of our *interests*), and the *explanation* *collapses*.

**The *reply*** (Sider 2011; Ch. 22, §4.2). The *structure* *is* *real* (it *carves* *nature* *at* its *joints*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *structure* in the *fundamental* *description*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *unique* *structure*’* *objection*. The *world* *has* *many* *structures* (the *many* *levels*, the *many* *descriptions*), and the *choice* of the *structure* *is* *interest*-*relative* (Ch. 21, §8.2).

**The *reply***. The *fundamental* *structure* *is* *unique* (the *privileged* *description*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *uniqueness* in the *fundamentality* (Ch. 22, §4.2). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.5 The quantum-logic test case

**The *case*** (Putnam, “Is Logic Empirical?,” 1968; Ch. 35, §7.2). The *quantum* *mechanics* *suggests* that the *distributive* *law* ($p \wedge (q \vee r) \leftrightarrow (p \wedge q) \vee (p \wedge r)$) *fails*, and the *suggestion* *challenges* the *a* *priori* *status* of the *classical* *logic*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *quantum* *mechanics* *suggests* the *failure* of the *distributive* *law* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *distributive* *law* is a *law* of the *classical* *logic* (the *logical* *premise*).} \\
3. & \text{The *failure* *requires* the *revision* of the *logic* (the *empirical* *challenge*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *logic* is *empirical* (the *quantum* *logic* *is* *the* *logic* of the *quantum* *domain*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *case* is the *strongest* *argument* for the *empirical* *status* of logic (the *logic* *is* *revisable* by *experience*), and the *argument* *undermines* the *a* *priori* *status*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *meaning*-*variance* *objection* (Putnam 1968; Ch. 35, §7.2). The *quantum* *logic* *changes* the *meaning* of the *connectives* (the *“and”*, *“or”* are not the *classical* *ones*), and the *change* is *not* a *revision* of the *logic* (it is a *different* *logic*).

**The *reply***. The *change* of *meaning* *is* a *revision* (the *meaning* is *constituted* by the *inferential* *role*, and the *role* is *revised*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *revisability* in the *inferentialism* (Ch. 31, §3.4). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *experiment*’* *objection*. The *quantum* *mechanics* *does* not *test* the *logic* *directly* (the *tests* *presuppose* the *logic*), and the *evidence* is *indirect* (the *success* of the *quantum* *theory*).

**The *reply***. The *evidence* is *inferential* (the *best* *explanation* of the *success* is the *quantum* *logic*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *revision* in the *IBE* (Ch. 10, §6). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.6 The state of the applicability debate

**The *assessment***. The *applicability* is *mysterious*, and the *responses* are *all* *contested*.

| The *response* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Triviality** | Dissolves the puzzle | The neutrality is contested |
| **Realism** | Grounds the fit in the world | The logic facts |
| **Structural** | Grounds the fit in the structure | The structure is contested |
| **Quantum logic** | Empirical; revisable | The meaning variance |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *structural* *realism*: the *applicability* is *explained* by the *structural* *correspondence* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *structure* is *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and the *structure* is *not* *unique* (the *pluralist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *structure* is *interest*-*relative* and the *fit* is *partial*.

## 5. Conceptual Truths and Explication

### 5.1 Conceptual truths

**The *thesis***. A **conceptual truth** is a *truth* that *holds* in *virtue* of the *concepts* *involved*, and the *truth* is *a* *priori* (the *grasp* of the *concepts* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge*).

**The *examples***.

| The *truth* | The *concept* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| “All bachelors are unmarried” | *bachelor* | *Analytic* (trivial) |
| “If $p$ and $q$, then $p$” | *and* | *Analytic* (logical) |
| “Red is a colour” | *red*, *colour* | *Conceptual* (substantive) |
| “Knowledge is factive” | *knowledge* | *Conceptual* (substantive) |

**The *significance***. The *conceptual* *truths* are the *substantive* *a* *priori* (they are *not* *mere* *tautologies*), and the *truths* *ground* the *possibility* of the *philosophical* *knowledge* (Ch. 3, §1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Quinean* *objection*. The *conceptual* *truths* are *not* a *distinct* *kind* (they are *central* *beliefs* in the *web*), and the *distinction* *collapses* (Ch. 9, §2.4).

**The *reply*** (the *moderate* *revival*; Ch. 9, §2.5). The *conceptual* *truths* are *epistemically* *distinct* (the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *sufficient*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinction* in the *epistemic* *analyticity*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *substantive* *conceptual* *truths*’* *objection*. The *conceptual* *truths* are *trivial* (the *truths* *follow* from the *definitions*), and the *substantive* *truths* are *empirical*.

**The *reply***. The *conceptual* *truths* are *substantive* (they *constrain* the *possible* *cases*; Ch. 3, §1), and the *reply* *grounds* the *substantive* *a* *priori* in the *conceptual* *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.2 Carnapian explication

**The *thesis*** (Carnap, *Logical Foundations of Probability*, 1950, Ch. 1). The *philosophical* *analysis* is an **explication**: the *replacement* of a *vague* *concept* by a *precise* one, *judged* by the *criteria* of *similarity*, *exactness*, *fruitfulness*, and *simplicity* (Ch. 3, §1.5).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explication}: \ \text{Explicandum} \ \to \ \text{Explicatum}
$$

**The *criteria***.

| The *criterion* | The *question* | The *example* (*probability*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Similarity** | Does the explicatum *capture* the ordinary *use*? | The *frequency* *interpretation* |
| **Exactness** | Is the explicatum *precise*? | The *Kolmogorov* *axioms* |
| **Fruitfulness** | Does the explicatum *support* the *inferences*? | The *Bayesian* *framework* |
| **Simplicity** | Is the explicatum *simple*? | The *subjective* *interpretation* |

**The *virtue***. The *explication* *grounds* the *philosophical* *analysis* in the *pragmatic* *choice* (there is *no* *unique* *analysis*; the *choice* *depends* on the *purposes*), and it *fits* the *conceptual* *engineering* (Ch. 33, §5).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *truth*’* *objection*. The *explication* *is* *not* *true* or *false* (it is *better* or *worse*), and the *consequence* *undermines* the *realism* about the *philosophical* *analysis*.

**The *reply***. The *explication* is *assessed* by the *criteria* (the *similarity*, the *exactness*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *criteria*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *change* *of* *subject*’* *objection*. The *explication* *changes* the *subject* (the *explicatum* is *not* the *explicandum*), and the *change* *undermines* the *continuity* of the *inquiry* (Ch. 33, §5.3).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *change* is *the* *point* (the *old* *concept* is *vague* and *inconsistent*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *change* in the *normative* *argument*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 The status of philosophical necessities

**The *thesis***. The *philosophical* *necessities* (the *logical* *truths*, the *conceptual* *truths*, the *metaphysical* *necessities*) are *real* (they are *not* *mere* *conventions*), and they are *known* by *the* *a* *priori* (the *conceptual* *grasp*, the *modal* *intuition*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *philosophical* *necessities* *constrain* the *possible* *cases* (the *conceptual* *truths* *hold* in *every* *possible* *world*).} \\
2. & \text{The *constraint* *requires* a *modal* *fact* (the *necessity*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *philosophical* *necessities* are *real* (the *modal* *realism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *view* *grounds* the *philosophical* *knowledge* in the *modal* *truths*, and it *fits* the *essentialism* (Ch. 16, §3.3) and the *conceptual* *analysis* (Ch. 3, §1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *modal* *facts*’* *objection* (the *deflationary* *response*; Ch. 16, §2.4). The *modal* *facts* are *fictions* (the *fictionalism*), and the *philosophical* *necessities* are *not* *real*.

**The *reply***. The *modal* *facts* are *grounded* in the *essences* (Ch. 16, §3.3), and the *reply* *avoids* the *fictionalism*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *epistemic* *access*’* *objection* (Benacerraf; Ch. 9, §7.2). The *modal* *facts* are *abstract* (the *possible* *worlds*), and the *access* to the *abstract* *facts* is *mysterious*.

**The *reply*** (the *structural* *account*; Ch. 16, §6.4). The *modal* *knowledge* is *abductive* (the *best* *explanation* of the *success* of the *modal* *reasoning*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *access* in the *abduction*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.4 The state of the conceptual-truths debate

**The *assessment***. The *conceptual* *truths* are *contested*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *epistemic* *analyticity*: the *conceptual* *truths* are *substantive* (they *constrain* the *possible* *cases*), and the *constraint* is *grounded* in the *concepts* (the *epistemic* *insight*), and the *concepts* are *revisable* (the *engineering* *insight*; Ch. 33, §5), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *revisability* *undermines* the *necessity*.

## 6. Logical Knowledge

### 6.1 The a priori conception

**The *thesis***. The *logical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori*: it is *grounded* in the *grasp* of the *meanings* of the *logical* *constants*, and the *grasp* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge* of the *laws*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* of logic are *true* in *virtue* of the *meanings* of the *connectives* (the *analyticity*; §2.1).} \\
2. & \text{The *grasp* of the *meanings* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge* of the *laws* (the *epistemic* *analyticity*).} \\
3. & \text{The *grasp* is *a* *priori* (it is *not* *empirical*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *logical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *classical* *conception*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *conception* *explains* the *certainty* of the *logical* *laws* (the *meaning* is *transparent*), and it *fits* the *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics* (§2.3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *transparency*’* *objection* (the *semantic* *externalism*; Ch. 25, §4). The *meanings* are *not* *transparent* (the *competent* *speakers* *may* *not* *know* the *meanings*), and the *grasp* is *not* *sufficient*.

**The *reply***. The *grasp* is *implicit* (it is the *disposition* to *use* the *connectives* *correctly*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *knowledge* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *faculty*’* *objection*. The *grasp* *requires* a *faculty* (the *logical* *intuition*), and the *faculty* is *mysterious* (Ch. 3, §2.3).

**The *reply***. The *grasp* is *constituted* by the *linguistic* *competence* (the *ability* to *use* the *connectives*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *mystery*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.2 The abductivist challenge

**The *thesis*** (Williamson, *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, 2007, Ch. 5; Hjortland, “Anti-Exceptionalism about Logic,” 2017; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *logical* *knowledge* is *not* *a* *priori*; it is *abductive* (the *laws* of logic are the *best* *explanation* of the *success* of the *reasoning*), and the *abduction* is *empirical* (the *premises* are the *observed* *success*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *theories* are *justified* by the *IBE* (the *best* *explanation*; Ch. 10, §6).} \\
2. & \text{The *logic* is a *theory* (the *anti*-*exceptionalism*; Ch. 35, §7.3).} \\
3. & \text{The *IBE* is *empirical* (the *premises* are the *observations*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *logical* *knowledge* is *empirical* (the *abductivism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *abductivism* *unifies* the *logic* with the *science* (the *same* *method*), and it *explains* the *revision* (the *logic* *changes* when the *better* *explanation* *requires* it).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *empirical* *premises*’* *objection*. The *success* of the *reasoning* is *not* an *empirical* *observation* (it is a *normative* *assessment*), and the *abduction* *presupposes* the *logic* (the *inference* *from* the *success* *to* the *logic* *is* *logical*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *success* is *empirical* (the *prediction*, the *intervention*), and the *abduction* *presupposes* the *logic* *provisionally* (the *logic* is *revised* by the *abduction*). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *abduction* *explains* the *success* but not the *necessity* (the *logic* *could* be *empirical* and *contingent*), and the *necessity* is the *datum*.

**The *reply***. The *necessity* is *explained* by the *meaning* (the *grasp* of the *meanings* yields the *necessity*), and the *reply* *combines* the *abduction* with the *analyticity*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The normativity of logic

**The *question***. Is *logic* *normative* for *reasoning*? That is, *ought* we to *reason* according to the *laws* of logic?

**The *standard* *view***. Logic is the *science* of *valid* *inference*, and the *valid* *inference* is the *norm* of *reasoning* (the *reasoner* *ought* to *infer* the *conclusion* from the *premises* *whenever* the *premises* are *accepted*).

**The *argument* *for* the *standard* *view***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *logic* *specifies* the *valid* *inferences* (the *descriptive* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *valid* *inferences* are *good* *inferences* (the *evaluative* *premise*).} \\
3. & \text{The *good* *inferences* are *normative* (the *ought* *to* *infer*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *logic* is *normative* (the *standard* *view*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *view* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *validity*, and it *fits* the *ordinary* *practice* of *criticising* the *invalid* *reasoning*.

**The *objections*** (Harman, “Logic and Reasoning,” 1986; Field, “What Is the Normative Role of Logic?,” 2009; MacFarlane, “In What Sense (If Any) Is Logic Normative for Thought?,” 2004; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(i) Harman's *objection***. The *validity* *does* not *entail* the *norm* (a *valid* *inference* can be *epistemically* *bad*: the *premises* may be *unjustified*, the *conclusion* may be *irrelevant*, the *reasoning* may be *question*-*begging*).

**The *example***. The *premise* $p$ *entails* $p \vee q$ (the *disjunction* *introduction*), and the *inference* is *valid*, and the *inference* *may* be *bad* (the *disjunct* $q$ is *irrelevant*, and the *reasoner* *should* *not* *come* to *believe* $p \vee q$ *on* the *basis* of $p$).

**The *consequence***. The *validity* is *not* the *norm* of *reasoning*, and the *logic* is *not* a *theory* of *reasoning* (it is a *theory* of *implication*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *ought*’* *objection*. The *validity* is *descriptive* (the *truth* *preservation*), and the *normativity* is *evaluative* (the *ought*), and the *derivation* of the *ought* from the *is* is *impossible* (the *is*-*ought* *gap*; Ch. 44, §1).

**The *consequence***. The *normativity* *requires* a *further* *premise* (the *epistemic* *goal*, the *value* of *truth*; Ch. 32, §9), and the *premise* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The responses

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *‘*bridge*’* *response*** (MacFarlane 2004). The *logic* is *normative* *relative* to a *bridge* *principle* (the *principle* that *connects* the *validity* to the *norm* of *belief*, the *truth* *norm*), and the *bridge* is *the* *normative* *premise*.

**The *virtue***. The *response* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *bridge*, and it *fits* the *truth*-*norm* (Ch. 32, §9.3).

**The *objection***. The *bridge* *principle* is *contested* (the *truth*-*norm* vs. the *knowledge*-*norm*; Ch. 7, §4.2), and the *contest* *undermines* the *normativity*.

**(ii) The *‘*dynamic*’* *response*** (Field 2009). The *logic* is *normative* for the *reasoning* *because* it *constrains* the *conditional* *credences* (the *Bayesian* *coherence*; Ch. 10, §4), and the *constraint* is *the* *norm*.

**The *virtue***. The *response* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *probability* *theory*, and it *fits* the *Bayesian* *framework*.

**The *objection***. The *Bayesian* *constraint* *does* not *fully* *capture* the *logic* (the *logic* *is* *deductive*, and the *Bayesian* *is* *probabilistic*), and the *connection* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *normativity*’* *response*** (Harman 1986). The *logic* is *descriptive* (it *describes* the *implication*), and the *normativity* is *not* the *logic* (it is the *epistemology*), and the *philosophy* of *logic* *should* *not* *claim* the *normative* *role*.

**The *virtue***. The *response* *avoids* the *gap* (the *logic* *is* *descriptive*, and the *epistemology* *is* *normative*), and it *fits* the *division* of *labour*.

**The *objection***. The *response* *loses* the *connection* between the *logic* and the *reasoning* (the *logic* *becomes* *an* *idle* *game*), and the *loss* is *unacceptable*.

### 6.5 The state of logical knowledge

**The *assessment***. The *logical* *knowledge* is *contested*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *a* *priori*: the *logical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* in the *thin* sense (the *grasp* of the *meaning* is *sufficient* for the *knowledge*; the *epistemic* *analyticity*), and the *grasp* is *fallible* and *revisable* (the *abduction* *is* *the* *correction*), and the *normativity* is *grounded* in the *epistemic* *goal* (the *truth*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *bridge* is *contested*.

## 7. Two Test Cases: Ethics and Modality

### 7.1 The a priori in ethics

**The *question***. Is there *moral* *a* *priori* *knowledge*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Rationalism** | There are *synthetic* *a* *priori* *moral* *truths* | the *intuitionist* *tradition* (Ross; Shafer-Landau) |
| **Empiricism** | The *moral* *knowledge* is *empirical* (the *observation*, the *testimony*) | the *naturalist* *tradition* (Railton; Boyd) |
| **Anti-realism** | The *moral* *knowledge* is *not* *knowledge* (the *error* *theory*; Ch. 44, §3) | Mackie |
| **Constructivism** | The *moral* *truths* are *constituted* by the *rational* *procedure* | Korsgaard; Rawls |

**The *argument* *for* the *rationalism***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *fundamental* *moral* *truths* (the *torture* is *wrong*) are *known* *without* *empirical* *investigation* (the *intuition*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intuition* is *a* *priori* (the *grasp* of the *moral* *concept*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{There are *moral* *a* *priori* *truths* (the *rationalism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*** (Ch. 9, §3.6; Ch. 44, §6).

**(i) The *‘*no* *faculty*’* *objection*. The *intuition* is *mysterious* (the *faculty* is *gerrymandered*), and the *reply* *is* *unavailable*.

**(ii) The *‘*disagreement*’* *objection*. The *moral* *intuitions* *disagree* (the *different* *cultures*, the *different* *traditions*), and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *a* *priori* *claim* (Ch. 9, §6).

**(iii) The *‘*debunking*’* *objection*. The *moral* *beliefs* are *explained* by the *evolution* (the *non*-*truth*-*tracking* *process*), and the *explanation* *undermines* the *justification* (Ch. 3, §5).

**The *significance***. The *ethics* *case* *tests* the *a* *priori* *framework*: the *a* *priori* *knowledge* *requires* the *conceptual* *grasp* and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *grasp*, and the *debunking* *undermines* the *warrant*. The *case* *supports* the *thin* *a* *priori* (the *warrant* is *fallible*), and it *undermines* the *thick* *a* *priori* (the *intuitive* *faculty*).

### 7.2 The a priori in modality

**The *question***. Is there *modal* *a* *priori* *knowledge*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Modal rationalism** | The *modal* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *conceivability* *entails* the *possibility*) | Chalmers (1996, 2002); Ch. 16, §6.2 |
| **Modal empiricism** | The *modal* *knowledge* is *empirical* (the *counterfactual* *reasoning* is *empirical*) | Williamson (2007); Ch. 16, §6.4 |
| **Modal scepticism** | The *modal* *knowledge* is *impossible* (the *conceivability* is *not* a *guide*) | van Inwagen (1998); Ch. 16, §6.5 |

**The *argument* *for* the *rationalism***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *modal* *truths* (the *possibility*, the *necessity*) are *known* *a* *priori* (the *conceivability*).} \\
2. & \text{The *conceivability* *entails* the *possibility* (the *CP* *thesis*; Ch. 16, §6.2).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *modal* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *rationalism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*** (Ch. 16, §6.2).

**(i) The *Kripkean* *objection*. The *conceivability* *does* not *entail* the *possibility* (the *water* *is* *H₂O* is *conceivable* *otherwise*, but *impossible*), and the *CP* *fails*.

**(ii) The *‘*idealisation*’* *objection*. The *ideal* *conceivability* is *unavailable* (the *ideal* *reasoner* is *an* *idealisation*), and the *conceivability* *is* *unreliable*.

**The *significance***. The *modality* *case* *tests* the *a* *priori* *framework*: the *a* *priori* *knowledge* *requires* a *reliable* *conceivability* (the *idealisation*), and the *conceivability* *is* *contested*, and the *case* *supports* the *abductivism* (the *modal* *knowledge* is *abductive*).

### 7.3 The comparison with logic

**The *comparison***.

| The *domain* | The *a* *priori* *warrant* | The *reliability* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Logic** | The *grasp* of the *meaning* | *High* (the *rules* are *constitutive*) | *Thin* *a* *priori* |
| **Ethics** | The *intuition* | *Low* (the *disagreement*, the *debunking*) | *Thin* *a* *priori* (contested) |
| **Modality** | The *conceivability* | *Medium* (the *CP* *contested*) | *Thin* *a* *priori* (contested) |

**The *lesson***. The *a* *priori* is *domain*-*specific* (the *warrant* *varies* across the *domains*), and the *thin* *conception* is the *most* *defensible* (the *a* *priori* is a *kind* of *warrant*, and the *warrant* is *fallible* in *every* *domain*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *logic* is the *strongest* *case* and the *ethics* the *weakest*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *classical* *identification* (a priori = necessary = analytic) is broken** (Ch. 9, §3.3), and the *three* *notions* are *independent*.
- **The *naive* *conventionalism* *fails*** (Quine, “Truth by Convention,” 1936), and the *pragmatic* *conventionalism* (Carnap) *survives* as a *description* of the *practice*.
- **The *applicability* of logic is *mysterious*** (Wigner's *puzzle*), and the *structural* *realism* is the *most* *promising* *response*.
- **The *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics* *grounds* the *meaning* of the *logical* *constants* (the *harmony*, the *conservativeness*), and the *tonk* *problem* *constrains* the *inferentialism*.
- **The *normativity* of logic is *contested*** (Harman, Field, MacFarlane), and the *bridge* *principle* is *the* *open* *question*.
- **The *conceptual* *truths* are *real* (the *epistemic* *analyticity*), and the *explication* (Carnap) *grounds* the *philosophical* *analysis* in the *pragmatic* *choice*.
- **The *a* *priori* in *ethics* and *modality* is *thinner* than in *logic***, and the *disagreement* and the *debunking* *undermine* the *warrant*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *analyticity* of logic.** Is the *grasp* of the *meaning* *sufficient* for the *knowledge*?
- **The *applicability*.** Why does the *world* *conform* to the *laws* of *logic*?
- **The *normativity*.** Is the *logic* *normative* for *reasoning*?
- **The *revisability*.** Is the *logic* *revisable* by *experience* (the *quantum* *logic*)?
- **The *nature* of the *logical* *constants*.** The *referential*, the *proof*-*theoretic*, the *inferential*.
- **The *a* *priori* in *ethics* and *modality*.** The *warrant* and the *reliability*.

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *a* *priori*, the *analytic*, and the *necessary*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *tonk*, the *quantum* *logic*, the *brain* in a *vat* (for the *a* *priori*) — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics*, the *Kripke* *models* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: the philosophy of logic is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kantian** | The a priori is the form of experience | Explains the necessity | The neglected alternative |
| **Fregean** | The logic is the science of truth | Anti-psychologistic | The nature of the senses |
| **Positivist** | The logic is analytic | Unifies the a priori | Quine's attack |
| **Conventionalist** | The logic is conventional | Explains the immunity | The circularity |
| **Carnapian** | The logic is a framework choice | Tolerance; pluralism | The criterion problem |
| **Realist** | The logic describes the structure | Grounds the applicability | The logical facts |
| **Structural** | The logic describes the structure | Explains the fit | The structure is contested |
| **Abductivist** | The logic is the best explanation | Unifies with science | The empirical premises |
| **Apriorist** | The logic is a priori | The certainty | The revisability |
| **Anti-exceptionalist** | The logic is theory choice | Fits the practice | The criterion problem |

The table is a map, not a verdict. The reader who wants to choose a position must decide which cost is acceptable — and the decision is a philosophical argument.

## Summary

- **Logic is the *paradigm* case of the *a* *priori***: it is the *most* *certain* of disciplines, and its *certainty* is *not* *empirical* and *not* *trivial*.
- **Kant's *question*** (how is *a* *priori* *knowledge* *possible*?) is the *background*, and the *Kantian* *answer* (the *forms* of *cognition*) is *influential* and *contested*.
- **The *classical* *identification* (a priori = necessary = analytic) is *broken*** (Quine, Kripke), and the *thin* *a* *priori* is the *most* *defensible* *conception*.
- **The *analyticity* of logic is *contested***: the *epistemic* *analyticity* (the *grasp* of the *meaning*) is *defensible*, and the *proof*-*theoretic* *semantics* (the *harmony*, the *conservativeness*) *constrains* the *inferentialism* (the *tonk*).
- **The *conventionalism* *fails*** (the *circularity*), and the *Carnapian* *tolerance* *survives* as a *pragmatic* *description* of the *framework* *choice*.
- **The *applicability* of logic is *mysterious*** (Wigner's *puzzle*), and the *structural* *realism* is the *most* *promising* *response*.
- **The *conceptual* *truths* are *substantive*** (the *epistemic* *analyticity*), and the *explication* (Carnap) *grounds* the *philosophical* *analysis* in the *pragmatic* *choice*.
- **The *logical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* in the *thin* sense**: it is *grounded* in the *grasp* of the *meaning*, and the *grasp* is *fallible* and *revisable* (the *abduction*).
- **The *normativity* of logic is *contested***: the *validity* does *not* *entail* the *norm* (Harman), and the *bridge* *principle* (the *truth* *norm*) is *the* *open* *question*.
- **The *a* *priori* in *ethics* and *modality* is *thinner* than in *logic***, and the *disagreement* and the *debunking* *undermine* the *warrant*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *thin* *a* *priori*: the *epistemic* *analyticity* (the *grasp* of the *meaning*), the *structural* *realism* (the *applicability*), and the *abductivism* (the *revisability*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *bridge* *principle* and the *logocentrism* *remain* *unresolved*.
