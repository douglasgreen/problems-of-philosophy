# Chapter 38 — Explanation and Understanding

> “To explain an event is to exhibit it as an instance of a general regularity.”
> — Carl Hempel, *Aspects of Scientific Explanation* (1965), the covering-law model, in a sentence

> “There is no such thing as ‘the’ explanation of an event; there are only answers to why-questions, and the questions carry their presuppositions with them.”
> — Bas van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image* (1980), Ch. 5, in effect

> “What distinguishes an explanation from a mere description is that the explanation tells us *why* the thing is so, and the description only tells us *that* it is so. Everything else is grammar.”
> — a formulation of the minimal consensus, in many mouths

---

Part V examined *language* — meaning, truth, paradox, speech acts, logic, the a priori, conditionals. Part VI turns to the *practice* in which language is used to *grasp* the world: **science**.

The chapter begins with *explanation*, because explanation is the *point* of science, and because the *philosophy* of explanation is the *clearest* case in which the *philosophy* of language (the *contrastive* structure of why-questions), the *philosophy* of science (the *structure* of theories), and the *metaphysics* (the *causation*, the *mechanism*, the *laws*) *meet*. It is also the *clearest* case in which the *contemporary* debate has *shifted*: the *classical* *question* — *what is the logical structure of an explanation?* — has been *replaced* by the *question* — *what is the point of an explanation, and what does it do for the agent who receives it?*

The chapter's *thesis* is that explanation is a *family* of *practices*, not a *single* *relation*, and that the *family* is *unified* not by a *single* *logical form* but by the *role* of explanation in *understanding*. The *role* is *causal* in the *empirical* *sciences* (the *causal* *and* *mechanistic* *insights*), *unificatory* in the *theoretical* *sciences* (the *unificationist* *insight*), *contrastive* in the *pragmatic* *context* (the *van Fraassen* *insight*), and *explanatory* in *mathematics* (the *Steiner*–*Mancosu*–*Lange* *insight*) — and *no* *single* *account* *captures* *all* *four*.

The chapter proceeds in nine movements. §1 examines the *covering-law* *model* and its *failures*. §2 examines the *causal* *theory*. §3 examines the *unificationist* *theory*. §4 examines the *mechanistic* *theory*. §5 examines the *pragmatic* and *contrastive* *theory*. §6 examines *understanding* and its *relation* to *knowledge*. §7 examines *explanation* across the *disciplines* (the *biology*, the *psychology*, the *history*). §8 examines the *mathematical* *explanation*. §9 examines *bad* *explanation*. §10 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. The Structure of Explanation: The Covering-Law Model

### 1.1 Hempel and Oppenheim

**The *thesis*** (Hempel & Oppenheim, “Studies in the Logic of Explanation,” 1948; Hempel, *Aspects of Scientific Explanation*, 1965, Chs. 10 and 12). An explanation is an *argument*: the *explanandum* (the *event* to be *explained*) is *deduced* from the *explanans* (the *laws* plus the *initial* *conditions*).

**The *deductive-nomological* (D-N) *schema***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(\mathrm{L}_1), \ldots, (\mathrm{L}_k) & \text{The laws of nature} \\
(\mathrm{C}_1), \ldots, (\mathrm{C}_n) & \text{The initial conditions} \\
\hline
(\mathrm{E}) & \text{The explanandum (the event described)}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conditions* of *adequacy***.

| The *condition* | The *demand* |
|---|---|
| **The *logical* *condition*** | The *explanandum* must be *deducible* from the *explanans* |
| **The *empirical* *condition*** | The *explanans* must be *true* (or *well*-*confirmed*) |
| **The *law* *condition*** | The *explanans* must *contain* at least one *law* |
| **The *nomological* *condition*** | The *laws* must be *empirical* and *testable* |

**The *example***. The *mercury* *in* the *thermometer* *rises* *because* (i) *mercury* *expands* *when* *heated* (the *law*), and (ii) *the* *temperature* *of* the *room* *increased* (the *initial* *condition*).

**The *virtue***. The *model* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *logic* (the *deduction*) and the *science* (the *laws*), and it *provides* a *precise* *criterion* of *adequacy* (the *four* *conditions*).

**The *symmetry* *thesis***. The *explanation* and the *prediction* have the *same* *logical* *structure* (the *deduction* from the *laws* and the *conditions*), and the *difference* is *pragmatic* (the *knowledge* *of* the *explanandum* is *prior* in the *explanation*, and *posterior* in the *prediction*).

### 1.2 The inductive-statistical model

**The *thesis*** (Hempel 1962, 1965, Ch. 12). The *statistical* *explanations* are *inductive* (the *explanans* *confers* a *high* *probability* *on* the *explanandum*, and the *explanation* is *the* *inductive* *argument*).

**The *schema***.

$$
\frac{P(E \mid C) = r \quad C}{\text{Therefore } E \quad \text{with probability } r}
$$

**The *requirement* *of* *maximal* *specificity*** (the *RMS*). The *statistical* *syllogism* is *ambiguous* (the *same* *case* can be *subsumed* under *different* *reference* *classes*), and the *RMS* *requires* the *explanans* to *use* the *most* *specific* *reference* *class* *available*.

**The *virtue***. The *model* *extends* the *covering*-*law* *framework* to the *statistical* *sciences*, and it *preserves* the *symmetry* *thesis* (the *explanation* and the *prediction* *share* the *structure*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *ambiguity* *objection*** (the *reference*-*class* *problem*; Salmon 1971, 1984, Ch. 1). The *statistical* *syllogism* *yields* *contradictory* *conclusions* (the *same* *case* is *explained* by *different* *reference* *classes* *with* *different* *probabilities*), and the *RMS* *does* not *resolve* the *ambiguity* (the *maximal* *specificity* *is* *not* *always* *available*, and the *maximal* *class* *may* *be* *the* *singleton* *with* *probability* 0 or 1).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*** (Salmon 1971). The *high* *probability* *is* *not* *sufficient* for the *explanation* (the *statistical* *correlation* *between* the *barometer* and the *storm* *is* *high*, and the *barometer* does *not* *explain* the *storm*), and the *explanation* *requires* the *causal* *structure* (the *causal* *theory*; §2).

**(iii) The *‘*irrelevance*’* *objection*** (Salmon 1971). The *explanans* can *raise* the *probability* *without* *explaining* (the *paresis* *case*: the *men* who *take* *birth*-*control* *pills* *do* *not* *get* *pregnant*, and the *pill* *does* *not* *explain* the *paresis*; the *statistical* *relevance* *is* *not* the *explanatory* *relevance*).

### 1.3 The objections to the D-N model

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *asymmetry* *objection*** (Bromberger, “An Approach to Explanation,” 1966; Salmon 1984, p. 240; Ch. 18, §2.4). The *flagpole* *and* the *shadow*: the *height* of the *flagpole* *explains* the *length* of the *shadow*, and the *length* of the *shadow* does *not* *explain* the *height* of the *flagpole* (the *causal* *direction*), and the *D-N* *model* *cannot* *distinguish* the *two* (the *laws* and the *conditions* are *symmetric*, and the *deduction* *runs* *both* *ways*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The flagpole height} \ (h) \text{ and the shadow length} \ (s) \text{ are connected by a law: } s = h / \tan \theta. \\
2. & \text{The law allows the deduction of } s \text{ from } h, \text{ and the deduction of } h \text{ from } s. \\
3. & \text{The explanation of } s \text{ by } h \text{ is legitimate; the explanation of } h \text{ by } s \text{ is not.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The D-N model confuses the symmetry of the deduction with the asymmetry of the explanation.}
\end{array}
$$

**(ii) The *irrelevance* *objection*** (Salmon 1971; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *D-N* *model* *admits* *explanations* that are *irrelevant* (the *hexed* *salt*: the *salt* *dissolves* *because* it is *hexed*, and the *hexing* is *irrelevant* to the *dissolving*; the *D-N* *model* *admits* the *argument* if the *law* is *true*, and the *law* is *false*; but the *argument* *illustrates* the *danger* of the *formal* *criterion*).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *laws*’* *objection*** (the *special* *sciences*; Scriven 1959; Cartwright 1983). The *special* *sciences* (the *biology*, the *psychology*, the *history*) *do* not *have* *strict* *laws* (the *ceteris* *paribus* *laws*; Ch. 39, §2), and the *D-N* *model* *excludes* the *explanations* of the *special* *sciences*.

**(iv) The *‘*singular* *causation*’* *objection***. The *singular* *event* (the *explosion* *of* *the* *bomb*) can be *explained* *without* a *universal* *law* (the *causal* *explanation* *of* the *particular*), and the *D-N* *model* *requires* the *law*.

### 1.4 What survives

**The *survivors***.

| The *element* | The *status* | The *source* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *deduction* *structure*** | *Necessary* (the *explanation* is an *argument*) | The *logical* *condition* |
| **The *law* *requirement*** | *Contested* (the *special* *sciences*) | The *nomological* *condition* |
| **The *symmetry* *thesis*** | *False* (the *asymmetry* *objection*) | The *causal* *direction* |
| **The *high* *probability*** | *Insufficient* (the *irrelevance*) | The *causal* *structure* |

**The *consequence***. The *D-N* *model* *survives* as a *model* of the *deductive* *explanations* in the *physical* *sciences*, and it *fails* as a *general* *account* of *explanation*.

## 2. The Causal Theory

### 2.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Salmon, *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World*, 1984; Lewis, “Causal Explanation,” 1986; Woodward, *Making Things Happen*, 2003, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*). To *explain* an *event* is to *identify* its *cause* (or its *causal* *structure*), and the *explanation* *works* by *exhibiting* the *causal* *relation* *between* the *explanans* and the *explanandum*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explanation}(E) = \text{the cause of } E \ (\text{or the causal mechanism})
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The scientific explanations identify the causes (the practice).} \\
2. & \text{The identification requires a causal relation (the asymmetry, the relevance).} \\
3. & \text{The causal relation explains the asymmetry and the relevance (the objections to the D-N model).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The causation is the core of explanation (the causal theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *asymmetry* (the *causes* *precede* the *effects*; Ch. 17, §6) and the *relevance* (the *causes* are the *difference*-*makers*; Ch. 18, §2), and it *fits* the *experimental* *method* (the *interventions*; Ch. 18, §3).

### 2.2 The statistical-relevance model

**The *thesis*** (Salmon, *Statistical Explanation and Statistical Relevance*, 1971; *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World*, 1984, Ch. 3). The *explanation* *works* by *partitioning* the *reference* *class* into *homogeneous* *cells*, and the *partition* *screens* *off* the *irrelevant* *properties* (the *screening*-*off* *relation*).

**The *structure***.

$$
P(E \mid C \wedge A) \neq P(E \mid C) \quad \text{(the relevance of } A\text{)}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *model* *captures* the *statistical* *explanations* (the *correlations* that *make* a *difference*), and it *avoids* the *high*-*probability* *requirement* (the *explanation* is *the* *relevance*, not the *probability*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *causation*’* *objection*** (Salmon 1984, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *screening*-*off* *relation* *is* *not* *sufficient* for the *explanation* (the *correlations* can *screen* *off* *without* *being* *causal*; the *barometer* *screens* *off* the *storm*), and the *causal* *structure* *is* *required*.

**The *reply*** (Salmon 1984, Ch. 7). The *causal* *structure* *is* *the* *underlying* *mechanism* (the *common* *cause*), and the *screening*-*off* *relation* *is* the *statistical* *signature* of the *causal* *mechanism*. The *reply* *combines* the *statistical* *and* the *causal* *accounts*, and it is *contested*.

### 2.3 The interventionist account

**The *thesis*** (Woodward, *Making Things Happen*, 2003, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 18, §3.1). The *explanation* *identifies* the *causal* *relation* *by* the *intervention*: the *explanation* *tells* us *what* *would* *happen* *if* *we* *intervened*.

**The *structure***.

$$
X \text{ explains } Y \quad \text{iff} \quad \text{an intervention on } X \text{ changes } Y
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *manipulability* (the *practical* *intuition*), and it *fits* the *experimental* *method* (the *randomized* *controlled* *trial*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *causal* *relation* (the *intervention* is *operational*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *intervention*’* *objection*** (the *fundamental* *physics*; Ch. 18, §3.2). The *intervention* is *not* *available* in the *fundamental* *physics* (the *experimenter* cannot *manipulate* the *particles*), and the *account* is *limited* to the *special* *sciences*.

**The *reply***. The *account* is *intended* for the *special* *sciences* (the *macroscopic* *domain*), and the *reply* *accepts* the *limitation*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *intervention* *tells* us *what* *would* *happen* (the *counterfactual*), and the *explanation* *requires* *more* (the *understanding* of *why*), and the *account* *loses* the *understanding* (§6).

**The *reply***. The *understanding* *is* the *grasp* of the *interventionist* *structure*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *understanding* in the *practical* *competence*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The state of the causal theory

**The *assessment***. The *causal* *theory* is the *most* *influential* *account*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| The *version* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Salmon (SR)** | The statistical relevance | The no-causation |
| **Salmon (causal)** | The causal mechanism | The mechanism is vague |
| **Lewis (counterfactual)** | The counterfactual dependence | The pre-emption |
| **Woodward (interventionist)** | The manipulability | The fundamental physics |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *interventionism* *combined* with the *mechanistic* *account* (§4): the *causation* is the *core* of the *explanation* (the *causal* *insight*), and the *mechanism* is the *vehicle* of the *causal* *relation* (the *mechanistic* *insight*), and the *two* *are* *complementary*.

## 3. The Unificationist Theory

### 3.1 Friedman's proposal

**The *thesis*** (Friedman, “Explanation and Scientific Understanding,” 1974). The *explanation* *works* by *unification*: the *explanations* are the *theories* that *reduce* the *number* of *independent* *phenomena* (the *“*global*”* *unification*), and the *understanding* is the *reduction* of the *independent* *phenomena* to the *fewer* *principles*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The scientific explanations unify the phenomena (the practice: the Newtonian mechanics unifies the terrestrial and the celestial motion).} \\
2. & \text{The unification reduces the number of independent phenomena.} \\
3. & \text{The reduction is the explanation (the understanding is the unity).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The explanation is the unification (the unificationist theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *theoretical* *virtue* of *explanation* (the *unification* is the *goal* of the *theoretical* *science*), and it *avoids* the *causal* *asymmetry* (the *unification* is *not* *causal*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *measure*’* *objection* (Kitcher, “Explanatory Unification,” 1981; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *unification* *requires* a *measure* of the *number* of *independent* *phenomena*, and the *measure* is *not* *available* (the *different* *formalizations* *yield* the *different* *counts*), and the *theory* *is* *incomplete*.

**The *reply*** (Kitcher 1981, 1989). The *measure* is the *number* of *argument* *patterns* (the *stringent* *patterns*), and the *patterns* *are* the *unifying* *structures*. The *reply* *grounds* the *measure* in the *argument* *patterns*, and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *unification* *is* *not* *sufficient* (the *unified* *theory* can *fail* to *explain* the *particular* *fact*; the *barometer* *is* *unified* with the *storm*, and the *barometer* does *not* *explain* the *storm*).

**The *reply*** (Kitcher 1989). The *unification* *applies* at the *level* of the *theory* (the *global* *unification*), and the *particular* *explanation* *derives* from the *theory*. The *reply* *distinguishes* the *global* *from* the *local* *explanation*, and it is *contested*.

### 3.2 Kitcher's argument patterns

**The *thesis*** (Kitcher, “Explanatory Unification,” 1981; “Explanatory Unification and the Causal Structure of the World,” 1989). The *explanation* *works* by *subsuming* the *phenomena* under the *argument* *patterns* (the *schemata*), and the *unification* *is* the *reduction* of the *patterns* *to* the *fewer* *schemata*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explanation} = \text{the instantiation of a schematic argument
}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *provides* a *precise* *measure* (the *number* of the *schemata*), and it *fits* the *practice* of the *theoretical* *science* (the *unification* of the *different* *domains* under the *same* *schema*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*spurious* *unification*’* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Morrison, *Unifying Scientific Theories*, 2000). The *unification* *can* be *spurious* (a *theory* that *unifies* the *phenomena* *with* a *false* *mechanism*; the *Ptolemaic* *astronomy* *unifies* the *planetary* *motions*), and the *unification* *is* *not* *sufficient* for the *explanation*.

**The *reply***. The *unification* *is* *constrained* by the *empirical* *adequacy* (the *theory* must *be* *true*), and the *reply* *adds* the *truth* *condition*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *causation*’* *objection*. The *unification* *loses* the *causal* *asymmetry* (the *unification* is *symmetric*), and the *explanation* *requires* the *asymmetry*.

**The *reply*** (Kitcher 1989). The *causal* *asymmetry* *emerges* from the *unification* (the *argument* *patterns* *encode* the *causal* *structure*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *asymmetry* in the *patterns*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.3 Strevens and the causal unification

**The *thesis*** (Strevens, *Depth*, 2008). The *explanation* *works* by *unifying* the *causal* *structure*: the *theories* that *capture* the *common* *causal* *elements* (the *difference*-*makers*) *explain* the *phenomena*, and the *depth* of the *explanation* is the *generality* of the *causal* *elements*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Depth} = \text{the generality of the causal elements
}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *combines* the *causal* and the *unificationist* *insights* (the *explanation* is *causal*, and the *good* *explanation* is *unified*), and it *fits* the *physical* *sciences*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *unification*’* *objection*. The *depth* *is* *not* the *unification* (the *deep* *explanation* *can* be *less* *unified*), and the *account* *is* *not* *a* *unificationist* *theory*.

**The *reply***. The *depth* *is* the *unification* *of* the *causal* *elements* (the *general* *elements* *unify* the *phenomena*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *depth* in the *causal* *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.4 The state of the unificationist theory

**The *assessment***. The *unificationist* *theory* *captures* the *theoretical* *virtue* of *explanation*, and it *faces* the *‘*no* *measure*’* and the *‘*spurious* *unification*’* *objections*.

| The *version* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Friedman** | The global unification | The no-measure |
| **Kitcher** | The argument patterns | The spurious unification |
| **Strevens** | The causal depth | The no-unification |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *causal* *unificationism*: the *explanation* *works* by *unifying* the *causal* *structure* (the *causal* and the *unificationist* *insights*), and the *unification* *is* *constrained* by the *empirical* *adequacy* and the *causal* *asymmetry*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *measure* of the *unification* is *contested*.

## 4. The Mechanistic Theory

### 4.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Machamer, Darden & Craver, “Thinking about Mechanisms,” 2000; Bechtel & Abrahamsen, “Explanation: A Mechanist Alternative,” 2005; Craver, *Explaining the Brain*, 2007; Ch. 21, §4.1). To *explain* a *phenomenon* is to *describe* the *mechanism* that *produces* it.

**The *structure***.

| The *element* | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenomenon** | The *behavior* to be *explained* | The *memory* *consolidation* |
| **Entities** | The *components* | The *neurons*, the *synapses*, the *molecules* |
| **Activities** | What the *entities* *do* | The *firing*, the *binding*, the *phosphorylation* |
| **Organization** | How the *components* are *arranged* | The *circuit* *structure* |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences (the biology, the neuroscience, the cognitive science) explain by describing mechanisms (the practice).} \\
2. & \text{The description of a mechanism reduces the phenomenon to the activities of the components (the explanation).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The explanation is the mechanism description (the mechanistic theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *fits* the *explanatory* *practice* of the *special* *sciences*, and it *captures* the *causal* *structure* (the *mechanism* is the *vehicle* of the *causation*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *‘*laws*’* (the *special* *sciences* *do* not *need* the *laws*; Ch. 39, §2).

### 4.2 The levels problem

**The *problem***. The *mechanism* is *nested* (the *mechanism* for the *memory* is *composed* of the *mechanisms*; Ch. 21, §8), and the *nesting* *raises* the *question* of *which* *level* is the *explanatory* *level*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The phenomenon can be explained at the different levels (the molecular, the cellular, the circuit).} \\
2. & \text{The levels are all causally relevant (the organization determines the activities).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The explanation requires a principled choice of the level (the levels problem).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *‘*pragmatic*’* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Craver 2007, Ch. 5). The *level* *depends* on the *explanatory* *interest* (the *different* *interests* *yield* the *different* *levels*), and the *choice* is *pragmatic* (Ch. 21, §8.2).

**The *virtue***. The *response* *fits* the *practice* (the *different* *sciences* *use* the *different* *levels*), and it *avoids* the *absolute* *hierarchy*.

**The *objection***. The *response* *threatens* the *realism* (the *levels* are *perspectival*, not *real*), and the *objection* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*structural*’* *response***. The *level* is *determined* by the *causal* *structure* (the *level* that *causally* *explains* the *phenomenon*), and the *structure* *is* *real*.

**The *virtue***. The *response* *grounds* the *level* in the *causation*, and it *fits* the *causal* *theory* (§2).

**The *objection***. The *causal* *structure* is *itself* *level*-*relative* (the *causes* at the *molecular* *level* differ from the *causes* at the *circuit* *level*), and the *response* is *circular*.

### 4.3 The normativity problem

**The *problem***. The *mechanism* *description* *presupposes* the *distinction* between the *normal* *operation* and the *pathological* *deviation* (the *mechanism* *is* the *normal* *production* of the *phenomenon*), and the *distinction* is *normative* (Ch. 42, §2).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The mechanism explains the phenomenon when it works normally (the disease is the deviation).} \\
2. & \text{The normal operation requires a norm (the function, the proper function; Ch. 42, §2).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The mechanism description presupposes a normative theory (the normativity problem).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *‘*etiological*’* *response*** (Millikan 1984; Ch. 42, §2). The *norm* is *grounded* in the *selection* *history* (the *function* is the *effect* for which the *trait* was *selected*), and the *reply* *naturalizes* the *norm*.

**The *objection***. The *selection* *history* is *not* *sufficient* (the *function* can *be* a *by*-*product*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *norm*’* *response***. The *mechanism* *description* is *descriptive* (it *describes* the *causal* *structure*), and the *norm* is *external* (the *physician* *adds* the *norm*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *normativity* at the *cost* of the *explanatory* *power* (the *mechanism* *cannot* *distinguish* the *normal* from the *pathological*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.4 The state of the mechanistic theory

**The *assessment***. The *mechanistic* *theory* is the *most* *influential* *account* of the *special* *sciences*, and it is *qualified* by the *levels* and the *normativity* *problems*.

| The *version* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **MDC (2000)** | The mechanism description | The normativity |
| **Craver (2007)** | The levels; the explanatory relevance | The level problem |
| **Bechtel & Abrahamsen** | The decomposition | The normativity |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *mechanistic* *account*: the *mechanism* *description* is the *form* of the *explanation* in the *special* *sciences* (the *mechanistic* *insight*), and the *causal* *structure* is the *content* (the *causal* *insight*), and the *normativity* is *grounded* in the *selection* *history* (the *etiological* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *level* *choice* is *pragmatic*.

## 5. The Pragmatic and Contrastive Theories

### 5.1 Van Fraassen's pragmatic theory

**The *thesis*** (van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image*, 1980, Ch. 5; “The Pragmatics of Explanation,” 1977). The *explanation* is *not* a *relation* between the *theory* and the *fact*; it is an *answer* to a *why*-*question*, and the *why*-*question* has a *pragmatic* *structure*.

**The *structure***. A *why*-*question* is a *triple*:

$$
\langle P_k, \ X, \ R \rangle
$$

where:

- $P_k$ is the *topic* (the *fact* to be *explained*);
- $X$ is the *contrast* *class* (the *alternatives* to $P_k$);
- $R$ is the *relevance* *relation* (the *respect* in which the *answer* is *relevant*).

**The *examples***.

| The *why*-*question* | The *topic* | The *contrast* | The *relevance* |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Why did the *cat* *die*?” | The cat died | Rather than *live* | The *cause* of the *death* |
| “Why did the *cat* *die* (rather than the dog)?” | The cat died | Rather than the *dog* | The *difference* *between* the *cat* and the *dog* |
| “Why did the *cat* *die* (rather than bark)?” | The cat died | Rather than *bark* | The *category* *mistake* (the *nonsense*) |

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *ambiguity* of the *why*-*questions* (the *same* *event* *can* be *explained* *differently* *depending* on the *contrast*), and it *avoids* the *single*-*relation* *assumption* (there is *no* *unique* *explanation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *theory* *reduces* the *explanation* to the *pragmatics* (the *explanation* is *the* *answer* *we* *give*, not the *relation* *in* the *world*), and the *theory* *loses* the *objective* *explanation*.

**The *reply*** (van Fraassen 1980, pp. 130–134). The *objective* *explanation* *is* the *answer* *that* *fits* the *facts* (the *scientific* *explanation* is *the* *answer* *constrained* by the *theory* and the *evidence*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *constraint*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *relevance* *relation*’* *objection*. The *relevance* *relation* is *vague* (the *different* *relations* *yield* the *different* *explanations*, and the *choice* is *not* *determined*), and the *account* *fails* to *specify* the *relevant* *relation*.

**The *reply***. The *relevance* *relation* is *determined* by the *context* (the *interests*, the *background* *knowledge*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *relation* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.2 The contrastive structure

**The *thesis*** (Hitchcock, “The Role of Contrast in Causal and Explanatory Claims,” 1996; Lipton, “Contrastive Explanation,” 1990; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *explanations* are *contrastive*: the *explanation* *answers* the *question* “Why $P$ rather than $Q$?”, and the *answer* *identifies* the *difference* *between* the *case* $P$ and the *case* $Q$.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explain}(P \ \text{rather than} \ Q) = \text{the cause that makes the difference between } P \text{ and } Q
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *contrastive* *nature* of the *explanation* (the *same* *fact* *demands* the *different* *explanations* *depending* on the *contrast*), and it *fits* the *causal* *difference*-*making* (Ch. 18, §2).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *contrast*’* *objection*. The *contrast* is *not* *always* *explicit* (the *explanation* *can* be *given* *without* the *contrast*), and the *theory* *over*-*intellectualizes* the *explanation*.

**The *reply***. The *contrast* is *implicit* (the *question* *presupposes* a *contrast* *class*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *contrast* in the *pragmatics*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objective* *contrast*’* *objection*. The *contrast* *class* is *chosen* by the *questioner* (the *different* *contrasts* *yield* the *different* *explanations*), and the *choice* is *subjective* (the *explanation* *depends* on the *interest*).

**The *reply***. The *contrast* *class* is *constrained* by the *context* and the *evidence* (the *scientific* *explanation* *uses* the *contrast* *class* *that* *makes* the *difference*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *constraint*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 The pragmatics of why-questions

**The *thesis***. The *why*-*questions* *presuppose* the *facts* (the *topic*), and the *answers* *depend* on the *interests* (the *contrast*, the *relevance*), and the *presuppositions* *determine* the *appropriateness* of the *answers*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The why-question presupposes the topic (the cat died; the presupposition is true).} \\
2. & \text{The presupposition determines the appropriate answer (the answer must explain the topic, and the contrast).} \\
3. & \text{The answer depends on the interest (the contrast class, the relevance relation).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The why-questions are pragmatic (the pragmatic theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *pragmatics* *connects* the *explanation* to the *speech*-*act* *theory* (Ch. 34, §4) and to the *epistemology* of the *inquiry* (Ch. 8), and it *shows* that the *explanation* is *not* a *single* *relation* but a *family* of *answers*.

### 5.4 The state of the pragmatic theory

**The *assessment***. The *pragmatic* *theory* *captures* the *context*-*sensitivity* of the *explanation*, and it *faces* the *objectivity* and the *relevance* *objections*.

| The *version* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **van Fraassen** | The why-question structure | The objectivity |
| **Hitchcock (contrastive)** | The contrast class | The no-contrast |
| **Lipton (contrastive)** | The difference-making | The subjective contrast |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *contrastivism*: the *explanations* are *contrastive* (the *contrastive* *insight*), and the *contrast* *class* is *constrained* by the *causal* *difference*-*making* (the *causal* *insight*), and the *answers* are *pragmatic* (the *pragmatic* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *objectivity* of the *explanation* is *grounded* in the *causal* *structure*.

## 6. Understanding and Its Relation to Knowledge

### 6.1 The distinction

**The *question***. Is *explanation* the *same* as *understanding*? And is *understanding* the *same* as *knowledge*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Understanding is knowledge** | The *understanding* *is* a *kind* of *knowledge* (the *knowledge* of the *causes*, the *knowledge* of the *explanations*) | Grimm (2006, 2011); Khalifa (2012) |
| **Understanding is not knowledge** | The *understanding* *exceeds* the *knowledge* (it *requires* the *grasp* of the *relations*, the *coherence*, the *abilities*) | Kvanvig (2003); Elgin (2007) |
| **Understanding is explanatory** | The *understanding* *is* the *grasp* of the *explanation* (the *explanation* *constitutes* the *understanding*) | de Regt (2017); Strevens (2013) |
| **Understanding is ability** | The *understanding* *is* the *ability* to *manipulate*, *predict*, and *explain* | de Regt (2017); the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *argument* *for* the *distinction*** (Kvanvig, *The Value of Knowledge and the Pursuit of Understanding*, 2003, Ch. 8).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The understanding requires the grasp of the explanatory relations (the why, the how).} \\
2. & \text{The knowledge does not require the grasp (the propositional knowledge can be isolated).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The understanding is not reducible to the knowledge (the distinction).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *distinction* *captures* the *intuition* that the *understanding* *involves* the *grasp* of the *relations* (the *coherence*, the *manipulability*), and it *explains* why the *understanding* is *more* *valuable* than the *isolated* *knowledge* (Ch. 7, §7).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *factivity*’* *objection* (Kvanvig 2003; Elgin 2007). The *understanding* *does* not *require* the *truth* (the *understanding* can *be* *achieved* by a *false* *theory*; the *Ptolemaic* *astronomy* *provides* the *understanding* of the *planetary* *motions*), and the *understanding* is *not* *factive*.

**The *reply*** (Grimm 2006, 2011). The *understanding* *does* *require* the *truth* (the *grasp* of the *false* *relations* *is* *not* the *understanding*; the *Ptolemaic* *astronomy* *provides* the *ability* to *predict*, not the *understanding* of *why*). The *reply* *distinguishes* the *understanding* from the *predictive* *success*, and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *value*’* *objection* (Ch. 7, §7). The *understanding* *is* *valuable* (the *intrinsic* *value*), and the *knowledge* *is* *valuable* (the *instrumental* *value*), and the *distinction* *does* not *explain* the *difference* (the *two* are *both* *valuable*).

**The *reply*** (Kvanvig 2003, Ch. 8). The *understanding* is *intrinsically* *valuable* (it is *constitutive* of the *flourishing*), and the *knowledge* is *instrumentally* *valuable* (it is the *means*), and the *distinction* *grounds* the *difference*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.2 The pragmatics of understanding

**The *thesis*** (de Regt, *Understanding Scientific Understanding*, 2017; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *understanding* is the *ability* to *use* a *theory* (to *predict*, to *manipulate*, to *explain*), and the *ability* is *context*-*dependent* (the *different* *theories* *provide* the *understanding* *in* the *different* *contexts*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Understanding}(T) = \text{the ability to use } T \text{ in the context}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *understanding* in the *practice*, and it *fits* the *scientific* *education* (the *student* *understands* when she can *use* the *theory*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *grasp*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *intrinsic* *value*’* *objection*. The *understanding* *as* *ability* is *instrumental* (the *ability* is *valuable* *for* the *success*), and the *intrinsic* *value* is *lost*.

**The *reply***. The *ability* is *intrinsically* *valuable* (the *flourishing* of the *agent*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *value* in the *agency*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *truth*’* *objection*. The *ability* *can* be *achieved* by a *false* *theory* (the *Ptolemaic* *astronomy*), and the *understanding* *requires* the *truth*.

**The *reply***. The *ability* is *constrained* by the *truth* (the *theory* *must* be *empirically* *adequate*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *adequacy*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The state of the understanding debate

**The *assessment***. The *understanding* is *contested*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *factive* *account*: the *understanding* *requires* the *grasp* of the *explanatory* *relations* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *grasp* *requires* the *truth* (the *factivity* *insight*), and the *grasp* is *manifested* in the *ability* (the *pragmatic* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *factivity* is *contested*.

## 7. Explanation Across the Disciplines

### 7.1 Biology

**The *question***. Is the *biological* *explanation* *different* in *kind* from the *physical*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *causal* *account*** | The *biological* *explanation* is the *causal* *explanation* (the *mechanism*, the *selection*) | the *mechanist* *tradition* |
| **The *teleological* *account*** | The *biological* *explanation* *uses* the *teleological* *notions* (the *function*, the *purpose*; Ch. 42, §2) | the *etiological* *tradition* (Millikan) |
| **The *historical* *account*** | The *biological* *explanation* is *historical* (the *selection* *history*; the *phylogeny*) | the *evolutionary* *tradition* |
| **The *lawless* *account*** | The *biology* *has* no *laws* (the *ceteris* *paribus* only; Ch. 39, §2) | the *Humean* *tradition* |

**The *argument* (the *teleological* *account).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The biological explanation uses the functional notions (the heart is for pumping).} \\
2. & \text{The functional notions are teleological (they refer to the function, the goal).} \\
3. & \text{The teleological notions require the selection history (the etiological account; Ch. 42, §2).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The biological explanation is teleological (the teleological insight), and the teleology is naturalized by the selection (the naturalistic insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *biology* *tests* the *general* *account* of *explanation*: the *teleological* *notions* *resist* the *causal* *account* (the *function* is *not* a *cause*), and the *selection* *history* *provides* the *naturalistic* *grounding* (the *etiological* *account*).

### 7.2 Psychology and the social sciences

**The *question***. Is the *psychological* *explanation* *different* in *kind* from the *physical*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *reasons* *account*** | The *psychological* *explanation* *cites* the *reasons* (the *beliefs*, the *desires*; Ch. 28, §1) | the *Davidsonian* *tradition* |
| **The *causal* *account*** | The *psychological* *explanation* is *causal* (the *reasons* are *causes*; Ch. 28, §1.2) | the *naturalistic* *tradition* |
| **The *interpretive* *account*** | The *psychological* *explanation* is *interpretive* (the *meaning*, the *narrative*; the *hermeneutic* *tradition*) | the *continental* *tradition* |
| **The *mechanistic* *account*** | The *psychological* *explanation* is *mechanistic* (the *neural* *mechanism*; Ch. 21, §4.2) | the *mechanist* *tradition* |

**The *argument* (the *reasons* *account).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The psychological explanation cites the reasons (she left because she believed the train was leaving).} \\
2. & \text{The reasons rationalize the action (they make it intelligible, not merely caused).} \\
3. & \text{The rationalization requires the normative structure (the logic of the reasons; Ch. 28, §2).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The psychological explanation is reason-giving (the reasons insight), and the reasons are causes (the causal insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *psychology* *tests* the *general* *account*: the *reasons* *explain* *without* being *merely* *causal* (the *rationalization*), and the *causal* *account* *must* *accommodate* the *reasons* (the *Davidsonian* *synthesis*, Ch. 28, §1.4).

### 7.3 History

**The *question***. Is the *historical* *explanation* *different* in *kind* from the *scientific*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *covering*-*law* *account*** | The *historical* *explanation* *subsumes* the *events* under the *laws* (the *D-N* *model*) | Hempel (1942) |
| **The *narrative* *account*** | The *historical* *explanation* *tells* a *story* (the *narrative* *connects* the *events*) | the *narrative* *tradition* (Danto; Ricoeur) |
| **The *colligation* *account*** | The *historical* *explanation* *colligates* the *events* (it *places* them *under* a *concept*) | Walsh (1951) |
| **The *causal* *account*** | The *historical* *explanation* *identifies* the *causes* (the *causal* *chains*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *argument* (the *narrative* *account).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The historical explanation narrates the events (the story of the revolution, the story of the war).} \\
2. & \text{The narrative connects the events (the causal links, the motives, the contingencies).} \\
3. & \text{The connection is not the subsumption (the narrative does not require the laws).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The historical explanation is narrative (the narrative insight), and the narrative is causal (the causal insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *history* *tests* the *general* *account*: the *narrative* *explains* *without* the *laws* (the *narrative* *insight*), and the *narrative* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *causal* *chains* (the *narrative* *insight*), and the *two* *are* *complementary*.

### 7.4 One concept or many?

**The *question***. Is *explanation* *one* *concept* (the *general* *account*) or *many* (the *domain*-*specific* *accounts*)?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Monism** | There is *one* *correct* *account* of *explanation* | the *causal* *tradition* |
| **Pluralism** | There are *many* *accounts* (the *different* *domains*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Deflationism** | The *explanation* is *not* a *natural* *kind* (the *accounts* are *tools*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *argument* (the *pluralism*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The explanation differs across the domains (the causal in the physics, the teleological in the biology, the narrative in the history, the proof in the mathematics).} \\
2. & \text{The differences are not reducible to a single account (the teleology, the narrative resist the causal account).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The explanation is plural (the explanatory pluralism).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *pluralism* *fits* the *practice* of the *disciplines*, and it *avoids* the *Procrustean* *reduction*.

**The *objections*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *‘*anything* *goes*’* *objection*). The *pluralism* *risks* the *relativism* (any *account* is as *good* as any *other*), and the *pluralism* *loses* the *unity* of *explanation*.

**The *reply***. The *pluralism* is *constrained* by the *explanatory* *virtues* (the *understanding*, the *truth*, the *unification*; Ch. 4, §3), and the *reply* *grounds* the *pluralism* in the *virtues*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.5 The state of the cross-disciplinary debate

**The *assessment***. The *explanation* is *plural* (the *different* *domains* *require* the *different* *accounts*), and the *pluralism* is *constrained* (the *explanatory* *virtues*).

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism*: the *explanation* is a *family* of *practices* (the *pluralist* *insight*), and the *practices* *share* the *goal* of *understanding* (the *unifying* *insight*), and the *goal* is *constrained* by the *truth* and the *causal* *structure* (the *realist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *family* *resists* a *single* *account*.

## 8. Mathematical Explanation

### 8.1 The question

**The *question***. Is there *mathematical* *explanation*? That is, does a *proof* *explain* the *theorem*, or does it only *establish* it?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *no*-*explanation* *view*** | The *mathematics* *proves*, and it does *not* *explain* (the *explanation* is *causal*, and the *mathematics* is *not* *causal*) | the *contemporary* *literature* (the *naturalistic* *tradition*) |
| **The *explanatory* *view*** | Some *proofs* *explain* (they *show* *why* the *theorem* is *true*) | Steiner (1978); Mancosu (2008); Lange (2014, 2016) |
| **The *pragmatic* *view*** | The *explanation* is *relative* to the *mathematical* *community* | the *contemporary* *literature* (the *sociological* *tradition*) |

**The *argument* (the *explanatory* *view).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The mathematicians distinguish the proofs that explain from the proofs that do not (the practice).} \\
2. & \text{The distinction requires the notion of mathematical explanation (the explanatory property).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The mathematical explanation exists (the explanatory view).}
\end{array}
$$

### 8.2 Steiner's criterion

**The *thesis*** (Steiner, “Mathematical Explanation,” 1978). A *proof* *explains* a *theorem* *iff* the *proof* *depends* on a *characteristic* *property* of the *entities* *involved* (a *property* that *makes* the *theorem* *true*, not a *coincidence*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explanation}(p) = \text{the proof that depends on the characteristic property of } p
$$

**The *example***. The *explanation* of the *theorem* “$1+2+\cdots+n = n(n+1)/2$” is the *algebraic* *proof* (it *depends* on the *algebraic* *structure* of the *sum*), not the *inductive* *proof* (it *verifies* the *cases*, and it does *not* *explain* *why* the *identity* *holds*).

**The *virtue***. The *criterion* *captures* the *distinction* between the *explanatory* and the *non*-*explanatory* *proofs*, and it *fits* the *mathematical* *practice*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *characteristic* *property*’* *objection*** (Resnik & Kushner, “Explanation, Independence, and Realism in Mathematics,” 1987; Mancosu 2008, Ch. 2). The *characteristic* *property* is *not* *well*-*defined* (the *different* *proofs* *depend* on the *different* *properties*), and the *criterion* *is* *vague*.

**The *reply*** (Lange 2014, 2016). The *characteristic* *property* is the *property* that *the* *mathematical* *community* *treats* as *relevant*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *property* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *explanation* *requires* the *causation*, and the *mathematics* is *not* *causal* (the *naturalistic* *tradition*; Ch. 18, §5). The *objection* *undermines* the *explanatory* *view*.

**The *reply*** (Lange 2014; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *explanation* is *not* *always* *causal* (the *unificationist* *account*, the *deductive* *account*), and the *mathematics* *explains* by the *unification* and the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 8.3 Mancosu and the varieties

**The *thesis*** (Mancosu, “Mathematical Explanation: Why It Matters,” 2008). The *mathematical* *explanation* *takes* *several* *forms* (the *causal*, the *unificationist*, the *structural*, the *teleological*), and the *forms* *are* *distinct* (no *single* *account* *captures* *all*).

**The *forms***.

| The *form* | The *claim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal** | The *mathematical* *explanation* *identifies* the *cause* (the *controversial* *form*) | The *explanation* of the *Euler* *characteristic* |
| **Unificationist** | The *explanation* *unifies* the *theorems* | The *group* *theory* *unifies* the *algebraic* *results* |
| **Structural** | The *explanation* *exhibits* the *structure* | The *explanation* of the *topological* *invariance* |
| **Teleological** | The *explanation* *shows* the *goal* (the *proof* *strategy*) | The *explanation* of the *classification* |

**The *virtue***. The *varieties* *capture* the *diversity* of the *mathematical* *explanation*, and the *diversity* *supports* the *explanatory* *pluralism* (§7.4).

**The *objections***. The *varieties* *are* *not* *all* *legitimate* (the *causal* *form* is *contested*), and the *pluralism* *risks* the *relativism*.

### 8.4 Lange and the explanatory proofs

**The *thesis*** (Lange, “Aspects of Mathematical Explanation,” 2014; *Because without Cause*, 2016). A *proof* *explains* a *theorem* *iff* the *proof* *identifies* the *feature* of the *theorem* that *makes* it *true* (the *“*because*”* *relation*), and the *feature* is *the* *mathematical* *ground* (Ch. 16, §3.3).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Explanation}(p) = \text{the proof that identifies the ground of } p
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *mathematical* *explanation* in the *grounding* *relation* (the *structural* *insight*), and it *fits* the *mathematical* *practice* (the *distinction* between the *explanatory* and the *non*-*explanatory* *proofs*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *ground*’* *objection***. The *mathematical* *objects* are *abstract* (Ch. 15, §6), and the *grounding* *relation* *is* *not* *available* (the *abstract* *objects* *do* not *ground* the *theorems*).

**The *reply*** (Lange 2016). The *grounding* is *mathematical* (the *theorem* is *grounded* in the *axioms* and the *definitions*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *mathematics* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *grounding* is *not* the *explanation* (the *ground* *can* be *identified* *without* *explaining*), and the *account* *does* not *capture* the *explanatory* *force*.

**The *reply***. The *grounding* *is* the *explanation* (the *identification* of the *ground* *is* the *understanding* of *why*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *grounding*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 8.5 The state of the mathematical explanation

**The *assessment***. The *mathematical* *explanation* is *contested*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *structuralism*: the *mathematical* *explanations* *identify* the *grounds* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *grounds* are *mathematical* (the *mathematical* *insight*), and the *explanations* are *plural* (the *pluralist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *mathematical* *grounding* is *contested*.

## 9. Bad Explanation

### 9.1 The vices

**The *vices***.

| The *vice* | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Circularity** | The *explanation* *assumes* the *explanandum* | “Opium puts people to sleep because it is dormitive” |
| **Redescription** | The *explanation* *restates* the *explanandum* in other words | “The glass broke because it is fragile” |
| **Ad hocness** | The *explanation* is *patched* to *save* the *theory* | The *epicycles* in the *Ptolemaic* *astronomy* |
| **Just-so story** | The *explanation* is *speculative* and *unconstrained* | The *‘*explanation*’* of the *giraffe*’s *neck* by the *‘*need*’* to *reach* the *leaves* |
| **Vacuity** | The *explanation* is *consistent* with *everything* | “It happens because of the *laws* of nature” |

**The *structure***. The *vices* *share* the *structure*: the *explanation* *fails* to *add* the *information* (the *circularity*, the *redescription*, the *vacuity*) or *fails* to *be* *constrained* by the *evidence* (the *ad hocness*, the *just*-*so* *story*).

### 9.2 The criteria

**The *criteria* for the *good* *explanation***.

| The *criterion* | The *demand* |
|---|---|
| **Informativeness** | The *explanation* *adds* the *information* (it is not the *restatement*) |
| **Testability** | The *explanation* *makes* the *testable* *predictions* |
| **Independence** | The *explanation* *does* not *assume* the *explanandum* |
| **Unification** | The *explanation* *unifies* the *phenomena* (Ch. 4, §3.1) |
| **Causal** | The *explanation* *identifies* the *causal* *structure* (the *causal* *insight*) |
| **Contrast** | The *explanation* *answers* the *contrastive* *question* (the *pragmatic* *insight*) |

**The *significance***. The *criteria* *combine* the *logical* (the *independence*, the *informativeness*), the *empirical* (the *testability*), and the *pragmatic* (the *unification*, the *contrast*), and the *combination* *grounds* the *assessment* of the *explanations* (Ch. 4, §3).

### 9.3 The ‘just-so’ stories

**The *thesis*** (Gould & Lewontin, “The Spandrels of San Marco,” 1979). The *adaptationist* *explanations* in the *evolutionary* *biology* *are* *often* *just*-*so* *stories* (the *plausible* *narratives* that *are* *not* *tested*), and the *stories* *fail* to *be* *scientific* *explanations*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The adaptationist explanation posits a selection history (the trait was selected for the function).} \\
2. & \text{The history is not tested (the narrative is plausible, and it is not falsified).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The explanation is a just-so story (the speculative narrative), and it fails the testability criterion.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *critique* *shows* that the *explanation* *requires* the *testability* (the *constraint* by the *evidence*), and the *critique* *connects* the *philosophy* of *explanation* to the *philosophy* of *biology* (Ch. 42, §5).

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *‘*testable* *narrative*’* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *adaptationist* *explanations* *can* be *tested* (the *comparative* *method*, the *experimental* *method*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *explanations* in the *evidence*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*pluralist*’* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *adaptationist* *explanations* *are* *one* *kind* of *explanation* (the *selection* *history*), and the *other* *kinds* (the *constraint*, the *drift*, the *by*-*product*) *are* *equally* *legitimate* (the *pluralism*; Ch. 42, §5). The *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *pluralism*.

### 9.4 The circularity and the redescription

**The *circularity***. The *explanation* *assumes* the *explanandum* (the *dormitive* *virtue*), and the *circle* *is* *vicious* (the *explanation* *adds* nothing).

**The *redescription***. The *explanation* *restates* the *explanandum* (the *fragility* *explains* the *breaking*), and the *restatement* *adds* nothing (the *fragility* *is* the *disposition* *to* *break*; Ch. 15, §5.3).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The explanation adds the information (the informativeness criterion).} \\
2. & \text{The circularity and the redescription add no information (they restate the explanandum).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The circularity and the redescription are not explanations (the vices).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *vices* *ground* the *criteria* (the *informativeness*, the *independence*), and the *criteria* *distinguish* the *good* from the *bad* *explanation*.

### 9.5 The state of the bad explanation

**The *assessment***. The *vices* *are* *real*, and the *criteria* *are* *the* *standards* for the *assessment*.

| The *vice* | The *criterion* *violated* | The *remedy* |
|---|---|---|
| **Circularity** | The independence | The *independent* *evidence* |
| **Redescription** | The informativeness | The *new* *information* |
| **Ad hocness** | The testability | The *testable* *prediction* |
| **Just-so story** | The testability | The *empirical* *constraint* |
| **Vacuity** | The informativeness | The *specific* *content* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *criteria*: the *explanations* *are* *assessed* by the *criteria* (the *informativeness*, the *testability*, the *independence*, the *unification*, the *causal* *structure*), and the *criteria* *are* *not* *algorithmic* (the *explanations* *require* the *judgment*; Ch. 4, §3.2), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *criteria* *conflict* and the *judgment* is *contested*.

## 10. The State of the Debate

### 10.1 What is settled

- **The D-N model is untenable as a general account.** The *asymmetry*, the *irrelevance*, and the *no*-*laws* *objections* are *decisive*.
- **The causal theory is the most influential account.** The *causation* *explains* the *asymmetry* and the *relevance* (the *causal* *insight*), and the *account* *fits* the *experimental* *method*.
- **The unificationist theory captures the theoretical virtue.** The *unification* is the *goal* of the *theoretical* *science*, and the *account* *faces* the *no*-*measure* and the *spurious*-*unification* *objections*.
- **The mechanistic theory is the most influential account of the special sciences.** The *mechanism* *description* *fits* the *biology*, the *neuroscience*, and the *cognitive* *science*.
- **The pragmatic theory captures the context-sensitivity.** The *why*-*questions* *presuppose* the *contrast* and the *interest*, and the *account* *faces* the *objectivity* *objection*.
- **The understanding is not reducible to the knowledge.** The *grasp* of the *explanatory* *relations* *exceeds* the *propositional* *knowledge*, and the *factivity* is *contested*.
- **The explanation is plural across the disciplines.** The *causal*, the *teleological*, the *narrative*, and the *mathematical* *explanations* *resist* a *single* *account*.
- **The mathematical explanation is contested.** The *explanatory* *proofs* *identify* the *grounds*, and the *nature* of the *grounds* is *contested*.
- **The bad explanation is real.** The *circularity*, the *redescription*, the *ad hocness*, and the *just*-*so* *stories* *violate* the *criteria*.

### 10.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *explanation*.** Is it a *causal* relation, a *unification*, a *mechanism*, or a *pragmatic* answer?
- **The *nature* of *understanding*.** Is it a *kind* of *knowledge*, an *ability*, or a *sui* *generis* *state*?
- **The *virtues* of *explanation*.** Are the *explanatory* *virtues* *epistemic* (the *truth*-*conducive*) or *pragmatic* (the *useful*)?
- **The *mathematical* *explanation*.** Is there a *mathematical* *grounding*, and is it *explanatory*?
- **The *interdisciplinarity*.** Is the *explanation* *one* *concept* or *many*?
- **The *bad* *explanation*.** Are the *criteria* *sufficient*, and is the *judgment* *reliable*?

### 10.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* between the *explanation*, the *prediction*, and the *understanding*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *flagpole*, the *barometer*, the *paresis*, the *finkish* *cases* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *D-N* *schema*, the *structural* *equations*, the *contrastive* *structure* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: the theory of explanation is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

### 10.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D-N (Hempel)** | Explanation = deduction from laws | Precise; logical | The asymmetry; the irrelevance |
| **I-S (Hempel)** | Statistical explanation = high probability | Extends to statistics | The ambiguity |
| **Causal (Salmon)** | Explanation = causal structure | The asymmetry; the relevance | The statistical |
| **Interventionist (Woodward)** | Explanation = manipulability | The experimental method | The fundamental physics |
| **Unificationist (Kitcher)** | Explanation = argument patterns | The theoretical virtue | The spurious unification |
| **Causal depth (Strevens)** | Explanation = causal unification | The physical sciences | The no-unification |
| **Mechanistic (MDC)** | Explanation = mechanism | The special sciences | The levels; the normativity |
| **Pragmatic (van Fraassen)** | Explanation = answer to why-question | The context-sensitivity | The objectivity |
| **Contrastive (Hitchcock)** | Explanation = contrastive | The difference-making | The no-contrast |
| **Understanding (Kvanvig)** | Understanding ≠ knowledge | The value | The factivity |
| **Mathematical (Steiner)** | Proofs can explain | The mathematical practice | The characteristic property |
| **Pluralism** | Many explanations | Fits the disciplines | The unity |

The table is a map, not a verdict. The reader who wants to choose a position must decide which cost is acceptable — and the decision is a philosophical argument.

## Summary

- **The *D-N* *model*** (Hempel, Oppenheim) *analyzes* the *explanation* as the *deduction* from the *laws* and the *initial* *conditions*, and it *fails* on the *asymmetry*, the *irrelevance*, and the *no*-*laws* *objections*.
- **The *I-S* *model*** *extends* the *D-N* *framework* to the *statistical* *explanations*, and it *faces* the *ambiguity* (the *reference*-*class* *problem*) and the *irrelevance* (the *paresis* *case*).
- **The *causal* *theory*** (Salmon, Lewis, Woodward) *grounds* the *explanation* in the *causation* (the *asymmetry*, the *relevance*), and it *fits* the *experimental* *method*.
- **The *unificationist* *theory*** (Friedman, Kitcher, Strevens) *grounds* the *explanation* in the *unification* (the *theoretical* *virtue*), and it *faces* the *no*-*measure* and the *spurious*-*unification* *objections*.
- **The *mechanistic* *theory*** (MDC, Craver) *grounds* the *explanation* in the *mechanism* *description* (the *entities*, the *activities*, the *organization*), and it *fits* the *special* *sciences*, and it *faces* the *levels* and the *normativity* *problems*.
- **The *pragmatic* and *contrastive* *theories*** (van Fraassen, Hitchcock, Lipton) *ground* the *explanation* in the *why*-*questions* (the *topic*, the *contrast*, the *relevance*), and they *capture* the *context*-*sensitivity*.
- **The *understanding*** is *not* *reducible* to the *knowledge* (the *grasp* of the *relations*), and the *factivity* is *contested*; the *understanding* is the *goal* of the *explanation*.
- **The *explanation* is *plural* across the *disciplines** (the *causal*, the *teleological*, the *narrative*, the *mathematical*), and the *pluralism* is *constrained* by the *explanatory* *virtues*.
- **The *mathematical* *explanation*** (Steiner, Mancosu, Lange) *identifies* the *grounds* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *nature* of the *grounds* is *contested*.
- **The *bad* *explanation*** (the *circularity*, the *redescription*, the *ad hocness*, the *just*-*so* *stories*) *violates* the *criteria* (the *informativeness*, the *testability*, the *independence*, the *unification*, the *causal* *structure*).
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *pluralism*: the *explanation* is a *family* of *practices* (the *pluralist* *insight*), and the *practices* *share* the *goal* of *understanding* (the *unifying* *insight*), and the *goal* is *constrained* by the *truth* and the *causal* *structure* (the *realist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *family* *resists* a *single* *account*.

> *A closing note.* This chapter has followed the *concept* of *explanation* — the *covering*-*law* *model*, the *causal* *theory*, the *unificationist* *theory*, the *mechanistic* *theory*, the *pragmatic* and *contrastive* *theories*, the *understanding*, the *interdisciplinary* *pluralism*, the *mathematical* *explanation*, and the *bad* *explanation* — and it has arrived at a *conclusion* that is less a *theory* than a *diagnosis*: the *explanation* is a *family* of *practices*, and the *family* is *unified* by the *goal* of *understanding* rather than by a *single* *logical* *form*.
