# Chapter 49 — Beauty, Art, and Aesthetic Judgment

> “The satisfaction which determines the judgement of taste is *disinterested*.”
> — Kant, *Critique of Judgement* (1790), §1, 5:205

> “Beauty is no quality in things themselves: It exists merely in the mind which contemplates them; and each mind perceives a different beauty.”
> — Hume, “Of the Standard of Taste” (1757)

> “To see something as art requires something the eye cannot descry — an atmosphere of artistic theory, a knowledge of the history of art: an *artworld*.”
> — Arthur Danto, “The Artworld” (1964), *Journal of Philosophy* 61

> “The whole of Tokyo is nothing but a vast and endless aesthetic surface.”
> — Tanizaki, *In Praise of Shadows* (1933), paraphrased

> “All art is quite useless.”
> — Oscar Wilde, Preface to *The Picture of Dorian Gray* (1890)

---

Chapter 45 examined the *content* of the moral judgment; Chapter 46 its *efficacy*; Chapter 47 its *architecture*; Chapter 48 its *point* (the *prudential* question). This chapter examines the *splendour* — the *aesthetic* *value*, which is neither the *moral* nor the *prudential* value, and which the *tradition* from Baumgarten to the *present* has treated as a *distinct* *kind* of value with its own *logic*, its own *objects*, and its own *authority*.

The *transition* is the *inversion* of the *prudential* *turn*. The *prudential* *judgment* is *agent*-*relative* (the *good for* *me*; Ch. 48, §1.1). The *aesthetic* *judgment* is *object*-*directed* — *it is about the object* (the *beautiful* *thing*, the *work*) — and yet it is *grounded* in the *subject*’s *experience* (the *pleasure*, the *delight*), and this *double* *aspect* is the *source* of the *whole* *problem*. Kant named the *antinomy*: the *judgment* of *taste* is *subjective* (it *rests* on the *feeling* of *pleasure*) and *universal* (it *claims* the *assent* of *everyone*), and the *two* *theses* are *both* *true* and *jointly* *puzzling*. The *chapter* is the *unpacking* of that *puzzle*.

The *chapter* proceeds in *eight* *movements*. §1 examines the *aesthetic* as a *domain* (the *properties*, the *experience*, the *judgment*). §2 examines *beauty* (the *subjectivism*, the *idealism*, the *formalism*, the *realism*). §3 examines the *theories* of *art* (the *imitation*, the *expression*, the *formalism*, the *institutional*, the *historical*, the *cluster*). §4 examines the *fiction* and the *paradox* of *emotional* *response*. §5 examines the *interpretation* and the *intention*. §6 examines the *testimony*, the *taste*, and the *expertise*. §7 examines the *value* of *art* (the *moralism*, the *autonomism*, the *immoralism*). §8 gives the *verdict*.

---

## 1. The Aesthetic

### 1.1 The structure of the question

**The *question***. The *aesthetic* *question* (*what is the aesthetic?*, *what is it to judge something beautiful?*, *what is a work of art?*) is *distinct* from the *moral* and the *prudential* *questions*, and the *distinction* is the *first* thing to *fix*, though it is *itself* *contestable* (the *aestheticism* *denies* it; §7).

**The *distinctions***.

| The *question* | The *content* | The *locution* |
|---|---|---|
| **Aesthetic** | The *beauty*, the *sublimity*, the *grace*, the *fittingness* of *form* | “*This* is *beautiful*” |
| **Artistic** | The *achievement*, the *significance*, the *art* *status* of the *object* | “*This* is *art*” |
| **Prudential** | The *good for* the *person* (Ch. 48, §1.1) | “*It* is *good* *for* *her*” |
| **Moral** | The *good* *simpliciter* (Ch. 45, §1.1) | “*It* is *good*” |

**The *significance***. The *aesthetic* is the *domain* in which the *value* is *borne* *by* the *object* *as* *experienced* (the *phenomenal* *insight*), and the *domain* is *presupposed* by the *practice* (the *criticism*, the *curation*, the *education*, the *everyday* *life*), and the *domain* is *contested* at its *borders* (the *conceptual* *art*, the *everyday* *aesthetics*, the *kitsch*).

**The *constraints***. The *theory* must *respect*:

| The *constraint* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *phenomenology*** | The *aesthetic* response is *immediate*, *non*-*inferential* (the *Sibley* *insight*) | The *perceptual* *model* |
| **The *normativity*** | The *judgment* *claims* *correctness* (the *no* *mere* *liking*) | The *realism* *pressure* |
| **The *disagreement*** | The *critics* *disagree* and the *disagreement* *matters* (the *no* *indifference*) | The *objectivity* *pressure* |
| **The *autonomy*** | The *aesthetic* value is *not* the *moral* or the *cognitive* (the *no* *reduction*) | The *autonomism* *pressure* |

**The *problem***. The *constraints* *pull* *apart*: the *phenomenology* *favors* the *subjectivism* (the *immediacy* of the *feeling*), the *normativity* *favors* the *realism* (the *claim* to *correctness*), the *disagreement* *favors* the *relativism* (the *no* *convergence*), and the *autonomy* *favors* the *formalism* (the *no* *content*).

### 1.2 Aesthetic properties

**The *thesis*** (Sibley, “Aesthetic Concepts,” 1959, *Philosophical Review* 68; Beardsley, *Aesthetics*, 1958, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* *properties* are *distinct* from the *non*-*aesthetic* *properties* (the *physical*, the *perceptual*, the *semantic*), and they are *dependent* on the *non*-*aesthetic* *properties* (the *supervenience* *insight*), and they are *known* by *taste* (the *perception* *insight*), and not by *inference* from *rules*.

**The *distinction***.

| The *class* | The *examples* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **Thin** | The *beautiful*, the *sublime*, the *ugly*, the *pretty* | The *evaluative* *core* |
| **Thick** | The *graceful*, the *dainty*, the *dumpy*, the *elegant*, the *sentimental*, the *vulgar* | The *characterizing* *richness* |
| **Non-aesthetic** | The *red*, the *square*, the *loud*, the *smooth* | The *base* |

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *properties* *supervene* on the *non*-*aesthetic* (the *base*; the *no* *free*-*floating* *beauty*).} \\
2. & \text{The *supervenience* does *not* *reduce* the *aesthetic* to the *non*-*aesthetic* (the *no* *definitive* *rules*; the *Sibley* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *gap* is *bridged* by *taste* (the *perception*, the *discernment*; the *non*-*inferential* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *aesthetic* *properties* are *real* (the *no* *projection*) and *emergent* (the *non*-*reducible*), and the *taste* is the *organon* (the *Sibley* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *phenomenology* (the *immediacy*, the *discernment*), and it *explains* the *disagreement* (the *taste* *differs*, the *base* *does* not), and it *grounds* the *criticism* (the *training* of *taste*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *distinctness* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *properties* *are* the *non*-*aesthetic* *properties* under a *description* (the *no* *autonomous* *class*), and the *realism* is the *redundant* (the *no* *new* *facts*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *aesthetic* *properties* *supervene* *without* *being* *identical* (the *multiple* *realizability*; the *graceful* is *not* the *curved*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *supervenience* (the *no* *identity*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *perception* *objection***. The *taste* is *not* a *perception* (the *no* *organ*, the *no* *representation*), and the *perception* *model* *mystifies* the *judgment*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *perceptual* *model*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *taste* is *perceptual* in the *broad* *sense* (the *non*-*inferential* *discrimination*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *taste* in the *discrimination*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 1.3 Aesthetic experience

**The *thesis*** (Beardsley, *Aesthetics*, 1958, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; Carroll, “Aesthetic Experience Revisited,” 2001, *BJA* 41). The *aesthetic* *experience* is a *distinctive* *kind* of *experience* (the *focused* *attention*, the *integration*, the *felt* *value*), and the *experience* *grounds* the *judgment* (the *Beardsley* *insight*), and the *experience* is the *object* of the *criticism*.

**The *criteria*** (the *Beardsley* *marks*).

| The *mark* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *attention*** | The *focused* *directedness* on the *object* | The *perceptual* *insight* |
| **The *coherence*** | The *integration* of the *parts* | The *formal* *insight* |
| **The *completeness*** | The *felt* *completeness* (the *no* *yearning*) | The *value* *insight* |
| **The *value*** | The *felt* *and* *attributed* *goodness* | The *normative* *insight* |

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *experience* has the *distinctive* *phenomenology* (the *attention*, the *coherence*, the *completeness*).} \\
2. & \text{The *phenomenology* is *not* the *ordinary* *experience* (the *no* *practical* *engagement*; the *no* *mere* *sensuous* *agreeableness*).} \\
3. & \text{The *experience* *grounds* the *judgment* (the *felt* *value*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *aesthetic* *experience* is *distinct* (the *Beardsley* *insight*), and it *anchors* the *aesthetic* *domain* (§1.1).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *first*-*person* *authority* (the *felt* *value*), and it *grounds* the *criticism* (the *training* of *attention*), and it *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *phenomenology* (Ch. 30) and to the *well*-*being* (the *flow*, the *absorption*; Ch. 48, §5.3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *distinct* *experience* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Dickie, “The Myth of the Aesthetic Attitude,” 1964, *APQ* 1; the *deflationary* *insight*). The *aesthetic* *experience* is the *ordinary* *perception* under the *attention* (the *no* *special* *state*), and the *distinctness* is the *myth* (the *no* *attitude*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Carroll 2001, §2). The *distinctness* is *phenomenological* (the *focused*, the *integrated*), and the *no* *myth* *is* the *no* *separate* *faculty* (the *no* *special* *organ*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *mode* of *attention* (the *no* *mystery*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *value* *neutrality* *objection***. The *Beardsley* *criteria* *presuppose* the *harmonious* *art* (the *classical*), and the *tragic*, the *sublime*, the *difficult* *art* *violates* the *criteria* (the *no* *completeness*, the *no* *coherence*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *criteria* are *schematic* (the *coherence* is *the* *kind* *appropriate* to the *work*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *criteria* in the *genres*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *neutral* *account*).

### 1.4 Aesthetic judgment

**The *thesis*** (Kant, *Critique of Judgement*, 1790, §1–§22; the *contemporary* *literature*; Zangwill, *The Metaphysics of Beauty*, 2001, Ch. 1). The *aesthetic* *judgment* is *disinterested* (the *no* *desire* for the *existence* of the *object*), *subjective* (the *ground* is the *feeling* of *pleasure*), *universal* (the *claim* to the *assent* of *everyone*), *purposive* *without* *purpose* (the *form* as *if* *designed*), and *necessary* (the *claim* of *exemplary* *necessity*).

**The *four* *moments*** (Kant, *CJ*, §1–§22).

| The *moment* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality** | The *disinterested* *satisfaction* | The *no* *desire* |
| **Quantity** | The *universal* *without* *concepts* | The *no* *rule* |
| **Relation** | The *purposiveness* *without* *purpose* | The *no* *end* |
| **Modality** | The *exemplary* *necessity* | The *no* *proof* |

**The *antinomy*** (Kant, *CJ*, §56).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Thesis:} & \text{The *judgment* of *taste* is *not* *grounded* on the *concepts*} \ (\text{the *no* *rule*}). \\
\text{Antithesis:} & \text{The *judgment* of *taste* *claims* the *assent* of *everyone*} \ (\text{the *universality*}). \\
\hline
\text{Resolution:} & \text{The *ground* is the *feeling* of the *free* *play* (the *no* *concept*), and the *claim* is the *universal* *voice*} \\
& \text{(the *sensus communis*; the *no* *private* *feeling*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *double* *aspect* (the *subjectivity* *and* the *universality*), and it *grounds* the *criticism* (the *exemplary* *necessity*, the *no* *proof*), and it *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *moral* (the *beauty* as the *symbol* of the *morality*; the *no* *reduction*) and to the *judgment* (the *reflective* *judgment*; the *no* *determinant*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *universality* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *disagreement* is *pervasive* (the *no* *convergence*), and the *universality* *claim* is the *illusion* (the *projection* of the *taste*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *Kantian* *insight*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *disagreement* is *compatible* with the *normative* *claim* (the *dispute* *presupposes* the *shared* *standard*; the *no* *indifference*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *universality* in the *practice* of *dispute*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *normative* *insight*).

**(ii) The *no* *disinterestedness* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *pleasure* *is* *interested* (the *desire*, the *possession*, the *identity*; the *fandom*, the *collector*), and the *disinterestedness* is the *ideology* of the *museum* (the *class*, the *capital*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Bourdieu, *Distinction*, 1979, the *sociological* *challenge*). The *disinterestedness* is the *ideal* (the *no* *mere* *consumption*), and the *sociology* *explains* the *violation* (the *no* *refutation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *ideal* in the *practice* (the *no* *ideology*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *class* *insight*).

### 1.5 The aesthetic and the everyday

**The *thesis*** (Saito, *Everyday Aesthetics*, 2007; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* is *not* *confined* to the *art* (the *no* *museum*), and the *everyday* *aesthetics* (the *domestic*, the *natural*, the *urban*, the *bodily*) *extends* the *domain*, and the *extension* *challenges* the *art*-*centered* *tradition*.

**The *significance***. The *extension* *grounds* the *environmental* *aesthetics* (Ch. 51, §6), and the *extension* *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *well*-*being* (the *everyday* *beauty*; Ch. 48, §1.4), and the *extension* *challenges* the *definition* of *art* (§3).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *response* *occurs* *everywhere* (the *garden*, the *kitchen*, the *street*, the *body*).} \\
2. & \text{The *art* *is* *not* the *necessary* *object* (the *no* *museum* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *response* *grounds* the *domain* (the *no* *art* *monopoly*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *aesthetic* is *not* *art*-*centered* (the *Saito* *insight*), and the *everyday* *aesthetics* *is* *part* of the *domain* (the *no* *elitism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *distinctness* *objection***. The *everyday* *response* *is* the *mere* *preference* (the *no* *normativity*), and the *aesthetic* *domain* *is* the *art* (the *no* *extension*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Saito 2007, Ch. 1). The *everyday* *response* *has* the *normative* *structure* (the *fitting*, the *unfitting*, the *criticism*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *domain* in the *normativity* (the *no* *mere* *preference*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

## 2. Beauty

### 2.1 The concept

**The *thesis*** (the *classical* *conception*; Plato, *Symposium*; Aristotle, *Poetics*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *beauty* is the *unity* in *variety* (the *harmony*, the *proportion*, the *integration*), and the *beauty* is the *perfection* of the *form* (the *no* *content* *requirement*), and the *experience* of the *beauty* is the *delight* in the *form* (the *no* *interest*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Beauty} \ = \ \text{the *unity* in *variety*} \ (\text{the *proportion*}, \text{the *integration*}, \text{the *fittingness*})
$$

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *content* | The *locus* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *beautiful*** | The *form*, the *harmony*, the *pleasure* | The *object* |
| **The *sublime*** | The *overwhelming*, the *awe*, the *no* *form* | The *nature*, the *art* |
| **The *pretty*** | The *charm*, the *delicacy*, the *no* *weight* | The *small*, the *domestic* |
| **The *ugly*** | The *dissonance*, the *revulsion*, the *no* *harmony* | The *modern* *art*, the *grotesque* |

**The *significance***. The *concept* *grounds* the *criticism* (the *evaluation*, the *comparison*), and the *concept* *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *mathematics* (the *proportion*, the *symmetry*; Ch. 43), and the *concept* *raises* the *metaphysical* *question* of the *reality* of the *beauty* (§2.5).

### 2.2 Subjectivism

**The *thesis*** (Hume, “Of the Standard of Taste,” 1757; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *objectivity*). The *beauty* *is* the *sentiment* (the *feeling* of the *spectator*), and the *no* *quality* *in* the *object* *corresponds* to the *beauty* (the *no* *real* *property*), and the *standard* of *taste* is the *agreement* of the *qualified* *judges* (the *no* *absolute* *standard*).

**The *Humean* *structure***.

$$
\text{Beauty} \ = \ \text{the *sentiment*} \ (\text{the *no* *object* *quality*}) \ \wedge \ \text{the *standard*} \ = \ \text{the *convergence* of the *true* *judges*}
$$

**The *argument*** (Hume 1757, ¶8).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *beauty* *is* *not* a *quality* of the *object* (the *no* *perception* of the *beauty* *by* the *senses*; the *no* *measurement*).} \\
2. & \text{The *beauty* *is* the *sentiment* of the *spectator* (the *gustatory* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *sentiment* *is* *grounded* in the *taste* (the *delicacy* of the *imagination*, the *practice*, the *comparison*, the *no* *prejudice*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *beauty* *is* *subjective* (the *sentimentalism*), and the *standard* is the *convergence* of the *true* *judges* (the *no* *absolute* *standard*; the *Humean* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *phenomenology* (the *immediacy* of the *feeling*), and it *explains* the *disagreement* (the *different* *tastes*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *aesthetic* *properties* (the *no* *queer* *entities*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *standard* *objection***. The *Humean* *standard* is *circular* (the *true* *judges* *are* the *ones* *who* *judge* *truly*), and the *convergence* *is* *not* the *norm* (the *disagreement* *persists*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Hume 1757, ¶10–15). The *true* *judge* is *specified* independently (the *delicacy*, the *practice*, the *no* *prejudice*, the *comparison*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *standard* in the *qualifications* (the *no* *circularity*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *ideal* *critic* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *robust* *convergence*).

**(ii) The *no* *normativity* *objection***. The *sentiment* *cannot* *ground* the *normative* *claim* (the *no* *ought* from the *feeling*), and the *criticism* *requires* the *standard* (the *no* *mere* *taste*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *ideal* *critic* *account*; the *no* *normative* *realism*). The *normative* *claim* is the *idealized* *response* (the *no* *queer* *fact*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *idealization* (the *no* *reduction*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *response*-*dependence* *insight*).

**(iii) The *no* *disinterestedness* *objection*** (the *sociological* *challenge*; Bourdieu 1979). The *taste* *is* the *social* *marker* (the *class*, the *distinction*), and the *subjectivism* *hides* the *power*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *sociology* *explains* the *transmission* (the *no* *refutation* of the *value*), and the *reply* *separates* the *genesis* from the *validity* (the *genetic* *fallacy*; Ch. 3, §5). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 2.3 Idealism and formalism

**The *thesis*** (the *Platonic* *tradition*; Plato, *Symposium* 210a–212a; the *idealist* *aesthetics*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *beauty* *is* the *Form* (the *ideal*, the *perfect*, the *no* *instance*), and the *beautiful* *things* *participate* in the *Form*, and the *experience* of the *beauty* is the *recollection* (the *no* *mere* *sensation*), and the *formalism* is the *secular* *descendant* (the *significant* *form*; the *no* *content*).

**The *formalism*** (Bell, *Art*, 1914, Ch. 1; Fry, *Vision and Design*, 1920).

| The *thesis* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *significant* *form*** | The *art* *is* the *significant* *form* (the *lines*, the *colors*, the *relations*) | The *no* *content* |
| **The *autonomy*** | The *aesthetic* *value* *is* *independent* of the *content* | The *no* *moral* *relevance* |
| **The *no* *representation*** | The *representation* *is* *irrelevant* to the *artistic* *value* | The *no* *literature* |

**The *argument*** (Bell 1914, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *emotion* *is* *triggered* by the *form* (the *lines*, the *colors*), and *not* by the *content* (the *no* *representation*).} \\
2. & \text{The *form* is the *significant* *form* (the *unity* in *variety*; the *no* *narrative*).} \\
3. & \text{The *form* *grounds* the *artistic* *value* (the *no* *content*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *formalism* *is* *true* (the *Bell* *insight*), and the *aesthetic* *value* *is* *autonomous* (the *no* *moral* *reduction*; §7).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *purity* of the *aesthetic* (the *no* *extra*-*aesthetic* *interest*), and it *grounds* the *autonomy* (the *no* *moralism*; §7), and it *explains* the *abstract* *art* (the *no* *representation*, the *no* *narrative*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *content* *objection***. The *literature*, the *film*, the *music* *with* *text* *depend* on the *content* (the *no* *mere* *form*), and the *formalism* *excludes* them (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *form* *includes* the *semantic* *form* (the *structure* of the *meaning*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *content* in the *form* (the *no* *exclusion*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *broad* *formalism*).

**(ii) The *no* *distinct* *emotion* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *emotion* *is* *not* *distinct* (the *no* *special* *feeling*), and the *formalism* *postulates* the *mystery*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* *emotion* *is* the *felt* *value* of the *experience* (the *no* *special* *faculty*), and the *reply* *grounds* it in the *phenomenology* (the *no* *mystery*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 2.4 The return of aesthetic realism

**The *thesis*** (Zangwill, *The Metaphysics of Beauty*, 2001, Ch. 1–2; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *robust* *realism*). The *beauty* is the *real* *property* of the *object* (the *no* *projection*, the *no* *mere* *sentiment*), and the *judgment* *tracks* the *property* (the *no* *mere* *response*), and the *disagreement* is the *error* (the *no* *relativism*), and the *beauty* *supervenes* on the *non*-*aesthetic* *base* (the *no* *free*-*floating* *form*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *judgment* *purports* to *track* the *value* (the *normative* *claim*; the *no* *mere* *liking*).} \\
2. & \text{The *purport* *is* *not* *systematically* *illusory* (the *no* *error* *theory*; the *charity*).} \\
3. & \text{The *value* *requires* the *property* (the *no* *projection*; the *no* *response* *only*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *beauty* is *real* (the *no* *subjectivism*), and the *judgment* *tracks* it (the *no* *mere* *feeling*), and the *disagreement* is the *error* (the *no* *relativism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *normativity* (the *no* *mere* *preference*), and it *explains* the *disagreement* (the *error*, the *no* *relativism*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *criticism* as the *correction*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *queerness* *objection*** (Mackie, *Ethics*, 1977, Ch. 1, *transposed*). The *aesthetic* *properties* are *queer* (the *no* *natural* *property*, the *no* *empirical* *access*), and the *realism* *postulates* the *occult* (the *no* *explanation*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *naturalistic* *realism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* *properties* *supervene* on the *natural* (the *no* *occult*), and the *access* is *perceptual* (the *taste*; the *no* *queer* *faculty*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *realism* in the *natural*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *supervenience* *insight*).

**(ii) The *no* *disagreement* *objection***. The *disagreement* is *persistent* and *unresolvable* (the *no* *convergence*), and the *realism* *predicts* the *convergence* (the *no* *fact*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *disagreement* is *explained* by the *error* (the *taste*, the *training*, the *bias*), and the *no* *convergence* *is* *not* *decisive* (the *ethics* *disagrees* *too*; Ch. 44, §5), and the *reply* *grounds* the *realism* in the *best* *explanation*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *robust* *convergence*).

**(iii) The *no* *verification* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *fact* *is* *not* *verifiable* (the *no* *test*), and the *realism* *is* *idle* (the *no* *difference*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *response*-*dependence* *insight*). The *verification* is the *idealized* *response* (the *no* *mere* *actual* *response*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *fact* in the *idealization* (the *no* *idle*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *qualified* *realism*).

### 2.5 The state of the debate about beauty

**The *assessment***. The *subjectivism* *captures* the *phenomenology* (the *feeling*), the *realism* *captures* the *normativity* (the *claim*), the *formalism* *captures* the *autonomy* (the *no* *content*), and the *response*-*dependence* *integrates* the *two*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Subjectivism** | The *phenomenology*; the *no* *queerness* | The *no* *normativity* |
| **Idealism** | The *perfection* | The *no* *instance* |
| **Formalism** | The *autonomy*; the *no* *content* | The *no* *representation* |
| **Realism** | The *normativity* | The *queerness*; the *disagreement* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *aesthetic* *realism* *with* *response*-*dependence*: the *beauty* is the *property* *that* *merits* the *pleasure* (the *merited* *response* *insight*; the *no* *mere* *projection*), the *property* *supervenes* on the *non*-*aesthetic* *base* (the *no* *occult*), the *realism* is *naturalistic* (the *no* *queer* *faculty*), the *disagreement* is *explained* by the *error* (the *no* *relativism*), and the *ideal* *critic* *anchors* the *response* (the *Humean* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *realism* and the *response*-*dependence* *remain* *contested*.

## 3. Theories of Art

### 3.1 The question

**The *question***. The *definitional* *question* (*what is art?*) is *distinct* from the *evaluative* (*what is good art?*) and the *classificatory* (*is this art?*; §3.8), and the *question* is *contested* at its *methodological* *level* (the *definition* *vs.* the *no* *definition*; §3.2).

**The *distinctions***.

| The *question* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **Definitional** | The *necessary* and *sufficient* *conditions* of the *art* | The *essentialism* |
| **Classificatory** | The *application* of the *concept* to the *case* | The *practice* |
| **Evaluative** | The *goodness* of the *work* (as *art*) | The *criticism* |
| **Ontological** | The *mode* of *existence* of the *work* (the *type*, the *token*) | The *metaphysics* |

**The *skeptical* *challenge*** (Weitz, “The Role of Theory in Aesthetics,” 1956, *JAAC* 15; the *anti*-*essentialism*). The *art* is the *open* *concept* (the *no* *closed* *set* of *conditions*), and the *definition* *always* *fails* (the *new* *art* *escapes* the *old* *definition*), and the *concept* is *held* *together* by the *family* *resemblance* (the *no* *essence*), and the *definitional* *project* *is* the *mistake*.

**The *argument*** (Weitz 1956, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *art* *is* *creative* and *expansive* (the *new* *forms* *continually* *appear*).} \\
2. & \text{The *definition* *would* *close* the *concept* (the *no* *new* *forms*).} \\
3. & \text{The *closure* *is* *false* to the *practice* (the *no* *adequacy*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* *cannot* be *defined* (the *no* *essence*), and the *concept* is *open* (the *Weitz* *insight*), and the *family* *resemblance* *suffices* (the *no* *definition*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *openness* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Mandelbaum, “Family Resemblances and Generalization concerning the Arts,” 1965, *APQ* 2). The *openness* *is* the *relation* *between* the *concept* and the *conditions* (the *no* *closed* *list*), and the *definition* *can* be *open* (the *relational* *definition*; the *historical*; §3.6), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *relational* *definitions* *reintroduce* the *closed* *concept* (the *no* *creativity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *openness* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *closure*).

**(ii) The *no* *family* *resemblance* *objection***. The *family* *resemblance* *suffices* for the *ordinary* *concepts* (the *no* *definition* *needed*), and the *art* *requires* the *normative* *structure* (the *no* *mere* *resemblance*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *cluster* *account*; §3.7). The *cluster* *captures* the *normative* *structure* (the *criteria*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *concept* in the *cluster*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.2 The imitation theory

**The *thesis*** (Plato, *Republic* X, 595a–608b; Aristotle, *Poetics*, 1447a–1448b; the *mimetic* *tradition*). The *art* is the *imitation* (the *mimesis*) of the *reality* (the *nature*, the *action*, the *emotion*), and the *value* of the *art* is the *fidelity* of the *imitation* (the *no* *originality* *requirement*), and the *pleasure* of the *art* is the *recognition* (the *no* *mere* *sensation*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Art} \ = \ \text{the *imitation*} \ (\text{the *representation* of the *reality*}) \quad (\text{the *mimetic* *insight*})
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *representational* *art* (the *painting*, the *drama*, the *novel*), and it *explains* the *pleasure* of the *recognition* (the *Aristotelian* *insight*), and it *grounds* the *criticism* (the *fidelity*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *imitation* *objection***. The *abstract* *art*, the *music*, the *architecture* *do* not *imitate* (the *no* *representation*), and the *theory* *excludes* them (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *broad* *mimesis*). The *imitation* *includes* the *expression* of the *emotion* (the *no* *mere* *copy*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *expression* (the *no* *exclusion*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *representation*).

**(ii) The *no* *value* *objection*** (Plato, *Republic* X). The *imitation* *is* the *copy* of the *copy* (the *no* *truth*), and the *art* *is* the *illusion* (the *no* *knowledge*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *Aristotelian* *insight*). The *imitation* *is* the *universal* (the *poetry* *is* *more* *philosophical* *than* the *history*; *Poetics* 1451b), and the *reply* *grounds* the *value* in the *universality* (the *no* *mere* *copy*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.3 The expression theory

**The *thesis*** (Tolstoy, *What Is Art?*, 1897, Ch. 5; Collingwood, *The Principles of Art*, 1938, Ch. 6–7; Croce, *Aesthetic*, 1902; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* is the *expression* of the *emotion* (the *no* *imitation*), and the *expression* *communicates* the *feeling* (the *no* *mere* *discharge*), and the *value* of the *art* is the *sincerity* and the *clarity* of the *expression* (the *no* *technical* *virtuosity*).

**The *structure*** (the *Tolstoyan* *form*).

$$
\text{Art} \ = \ \text{the *infection*} \ (\text{the *communication* of the *emotion*} \ \text{from the *artist* to the *audience*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Tolstoy 1897, Ch. 5).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *art* *arises* from the *emotion* of the *artist* (the *no* *mere* *craft*).} \\
2. & \text{The *emotion* is *communicated* to the *audience* (the *infection*).} \\
3. & \text{The *communication* *grounds* the *artistic* *value* (the *no* *mere* *form*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* *is* the *expression* (the *Tolstoyan* *insight*), and the *value* is the *sincerity* and the *clarity* (the *no* *virtuosity*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *emotional* *power* of the *art* (the *no* *mere* *formalism*), and it *grounds* the *art* in the *human* *connection* (the *no* *elitism*), and it *explains* the *value* of the *sincere* *art*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *expressive* *fallacy*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Hospers, “The Concept of Artistic Expression,” 1955, *PAS* 55). The *expression* *is* *not* the *communication* (the *no* *audience* *required*; the *private* *art*), and the *expression* *is* *not* the *art* (the *no* *mere* *feeling*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Collingwood 1938, Ch. 6). The *expression* *is* the *articulation* (the *no* *mere* *discharge*; the *clarity* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *articulation* (the *no* *mere* *emotion*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Collingwood* *insight*).

**(ii) The *no* *emotion* *objection***. The *abstract* *art*, the *architecture*, the *music* *do* not *express* the *emotion* (the *no* *content*), and the *theory* *excludes* them (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *expression* *includes* the *expressive* *properties* (the *sadness*, the *energy*; the *no* *actual* *emotion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *expression* in the *properties* (the *no* *exclusion*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *no* *value* *objection*** (the *Tolstoyan* *moralism*). The *infection* *validates* the *bad* *emotion* (the *no* *moral* *condition*), and the *theory* *grounds* the *art* in the *infection* (the *no* *value*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *value* *requires* the *moral* *condition* (the *Tolstoyan* *insight*; the *no* *mere* *infection*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *value* in the *moral* (the *no* *autonomism*; §7). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.4 The institutional theory

**The *thesis*** (Danto, “The Artworld,” 1964, *Journal of Philosophy* 61; Dickie, *Art and the Aesthetic*, 1974, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* is the *artifact* *conferred* the *status* of *art* by the *artworld* (the *artists*, the *critics*, the *curators*, the *institutions*), and the *status* *is* the *social* *conferral* (the *no* *aesthetic* *property*), and the *theory* *explains* the *readymade* (the *Duchamp* *insight*) and the *conceptual* *art*.

**The *structure*** (the *Dickie* *form*).

$$
\text{Art} \ = \ \text{the *artifact*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *status* *conferred* by the *artworld*} \ (\text{the *no* *aesthetic* *requirement*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Dickie 1974, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *readymade* *is* *art* (the *Duchamp* *insight*; the *Fountain*, 1917).} \\
2. & \text{The *readymade* *has* *no* *aesthetic* *property* *distinctive* of the *art* (the *no* *significant* *form*).} \\
3. & \text{The *art* *status* *requires* the *conferral* (the *no* *intrinsic* *property*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* *is* the *institutional* *status* (the *Dickie* *insight*), and the *artworld* *grounds* the *status* (the *no* *aesthetic* *essentialism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *explains* the *readymade*, the *conceptual* *art*, the *boundary* *cases* (the *no* *aesthetic* *essentialism*), and it *grounds* the *art* in the *practice* (the *no* *mystery*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *circularity* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *artworld* *is* *defined* by the *art* (the *artists*, the *critics*), and the *definition* *is* *circular* (the *no* *explanatory* *power*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Dickie 1974, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *artworld* *is* the *social* *institution* (the *roles*, the *practices*), and the *institution* *is* *specifiable* *independently* (the *no* *circularity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *institution*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *sociological* *insight*).

**(ii) The *no* *value* *objection***. The *institutional* *status* *validates* the *bad* *art* (the *no* *value*), and the *theory* *cannot* *distinguish* the *art* from the *non*-*art* (the *no* *criteria*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *institution* *has* the *standards* (the *criticism*, the *evaluation*), and the *status* *is* *not* the *value* (the *no* *conflation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *value* in the *criticism*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *no* *first* *art* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Levinson, “Defining Art Historically,” 1979, *BJA* 19). The *institution* *presupposes* the *prior* *art* (the *no* *first* *art*), and the *regress* *is* *vicious* (the *no* *origin*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *historical* *definition*; §3.5). The *first* *art* *is* the *ur*-*art* (the *no* *institutional* *status*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *history* (the *no* *regress*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.5 The historical definition

**The *thesis*** (Levinson, “Defining Art Historically,” 1979, *BJA* 19; “Refining Art Historically,” 1989, *JAAC* 47; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* is the *artifact* *intended* to be *regarded* as the *predecessors* were *regarded* (the *no* *mere* *institution*), and the *definition* is the *historical* *relation* (the *no* *closed* *list*), and the *theory* *explains* the *first* *art* (the *ur*-*art*) and the *continuity* of the *practice*.

**The *structure*** (the *Levinson* *form*).

$$
\text{Art} \ = \ \text{the *artifact*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *intention* to be *regarded* *as* the *predecessors* \ were *regarded*}
$$

**The *argument*** (Levinson 1979, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *art* *is* the *practice* *extended* *through* *time* (the *no* *atemporal* *essence*).} \\
2. & \text{The *extension* *requires* the *relation* to the *predecessors* (the *no* *first* *art* *problem*).} \\
3. & \text{The *relation* *grounds* the *status* (the *no* *mere* *institution*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* is the *historical* *intention* (the *Levinson* *insight*), and the *definition* is *open* (the *no* *closed* *list*), and the *first* *art* is the *ur*-*art* (the *no* *regress*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *explains* the *first* *art* (the *no* *institutional* *regress*), and it *captures* the *continuity* of the *practice* (the *no* *arbitrary* *boundary*), and it *fits* the *intentional* *structure* (the *no* *mere* *conferral*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *intention* *objection***. The *art* *is* *created* *without* the *intention* (the *outsider* *art*, the *child* *art*, the *animal* *art*), and the *theory* *excludes* them (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intention* *is* the *retrospective* *interpretation* (the *no* *actual* *mental* *state*; the *no* *exclusion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *interpretation*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *success* *objection***. The *intention* *is* *not* the *success* (the *failed* *art* *is* *still* *art*), and the *theory* *conflates* the *attempt* with the *achievement* (the *no* *value*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *classificatory*/*evaluative* *distinction*). The *definition* is *classificatory* (the *attempt*), and the *value* is *evaluative* (the *success*), and the *reply* *separates* the *two* (the *no* *conflation*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.6 The cluster account

**The *thesis*** (Gaut, “‘Art’ as a Cluster Concept,” 2000, in *Theories of Art Today*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* is a *cluster* *concept* (the *criteria* are *disjunctively* *necessary* and *jointly* *sufficient*), and the *criteria* (the *aesthetic* *property*, the *expression*, the *representation*, the *originality*, the *skill*, the *institution*, the *history*, the *genre*) *hang* *together* (the *cluster*), and the *concept* *admits* of the *borderline* *cases* (the *no* *sharp* *boundary*).

**The *structure*** (the *Gaut* *form*).

$$
\text{Art}(x) \iff x \ \text{satisfies a *weighted* *subset* of the *criteria*} \ C_1, \dots, C_n \ (\text{the *no* *single* *necessary* *condition*})
$$

**The *criteria*** (Gaut 2000, §2).

| The *criterion* | The *content* | The *weight* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *aesthetic* *property*** | The *beauty*, the *grace*, the *fittingness* | High |
| **The *expression*** | The *emotion*, the *attitude*, the *vision* | High |
| **The *representation*** | The *mimesis*, the *aboutness* | Medium |
| **The *originality*** | The *novelty*, the *creativity* | High |
| **The *skill*** | The *virtuosity*, the *craft* | Medium |
| **The *institution*** | The *artworld* *status* | Medium |
| **The *history*** | The *relation* to the *predecessors* | Medium |
| **The *genre*** | The *kind*, the *tradition* | Low |

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *definition* *fails* (the *no* *single* *necessary* *condition*; the *Weitz* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *concept* *is* *not* *empty* (the *no* *mere* *resemblance*; the *criteria* *matter*).} \\
3. & \text{The *criteria* *cluster* (the *no* *single* *essence*; the *weighted* *subset*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* is a *cluster* *concept* (the *Gaut* *insight*), and the *definition* *is* *pluralistic* (the *no* *monism*), and the *borderline* *cases* are *real* (the *no* *sharp* *boundary*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *pluralism* (the *many* *criteria*), and it *avoids* the *no* *essence* (the *no* *empty* *concept*), and it *explains* the *borderline* *cases* (the *no* *sharp* *boundary*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *criticism* as the *weighing*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *sufficiency* *objection***. The *cluster* *admits* the *non*-*art* (the *sunset*, the *child*’s *drawing*, the *propaganda*), and the *no* *single* *condition* *allows* the *false* *positives*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *weighting*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *weights* *exclude* the *false* *positives* (the *sunset* *lacks* the *intention*, the *propaganda* *lacks* the *originality*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *sufficiency* in the *weights*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *criteria* *objection***. The *criteria* *are* *arbitrary* (the *why* *these*?), and the *cluster* *is* the *descriptive* *sociology* (the *no* *normative* *structure*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *criteria* *are* *grounded* in the *practice* (the *no* *arbitrary*; the *no* *mere* *sociology*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *criteria* in the *history* of the *art*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.7 The aesthetic definition and the state of the debate

**The *thesis*** (Zangwill, “The Creative Theory of Art,” 2007, *BJA* 47; Iseminger, *The Aesthetic Function of Art*, 2004; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* is the *object* *of* the *aesthetic* *interest* (the *no* *mere* *institution*), and the *aesthetic* *interest* is the *property* *of* the *work* (the *no* *mere* *response*), and the *theory* *grounds* the *value* in the *aesthetic* (the *no* *autonomism*; §7).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Art} \ = \ \text{the *artifact* *whose* *function* is the *aesthetic* *experience*} \ (\text{the *aesthetic* *definition*})
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *function* *objection***. The *conceptual* *art* (the *no* *aesthetic* *experience*; the *Duchamp* *insight*), and the *theory* *excludes* it (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *broad* *aesthetic* *interest*). The *conceptual* *art* *engages* the *aesthetic* *interest* (the *ideas*, the *form* of the *thought*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *art* in the *broad* *interest*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**The *state* of the *debate***.

| The *theory* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Imitation** | The *representation* | The *no* *abstract* *art* |
| **Expression** | The *emotion* | The *expressive* *fallacy* |
| **Formalism** | The *autonomy* | The *no* *content* |
| **Institutional** | The *readymade* | The *circularity* |
| **Historical** | The *continuity* | The *no* *intention* |
| **Cluster** | The *pluralism* | The *no* *sufficiency* |
| **Aesthetic** | The *value* | The *no* *function* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *hybrid*: the *art* is the *cluster* (the *Gaut* *insight*), *anchored* in the *historical* *practice* (the *Levinson* *insight*), *oriented* to the *aesthetic* *interest* (the *Iseminger* *insight*), *open* to the *institutional* *conferral* (the *Dickie* *insight*), and *resistant* to the *single* *essence* (the *Weitz* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *criteria* and the *borderline* *cases* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Fiction and the Paradox of Emotional Response

### 4.1 The paradox

**The *paradox*** (Radford, “How Can We Be Moved by the Fate of Anna Karenina?,” 1975, *PASS* 49; the *contemporary* *literature*). We *feel* *emotions* for the *fictional* *characters* (we *pity* Anna, we *fear* for the *hero*), and we *believe* that the *characters* do *not* *exist* (the *no* *actual* *object*), and we *do* not *feel* *emotions* for what we *believe* does *not* *exist* (the *no* *actual* *object*). The *three* *propositions* *cannot* *all* be *true* (the *no* *consistency*), and the *denial* of *any* of them *is* *counter*-*intuitive*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(1) & \text{We *feel* *emotions* for the *fictions*} \ (\text{the *datum*}). \\
(2) & \text{We *believe* the *fictions* do *not* *exist*} \ (\text{the *datum*}). \\
(3) & \text{The *emotion* *requires* the *belief* in the *existence*} \ (\text{the *datum*}). \\
\hline
& \therefore \ (1), (2), (3) \ \text{cannot *all* be *true*} \ (\text{the *Radford* *paradox*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *solutions***.

| The *solution* | The *content* | The *cost* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *illusion*** (Radford) | The *emotion* is *inconsistent* (the *no* *rationality*) | The *irrationality* |
| **The *pretense*** (Walton) | The *emotion* is *quasi* (the *make*-*believe*) | The *no* *genuine* *emotion* |
| **The *thought*** (Lamarque) | The *thought* *suffices* (the *no* *belief*) | The *no* *connection* to the *reality* |
| **The *simulation*** (Currie) | The *simulation* *generates* the *emotion* | The *no* *actual* *emotion* |

### 4.2 The pretense theory

**The *thesis*** (Walton, “Fearing Fictions,” 1978, *Journal of Philosophy* 75; *Mimesis as Make*-*Believe*, 1990, Ch. 1). The *fictional* *emotion* is the *quasi*-*emotion* (the *make*-*believe* *fear*, the *pretend* *pity*), and the *quasi*-*emotion* is *generated* by the *game* of *make*-*believe* (the *props*, the *rules*, the *fictional* *truths*), and the *quasi*-*emotion* is *not* the *genuine* *emotion* (the *no* *action* *tendency*, the *no* *belief*), and the *paradox* *dissolves* (the *no* *inconsistency*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Quasi-emotion} \ = \ \text{the *make*-*believe* *emotion*} \ (\text{the *fictional* *truth*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *no* *belief*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Walton 1978, §2–3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *fiction* *is* the *prop* in the *game* of *make*-*believe* (the *no* *belief* *required*).} \\
2. & \text{The *game* *generates* the *quasi*-*emotion* (the *no* *genuine* *emotion*).} \\
3. & \text{The *quasi*-*emotion* *explains* the *phenomenology* (the *felt* *pity*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *paradox* *dissolves* (the *no* *inconsistency*), and the *fiction* *engages* the *emotion* (the *quasi*-*insight*), and the *genuine* *emotion* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *belief*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *dissolves* the *paradox* (the *no* *inconsistency*), and it *captures* the *phenomenology* (the *felt* *emotion*), and it *explains* the *fiction* *generally* (the *make*-*believe*; the *no* *mere* *representation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *genuine* *emotion* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Carroll, “The Paradox of Suspense,” 2001; the *no* *quasi*). The *emotion* *feels* *genuine* (the *tears*, the *heart* *rate*, the *no* *pretense*), and the *quasi*-*emotion* *is* the *diminution* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Walton 1990, Ch. 7; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *genuine* *emotion* *is* *not* *distinct* from the *quasi* (the *no* *separate* *state*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *emotion* in the *game* (the *no* *diminution*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *genuine* *emotion*).

**(ii) The *no* *action* *objection***. The *emotion* *has* the *action* *tendency* (the *no* *mere* *feeling*), and the *fiction* *does* not (the *no* *action*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *action* *tendency* *is* *defeated* by the *belief* (the *no* *possibility*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *emotion* in the *belief* (the *no* *action* *required*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 4.3 The thought theory

**The *thesis*** (Lamarque, “How Can We Fear and Pity Fictions?,” 1981, *BJA* 21; Carroll, *The Philosophy of Horror*, 1990, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *emotion* *requires* the *thought* (the *no* *belief*), and the *thought* of the *character* (the *propositional* *attitude*, the *imagination*) *suffices* for the *emotion*, and the *fiction* *generates* the *thought* (the *no* *pretense* *required*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Emotion} \ = \ \text{the *thought*} \ (\text{the *propositional* *content*}) \ \wedge \ \text{the *evaluation*} \ (\text{the *no* *belief* *required*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Lamarque 1981, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *emotion* *requires* the *thought* of the *object* (the *no* *merely* *perceptual* *givenness*).} \\
2. & \text{The *thought* *does* not *require* the *belief* in the *existence* (the *no* *existence* *condition*).} \\
3. & \text{The *fiction* *generates* the *thought* (the *no* *pretense* *required*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *fiction* *generates* the *genuine* *emotion* (the *Lamarque* *insight*), and the *paradox* *dissolves* (the *no* *belief*), and the *emotion* *is* *rational* (the *thought* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *genuine* *emotion* (the *no* *quasi*), and it *avoids* the *belief* (the *no* *inconsistency*), and it *explains* the *rationality* of the *response* (the *thought* *is* the *reason*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *specificity* *objection***. The *thought* *is* *too* *thin* (the *no* *specificity* of the *emotion*), and the *emotion* *requires* the *particular* *object* (the *no* *mere* *proposition*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *thought* *can* be *specific* (the *mental* *image*, the *imagination*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *specificity* in the *imagery*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *motivation* *objection***. The *thought* *does* not *motivate* (the *no* *action*), and the *emotion* *requires* the *motivation* (the *no* *mere* *cognition*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *fiction* *does* not *require* the *motivation* (the *no* *action* *possible*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *emotion* in the *contemplation* (the *no* *action*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 4.4 The paradox of tragedy

**The *thesis*** (Hume, “Of Tragedy,” 1757; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *paradox* of the *negative* *emotion*). The *tragedy* *produces* the *pleasure* *from* the *pain* (the *no* *mere* *masochism*), and the *pleasure* *is* *grounded* in the *art* (the *form*, the *eloquence*, the *insight*), and the *conversion* *explains* the *paradox* (the *no* *inconsistency*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *pleasure*} \ = \ \text{the *conversion* of the *pain*} \ (\text{the *form*}, \text{the *insight*}, \text{the *no* *mere* *pain*})
$$

**The *accounts***.

| The *account* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *conversion*** (Hume) | The *eloquence* *converts* the *pain* | The *no* *pure* *form* |
| **The *cognitive*** | The *insight* *into* the *human* *condition* | The *no* *mere* *pleasure* |
| **The *meta*-*response*** | The *pleasure* *in* the *response* (the *no* *mere* *pain*) | The *no* *direct* *pleasure* |
| **The *control*** | The *safety* (the *no* *real* *threat*) | The *no* *tragedy* |

**The *argument*** (Hume 1757, ¶4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *tragedy* *produces* the *pain* (the *sadness*, the *horror*).} \\
2. & \text{The *tragedy* *produces* the *pleasure* (the *delight*, the *engagement*).} \\
3. & \text{The *pleasure* *of* the *art* *converts* the *pain* (the *no* *inconsistency*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *paradox* *dissolves* (the *conversion* *insight*), and the *negative* *emotion* *grounds* the *aesthetic* *value* (the *no* *mere* *positive* *emotion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *tragic* *pleasure* (the *no* *mere* *pain*), and it *grounds* the *value* in the *art* (the *conversion*), and it *explains* the *sublime* (§2.1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *conversion* *objection***. The *pain* *is* *not* *converted* (the *tears* *are* *real*), and the *pleasure* *is* *separate* (the *no* *conversion*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *pleasure* *is* the *complex* (the *mixed* *emotion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *pleasure* in the *complex* (the *no* *conversion*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *value* *objection***. The *pleasure* *in* the *pain* is *perverse* (the *no* *value*), and the *theory* *validates* the *sadism* (the *no* *morality*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *pleasure* *is* the *aesthetic* (the *no* *sadism*), and the *reply* *separates* the *aesthetic* *pleasure* from the *moral* *sadism* (the *no* *validation*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 4.5 The state of the debate about fiction

**The *assessment***. The *pretense* *theory* is the *dominant* (the *Walton* *insight*), and the *thought* *theory* *captures* the *genuine* *emotion* (the *Lamarque* *insight*), and the *paradox* of *tragedy* *is* *explicable* (the *conversion* *insight*), and the *ontology* of the *fictional* *characters* *remains* *open* (the *no* *mere* *representation*; Ch. 14, §5).

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Illusion** | The *inconsistency* | The *irrationality* |
| **Pretense** | The *dissolution* | The *no* *genuine* *emotion* |
| **Thought** | The *genuine* *emotion* | The *no* *motivation* |
| **Simulation** | The *no* *belief* | The *no* *actual* *emotion* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *thought* *theory* *combined* with the *pretense* *insight*: the *emotion* *is* *generated* by the *thought* (the *Lamarque* *insight*), *framed* by the *make*-*believe* (the *Walton* *insight*), *specific* in its *object* (the *imagery*), *rational* in its *ground* (the *thought*), and *genuine* in its *phenomenology* (the *no* *quasi*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *fictional* *emotion* *remains* *contested*.

## 5. Interpretation and Intention

### 5.1 The question

**The *question***. The *interpretive* *question* (*what does the work mean?*) is *distinct* from the *evaluative* (*is it good?*) and the *descriptive* (*what is it?*), and the *question* is *contested* at the *methodological* *level* (the *pluralism* of *interpretations*, the *no* *single* *meaning*; §5.6).

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *content* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual intentionalism** | The *meaning* is the *author*’s *intention* | Hirsch 1967; Carroll 1992 |
| **Hypothetical intentionalism** | The *meaning* is the *best* *hypothesis* of the *intention* | Levinson 1992 |
| **Anti-intentionalism** | The *meaning* is the *text* (the *no* *author*) | Wimsatt & Beardsley 1946 |
| **Value-maximizing** | The *meaning* is the *most* *valuable* *reading* | Davies 1982 |

### 5.2 Intentionalism

**The *thesis*** (Hirsch, *Validity in Interpretation*, 1967, Ch. 1; Carroll, “Art, Intention, and Conversation,” 1992; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* of the *work* is the *author*’s *intention* (the *no* *mere* *text*), and the *interpretation* *aims* at the *intention* (the *no* *mere* *private* *association*), and the *intention* *secures* the *determinacy* (the *no* *infinite* *readings*), and the *text* is the *evidence* of the *intention* (the *no* *infallible* *access*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *meaning* of the *work*} \ = \ \text{the *author*’s *intention*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *text*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Hirsch 1967, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *text* *underdetermines* the *meaning* (the *no* *unique* *reading*).} \\
2. & \text{The *determinacy* *requires* the *intention* (the *no* *arbitrary* *meaning*).} \\
3. & \text{The *intention* is *recoverable* from the *text* (the *no* *infallible* *access*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *author*’s *intention* (the *Hirsch* *insight*), and the *interpretation* is *determinate* (the *no* *infinite* *readings*), and the *text* *evidences* the *intention* (the *no* *mystery*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *secures* the *determinacy* (the *no* *infinite* *readings*), and it *fits* the *conversational* *model* (the *no* *mere* *text*), and it *grounds* the *criticism* (the *author* *matters*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *intentional* *fallacy*** (Wimsatt & Beardsley, “The Intentional Fallacy,” 1946, *Sewanee Review* 54). The *intention* is *not* *available* (the *no* *private* *access*), and the *appeal* to the *intention* *confuses* the *work* with its *origin* (the *no* *genetic* *criticism*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intention* *is* *evidenced* by the *text* (the *no* *private* *access* *required*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *intention* in the *evidence* (the *no* *fallacy*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *actual* *intentionalism*), and *contested* (the *no* *determinacy*).

**(ii) The *no* *intention* *objection***. The *work* *escapes* the *intention* (the *unintended* *meaning*, the *slip*, the *genius*), and the *intentionalism* *narrows* the *meaning* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *modest* *intentionalism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* *includes* the *conventional* *meaning* (the *no* *mere* *private* *intention*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *convention* (the *no* *narrowing*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 5.3 Anti-intentionalism

**The *thesis*** (Wimsatt & Beardsley 1946; Barthes, “The Death of the Author,” 1967; Foucault, “What Is an Author?,” 1969; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* of the *work* is the *text* (the *no* *author*’s *intention*), and the *interpretation* *aims* at the *text* (the *no* *genetic* *criticism*), and the *author* is the *reader* (the *no* *privileged* *origin*), and the *work* is the *public* *object* (the *no* *private* *meaning*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *meaning* of the *work*} \ = \ \text{the *text*} \ (\text{the *no* *author*’s *intention*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Wimsatt & Beardsley 1946, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *intention* is *not* *available* (the *no* *private* *access*).} \\
2. & \text{The *text* is *public* (the *no* *author*’s *monopoly*).} \\
3. & \text{The *public* *object* *grounds* the *meaning* (the *no* *genetic* *criticism*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *text* (the *anti*-*intentionalism*), and the *author* *intention* *is* the *fallacy* (the *no* *origin* *authority*), and the *reader* *constitutes* the *meaning* (the *no* *privileged* *origin*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *secures* the *publicity* of the *meaning* (the *no* *private* *access*), and it *avoids* the *genetic* *fallacy* (the *no* *origin* *authority*), and it *grounds* the *interpretation* in the *text* (the *no* *mystery*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *determinacy* *objection*** (Hirsch 1967, Ch. 1). The *text* *underdetermines* the *meaning* (the *no* *unique* *reading*), and the *anti*-*intentionalism* *licenses* the *arbitrary* *readings* (the *no* *validity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *convention* *insight*). The *convention* *determines* the *meaning* (the *no* *arbitrary*; the *public* *norms*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *convention*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *no* *authorship* *required*).

**(ii) The *no* *author* *objection***. The *author* *is* *relevant* (the *irony*, the *parody*, the *allusion*), and the *anti*-*intentionalism* *ignores* the *evidence* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *hypothetical* *intentionalism*; §5.4). The *author* *is* *hypothesized* (the *best* *reading*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *author* in the *hypothesis*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 5.4 Hypothetical intentionalism

**The *thesis*** (Levinson, “Intention and Interpretation: A Last Look,” 1992; “Intention and Interpretation in Literature,” 1996; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* of the *work* is the *best* *hypothesis* of the *author*’s *intention* (the *hypothetical* *intention*), and the *hypothesis* is *constrained* by the *text* (the *no* *free* *invention*), and the *hypothesis* *captures* the *intention* *without* the *private* *access* (the *no* *genetic* *fallacy*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *meaning* of the *work*} \ = \ \text{the *best* *hypothesis* of the *author*’s intention} \ (\text{the *no* *private* *access*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Levinson 1992, §4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *intention* *matters* (the *no* *mere* *text*; the *no* *arbitrary* *reading*).} \\
2. & \text{The *actual* *intention* is *not* *available* (the *no* *private* *access*).} \\
3. & \text{The *hypothesis* *captures* the *intention* (the *no* *genetic* *fallacy*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *hypothetical* *intention* (the *Levinson* *insight*), and the *interpretation* *avoids* the *fallacy* (the *no* *private* *access*), and the *author* *matters* (the *no* *death*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *avoids* the *intentional* *fallacy* (the *no* *private* *access*), and it *preserves* the *author* (the *no* *death*), and it *captures* the *determinacy* (the *no* *arbitrary* *reading*), and it *fits* the *conversational* *model* (the *no* *mere* *text*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *difference* *objection***. The *hypothesis* *collapses* into the *actual* *intention* (the *no* *difference*), and the *theory* *is* *verbal* (the *no* *substance*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *hypothesis* *can* *diverge* from the *actual* *intention* (the *unintended* *meaning*, the *author*’s *error*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *difference* in the *case*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *choice* *objection***. The *best* *hypothesis* *requires* the *criterion* (the *no* *neutral* *standard*), and the *theory* *smuggles* the *value* (the *no* *descriptive* *meaning*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *value*-*maximizing*; §5.5). The *criterion* is the *value* (the *best* *reading*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *value*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *descriptive* *meaning*).

### 5.5 The value-maximizing account

**The *thesis*** (Davies, “The Aesthetic Relevance of Authors’ and Painters’ Intentions,” 1982, *JAAC* 41; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* of the *work* is the *interpretation* *that* *makes* the *work* *best* (the *value*-*maximizing* *insight*), and the *interpretation* is *constrained* by the *text* (the *no* *free* *improvement*), and the *value* *grounds* the *meaning* (the *no* *mere* *description*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *meaning* of the *work*} \ = \ \text{the *interpretation* *that* *maximizes* the *value*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *description*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Davies 1982, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *interpretation* *aims* at the *value* of the *work* (the *no* *mere* *description*).} \\
2. & \text{The *value* *selects* the *reading* (the *no* *arbitrary* *meaning*).} \\
3. & \text{The *reading* *is* *constrained* by the *text* (the *no* *free* *improvement*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *value*-*maximizing* *reading* (the *Davies* *insight*), and the *criticism* *grounds* the *meaning* (the *no* *mere* *description*), and the *text* *constrains* the *reading* (the *no* *arbitrary*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *critical* *practice* (the *no* *mere* *description*), and it *grounds* the *interpretation* in the *value* (the *no* *arbitrary*), and it *fits* the *text* (the *no* *free* *improvement*).

**The *objections***.

## 6. Aesthetic Testimony, Taste, and Expertise

### 6.1 The puzzle

**The *puzzle*** (Hopkins, “Beauty and Testimony,” 2000, in *Philosophy and the Arts*; “How to Be a Pessimist about Aesthetic Testimony,” 2011, *Journal of Philosophy* 108; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* *knowledge* *seems* to *require* the *first*-*hand* *experience* (the *no* *testimony*), and the *testimony* *seems* *incapable* of *transmitting* the *aesthetic* *knowledge* (the *no* *transfer*), and yet the *criticism* *relies* on the *testimony* (the *review*, the *guide*, the *teacher*), and the *three* *propositions* *cannot* *all* be *true* (the *no* *consistency*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(1) & \text{The *aesthetic* *knowledge* *requires* the *acquaintance*} \ (\text{the *first*-*hand* *datum*}). \\
(2) & \text{The *testimony* *is* *not* the *acquaintance*} \ (\text{the *second*-*hand* *datum*}). \\
(3) & \text{The *criticism* *transmits* the *aesthetic* *knowledge*} \ (\text{the *practice*}). \\
\hline
& \therefore \text{The *three* *cannot* *all* be *true*} \ (\text{the *Hopkins* *puzzle*).}
\end{array}
$$

### 6.2 The pessimism

**The *thesis*** (Kant, *CJ*, §32–§35; Hopkins 2011, §2; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *testimony*). The *aesthetic* *judgment* *rests* on the *first*-*hand* *experience* (the *no* *inference*), and the *testimony* *cannot* *transmit* the *experience* (the *no* *transfer*), and the *aesthetic* *knowledge* *is* *incommunicable* (the *no* *teaching*), and the *criticism* *is* the *prompt* to the *experience* (the *no* *knowledge*).

**The *argument*** (Hopkins 2011, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *judgment* *rests* on the *experience* (the *no* *inference* from the *testimony*).} \\
2. & \text{The *testimony* *does* not *provide* the *experience* (the *no* *transfer*).} \\
3. & \text{The *knowledge* *requires* the *warrant* (the *no* *mere* *belief*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *testimony* *cannot* *transmit* the *aesthetic* *knowledge* (the *pessimism*), and the *criticism* *is* the *prompt* (the *no* *knowledge*), and the *acquaintance* *is* *necessary* (the *no* *second*-*hand*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *first*-*person* *authority* (the *no* *deference*), and it *explains* the *no* *teaching* of the *taste* (the *Kantian* *insight*), and it *grounds* the *autonomy* of the *judgment* (the *no* *authority*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *practice* *objection***. The *practice* *does* *transmit* the *knowledge* (the *critic* *teaches*, the *teacher* *trains*), and the *pessimism* *denies* the *practice* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *optimism*; §6.3). The *practice* *transmits* the *knowledge* (the *no* *mere* *prompt*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *transmission* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *distinctness* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *knowledge* *is* *not* *distinct* from the *perceptual* *knowledge* (the *no* *special* *acquaintance*), and the *testimony* *transmits* the *perceptual* *knowledge* (the *no* *exception*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *aesthetic* *knowledge* *requires* the *taste* (the *no* *mere* *perception*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *taste*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The optimism

**The *thesis*** (Meskin, “Aesthetic Testimony: What Can We Learn from Others about Beauty and Art?,” 2004, *PPR* 69; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *testimony* *insight*). The *aesthetic* *testimony* *can* *transmit* the *aesthetic* *knowledge* (the *no* *acquaintance* *necessary*), and the *testimony* *grounds* the *belief* (the *no* *mere* *prompt*), and the *critic* *is* the *expert* (the *no* *egalitarianism*), and the *knowledge* *is* *transmissible* (the *no* *incommunicability*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *aesthetic* *knowledge*} \ = \ \text{the *testimony*} \ (\text{the *warrant*}) \ \wedge \ \text{the *reliability* of the *testifier*} \ (\text{the *no* *acquaintance* *necessary*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Meskin 2004, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *testimony* *is* a *basic* *source* of *knowledge* (the *no* *aesthetic* *exception*; Ch. 11, §1).} \\
2. & \text{The *reliability* of the *testifier* *grounds* the *warrant* (the *no* *mere* *belief*).} \\
3. & \text{The *knowledge* *does* not *require* the *acquaintance* (the *no* *first*-*hand* *condition*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *testimony* *transmits* the *aesthetic* *knowledge* (the *optimism*), and the *critic* *is* the *expert* (the *no* *egalitarianism*), and the *acquaintance* *is* *not* *necessary* (the *no* *privilege*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *fits* the *practice* (the *criticism*, the *teaching*), and it *grounds* the *knowledge* in the *testimony* (the *no* *mystery*), and it *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *social* *epistemology* (Ch. 11).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *transfer* *objection*** (Hopkins 2011, §4). The *testimony* *transmits* the *belief* (the *no* *experience*), and the *aesthetic* *knowledge* *requires* the *experience* (the *no* *mere* *belief*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *experience* *is* *not* the *necessary* *condition* (the *no* *acquaintance* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *knowledge* in the *reliability* (the *no* *experience*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *first*-*person* *authority*).

**(ii) The *no* *expertise* *objection*** (the *egalitarianism* of *taste*). The *taste* *is* *universal* (the *no* *expert*), and the *critic* *is* *not* *authority* (the *no* *deference*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *critic* *has* the *trained* *perception* (the *no* *mere* *opinion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *expertise* in the *training* (the *no* *elitism*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 6.4 The expertise

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *critic* as the *expert*; the *no* *mere* *taste*). The *aesthetic* *expertise* *consists* in the *trained* *perception* (the *discrimination* of the *properties*), the *knowledge* of the *tradition* (the *context*, the *genre*), and the *articulation* of the *response* (the *criticism*), and the *expertise* *grounds* the *testimony* (the *no* *mere* *authority*).

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *deference* (the *no* *mere* *authority*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *training*, the *criticism*), and it *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *virtue* *epistemology* (the *perceptual* *expertise*; Ch. 8, §7).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *expertise* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *expertise* *is* the *social* *construction* (the *no* *real* *authority*), and the *deference* *is* the *ideology* (the *no* *knowledge*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *expertise* *is* *testable* (the *agreement* of the *trained*, the *no* *mere* *construction*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *expertise* in the *evidence*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *articulation* *objection***. The *critic* *does* not *articulate* the *response* (the *no* *reasons*), and the *expertise* *is* *tacit* (the *no* *transmission*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *articulation* *is* *possible* (the *reasons*, the *description*, the *comparison*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *expertise* in the *articulation*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**The *assessment***. The *pessimism* *captures* the *acquaintance* (the *no* *deference*), the *optimism* *captures* the *practice* (the *criticism*), and the *expertise* *integrates* the *two* (the *trained* *perception*).

## 7. The Value of Art

### 7.1 The question

**The *question***. The *evaluative* *question* (*is the work good?*) *connects* to the *moral* *question* (*is it morally good?*), and the *connection* is *contested* (the *moralism* *vs.* the *autonomism*; §7.2–7.4), and the *question* is *pressing* (the *propaganda*, the *pornography*, the *kitsch*, the *politically* *repellent* *art*).

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *content* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Moralism (ethicism)** | The *moral* *defect* is the *aesthetic* *defect* | Carroll 1996 |
| **Autonomism** | The *moral* and the *aesthetic* are *independent* | the *formalist* *tradition* |
| **Immoralism** | The *immoral* *stance* *can* be the *aesthetic* *merit* | Eaton 2012 |
| **Aestheticism** | The *aesthetic* is the *only* *value* (the *no* *moral*) | the *fin*-*de*-*siècle* |

### 7.2 Moralism

**The *thesis*** (Carroll, “Moderate Moralism,” 1996, *BJA* 36; “Art, Narrative, and Moral Understanding,” 1998; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *defect* of the *work* is the *aesthetic* *defect* *when* the *work* *prescribes* the *immoral* *response* (the *no* *mere* *depiction*), and the *moral* *merit* is the *aesthetic* *merit* *when* the *work* *prescribes* the *moral* *response*, and the *connection* *is* *moderate* (the *no* *total* *reduction*).

**The *structure*** (the *Carroll* *form*).

$$
\text{The *aesthetic* *value*} \ = \ \text{the *moral* *value*} \ \text{when the *moral* *stance* is *prescribed*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *depiction*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Carroll 1996, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *work* *prescribes* the *response* (the *no* *mere* *depiction*; the *narrative* *structure*).} \\
2. & \text{The *prescribed* *response* *is* *morally* *evaluable* (the *no* *neutrality*).} \\
3. & \text{The *evaluable* *response* *affects* the *aesthetic* *value* (the *no* *independence*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moralism* *is* *true* (the *Carroll* *insight*), and the *moral* *defect* *is* the *aesthetic* *defect* (the *no* *autonomism*), and the *connection* *is* *moderate* (the *no* *reduction*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *narrative* *engagement* (the *no* *mere* *formalism*), and it *grounds* the *value* in the *response* (the *no* *independence*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *criticism* of the *racist* *film*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *prescription* *objection***. The *work* *depicts* *without* *prescribing* (the *no* *stance*), and the *moralism* *confuses* the *depiction* with the *endorsement* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Carroll 1996, §4). The *prescription* *is* the *narrative* *structure* (the *no* *mere* *explicit* *statement*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *prescription* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *aesthetic* *defect* *objection***. The *morally* *bad* *art* *can* be *aesthetically* *excellent* (the *Triumph of the Will*, the *Leni* *Riefenstahl* *case*), and the *moralism* *denies* the *fact* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *moderate* *moralism*). The *work* *can* be *aesthetically* *excellent* *and* *morally* *defective* (the *no* *total* *reduction*), and the *reply* *separates* the *two* *dimensions* (the *no* *monism*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 7.3 Autonomism

**The *thesis*** (the *formalist* *tradition*; Bell 1914; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *aesthetic* *autonomy*). The *aesthetic* *value* *is* *independent* of the *moral* *value* (the *no* *connection*), and the *work* *is* *evaluated* *as* *art* (the *no* *moral* *criticism*), and the *moral* *criticism* is the *category* *mistake* (the *no* *relevance*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *aesthetic* *value*} \ \bot \ \text{the *moral* *value*} \ (\text{the *no* *connection*})
$$

**The *argument*** (the *formalist* *insight*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *aesthetic* *value* *supervenes* on the *formal* *properties* (the *no* *moral* *property*).} \\
2. & \text{The *formal* *properties* are *independent* of the *moral* *content* (the *no* *entailment*).} \\
3. & \text{The *independent* *properties* *ground* the *evaluation* (the *no* *reduction*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *aesthetic* *value* is *autonomous* (the *autonomism*), and the *moral* *criticism* is the *category* *mistake* (the *no* *relevance*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *autonomy* of the *art* (the *no* *moralism*), and it *avoids* the *censorship* (the *no* *moral* *control*), and it *fits* the *formalism* (§2.3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *form* *objection***. The *narrative* *and* the *representational* *art* *engage* the *moral* *content* (the *no* *mere* *form*), and the *autonomism* *ignores* the *content* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *broad* *formalism*). The *form* *includes* the *semantic* *structure* (the *no* *mere* *shape*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *content* in the *form*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *autonomy*).

**(ii) The *no* *value* *objection***. The *art* *shapes* the *perception* and the *moral* *judgment* (the *no* *neutrality*), and the *autonomism* *ignores* the *influence* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *influence* *is* *empirical* (the *no* *aesthetic* *value*), and the *reply* *separates* the *causal* *effect* from the *value* (the *no* *conflation*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.4 Immoralism

**The *thesis*** (Eaton, “Robust Immoralism,” 2012, *JAAC* 70; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *immoral* *stance* of the *work* *can* be the *aesthetic* *merit* (the *no* *mere* *defect*), and the *immoralism* *is* *robust* (the *no* *mere* *possibility*), and the *value* *interaction* *is* *plural* (the *no* *single* *direction*).

**The *structure***.

$$

**The *argument*** (Eaton 2012, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}

2. & \text{The *engagement* *can* be the *merit* (the *no* *mere* *tolerance*).} \\
3. & \text{The *merit* *is* *aesthetic* (the *no* *mere* *moral* *interest*).} \\
\hline

$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *transgressive* *art* (the *no* *mere* *moralism*), and it *grounds* the *value* in the *engagement* (the *no* *mere* *form*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *noir*, the *satire*, the *provocation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *merit* *objection*** (Carroll 1996, §5). The *immoral* *stance* *is* the *defect* (the *no* *merit*), and the *robust* *immoralism* *confuses* the *interest* with the *value* (the *no* *adequacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Eaton 2012, §3). The *engagement* *is* the *merit* (the *no* *mere* *interest*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *merit* in the *aesthetic* *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *no* *morality* *objection***. The *immoralism* *validates* the *harm* (the *no* *moral* *constraint*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *aesthetic* *merit* *is* not the *moral* *permission* (the *no* *validation*), and the *reply* *separates* the *value* from the *permission* (the *no* *harm*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 7.5 The value of art and the human good

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Nussbaum, *Love’s Knowledge*, 1990, Ch. 1; the *cognitive* *insight*). The *art* *has* the *cognitive* *value* (the *knowledge* of the *human* *condition*, the *imaginative* *engagement*), the *emotional* *value* (the *education* of the *feeling*, the *no* *mere* *sentimentality*), and the *social* *value* (the *solidarity*, the *critique*, the *no* *mere* *entertainment*), and the *values* *connect* the *art* to the *well*-*being* (Ch. 48, §3.4) and to the *moral* *life* (Ch. 45).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The *value* of the *art*} \ = \ \text{the *cognitive*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *emotional*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *social*} \ \wedge \ \text{the *aesthetic*}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *art* *enlarges* the *experience* (the *imagination*, the *perspective*).} \\
2. & \text{The *enlargement* *grounds* the *knowledge* and the *feeling* (the *no* *mere* *entertainment*).} \\
3. & \text{The *knowledge* and the *feeling* *connect* to the *good* *life* (the *no* *isolation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *art* *has* the *cognitive*, the *emotional*, the *social*, and the *aesthetic* *value* (the *plural* *insight*), and the *value* *connects* to the *well*-*being* (the *no* *autonomism* *of* *value*), and the *autonomism* *of* *the* *evaluation* *is* *compatible* with the *plurality* *of* the *value* (the *no* *reduction*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *plurality* of the *value* (the *no* *monism*), and it *connects* the *aesthetics* to the *ethics* (the *no* *isolation*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *education*, the *criticism*, the *engagement*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *cognitive* *value* *objection***. The *art* *does* not *give* the *knowledge* (the *fiction*, the *imagination*, the *no* *truth*), and the *cognitive* *value* is the *illusion* (the *no* *knowledge*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *art* *gives* the *understanding* (the *no* *mere* *propositional* *knowledge*; the *Nussbaum* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *value* in the *understanding*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *plurality* *objection***. The *aesthetic* *value* *absorbs* the *other* *values* (the *no* *plurality*), and the *pluralism* *is* *verbal* (the *no* *substance*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *moderate* *insight*). The *values* *interact* *without* *reducing* (the *no* *absorption*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *plurality* in the *interaction*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 7.6 The state of the debate about the value of art
