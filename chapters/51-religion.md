# Chapter 50 — Religion, Faith, and the Limits of Reason

> “For I do not seek to understand that I may believe, but I believe in order that I may understand. For this too I believe, that unless I first believe, I shall not understand.”
> — Anselm, *Proslogion* (1078), ch. 1

> “A miracle is a violation of the laws of nature; and as a firm and unalterable experience has established these laws, the proof against a miracle, from the very nature of the fact, is as entire as any argument from experience can possibly be imagined.”
> — David Hume, “Of Miracles” (1748), *Enquiry*, §X

> “Our passional nature not only lawfully may, but must, decide an option between propositions, whenever it is a genuine option that cannot by its nature be decided on intellectual grounds.”
> — William James, “The Will to Believe” (1896)

> “It is not hard to imagine that a perfectly loving God would remain constantly open to personal relationship with every finite person, and would do whatever is necessary to make it possible for each to enter into that relationship.”
> — J. L. Schellenberg, *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason* (1993), ch. 1

> “God does not exist. He is being itself beyond essence and existence. Therefore to argue that God exists is to deny him.”

---

Chapter 49 examined the *splendour* — the *aesthetic* *value* of the *object* *as* *experienced*. This chapter examines the *sacred* — the *value* that is *borne* by the *whole* of *reality* *as* *transcendent*, *as* *holy*, *as* *the* *source* of the *existence* and the *meaning* and the *hope*. The *transition* is the *inversion* of the *aesthetic* *turn*. The *aesthetic* *judgement* is *grounded* in the *object* *as* *presented* (the *no* *beyond*). The *religious* *judgement* *purports* to *reach* the *object* *beyond* the *presentation* (the *no* *mere* *immanence*), and the *reach* is the *distinctive* *problem*: the *aesthetic* *has* the *trained* *perception* *for* its *organon*, and the *religious* *has* only the *faith*, and the *faith* is the *most* *contested* *cognitive* *posture* in the *whole* of the *field*.

The *chapter* proceeds in *eight* *movements*. §1 examines the *concept* of *God* (the *classical* *theism*, the *pantheism*, the *deism*, the *process*, the *naturalism*). §2 examines the *arguments* *for* the *theism*. §3 examines the *arguments* *against*. §4 examines the *problem* of *evil* *in* *depth* (the *crux* of the *field*). §5 examines the *faith* and the *reason* (the *fideism*, the *will* *to* *believe*, the *Reformed* *epistemology*). §6 examines the *religious* *language*. §7 examines the *miracles* and the *testimony*. §8 gives the *verdict*.

## 1. The Concept of God

### 1.1 The structure of the question

**The *question***. The *question* (*does* *God* *exist*?) *presupposes* the *concept* of *God*, and the *concept* is *contested* (the *classical* *theism*, the *pantheism*, the *process* *theism*, the *naturalism*), and the *arguments* *depend* on the *concept* (the *no* *single* *question*), and the *first* *task* is the *clarification*.

**The *distinctions***.

| The *position* | The *content* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical theism** | The *one* *God*, *simple*, *immutable*, *eternal*, *creator* | Aquinas; Maimonides; al-Ghazālī |
| **Pantheism** | The *God* is the *universe* (the *identity*) | Spinoza; the *Stoics* |
| **Panentheism** | The *God* *includes* the *universe* (the *no* *identity*) | the *process* *theism*; Hartshorne |
| **Deism** | The *God* *creates* and *withdraws* (the *no* *intervention*) | the *Enlightenment* |
| **Process theism** | The *God* is *dipolar* (the *persuasion*, the *no* *coercion*) | Whitehead; Hartshorne |
| **Religious naturalism** | The *religious* *attitude* *without* the *God* | Dewey; Crosby |

**The *significance***. The *concept* *determines* the *arguments* (the *cosmological* *needs* the *creator*, the *ontological* *needs* the *maximal* *being*, the *problem* of *evil* *needs* the *omnipotent* *and* the *omniscient*), and the *concept* *determines* the *stakes* (the *theism* *of* the *philosophers* is *not* the *theism* of the *pews*), and the *clarification* *is* the *first* *philosophical* *contribution*.

**The *constraints***.

| The *constraint* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *worship-worthiness*** | The *God* *must* *merit* the *worship* (the *no* *mere* *power*) | The *axiological* *constraint* |
| **The *consistency*** | The *attributes* *must* be *compossible* (the *no* *contradiction*) | The *logical* *constraint* |
| **The *revelation*** | The *God* *must* be *knowable* (the *no* *total* *hiddenness*) | The *epistemic* *constraint* |
| **The *freedom*** | The *God* *must* be *free* (the *no* *necessity* *of* the *creation*) | The *modal* *constraint* |

**The *problem***. The *constraints* *pull* *apart*: the *worship-worthiness* *requires* the *goodness* (the *problem* of *evil*), the *consistency* *requires* the *simplicity* (the *problem* of the *attributes*), the *revelation* *requires* the *hiddenness* (the *problem* of the *divine* *hiddenness*), and the *freedom* *requires* the *no* *necessity* (the *problem* of the *creation*).

### 1.2 Classical theism

**The *thesis*** (Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, I, q. 2–26; Maimonides, *Guide of the Perplexed*, 1190, I.50–60; al-Ghazālī, *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa*, 1095; the *contemporary* *literature*; Davies, *An Introduction to the Philosophy of Religion*, 1982, ch. 1). The *God* is the *simple* (the *no* *composition*), the *immutable* (the *no* *change*), the *eternal* (the *no* *succession*), the *self*-*existent* (the *no* *cause*), the *omniscient*, the *omnipotent*, the *omnibenevolent*, and the *creator* *ex* *nihilo*, and the *attributes* *are* the *divine* *essence* *under* the *different* *descriptions* (the *no* *real* *distinction*), and the *knowledge* of the *God* is *analogical* (the *no* *univocal*, the *no* *equivocal*; §6.2).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{God} \ = \ \text{the *subsistent* *being* *itself*} \ (\text{the } \textit{ipsum esse subsistens}; \text{the *no* *composition*})
$$

**The *attributes***.

| The *attribute* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *simplicity*** | The *no* *distinction* of the *essence* and the *existence* | The *no* *person* |
| **The *immutability*** | The *no* *change* | The *no* *response* to the *prayer* |
| **The *eternity*** | The *no* *succession* (the *totum* *simul*) | The *no* *history* |
| **The *omniscience*** | The *knowledge* of the *all* (the *no* *ignorance*) | The *no* *free* *will* (the *foreknowledge*) |
| **The *omnipotence*** | The *power* *over* the *all* (the *no* *limit*) | The *no* *evil* (the *problem*; §3) |
| **The *omnibenevolence*** | The *goodness* (the *no* *defect*) | The *no* *evil* (the *problem*; §3) |

**The *argument*** (the *Thomistic* *insight*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *God* *is* the *perfection* of the *being* (the *no* *defect*; the *worship-worthiness*).} \\
2. & \text{The *perfection* *excludes* the *composition* (the *no* *parts*, the *no* *potentiality*).} \\
3. & \text{The *composition* *is* the *ground* of the *becoming* (the *no* *change*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *God* *is* the *simple*, the *immutable*, the *eternal* (the *classical* *insight*), and the *attributes* *follow* the *perfection* (the *no* *anthropomorphism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *transcendence* (the *no* *mere* *idol*), and it *grounds* the *worship* (the *no* *mere* *power*), and it *explains* the *creation* (the *no* *necessity*), and it *connects* the *theism* to the *metaphysics* (Ch. 14–22).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *person* *objection***. The *simplicity* and the *immutability* *exclude* the *person* (the *no* *emotion*, the *no* *response*, the *no* *relationship*), and the *biblical* *God* *is* the *personal* (the *anger*, the *love*, the *regret*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *classical* *insight*; the *contemporary* *literature*; Davies 1982, ch. 5; Stump, “Simplicity,” 2016). The *personhood* *is* the *perfection* (the *no* *mere* *human* *emotion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *person* in the *perfection* (the *analogical* *insight*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *classical* *theism*), and *contested* (the *no* *biblical* *dynamism*).

**(ii) The *no* *coherence* *objection***. The *simplicity* *destroys* the *distinction* *between* the *attributes* (the *knowledge* *is* the *power* *is* the *goodness*), and the *no* *distinction* *makes* the *attributes* *unintelligible* (the *no* *content*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Brower, “Making Sense of Divine Simplicity,” 2008; the *truthmaker* *account*). The *attributes* *are* the *divine* *essence* *under* the *different* *descriptions* (the *no* *real* *distinction*; the *no* *mere* *verbal*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *attributes* in the *essence*. The *reply* is *defensible*, and *contested* (the *no* *conceptual* *content*).

### 1.3 Pantheism and panentheism

**The *thesis*** (Spinoza, *Ethics*, 1677, I, prop. 1–15; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *transcendence*). The *God* *is* the *substance* (the *one*, the *infinite*, the *necessary*), and *everything* *else* is the *mode* (the *attribute*, the *modification*), and the *God* *is* the *nature* (the *Deus* *sive* *Natura*; the *identity*), and the *pantheism* *avoids* the *creation* (the *no* *free* *act*), and the *romantic* *pantheism* (the *Goethe*, the *Wordsworth*) *expresses* the *immanence*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{God} \ = \ \text{the *substance*} \ = \ \text{the *nature*} \quad (\text{the *identity*; the *no* *transcendence*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Spinoza, *Ethics*, I, prop. 11–15).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *substance* *is* the *necessary* *existence* (the *no* *contingency*).} \\
2. & \text{The *substance* *is* the *only* *substance* (the *no* *plurality*; the *monism*).} \\
3. & \text{The *God* *is* the *substance* (the *definition*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *God* *is* the *nature* (the *pantheism*), and the *everything* *is* the *mode* (the *no* *transcendence*), and the *identity* *excludes* the *creation* (the *necessity*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *immanence* (the *no* *foreign* *God*), and it *avoids* the *creation* *problem* (the *no* *free* *act*), and it *fits* the *naturalistic* *sensibility* (the *no* *supernatural*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *transcendence* *objection***. The *pantheism* *collapses* the *God* into the *world* (the *no* *worship*, the *no* *horizon*), and the *religious* *attitude* *requires* the *transcendence* (the *no* *mere* *immanence*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *panentheism*; Hartshorne, *The Divine Relativity*, 1948, ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *God* *includes* the *world* (the *no* *identity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *transcendence* in the *inclusion* (the *no* *collapse*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *panentheism*).

**(ii) The *no* *freedom* *objection***. The *pantheism* *excludes* the *freedom* (the *necessity*, the *no* *choice*), and the *creation* *requires* the *free* *act* (the *no* *necessity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *freedom* *is* the *necessity* *of* the *nature* (the *no* *arbitrary* *choice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *freedom* in the *perfection* (the *no* *mere* *necessity*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.4 Deism and process theism

**The *thesis***. The *deism* (the *Enlightenment*; Toland, *Christianity Not Mysterious*, 1696; Paine, *The Age of Reason*, 1794) *retains* the *creator* (the *cosmological* *argument*) and *rejects* the *intervention* (the *miracle*, the *revelation*, the *providence*), and the *God* is the *watchmaker* (the *no* *continuing* *agency*); the *process* *theism* (Whitehead, *Process and Reality*, 1929, ch. 5; Hartshorne 1948) *retains* the *agency* and *rejects* the *omnipotence* (the *no* *coercion*, the *persuasion*), and the *God* is the *dipolar* (the *primordial* *and* the *consequent* *nature*).

**The *structures***.

$$
\text{Deism} \ = \ \text{the *creator*} \ \wedge \ \neg \ (\text{the *intervention*}) \qquad \text{Process} \ = \ \text{the *persuasion*} \ \wedge \ \neg \ (\text{the *coercion*})
$$

**The *significance***. The *deism* *avoids* the *miracle* *problem* (§7) and the *providence* *problem* (§4), and the *process* *theism* *avoids* the *omnipotence* *problem* (§3.4) and the *evil* *problem* (§4), and the *two* *positions* *show* that the *classical* *theism* is *not* the *only* *theism* (the *no* *single* *target*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *religion* *objection*** (the *deism*). The *watchmaker* *God* *does* not *merit* the *worship* (the *no* *relationship*, the *no* *response*), and the *deism* *is* the *philosophy* *without* the *religion* (the *no* *piety*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *natural* *religion*). The *worship* *is* the *admiration* *of* the *order* (the *no* *mere* *petition*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *religion* in the *awe* (the *no* *mere* *relationship*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *natural* *piety*).

**(ii) The *no* *omnipotence* *objection*** (the *process*). The *persuasion* *God* *is* *not* the *traditional* *God* (the *no* *control*, the *no* *guarantee*), and the *religion* *requires* the *security* (the *no* *mere* *risk*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Hartshorne 1948, ch. 2). The *persuasion* *is* the *higher* *power* (the *no* *mere* *weakness*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *power* in the *love* (the *no* *coercion*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *process* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *guarantee*).

### 1.5 Religious naturalism

**The *thesis*** (Dewey, *A Common Faith*, 1934, ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; Crosby, *Living with Ambiguity*, 2008; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *religious* *attitude* *is* the *orientation* *toward* the *whole* (the *gratitude*, the *acceptance*, the *commitment*), and the *attitude* *does* not *require* the *supernatural* (the *no* *God*), and the *naturalism* *sustains* the *piety* (the *no* *superstition*), and the *religion* *survives* the *death* of the *metaphysics* (the *no* *mere* *secularism*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Religious naturalism} \ = \ \text{the *religious* *attitude*} \ \wedge \ \neg \ (\text{the *supernatural* *object*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Dewey 1934, ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *religious* *experience* *has* the *natural* *causes* (the *awe*, the *gratitude*, the *solidarity*).} \\
2. & \text{The *natural* *causes* *suffice* for the *attitude* (the *no* *supernatural* *object* *required*).} \\
3. & \text{The *attitude* *grounds* the *religion* (the *no* *mere* *belief*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *religion* *survives* the *naturalism* (the *Dewey* *insight*), and the *piety* *is* the *natural* (the *no* *superstition*), and the *secularism* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *dichotomy*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *depth* of the *religious* *experience* (the *no* *mere* *metaphysics*), and it *avoids* the *supernatural* (the *naturalistic* *insight*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *ethical* *commitment*, the *cosmic* *humility*), and it *connects* the *religion* to the *well*-*being* (Ch. 48, §3.4) and to the *environmental* *ethics* (Ch. 51, §6).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *religion* *objection***. The *attitude* *without* the *object* *is* *not* the *religion* (the *no* *transcendence*, the *no* *worship*), and the *naturalism* *is* the *philosophy* *without* the *faith* (the *no* *piety*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Crosby 2008, ch. 1). The *religion* *is* the *orientation* (the *no* *mere* *object*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *religion* in the *attitude* (the *no* *mere* *belief*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *functional* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *transcendence*).

**(ii) The *no* *consolation* *objection***. The *naturalism* *leaves* the *no* *hope* (the *no* *afterlife*, the *no* *providence*), and the *religion* *requires* the *consolation* (the *no* *mere* *attitude*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *Stoic* *insight*; Ch. 48, §7.3). The *consolation* *is* the *acceptance* (the *no* *mere* *compensation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *religion* in the *tranquility* (the *no* *illusion*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *no* *mere* *hope*).

### 1.6 The state of the debate about the concept

**The *assessment***. The *classical* *theism* *captures* the *transcendence* (the *no* *idol*), the *pantheism* *captures* the *immanence* (the *no* *foreign* *God*), the *process* *captures* the *persuasion* (the *no* *coercion*), and the *naturalism* *captures* the *piety* *without* the *metaphysics* (the *no* *superstition*).

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical theism** | The *transcendence* | The *no* *person*; the *no* *response* |
| **Pantheism** | The *immanence* | The *no* *transcendence* |
| **Process theism** | The *persuasion* | The *no* *guarantee* |
| **Deism** | The *no* *miracle* | The *no* *relationship* |
| **Religious naturalism** | The *piety* | The *no* *transcendence* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *panentheism* *combined* with the *classical* *theism* *and* the *naturalistic* *humility*: the *God* *includes* the *world* (the *panentheist* *insight*), the *God* *is* the *perfection* (the *classical* *insight*), the *persuasion* *is* the *mode* of the *agency* (the *process* *insight*), the *language* *is* the *analogical* (the *no* *anthropomorphism*; §6.2), and the *naturalistic* *humility* *limits* the *claims* (the *no* *over*-*belief*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *concept* *and* the *attributes* *remain* *contested*.

## 2. Arguments for Theism

### 2.1 The structure of the arguments

**The *question***. The *arguments* *for* the *theism* *aim* at the *rational* *justification* (the *no* *mere* *faith*; §5), and the *arguments* *fall* into the *two* *families* (the *a priori* and the *a posteriori*), and the *two* *families* *raise* the *different* *issues*.

**The *families***.

| The *family* | The *arguments* | The *structure* |
|---|---|---|
| **A priori** | The *ontological* | The *concept* *entails* the *existence* |
| **A posteriori** | The *cosmological*, the *teleological*, the *moral*, the *experience*, the *consciousness* | The *evidence* *supports* the *hypothesis* |

**The *criteria***. The *argument* *must* *satisfy* the *criteria* of Ch. 4, §3 (the *validity*, the *soundness*, the *explanatory* *power*, the *simplicity*, the *adequacy* to the *evidence*), and the *argument* *must* *respect* the *defeaters* (the *evil*, the *hiddenness*, the *diversity*; §3–4).

### 2.2 The cosmological argument

**The *thesis*** (al-Kindi, *On First Philosophy*, c. 870; al-Ghazālī 1095; Aquinas, *ST*, I, q. 2, a. 3; Leibniz, *Monadology*, 1714, §32–39; the *contemporary* *literature*; Craig, *The Kalam Cosmological Argument*, 1979). The *universe* *has* a *cause* (the *no* *infinite* *regress* of the *essentially* *ordered* *series*; the *no* *beginningless* *successive* *series*; the *no* *sufficient* *reason* *immanent*), and the *cause* *is* the *uncaused*, the *beginningless*, the *necessary*, the *personal* (the *no* *mere* *physical* *cause*), and the *cause* *is* *God*.

**The *varieties***.

| The *version* | The *premise* | The *conclusion* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *Kalam*** | The *universe* *began* (the *no* *actual* *infinite*) | The *cause* *began* the *universe* |
| **The *Thomistic*** | The *essentially* *ordered* *series* *requires* the *first* | The *first* *cause* *sustains* the *series* |
| **The *Leibnizian*** | The *PSR* *requires* the *sufficient* *reason* | The *necessary* *being* *grounds* the *contingent* |

**The *Kalam* *argument*** (Craig 1979, ch. 2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *everything* *that* *begins* *to* *exist* *has* a *cause* (the *causal* *principle*).} \\
2. & \text{The *universe* *began* *to* *exist* (the *Big* *Bang*, the *thermodynamics*, the *no* *actual* *infinite*).} \\
3. & \text{The *cause* *is* the *beginningless*, the *personal* (the *no* *physical* *cause*, the *no* *mere* *event*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *universe* *has* a *cause*, and the *cause* *is* *God* (the *Kalam* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *Leibnizian* *argument*** (Leibniz 1714, §32–39).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *every* *contingent* *fact* *has* a *sufficient* *reason* (the *PSR*).} \\
2. & \text{The *existence* of the *universe* *is* a *contingent* *fact* (the *no* *necessity*).} \\
3. & \text{The *reason* *cannot* be the *contingent* *series* (the *no* *sufficient* *reason* *immanent*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *reason* *is* a *necessary* *being* (the *Leibnizian* *insight*), and the *necessary* *being* *is* *God* (the *no* *mere* *brute* *fact*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *arguments* *capture* the *intelligibility* of the *world* (the *no* *mere* *brute* *fact*), and they *ground* the *contingency* (the *no* *self*-*explanation*), and they *fit* the *physics* (the *Big* *Bang*; Ch. 41, §2), and they *connect* the *theism* to the *metaphysics* (the *grounding*; Ch. 22).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *ex* *nihilo* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *quantum* *cosmology*; Grünbaum, “The Pseudo-Problem of Creation,” 1989). The *causal* *principle* *applies* *within* the *universe* (the *no* *beyond*), and the *no* *time* *before* the *Big* *Bang* (the *no* *cause*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Craig & Sinclair, *Naturalism*, 2009, ch. 4). The *causal* *principle* *applies* to the *all* *that* *begins* (the *no* *mere* *intra*-*mundane*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *cause* in the *principle* (the *no* *exception*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *independent* *evidence*).

**(ii) The *no* *first* *cause* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *actual* *infinite*; Oppy, *Arguing about Gods*, 2006, ch. 2). The *infinite* *regress* *is* *possible* (the *no* *first*), and the *first* *cause* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *conclusion*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Craig 1979, ch. 1). The *actual* *infinite* *is* *impossible* (the *no* *traversal* of the *infinite*, the *Hilbert* *hotel*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *first* *cause* in the *impossibility* (the *no* *regress*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *intuition* *of* the *impossibility*).

**(iii) The *no* *God* *objection*** (the *gap* *problem*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *cause* *is* *not* *the* *God* (the *no* *omniscience*, the *no* *omnibenevolence*, the *no* *worship-worthiness*), and the *argument* *establishes* *at* *most* the *first* *cause* (the *no* *theism*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *cause* *is* the *uncaused*, the *beginningless*, the *personal* (the *no* *mere* *force*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *attributes* in the *cause* (the *no* *mere* *first* *cause*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *omnibenevolence*).

### 2.3 The teleological argument

**The *thesis*** (Paley, *Natural Theology*, 1802, ch. 1–2; the *contemporary* *literature*; Swinburne, *The Existence of God*, 1979, ch. 8; Leslie, *Universes*, 1989; the *fine*-*tuning* *insight*). The *world* *exhibits* the *order* (the *means*-*end* *structure*, the *fine*-*tuning* of the *constants*), and the *order* is *improbable* under the *chance* (the *no* *natural* *necessity*), and the *order* *supports* the *design* (the *inference* to the *best* *explanation*), and the *designer* *is* *God*.

**The *structure*** (the *fine*-*tuning* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *constants* are *fine*-*tuned* for the *life*} \ (\text{the *life*-*permitting* *range* is *narrow*}: \ \text{the } \Omega_\Lambda, \ \alpha, \ \text{the *mass* *ratio*}). \\
2. & \text{The *fine*-*tuning* is *improbable* under the *chance*} \ (\text{the *no* *natural* *necessity*}). \\
3. & \text{The *design* *explains* the *fine*-*tuning*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *chance*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *fine*-*tuning* *supports* the *design*} \ (\text{the *teleological* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *designer* is *God*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *chance*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *captures* the *order* (the *no* *mere* *chaos*), and it *fits* the *physics* (the *fine*-*tuning*; Ch. 41, §2), and it *puts* the *theism* *in* the *explanatory* *competition* (the *no* *mere* *a priori*), and it *connects* the *theism* to the *philosophy* of *science* (Ch. 39–40).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *multiverse* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *inflationary* *cosmology*; Susskind, *The Cosmic Landscape*, 2005). The *many* *universes* *make* the *fine*-*tuning* *probable* (the *no* *improbability*), and the *design* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *inference*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Swinburne 1979, ch. 8; the *no* *free* *lunch*). The *multiverse* *requires* the *fine*-*tuning* of the *generator* (the *no* *escape*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *design* in the *generator* (the *no* *regress*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *evidence* for the *multiverse*).

**(ii) The *observer* *selection* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *anthropic* *principle*; the *no* *surprise*). The *we* *observe* the *fine*-*tuning* *because* the *we* *exist* (the *selection* *effect*), and the *improbability* *is* *the* *illusion* (the *no* *evidence*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *inverse* *gambler* *fallacy*). The *selection* *effect* *explains* the *observation* *but* *not* the *fine*-*tuning* (the *no* *mere* *selection*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *design* in the *fine*-*tuning*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *probability* *space*).

**(iii) The *no* *probability* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *fine*-*tuning* *requires* the *measure* (the *no* *well*-*defined* *space*), and the *probability* *is* *undefined* (the *no* *improbability*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *measure* *is* the *life*-*permitting* *range* (the *no* *arbitrary*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *probability* in the *range*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *unique* *measure*).

### 2.4 The ontological argument

**The *thesis*** (Anselm, *Proslogion*, 1078, ch. 2–3; Descartes, *Meditations*, 1641, V; Leibniz, 1714; the *contemporary* *literature*; Plantinga, *The Nature of Necessity*, 1974, ch. 10; Gödel, the *modal* *collapse*, 1970). The *God* *is* the *maximal* *being* (the *than* *which* *nothing* *greater* *can* be *conceived*), and the *existence* *is* the *perfection* (the *no* *mere* *concept*), and the *concept* *entails* the *existence* (the *no* *mere* *contingency*), and the *argument* is *a priori* (the *no* *evidence* *required*).

**The *Plantinga* *version*** (1974, ch. 10).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \Diamond \exists x \, \Box G(x) \quad (\text{the *possibility* of the *maximal* *greatness*}). \\
2. & \Box \big( \exists x \, \Box G(x) \rightarrow \Box \exists x \, \Box G(x) \big) \quad (\text{the *definition* of the *maximal* *greatness*}). \\
3. & \Diamond p \rightarrow \Box \Diamond p \quad (\text{the } S5 \text{ *axiom*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \Box \exists x \, \Box G(x) \quad (\text{the *necessity*}), \ \text{hence} \ \exists x \, \Box G(x) \ (\text{the *actuality*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *establishes* the *existence* *from* the *concept* (the *no* *evidence*), and it *captures* the *necessity* of the *God* (the *no* *contingency*), and it *grounds* the *theism* in the *logic* (the *no* *mere* *experience*), and it *connects* the *theism* to the *modality* (Ch. 16).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Gaunilo* *objection*** (Gaunilo, *On Behalf of the Fool*, 1078; the *perfect* *island*). The *argument* *proves* the *perfect* *island* (the *no* *restriction* to the *God*), and the *parody* *undermines* the *argument* (the *no* *validity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Plantinga 1974, ch. 10). The *greatness* *admits* of the *maximal* *degree* (the *no* *island* can be *maximal*; the *no* *intrinsic* *maximum*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *argument* in the *maximality* (the *no* *parody*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Plantinga* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *intuition* of the *maximality*).

**(ii) The *Kant* *objection*** (Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, 1781, A592–602/B620–630). The *existence* *is* *not* a *predicate* (the *no* *addition* to the *concept*), and the *argument* *smuggles* the *existence* into the *concept* (the *no* *validity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Plantinga 1974, ch. 10). The *argument* *does* not *treat* the *existence* as a *first*-*order* *predicate* (the *no* *mere* *addition*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *argument* in the *modal* *perfection* (the *no* *smuggling*). The *reply* is *defensible*, and *contested* (the *no* *S5* *axiom*).

**(iii) The *no* *possibility* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Oppy 2006, ch. 3). The *premise* 1 is the *contested* (the *possibility* *of* the *maximal* *greatness*), and the *atheist* *denies* it (the *no* *possibility*), and the *argument* *begs* the *question* (the *no* *validity*). The *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *possibility* *is* *prima* *facie* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and the *atheist* *must* *provide* the *impossibility* (the *no* *mere* *denial*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *possibility* in the *concept*. The *reply* is *defensible*, and *contested* (the *no* *parity*).

### 2.5 The moral argument

**The *thesis*** (Kant, *Critique of Practical Reason*, 1788, the *postulate*; Newman, *A Grammar of Assent*, 1870; Adams, *Finite and Infinite Goods*, 1999, ch. 1; Craig, *Reasonable Faith*, 1994, ch. 4). The *objective* *moral* *facts* *exist* (the *moral* *realism*; Ch. 44, §2), and the *objective* *facts* *require* the *ground* (the *no* *brute* *normativity*), and the *ground* is the *God* (the *goodness* *itself*, the *moral* *lawgiver*), and the *theism* *explains* the *moral* *experience* (the *no* *mere* *projection*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *objective* *moral* *facts* *exist*} \ (\text{the *moral* *realism*}; \text{Ch. 44, §2}). \\
2. & \text{The *objective* *moral* *facts* *require* a *ground*} \ (\text{the *no* *brute* *normativity*}). \\
3. & \text{The *ground* is the *God*} \ (\text{the *goodness* *itself*}; \text{the *Adams* *insight*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *God* *exists*} \ (\text{the *moral* *argument*}), \ \text{and the *moral* *realism* *supports* the *theism*} \ (\text{the *no* *neutrality*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *connects* the *theism* to the *metaethics* (the *no* *mere* *isolation*), and it *captures* the *moral* *experience* (the *no* *mere* *convention*), and it *grounds* the *value* in the *God* (the *no* *queerness*; Ch. 44, §2.4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *realism* *objection*** (the *anti*-*realism*; Ch. 44, §3). The *moral* *facts* *are* *not* *objective* (the *expressivism*, the *error* *theory*), and the *argument* *begs* the *question* (the *no* *premise*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *robust* *realism*; Ch. 44, §2). The *realism* *is* *defensible* (the *no* *mere* *projection*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *argument* in the *realism*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *consensus*).

**(ii) The *Euthyphro* *objection*** (Plato, *Euthyphro*, c. 399 BCE, 10a–11b). The *good* *is* the *good* *because* the *God* *commands* it (the *arbitrary*), or the *God* *commands* it *because* it is the *good* (the *no* *need* of the *God*), and the *dilemma* *undermines* the *argument* (the *no* *ground*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Adams 1999, ch. 1). The *good* *is* the *God* *himself* (the *no* *command*, the *no* *arbitrary*; the *identity*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *dilemma* (the *no* *third* *option*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Adams* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *independent* *good*).

**(iii) The *no* *God* *objection***. The *moral* *facts* *can* be *grounded* in the *nature* (the *naturalistic* *realism*; Ch. 44, §2.3; Ch. 45, §3), and the *God* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *inference*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nature* *ground* *leaves* the *normativity* *unexplained* (the *no* *brute* *normativity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *God*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *realism*).

\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *experience* *grounds* the *belief*} \ (\text{the *Alston* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *belief* is *rational*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *feeling*}).}

$$

**The *consciousness* *argument*** (Swinburne 1986; the *contemporary* *literature*).

\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *consciousness* *supports* the *theism*} \ (\text{the *Swinburne* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *physicalism* is *incomplete*} \ (\text{the *no* *closure*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *arguments* *connect* the *theism* to the *experience* (the *no* *mere* *inference*) and to the *mind* (the *no* *mere* *metaphysics*), and they *ground* the *belief* in the *phenomenology* (the *no* *mere* *argument*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *perception* *objection***. The *experience* *is* *not* the *perception* (the *no* *object*, the *no* *sensory* *given*), and the *perceptual* *model* *mystifies* the *belief* (the *no* *analogy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Alston 1991, ch. 1). The *experience* *is* *perceptual* in the *broad* *sense* (the *no* *mere* *feeling*; the *presentation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *model* in the *presentation*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *broad* *perception*), and *contested* (the *no* *check* on the *appearance*).

**(ii) The *no* *defeater* *objection***. The *defeaters* *are* *present* (the *diversity*, the *neuroscience*, the *reduction*; §3.5; the *contemporary* *literature*), and the *experience* *does* not *ground* the *belief* (the *no* *warrant*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *defeaters* are *contested* (the *no* *decisive* *reduction*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *belief* in the *no* *decisive* *defeat*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *robust* *warrant*).

**(iii) The *no* *theism* *objection*** (the *consciousness*). The *consciousness* *supports* the *panpsychism* or the *dualism* (the *no* *theism*), and the *God* *is* *not* *required* (the *no* *inference*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *theism* is the *simpler* *explanation* (the *one* *mind* vs. the *many* *minds*; the *no* *combination* *problem*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *inference* in the *simplicity*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *simplicity*).

## 3. Arguments against Theism

### 3.1 The logical problem of evil

**The *thesis*** (Mackie, “Evil and Omnipotence,” 1955, *Mind* 64; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *classical* *formulation*; the *no* *consistency*). The *theism* *asserts* the *omnipotence*, the *omniscience*, the *omnibenevolence*, and the *evil* *exists*, and the *four* *propositions* *cannot* *all* be *true* (the *no* *consistency*), and the *theism* is *logically* *inconsistent* (the *no* *rationality*), and the *God* does *not* *exist* (the *no* *theism*).

**The *structure*** (the *Mackie* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *God* is *omnipotent*} \ (\text{the *can* *prevent* the *evil*}). \\
2. & \text{The *God* is *omniscient*} \ (\text{the *knows* the *evil*}). \\
3. & \text{The *God* is *omnibenevolent*} \ (\text{the *would* *prevent* the *evil*}). \\
4. & \text{The *evil* *exists*} \ (\text{the *datum*}). \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The *four* are *inconsistent*} \ (\text{the *no* *consistency*}), \ \text{and the *theism* is *irrational*} \ (\text{the *Mackie* *insight*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *captures* the *problem* (the *no* *mere* *emotional* *reaction*), and it *grounds* the *atheism* in the *logic* (the *no* *mere* *doubt*), and it *sets* the *agenda* for the *theodicy* (§4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *free*-*will* *defence*** (Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil*, 1974, Part I; §4.2). The *logical* *problem* *requires* the *necessary* *inconsistency* (the *no* *mere* *prima* *facie*), and the *free* *will* *defence* *exhibits* a *possible* *world* (the *free* *creatures* *who* *do* *evil*; the *transworld* *depravity*), and the *consistency* *is* *established* (the *no* *contradiction*), and the *reply* *succeeds* (the *Plantinga* *insight*).

**The *reply*.** The *reply* is *defensible* (the *logical* *problem* *is* *widely* *conceded* *to* *have* *failed*; the *contemporary* *literature*), and the *debate* *moves* to the *evidential* *problem* (§3.2).

**(ii) The *no* *free* *will* *objection***. The *free* *will* *defence* *depends* on the *libertarian* *freedom* (the *no* *compatibilist* *option*), and the *natural* *evil* *is* *not* the *free* *will* (the *earthquake*, the *disease*), and the *defence* *is* *incomplete* (the *no* *sufficiency*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Plantinga 1974, Part I; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *natural* *evil* *is* *traced* to the *free* *will* of the *demons* (the *no* *mere* *nature*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *defence* in the *possible* *world* (the *no* *actuality*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *plausibility*).

### 3.2 The evidential problem of evil

**The *thesis*** (Rowe, “The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism,” 1979, *APQ* 16; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *gratuitous* *evil*). The *evidential* *problem* *does* not *claim* the *inconsistency* (the *no* *contradiction*) but the *improbability* (the *no* *likelihood*), and the *intense* *suffering* *that* *no* *known* *good* *explains* (the *fawn* *case*, the *Holocaust*) *counts* *against* the *theism* (the *no* *indifference*), and the *theism* is *improbable* *on* the *evidence* (the *no* *rationality*).

**The *structure*** (the *Rowe* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{There is *intense* *suffering* that the *omniscient*, the *omnipotent*, the *omnibenevolent* *being* *could* *prevent* without the *loss* of a *greater* *good*} \ (\text{the *fawn* *case*}). \\
2. & \text{The *omnibenevolent* *being* *would* *prevent* the *pointless* *suffering*} \ (\text{the *no* *gratuitous* *evil*}). \\
3. & \text{The *suffering* is *pointless*} \ (\text{the *no* *known* *reason*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *theism* is *improbable*} \ (\text{the *Rowe* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *evidence* *counts* *against* the *God*} \ (\text{the *no* *indifference*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *captures* the *depth* of the *problem* (the *no* *mere* *logic*), and it *fits* the *experience* (the *no* *mere* *abstraction*), and it *grounds* the *atheism* in the *evidence* (the *no* *mere* *doubt*), and it *sets* the *task* for the *skeptical* *theism* (§4.4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *skeptical* *theism* *reply*** (Wykstra, “The Humean Obstacle to Evidential Arguments from Suffering,” 1984, *IJPR* 16; Bergmann, “Skeptical Theism and Rowe’s New Evidential Argument,” 2001, *Noûs* 35; §4.4). The *inference* from the *no* *known* *reason* to the *no* *reason* *requires* the *epistemic* *access* (the *CORNEA*), and the *access* *is* *denied* (the *no* *omniscience*, the *no* *goodness*), and the *suffering* *may* *have* the *unknown* *reason* (the *no* *pointless*), and the *reply* *succeeds*.

**The *reply*.** The *reply* *succeeds* *formally* (the *no* *inference*), and the *cost* is the *moral* *skepticism* (the *no* *confidence* in the *value* *judgements*; §4.4), and the *debate* *is* *active*.

**(ii) The *no* *probability* *objection***. The *evidential* *argument* *requires* the *prior* *probability* (the *no* *neutral* *prior*), and the *evidence* *is* *contested* (the *no* *likelihood*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *faith* *can* *include* the *protest* (the *Job*, the *no* *mere* *resignation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *faith* in the *protest* (the *no* *mere* *justification*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Job* *insight*).

### 4.6 The state of the problem of evil

The *logical* *problem* *is* *defeated* (the *free* *will* *defence*), the *evidential* *problem* *disfavours* the *theism* (the *skeptical* *theism* *is* *contested*), the *soul*-*making* *theodicy* *explains* the *some* (the *no* *all*), the *anti*-*theodicy* *raises* the *moral* *depth* (the *no* *theory*), and the *skeptical* *theism* *answers* the *inference* (the *no* *moral* *skepticism*).

## 5. Faith and Reason

### 5.1 The question

**The *question***. The *question* (*is* the *faith* *rational*?) *presupposes* the *distinction* between the *faith* (the *trust*, the *commitment*) and the *belief* (the *assent* to the *proposition*), and the *distinction* *determines* the *answer* (the *faith* *can* be *rational* *without* the *propositional* *evidence*; the *belief* *requires* the *evidence*), and the *evidentialist* *challenge* (the *no* *belief* *without* the *evidence*) is the *starting* *point*.

**The *evidentialist* *challenge*** (Clifford, “The Ethics of Belief,” 1877, *Contemporary Review* 29).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *belief* *requires* the *sufficient* *evidence*} \ (\text{the *no* *credulity*}). \\
2. & \text{The *religious* *belief* *lacks* the *sufficient* *evidence*} \ (\text{the *no* *proof*}). \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *religious* *belief* is *irrational*} \ (\text{the *Clifford* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *faith* is the *vice*} \ (\text{the *no* *epistemic* *virtue*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *challenge* *sets* the *task* for the *faith* *and* the *reason* *debate* (the *fideism* *denies* the *premise* 1; the *James* *denies* the *premise* 2; the *Reformed* *epistemology* *denies* the *premise* 1 *in* the *religious* *case*), and the *challenge* *connects* the *religion* to the *epistemology* (Ch. 8, §5) and to the *ethics* *of* *belief* (Ch. 4, §4).

### 5.2 Fideism

**The *thesis*** (Tertullian, *De Carne Christi*, c. 210, §5; Kierkegaard, *Philosophical Fragments*, 1844, ch. 3; Wittgenstein, *Culture and Value*, 1980, §32; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *reason*). The *faith* *is* *not* *the* *conclusion* of the *reason* (the *no* *inference*), and the *faith* *is* *the* *commitment* *beyond* the *evidence* (the *no* *proof*), and the *reason* *is* *not* the *judge* of the *faith* (the *no* *tribunal*), and the *faith* *is* *rational* *in* its *own* *terms* (the *no* *external* *standard*).

**The *structure*** (the *Kierkegaardian* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *faith* *transcends* the *reason*} \ (\text{the *no* *inference*}; \text{the *no* *proof*}). \\
2. & \text{The *reason* is *not* the *judge*} \ (\text{the *no* *tribunal*}). \\
3. & \text{The *faith* is the *commitment*} \ (\text{the *leap*}; \text{the *no* *mere* *assent*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *faith* is *rational* *in* its *own* *terms*} \ (\text{the *Kierkegaard* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *reason* is not the *standard*} \ (\text{the *no* *tribunal*}), \ \text{and the *leap* is the *condition*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere} \ \text{evidence}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *existential* *dimension* (the *no* *mere* *proposition*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *commitment*, the *risk*), and it *grounds* the *faith* in the *whole* *person* (the *no* *mere* *intellect*), and it *connects* the *faith* to the *existentialism* (the *no* *mere* *epistemology*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *rationality* *objection***. The *fideism* *licenses* the *arbitrary* (the *no* *standard*), and the *faith* *can* *commit* to the *any* (the *no* *truth*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *faith* *has* the *internal* *standard* (the *no* *arbitrary*, the *coherence*, the *transformation* of the *life*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *rationality* in the *internal* *standard* (the *no* *external*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *fideist* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *truth*).

**(ii) The *no* *content* *objection***. The *faith* *without* the *proposition* *is* *not* the *faith* (the *no* *content*), and the *religion* *requires* the *belief* (the *no* *mere* *attitude*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *faith* *includes* the *proposition* *as* the *expression* (the *no* *mere* *attitude*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *content* in the *expression*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *no* *mere* *fideism*).

### 5.3 The will to believe

**The *thesis*** (James, “The Will to Believe,” 1896; *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, 1902, ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *evidentialism*). The *faith* *is* the *genuine* *option* (the *live*, the *forced*, the *momentous*), and the *evidence* *is* *not* *decisive* (the *no* *intellectual* *verdict*), and the *suspension* *is* a *decision* (the *no* *neutrality*), and the *passional* *nature* *may* *legitimately* *decide* (the *no* *mere* *evidence*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *option* is *live*, *forced*, *momentous*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *theoretical* *question*}). \\
2. & \text{The *evidence* is *not* *decisive*} \ (\text{the *no* *intellectual* *verdict*}). \\
3. & \text{The *suspension* is a *decision*} \ (\text{the *no* *neutrality*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *belief* is *permissible*} \ (\text{the *James* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *passional* *nature* *grounds* the *choice*} \ (\text{the *no* *evidentialism*}), \ \text{and the *risk* is the *condition* of the *truth*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere} \ \text{safety}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *existential* *structure* of the *belief* (the *no* *mere* *theoretical*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *commitment* *without* the *proof*), and it *grounds* the *rationality* in the *passional* *nature* (the *no* *mere* *intellect*), and it *connects* the *faith* to the *pragmatism* (Ch. 32, §4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *truth* *objection***. The *will* *to* *believe* *licenses* the *wishful* *thinking* (the *no* *evidence*), and the *belief* *can* *be* *false* (the *no* *truth*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *will* *to* *believe* *applies* *only* to the *genuine* *option* (the *no* *mere* *wish*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *rationality* in the *genuine* *option* (the *no* *arbitrary*). The *reply* is *defensible*, and *contested* (the *no* *truth*-*tracking*).

**(ii) The *no* *evidence* *objection***. The *belief* *without* the *evidence* *is* the *vice* (the *Clifford* *insight*), and the *James* *licenses* the *credulity* (the *no* *epistemic* *virtue*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *epistemic* *virtue* *includes* the *passional* *evidence* (the *no* *mere* *propositional*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *belief* in the *passional* *evidence*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *standard*).

### 5.4 Pascal's wager

**The *thesis*** (Pascal, *Pensées*, 1670, §233; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *decision*-*theoretic* *structure*). The *belief* *is* the *rational* *bet* (the *infinite* *payoff*, the *finite* *cost*), and the *unbelief* *risks* the *infinite* *loss* (the *no* *finite* *compensation*), and the *expected* *value* *favours* the *belief* (the *no* *mere* *prudence*), and the *faith* *is* the *reasonable* (the *no* *mere* *gamble*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{l|cc}
& \text{The *God* *exists*} & \text{The *God* does *not* *exist*} \\
\hline
\text{The *belief*} & +\infty & -\epsilon \\
\text{The *unbelief*} & -\infty & +\epsilon
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *belief* *risks* the *finite* *cost*} \ (\text{the } -\epsilon) \ \text{and *gains* the *infinite* *payoff*} \ (\text{the } +\infty). \\
2. & \text{The *unbelief* *gains* the *finite* \ (\text{the } +\epsilon) \ \text{and *risks* the *infinite*} \ (\text{the } -\infty). \\
3. & \text{The *expected* *value* *favours* the *belief*} \ (\text{the *decision*} \ \text{theory}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *belief* is the *rational* *bet*} \ (\text{the *Pascal* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *faith* is the *reasonable*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *gamble*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *wager* *grounds* the *faith* in the *prudence* (the *no* *mere* *evidentialism*), and it *fits* the *decision* *theory* (the *no* *mere* *intuition*), and it *connects* the *faith* to the *rationality* (Ch. 46, §3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *many* *gods* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Diderot, *Pensées philosophiques*, 1746; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *wager* *does* not *specify* *which* *God* (the *many* *gods*, the *competing* *payoffs*), and the *expected* *value* *cancels* (the *no* *unique* *bet*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *wager* *selects* the *Christian* *God* (the *tradition*, the *no* *arbitrary*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *bet* in the *tradition*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *unique* *selection*).

**(ii) The *no* *belief* *objection***. The *belief* *is* *not* *voluntary* (the *no* *choice*, the *no* *doxastic* *voluntarism*), and the *wager* *presupposes* the *choice* (the *no* *power*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *belief* *can* be *cultivated* (the *practice*, the *habit*, the *no* *mere* *choice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *wager* in the *cultivation*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Pascal* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *direct* *voluntarism*).

**(iii) The *no* *prudence* *objection***. The *faith* *must* be *the* *truth*-*directed* (the *no* *mere* *prudence*), and the *wager* *corrupts* the *motive* (the *no* *genuine* *faith*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *prudential* *reason* *can* *coexist* with the *truth*-*directed* (the *no* *mere* *motive*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *faith* in the *whole* *person* (the *no* *mere* *calculation*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *genuine* *faith*).

### 5.5 Reformed epistemology

**The *thesis*** (Plantinga, “Reason and Belief in God,” 1983; *Warranted Christian Belief*, 2000, ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *evidentialism*). The *belief* in the *God* *can* be *properly* *basic* (the *no* *inferential* *support*), and the *proper* *basicality* *requires* the *warrant* (the *sensus* *divinitatis*, the *no* *mere* *fideism*), and the *warrant* *is* *defeated* *only* by the *defeater* (the *no* *presumption* of the *atheism*), and the *naturalistic* *epistemology* *is* *false* (the *no* *universal* *evidentialism*).

**The *structure*** (the *Plantinga* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *belief* *can* be *properly* *basic*} \ (\text{the *no* *inferential* *support*}). \\
2. & \text{The *proper* *basicality* *requires* the *warrant*} \ (\text{the *sensus* *divinitatis*}; \text{the *no* *mere* *fideism*}). \\
3. & \text{The *warrant* is *defeated* *only* by the *defeater*} \ (\text{the *no* *presumption*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *belief* is *rational* *without* the *argument*} \ (\text{the *Plantinga* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *evidentialism* is *false*} \ (\text{the *no* *universal* *evidentialism*}), \ \text{and the *faith* is the *knowledge*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere} \ \text{opinion}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *defends* the *rationality* of the *faith* (the *no* *mere* *argument*), and it *fits* the *practice* of the *believer* (the *no* *inference*), and it *grounds* the *faith* in the *warrant* (the *no* *mere* *fideism*), and it *connects* the *religion* to the *epistemology* (Ch. 8, §2; Ch. 7, §4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Great* *Pumpkin* *objection*** (Plantinga 1983, §1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *parity* *licenses* the *any* *belief* (the *no* *distinction*), and the *proper* *basicality* *has* no *content* (the *no* *criterion*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Plantinga 2000, ch. 1). The *sensus* *divinitatis* *is* the *proper* *function* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and the *Great* *Pumpkin* *has* the *no* *faculty* (the *no* *parity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *warrant* in the *faculty*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *no* *mere* *parity*), and *contested* (the *no* *independent* *evidence* for the *faculty*).

**(ii) The *no* *defeater* *objection***. The *defeaters* *are* *present* (the *evil*, the *hiddenness*, the *diversity*; §3–4), and the *belief* *is* *defeated* (the *no* *warrant*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *defeaters* are *contested* (the *no* *decisive*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *warrant* in the *no* *decisive* *defeat*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *robust* *warrant*).

The *fideism* *captures* the *existential* *risk* (the *no* *mere* *proposition*), the *James* *captures* the *genuine* *option* (the *no* *evidentialism*), the *Pascal* *captures* the *prudential* *structure* (the *no* *mere* *prudence*), and the *Reformed* *epistemology* *captures* the *warrant* (the *no* *mere* *argument*).

### 6.2 Verificationism and falsificationism

**The *thesis*** (Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, ch. 6; Flew, “Theology and Falsification,” 1950, in *New Essays in Philosophical Theology*; the *no* *meaning*). The *religious* *statements* *have* *no* *verification* (the *no* *observation*, the *no* *test*), and the *no* *verification* *makes* them *meaningless* (the *no* *cognitive* *content*), and the *religious* *assertion* *survives* *every* *evidence* (the *no* *falsifier*), and the *survival* *shows* the *no* *assertion* (the *Flew* *insight*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}

\text{The *falsification*:} & \text{The *assertion*} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *falsifier*} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *no* *falsifier*} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *no* *assertion*}.
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument*** (Flew 1950, §1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}

\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *religious* *assertion* *dies* the *death* of a *thousand* *qualifications*} \ (\text{the *Flew* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *no* *content*} \ (\text{the *no* *assertion*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *challenges* *raise* the *meaning* *question* (the *no* *mere* *assumption*), and they *fit* the *verificationist* *framework* (the *no* *mere* *theology*), and they *ground* the *debate* in the *language* (the *no* *mere* *metaphysics*), and they *connect* the *religion* to the *philosophy* of *language* (Ch. 31–34).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *self*-*refutation* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *verification* *principle* *is* *not* *itself* *verifiable* (the *no* *meaning* by its own standard), and the *challenge* *dissolves* (the *no* *consistency*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *principle* *is* the *proposal* (the *no* *dogma*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *principle* in the *practice* (the *no* *mere* *self*-*refutation*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *emotivism* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *meaning*).

**(ii) The *no* *falsification* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Hick, “Theology and Verification,” 1960, *Theology Today* 17). The *eschatological* *verification* *is* *possible* (the *afterlife*, the *final* *revelation*), and the *no* *falsifier* *in* this *life* *does* not *entail* the *no* *meaning* (the *no* *conclusion*), and the *reply* *succeeds*.

**The *reply*.** The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Hick* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *verification* *in* the *life*).

### 6.3 Analogy and the via negativa

**The *thesis*** (Aquinas, *ST*, I, q. 13; Maimonides, *Guide*, I.50–60; Scotus, *Ordinatio*, I, dist. 3; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *univocity*). The *religious* *language* *is* *analogical* (the *no* *univocal*, the *no* *equivocal*), and the *analogy* *affirms* the *perfection* (the *goodness*, the *wisdom*) *and* *denies* the *mode* (the *no* *creaturely*), and the *via* *negativa* *removes* the *inadequate* *predicates* (the *no* *anthropomorphism*), and the *language* *reaches* the *God* *through* the *imperfection* of the *creature* (the *no* *univocal*).

**The *structure*** (the *Thomistic* *analogy*).

$$
\text{The *predicate} \ P \ \text{in the *creature*} \ \sim \ \text{the *perfection*} \ \text{in the *God*} \ (\text{the *no* *univocal*}, \ \text{the *no* *equivocal*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Aquinas, *ST*, I, q. 13, a. 5).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *univocal* *language} \ \text{applies to the *God* and the *creature*} \ \text{in the *same* *sense*} \ (\text{the *no* *transcendence*}). \\
2. & \text{The *equivocal* *language} \ \text{applies in the *different* *sense*} \ (\text{the *no* *knowledge*}). \\
3. & \text{The *analogical* *language} \ \text{affirms the *perfection* and *denies* the *mode*} \ (\text{the *no* *anthropomorphism*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *analogy* is the *condition* of the *religious* *language*} \ (\text{the *Aquinas* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *via* *negativa* is the *discipline*} \ (\text{the *no* *idol*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *transcendence* (the *no* *anthropomorphism*), and it *avoids* the *agnosticism* (the *no* *equivocity*), and it *grounds* the *language* in the *creation* (the *no* *mere* *convention*), and it *connects* the *theology* to the *metaphysics* (the *analogy* *of* *being*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *content* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *analogy* *is* *not* *the* *univocal* (the *no* *content*), and the *no* *content* *makes* the *language* *empty* (the *no* *meaning*), and the *reply* *fails*.

## 7. Miracles and Testimony

### 7.1 The concept of miracle

**The *thesis*** (Hume, “Of Miracles,” 1748, §X; Lewis, *Miracles*, 1947, ch. 1; Swinburne, *The Concept of Miracle*, 1970, ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *miracle* *is* the *violation* of the *natural* *law* (the *no* *mere* *wonder*, the *no* *mere* *improbable* *event*), and the *violation* *requires* the *law* (the *no* *mere* *ignorance* of the *nature*), and the *miracle* *is* the *sign* (the *no* *mere* *anomaly*), and the *concept* *is* *contested* (the *no* *mere* *definition*).

**The *distinctions***.

| The *concept* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *violation*** | The *exception* to the *natural* *law* | The *no* *law* *at* the *moment* |
| **The *sign*** | The *event* with the *religious* *meaning* | The *no* *exception* |
| **The *improbable*** | The *unlikely* *event* (the *no* *exception*) | The *no* *miracle* |
| **The *supernatural*** | The *divine* *action* (the *no* *mere* *nature*) | The *no* *observation* |

**The *significance***. The *concept* *determines* the *epistemology* (the *violation* *requires* the *strong* *evidence*, the *sign* *requires* the *interpretation*), and the *concept* *connects* the *religion* to the *philosophy* of *science* (the *laws*; Ch. 39, §1–2), and the *concept* *raises* the *testimony* *question* (§7.2–7.4).

### 7.2 Hume's argument

**The *thesis*** (Hume, “Of Miracles,” 1748, §X; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *credible* *miracle*). The *miracle* *is* the *violation* of the *natural* *law* (the *no* *mere* *wonder*), and the *natural* *law* *rests* on the *uniform* *experience* (the *firm* *and* *unalterable* *evidence*), and the *testimony* is *never* *equal* to the *uniform* *experience* (the *no* *balance*), and the *miracle* *is* *never* *credible* (the *no* *reasonable* *belief*).

**The *structure*** (the *Hume* *form*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *miracle* is the *violation* of the *natural* *law*} \ (\text{the *definition*}). \\
2. & \text{The *natural* *law* *rests* on the *uniform* *experience*} \ (\text{the *firm* *and* *unalterable* *evidence*}). \\
3. & \text{The *testimony* is *never* *equal* to the *uniform* *experience*} \ (\text{the *no* *balance*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *miracle* is *never* *credible*} \ (\text{the *Hume* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *belief* is the *superstition*} \ (\text{the *no* *evidence*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *captures* the *improbability* (the *no* *mere* *credulity*), and it *fits* the *method* of the *testimony* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*; Ch. 11, §1), and it *grounds* the *skepticism* in the *evidence* (the *no* *mere* *doubt*), and it *connects* the *miracle* to the *probability* (Ch. 10, §4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *uniform* *experience* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Earman, *Hume’s Abject Failure*, 2000, ch. 2; Swinburne 1970, ch. 1). The *uniform* *experience* is *not* the *universal* (the *no* *mere* *uniformity*), and the *testimony* *can* *outweigh* the *mere* *uniformity* (the *no* *balance*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *testimony* *outweighs* *only* *where* the *evidence* is *strong* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *argument* in the *evidence* (the *no* *mere* *uniformity*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *decisive* *argument*).

**(ii) The *Bayesian* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Earman 2000, ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *Humean* *argument* *conflates* the *prior* with the *likelihood* (the *no* *mere* *balance*), and the *testimony* *can* *raise* the *probability* (the *no* *mere* *uniformity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *testimony* *raises* the *probability* *only* *if* the *testifier* is *reliable* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *argument* in the *reliability*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *no* *decisive* *refutation*), and *contested* (the *no* *reasonable* *belief*).

### 7.3 The Bayesian analysis

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Earman 2000, ch. 2; Swinburne, *The Existence of God*, 1979, ch. 13; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *miracle* *is* the *improbable* *event* (the *low* *prior*), and the *evidence* *raises* the *probability* (the *likelihood*), and the *testimony* *transmits* the *evidence* (the *reliability*), and the *belief* *is* *rational* *where* the *posterior* *exceeds* the *prior* (the *no* *mere* *prior*).

**The *structure*** (the *Bayes* *form*).

$$
P(M \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid M) \, P(M)}{P(E \mid M) \, P(M) + P(E \mid \neg M) \, P(\neg M)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *prior* of the *miracle* *is* *low*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere* *credulity*}). \\
2. & \text{The *likelihood* of the *testimony* *is* *high*} \ (\text{the *reliability* of the *testifier*}). \\
3. & \text{The *posterior* *exceeds* the *prior*} \ (\text{the *evidence* *raises* the *probability*}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *belief* is *rational* *where* the *testimony* is *strong*} \ (\text{the *Earman* *insight*}), \ \text{and the *Humean* *argument* *fails*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere} \ \text{balance*}), \ \text{and the *prior* is the *key*} \ (\text{the *no* *mere} \ \text{testimony*}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *structure* of the *evidence* (the *no* *mere* *balance*), and it *fits* the *Bayesian* *epistemology* (Ch. 10, §4), and it *grounds* the *rationality* in the *reliability* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and it *connects* the *miracle* to the *testimony* (Ch. 11, §1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *reliability* *objection***. The *reliability* *of* the *testifier* *is* *not* *established* (the *no* *independent* *evidence*), and the *posterior* *does* not *exceed* the *prior* (the *no* *revision*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reliability* *is* *established* by the *track* *record* (the *no* *mere* *assertion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *reliability* in the *track* *record*. The *reply* is *defensible*, and *contested* (the *no* *track* *record* *of* the *miracles*).

**(ii) The *no* *prior* *objection***. The *prior* of the *miracle* *is* *not* *assignable* (the *no* *neutral* *prior*), and the *analysis* *is* *circular* (the *no* *independence*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *prior* *is* *derived* from the *natural* *law* (the *no* *arbitrary*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *prior* in the *law*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *single* *prior*).
