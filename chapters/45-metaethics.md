# Chapter 44 — Metaethics: The Nature of Moral Judgment

> “I am surpriz'd to find, that instead of the usual copulations of propositions, *is*, and *is not*, I meet with no proposition that is not connected with an *ought*, or an *ought not*. … For as this *ought*, or *ought not*, expresses some new relation or affirmation, 'tis necessary that it shou'd be observ'd and explain'd; and at the same time that a reason should be given, for what seems altogether inconceivable, how this new relation can be a deduction from others, which are entirely different from it.”
> — David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–40), III.i.1

> “If there were objective values, then they would be entities or qualities or relations of a very strange sort, utterly different from anything else in the universe.”
> — J. L. Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* (1977), §1

> “Good is good, and that is the end of the matter.”
> — G. E. Moore, *Principia Ethica* (1903), §6, the conclusion the open-question argument is meant to force

---

Part VI examined the *sciences* — the domains in which the *naturalistic* *program* is *most* at *home*, and in which its *limits* were *revealed*: the *teleology* of biology (Ch. 42, §2), the *abstract* objects of mathematics (Ch. 43, §1), the *values* in the science itself (Ch. 39, §6). Part VII turns to the domain in which the *limits* become the *subject*: **value**.

This chapter is **metaethics** — the *study* of the *nature* of *moral* *judgment*, not the *study* of *which* moral judgments are *correct* (that is the *normative* *ethics* of Ch. 45, the *practical* *reason* of Ch. 46, the *political* *philosophy* of Ch. 47, and the *applied* *ethics* of Ch. 51). The *distinction* is the *distinction* between the *logic* and the *content* of the moral *discourse*, and it is the *distinction* the chapter *develops*.

The chapter has a *peculiar* status in the book, because it is the *place* where the *whole* book *converges*. The *semantics* of Chs. 31–32 asks whether moral sentences have *truth conditions*; the *metaphysics* of Chs. 14–22 asks whether there are *moral* *facts*; the *epistemology* of Chs. 7–13 asks how we could *know* them; the *philosophy* of *mind* of Chs. 24–30 asks whether moral judgments are *beliefs* or *attitudes*; and the *philosophy* of *science* of Ch. 39 asks whether the *debunking* of moral belief *undermines* its *justification*. The chapter's thesis is that the *convergence* is the *point*: metaethics is the *test* of the *whole* *naturalistic* *program*, and the *program* *survives* — not in the *reductive* form but in the *qualified* form, and the *qualification* is the *subject* of the chapter.

The chapter proceeds in seven movements. §1 examines the *fact/value* *gap* and the *naturalistic* *fallacy*. §2 examines *moral* *realism*. §3 examines the *anti*-*realist* *alternatives* (error theory, expressivism, quasi-realism). §4 examines *constructivism*. §5 examines *moral* *disagreement* and the *companions* in *guilt*. §6 examines *moral* *epistemology*. §7 examines *thick* *ethical* *concepts* and the *entanglement* of *fact* and *value*. §8 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. The Fact/Value Gap

### 1.1 The two gaps

The *phrase* “the *fact/value* gap” *names* two *distinct* *theses*, and the *conflation* of the *two* has *generated* a *century* of *confusion*.

**The *logical* *gap***. No *set* of *purely* *descriptive* *premises* *entails* an *essentially* *normative* *conclusion*.

**The *metaphysical* *gap***. *Normative* *facts* (if they *exist*) are *not* *identical* to *descriptive* *facts*; they are a *distinct* *kind*.

**The *distinction*.** The *logical* *gap* is a *thesis* about the *relation* between *kinds* of *proposition*; the *metaphysical* *gap* is a *thesis* about the *relation* between *kinds* of *fact*. The *first* is *compatible* with the *denial* of the *second*: one *can* hold that no *descriptive* *premises* *entail* a *normative* *conclusion*, and *still* hold that *normative* *facts* are *natural* *facts* (the *naturalistic* *realism*; §2.3). The *first* is also *compatible* with the *denial* that there are any *normative* *facts* at all (the *error* *theory*; §3.1).

**The *table***.

| The *thesis* | The *form* | The *commitment* |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical gap** | No descriptive premises entail a normative conclusion | The *logic* (the *is*/*ought* *distinction*) |
| **Metaphysical gap** | Normative facts ≠ descriptive facts | The *ontology* (the *autonomy* of the *normative*) |
| **Semantic gap** | Normative predicates do not mean descriptive predicates | The *semantics* (the *non*-*reducibility* of the *meaning*) |
| **Motivational gap** | Descriptive beliefs alone cannot motivate action | The *psychology* (the *Humean* *theory*; Ch. 28, §2.2) |

**The *significance*.** The *four* *theses* are *logically* *independent*: one can *accept* the *logical* gap and *deny* the *metaphysical* gap (the *Cornell* *realism*; §2.3), *accept* the *metaphysical* gap and *deny* the *semantic* gap (the *non*-*naturalism*; §2.4), *deny* all *four* (the *reductive* *naturalism*), and so on. The *standard* *metaethical* *position* is *defined* by the *combination* of the *theses* it *accepts*.

**The *connection* to Ch. 2, §6.3.** The *logical* gap was *presented* in Ch. 2 as an *argument* (the *no*-*ought*-*from*-*is* *thesis*), and the *chapter* *concluded* that the *gap* is *not* a *refutation* of *moral* *realism* (it is a *constraint* on the *form* of the *realist* *theory*). The *present* *chapter* *develops* the *constraint* and the *theory*.

### 1.2 Hume's guillotine

**The *passage*** (*Treatise*, III.i.1, quoted in the epigraph). Hume *observes* that the *moral* *writers* *move* *imperceptibly* from *is* to *ought*, and he *demands* that the *transition* be *explained*.

**The *argument*** (the *standard* *reconstruction*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Every *valid* *argument* *preserves* the *kind* of its *premises* (the *logical* *principle*).} \\
2. & \text{A *descriptive* *premise* is of a *different* *kind* from a *normative* *conclusion* (the *semantic* *premise*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{No *descriptive* *premises* *entail* a *normative* *conclusion* (the *no*-*ought*-*from*-*is* *thesis*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *logical* *form***. Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the *set* of *purely* *descriptive* *propositions*, and let $O$ be an *essentially* *normative* *proposition*. Then:

$$
\forall \Gamma \subseteq \mathcal{D}, \ \Gamma \nvDash O
$$

**The *significance*.** The *thesis* is a *logical* *constraint*: the *normative* *conclusion* *requires* at least *one* *normative* *premise*. The *constraint* is *widely* *accepted* (the *debate* is about the *scope* and the *exceptions*; §1.4), and it is *not* a *refutation* of *moral* *realism* (the *realist* can *accept* the *constraint* and *argue* that the *normative* *premise* is *justified*).

**The *four* *interpretations* *of* the *gap*** (Ch. 2, §6.3, *developed*).

| The *interpretation* | The *content* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical** | The descriptive premises do not entail the normative conclusion | *Widely* *accepted* |
| **Epistemic** | The descriptive evidence cannot *justify* the normative belief | *Much* *stronger*; *probably* *false* (the *moral* *perception*; §6.2) |
| **Motivational** | The descriptive beliefs cannot *motivate* the action | *Humean*; *contested* (Ch. 28, §2.2) |
| **Semantic** | The normative predicates do not *mean* the descriptive predicates | *Widely* *accepted*; *does* *not* *settle* the *metaphysics* |
| **Metaphysical** | There are *no* *normative* *facts* | **Does not follow from any of the above** |

**The *final* *row* is the *crucial* *one*.** The *logical* *gap* is *often* *cited* as though it *refuted* the *moral* *realism*. It does *not*. It *constrains* the *form* of the *realist* *theory* (the *realist* must *hold* that the *normative* *facts* are *not* *identical* to the *descriptive* *facts*, and must *explain* the *relation*), and it *leaves* the *metaphysics* *open*. The *confusion* of the *logical* *thesis* with the *metaphysical* *thesis* is *the* *commonest* *error* in *metaethics*, and the *discipline* of *separating* the *two* is a *paradigm* of the *method* of Ch. 2.

### 1.3 Moore's open-question argument

**The *argument*** (Moore, *Principia Ethica*, 1903, §§5–15; the *naturalistic* *fallacy*).

**The *target***. The *naturalistic* *definition* of *good*: $G = N$, where $N$ is a *natural* *property* (the *pleasure*, the *desire*, the *evolutionary* *fitness*).

**The *test***. For *any* *proposed* *definition* $G = N$, consider the *question*:

$$
\text{“I agree that } x \text{ is } N; \text{ but is } x \text{ good?”}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If } G = N, \text{ then the question "} x \text{ is } N, \text{ but is } x \text{ good?" is *closed* (the answer is settled by the *meaning*).} \\
2. & \text{The question is *open* (the *competent* *speaker* does *not* *settle* it by the *meaning* alone).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore G \neq N \text{ (the *naturalist* *definition* *fails*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion***. The *good* is *not* *definable* in *natural* *terms*; it is a *simple*, *non*-*natural* *property* (the *non*-*naturalism*; §2.4).

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *captures* the *intuition* that the *moral* *question* is *not* *closed* by the *natural* *facts* (the *is*/*ought* *intuition*), and it *avoids* the *reductive* *definitions* (the *good* is not *the pleasant*, not *the desired*), and it *fits* the *ordinary* *practice* (the *moral* *disagreement* about the *definition* of *good* is *real*).

**The *objections*** (the *standard* *critique*; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 3, §1.2).

**(i) The *‘*proves* *too* *much*’* *objection*. The *open* *question* *test* *rejects* *every* *informative* *analysis*. Consider the *definition* “knowledge = justified true belief” (Ch. 7, §1); the *question* “I agree that $x$ is JTB, but is $x$ *knowledge*?” was *open* (and *Gettier* *showed* that the *openness* *tracked* the *falsity* of the *definition*, but the *openness* *also* *occurred* for the *correct* *definitions*). The *openness* *tracks* the *informativity* of the *analysis*, not its *correctness*. (The *reply*: Moore’s *argument* *applies* to *simple* *properties* (the *good*, the *yellow*), not to *complex* ones (the *knowledge*). The *reply* *restricts* the *scope* of the *argument*, and it is *contested*.)

**(ii) The *‘*no* *non*-*natural* *property*’* *objection*. The *argument* *presupposes* that the *good* is a *property* (the *non*-*natural* *property*), and the *presupposition* is *precisely* what the *anti*-*realist* *denies*, and the *argument* *begs* the *question*.

**The *reply*** (Moore 1903; the *contemporary* *non*-*naturalism*; Parfit, *On What Matters*, 2011, Vol. II). The *good* *is* a *property* (the *non*-*natural* *insight*), and the *reply* *offers* the *positive* *account* (§2.4). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*Moral* *Twin* *Earth*’* *objection* (Horgan & Timmons, “Troubles for New Wave Moral Semantics,” 1992; Ch. 25, §4). The *externalist* *semantics* *suggests* that the *moral* *terms* *refer* to the *natural* *properties* that the *community* *tracks* (the *naturalistic* *realism*), and the *open* *question* *argument* is *a* *argument* *against* the *internalist* *semantics*, not against the *naturalistic* *realism*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *Twin* *Earth* *case* *supports* the *non*-*naturalism* (both communities refer to the *same* *non*-*natural* *property*), and the *reply* *reframes* the *argument*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.4 The naturalistic fallacy

**The *thesis*** (Moore 1903, §10; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *naturalistic* *fallacy* is the *error* of *identifying* the *good* with a *natural* *property*, and it is *distinct* from the *is*/*ought* *gap*.

**The *distinction***.

| The *fallacy* | The *content* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Naturalistic fallacy (Moore)** | Defining the *good* as a *natural* *property* | “Good = pleasure” |
| **Is/ought gap (Hume)** | Deriving an *ought* from an *is* | “It is pleasant, therefore one ought to do it” |
| **Definist fallacy** | Defining a *moral* *term* by a *non*-*moral* *term* | “Right = maximizes utility” |

**The *argument* *for* the *distinction***. The *naturalistic* *fallacy* is a *semantic* *thesis* (the *good* is *not* *definable* in *natural* *terms*), and the *is*/*ought* *gap* is a *logical* *thesis* (no *descriptive* *premises* *entail* a *normative* *conclusion*), and the *two* are *independent* (one can *accept* one and *deny* the other).

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *clarifies* the *metaethical* *landscape*: the *naturalistic* *realism* (the *Cornell* *realism*; §2.3) *denies* the *naturalistic* *fallacy* (the *good* *is* a *natural* *property*) and *accepts* the *is*/*ought* *gap* (the *normative* *conclusion* *requires* a *normative* *premise*); the *non*-*naturalism* (§2.4) *accepts* the *naturalistic* *fallacy* and the *metaphysical* *gap*.

**The *application***. The *distinction* is the *key* to the *famous* *debate* between *Moore* and the *naturalists*: the *naturalists* (the *contemporary* *Cornell* *realism*) *argue* that Moore’s *argument* *confuses* the *semantic* *question* (the *meaning* of *good*) with the *metaphysical* *question* (the *nature* of *good*), and the *confusion* is the *naturalistic* *fallacy* *itself* (the *naturalist* *fallacy* in the *reverse* *direction*).

### 1.5 The thick concepts

**The *thesis*** (Williams, *Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy*, 1985, Ch. 7; Putnam, *The Collapse of the Fact/Value Dichotomy*, 2002; Ch. 13, §5.4; Ch. 44, §7). The *thick* *ethical* *concepts* (the *cruel*, the *courageous*, the *generous*, the *brutal*, the *lewd*) are *both* *descriptive* and *evaluative*, and their *existence* *pressures* the *fact/value* *dichotomy*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *thick* *concepts* are *descriptive* (they *describe* the *situation*) and *evaluative* (they *evaluate* it).} \\
2. & \text{The *description* *entails* the *evaluation* (the *“*cruel*”* *is* *wrong* *by* *definition*).} \\
3. & \text{The *entailment* *is* *not* *defeasible* (the *concept* *cannot* be *applied* *without* the *evaluation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *fact/value* *dichotomy* *cannot* *accommodate* the *thick* *concepts* (the *entanglement* *thesis*; Ch. 44, §7), and the *dichotomy* is *not* *sharp* (the *no*-*sharp*-*line* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *argument* *is* the *pressure* *point* of the *fact/value* *dichotomy*, and it is *developed* in §7, and it is the *bridge* between the *metaethics* and the *normative* *ethics* (Ch. 45).

**The *connection* to the *logical* *gap*.** The *thick* *concepts* *do* *not* *refute* the *logical* *gap* (the *evaluation* is *contained* in the *description*, and the *entailment* is *analytic*, and the *conclusion* is *not* a *new* *kind* of *proposition*), and they *do* *refute* the *sharp* *semantic* *gap* (the *description* and the *evaluation* are *entangled*, and the *dichotomy* is *false*), and the *distinction* is *the* *subtle* *point* the *chapter* *develops*.

### 1.6 The state of the fact/value debate

**The *assessment***. The *logical* *gap* is *widely* *accepted*, and the *metaphysical* *gap* is *contested*, and the *thick* *concepts* *complicate* the *semantic* *gap*, and the *distinction* between the *logical* and the *metaphysical* *is* the *key*.

| The *thesis* | The *status* | The *challenge* |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical gap** | The *widely* *accepted* | The *no* *exception* (the *thick* *concepts*) |
| **Metaphysical gap** | The *contested* | The *Cornell* *realism* |
| **Semantic gap** | The *contested* | The *thick* *concepts* |
| **Motivational gap** | The *Humean*; *contested* | The *besire* (Ch. 28, §2.2) |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *acceptance* of the *logical* *gap* (the *normative* *conclusion* *requires* the *normative* *premise*) *combined* with a *rejection* of the *sharp* *metaphysical* *gap* (the *normative* *facts* can be *natural*, and the *thick* *concepts* *show* the *entanglement*) and a *rejection* of the *sharp* *semantic* *gap* (the *thick* *concepts* *are* *entangled*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *entanglement* *does* not *settle* the *metaphysical* *question* (the *is*/*ought* *gap* *remains*).

## 2. Moral Realism

### 2.1 The thesis

**The *view***. There are *mind*-*independent* *moral* *facts* (the *truths* about the *good*, the *right*, the *obligatory*), and the *moral* *judgments* *express* the *propositions* about them, and some of the *propositions* are *true*.

**The *three* *components*** (Ch. 40, §5.1, *adapted*).

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic** | The moral judgments express the *propositions* (the *cognitivism*) | The *default* |
| **Metaphysical** | Some of the *propositions* are *true* (the *realism*) | The *contested* |
| **Epistemic** | We *know* some of the *truths* (the *epistemic* *realism*) | The *contested* |

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *fact* is | The *access* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-naturalism** | A *sui* *generis* *normative* *property* | The *intuition* (the *a* *priori*; Ch. 36, §7.1) | Moore; Parfit; Scanlon; Dancy |
| **Cornell realism** | A *natural* *property* (the *homeostatic* *cluster*) | The *empirical* *investigation* (the *science*) | Sturgeon; Boyd; Railton |
| **Robust realism** | The *normative* *facts* (not *reducible*, not *natural*) | The *intuition* (the *non*-*natural* *access*) | Shafer-Landau; Enoch |
| **Constructivism** | The *facts* *constituted* by the *procedure* | The *procedure* (the *reason*) | Korsgaard; Scanlon; §4 |

**The *significance*.** The *varieties* *divide* on the *nature* of the *moral* *fact* and the *access*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *theory* of the *properties* (Ch. 15) and the *theory* of *truth* (Ch. 32).

### 2.2 The arguments for realism

**The *arguments***.

**(i) The *phenomenological* *argument***. The *moral* *experience* *presents* the *values* as *objective* (the *revulsion* at *cruelty* *presents* the *cruelty* as *wrong*, not as a *mere* *preference*), and the *presentation* is *evidence* for the *realism* (Ch. 30, §2.4).

**(ii) The *truth*-*aptness* *argument***. The *moral* *sentences* have the *truth* *conditions* (they are *assertible*, the *debate* *is* *genuine*, the *logical* *inferences* are *valid*), and the *truth*-*aptness* *supports* the *cognitivism* (Ch. 32, §5; §3.2).

**(iii) The *deliberative* *indispensability* *argument*** (Enoch, *Taking Morality Seriously*, 2011, Ch. 3). The *moral* *facts* are *indispensable* to the *practical* *deliberation* (the *deliberation* *requires* the *normative* *facts*, and the *deliberation* *is* *justified*), and the *indispensability* *supports* the *realism* (the *IBE*; Ch. 10, §6).

**(iv) The *moral* *progress* *argument***. The *moral* *history* *shows* the *progress* (the *abolition* of *slavery*, the *recognition* of the *women*’s *rights*), and the *progress* *is* *best* *explained* by the *realism* (the *discovery* of the *moral* *facts*), and the *anti*-*realism* *cannot* *explain* the *progress* (the *change* of the *attitudes* is *not* the *progress*).

**(v) The *companions* in *guilt* *argument*** (§5.3). The *argument* *against* the *moral* *realism* (the *disagreement*, the *queerness*) *applies* *equally* to the *other* *domains* (the *mathematics*, the *modality*, the *epistemic* *normativity*), and the *other* *domains* are *not* *refuted*, and the *argument* is *too* *strong* (§5).

**The *significance*.** The *arguments* *are* *convergent* (the *phenomenology*, the *semantics*, the *deliberation*, the *history*, the *companions*), and the *convergence* *supports* the *realism* (the *cumulative* *case*), and the *arguments* *are* *not* *decisive* (the *objections*; §2.5).

### 2.3 Cornell realism

**The *thesis*** (Sturgeon, “Moral Explanations,” 1985; Boyd, “How to Be a Moral Realist,” 1988; Railton, “Moral Realism,” 1986). The *moral* *properties* are *natural* *properties* (the *homeostatic* *property* *clusters*; Ch. 39, §3.2), and the *moral* *facts* are *natural* *facts*, and the *moral* *knowledge* is *empirical* (the *science* of the *morality*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral property} \ M \ = \ \text{a *homeostatic* *cluster* of the *natural* *properties*} \ (N_1, \ldots, N_k)
$$

**The *argument*** (the *moral* *explanation*; Sturgeon 1985, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *properties* *explain* the *non*-*moral* *phenomena* (the *slavery* *explains* the *misery*, the *cruelty* *explains* the *suffering*).} \\
2. & \text{The *explanatory* *power* *is* the *mark* of the *real* *property* (the *IBE*; Ch. 10, §6).} \\
3. & \text{The *explanation* *requires* the *moral* *property* (the *moral* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *properties* *are* *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and they are *natural* (the *Cornell* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *avoids* the *queerness* *objection* (the *moral* *properties* are *natural*, and the *natural* *properties* are *not* *queer*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *knowledge* in the *empirical* *investigation*, and it *fits* the *naturalism* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *part* of the *natural* *world*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection* (Harman, “Ethics and Observation,” 1977; Ch. 40, §4.3). The *moral* *properties* *do* not *explain* the *observations* (the *observation* of the *suffering* is *explained* by the *physical* *facts*, and the *moral* *property* *adds* *nothing* to the *explanation*), and the *non*-*natural* *properties* are *not* *needed* for the *explanation* (the *“*no* *explanatory* *role”* *objection*).

**The *reply*** (Sturgeon 1985, §3; Boyd 1988, §4). The *moral* *properties* *do* *explain* the *phenomena* (the *slavery* *explains* the *misery* *in* *virtue* of the *moral* *property*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *moral* *facts* (the *causal* *role*; the *moral* *facts* are *causally* *efficacious*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *causal* *role* of the *moral* *properties* is *the* *question*; Ch. 23, §5).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *identity*’* *objection*. The *moral* *properties* *cannot* be *identical* to the *natural* *properties* (the *is*/*ought* *gap*; §1.2), and the *identity* *fails* (the *normative* *is* not the *descriptive*).

**The *reply*** (Boyd 1988, §5). The *identity* is *a* *posteriori* (the *moral* *property* is *the* *natural* *property* *that* *plays* the *moral* *role*; the *water*/*H₂O* *analogy*; Ch. 9, §3.3), and the *reply* *grounds* the *identity* in the *science* (the *natural* *property* is *discovered*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *moral* *property* *has* the *normative* *force* that the *natural* *property* *lacks*).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *normativity*’* *objection*. The *Cornell* *realism* *reduces* the *normativity* to the *natural* *facts* (the *moral* *property* is the *natural* *property*), and the *normativity* is *lost* (the *ought* *is* not the *is*), and the *reduction* *fails* (the *is*/*ought* *gap*; §1.2).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *normativity* is *grounded* in the *natural* *facts* (the *natural* *property* *grounds* the *norm*; the *grounding* *is* not the *identity*; Ch. 16, §3.3), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *grounding* from the *identity*, and the *reply* is *defensible* (the *grounding* *account*).

### 2.4 Non-naturalism

**The *thesis*** (Moore 1903; Parfit, *On What Matters*, 2011, Vol. II; Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, 1998; Dancy, *Moral Reasons*, 1993; Shafer-Landau, *Moral Realism*, 2003). The *moral* *properties* are *sui* *generis* *normative* *properties* (they are *not* *natural*, *not* *reducible* to the *natural*), and the *moral* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*; Ch. 36, §7.1).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral property} \ M \ = \ \text{a *sui* *generis* *normative* *property*} \ (\text{not reducible to the natural})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *open* *question* *argument* *shows* that the *good* is *not* *definable* in the *natural* *terms*} \ (\S 1.3). \\
2. & \text{The *normative* *force* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *descriptive* *facts*} \ (\S 1.2). \\
3. & \text{The *irreducibility* *requires* a *distinct* *kind* of *property*} \ (\text{the *non*-*natural* *insight*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *properties* are *non*-*natural* (the *non*-*naturalism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *irreducibility* of the *normativity* (the *is*/*ought* *gap*), and it *fits* the *phenomenology* of the *moral* *experience* (the *objective* *demand*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *knowledge* in the *a* *priori* (the *intuition*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *queerness* *objection* (Mackie 1977, §1; §3.1). The *non*-*natural* *properties* are *queer* (they are *not* *in* *space* and *time*, *not* *causally* *efficacious*, *not* *observable*, and yet *they* *command* *action*), and the *queerness* *is* the *strongest* *argument* *against* the *view*.

**The *reply*** (Parfit 2011, Vol. II, Ch. 25; Enoch 2011, Ch. 7). The *queerness* is *not* a *defect* (the *normative* *facts* are *a* *distinct* *kind* of *fact*, and the *distinctness* *is* the *point*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *normative* *facts* in the *distinct* *kind*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *naturalistic* *prejudice* is the *strongest* *obstacle*).

**(ii) The *access* *objection* (Mackie 1977, §1; the *Benacerraf* *analogue*; Ch. 43, §1.2). The *non*-*natural* *properties* are *epistemically* *inaccessible* (the *intuition* is *mysterious*), and the *access* *is* *not* *explained*, and the *knowledge* *is* *not* *justified*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *intuitionism*; Audi, *The Good in the Right*, 2004, Ch. 1). The *intuition* is the *a* *priori* *grasp* (the *self*-*evidence*, the *reflective* *equilibrium*; Ch. 3, §4), and the *reply* *grounds* the *access* in the *a* *priori*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *causal* *impotence* *objection* (Ch. 23, §5; Ch. 18, §4). The *non*-*natural* *properties* are *causally* *inert* (they are *not* *in* the *causal* *order*), and the *moral* *properties* *cannot* *explain* the *action* (the *moral* *judgment* *cannot* *cause* the *action*), and the *moral* *practice* *requires* the *causal* *efficacy* (the *moral* *judgment* *motivates*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *judgment* is a *belief* (the *cognitive* *insight*), and the *belief* *causes* the *action* *through* the *desire* (the *Humean* *theory*; Ch. 28, §2.2), and the *reply* *grounds* the *efficacy* in the *belief*-*desire* *pair*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *non*-*natural* *property* *explains* the *belief*, and the *explanation* *is* *mysterious*).

### 2.5 The state of the realism

**The *assessment***. The *realism* is *defensible* in the *non*-*naturalist* and the *Cornell* forms, and the *objections* are *serious*, and *neither* the *realism* *nor* the *anti*-*realism* is *decisive*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-naturalism** | The irreducibility | The queerness; the access |
| **Cornell realism** | The naturalism; the explanation | The identity; the normativity |
| **Robust realism** | The autonomy | The queerness |
| **Constructivism** | The procedure | The authority (Ch. 44, §4) |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *non*-*naturalism*: the *moral* *properties* are *sui* *generis* (the *irreducibility* *insight*), and the *access* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition* *insight*), and the *realism* *survives* the *objections* at a *cost* (the *queerness*, the *access*), and the *Cornell* *realism* is the *most* *promising* *naturalist* *alternative*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *identity* and the *normativity* are *contested*.

## 3. Anti-Realism

### 3.1 The error theory

**The *thesis*** (Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong*, 1977, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; Joyce, *The Myth of Morality*, 2001). The *moral* *judgments* *presuppose* the *objective* *moral* *facts*, and the *presupposition* *is* *false*, and the *judgments* are *systematically* *false*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral judgment} \ j \ \text{presupposes} \ \exists x\, M(x) \ \wedge \ \neg\exists x\, M(x) \ \Rightarrow \ j \ \text{is *false*}
$$

**The *two* *arguments***.

**(i) The *argument* *from* *queerness*** (Mackie 1977, §1). The *objective* *moral* *values* *would* be *queer* (the *metaphysical* *queerness*: the *values* *have* the *objective* *prescriptive* *force* that *nothing* *in* the *natural* *world* *has*), and the *queerness* *is* *incredible*, and the *values* *do* *not* *exist*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *objective* *moral* *values* *have* the *metaphysical* *queerness* (the *objective* *prescriptivity*, the *categorical* *force*).} \\
2. & \text{The *queerness* is *not* *found* *in* the *natural* *world* (the *science*, the *experience*).} \\
3. & \text{The *queerness* is *incredible* (the *parsimony*; Ch. 14, §4).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *objective* *moral* *values* *do* *not* *exist* (the *error* *theory*).}
\end{array}
$$

**(ii) The *argument* *from* *relativity*** (Mackie 1977, §1; Ch. 44, §5). The *moral* *disagreement* is *radical* and *pervasive* (the *different* *cultures*, the *different* *moral* *codes*), and the *disagreement* *is* *best* *explained* by the *non*-*existence* of the *moral* *facts* (the *anti*-*realist* *insight*), and the *realism* *cannot* *explain* the *disagreement* (the *realist* *weakness*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *disagreement* is *radical* (the *fundamental* *moral* *differences*).} \\
2. & \text{The *disagreement* is *best* *explained* by the *no*-*moral*-*facts* *hypothesis* (the *anti*-*realist* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *realism* *cannot* *explain* the *disagreement* (the *realist* *weakness*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *facts* *do* *not* *exist* (the *anti*-*realist* *conclusion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *error* *theory* *preserves* the *cognitivism* (the *moral* *judgments* are *propositions*) and the *truth*-*aptness* (the *judgments* are *true* or *false*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *values* *are* *not* *posited*), and it *fits* the *naturalism*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *self*-*undermining* *objection* (Ch. 32, §6.2). The *error* *theory* *claims* to be *true* (the *theory* *is* the *correct* *metaethical* *account*), and the *claim* *presupposes* the *epistemic* *norms* (the *truth*, the *justification*), and the *norms* *are* *normative*, and the *error* *theory* *denies* the *normative* *facts*, and the *theory* *is* *self*-*undermining*.

**The *reply*** (Joyce 2001, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 32, §6.2). The *epistemic* *norms* are *different* from the *moral* *facts* (the *error* *theory* is *about* the *moral* *facts*, not the *epistemic* *norms*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *epistemic* *norms* in the *epistemic* *practice*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *epistemic* *norms* are *normative*, and the *error* *theory* *cannot* *consistently* *accept* the *specifically* *normative* *norms* without *explaining* the *exception*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *presupposition*’* *objection*. The *moral* *judgments* *do* not *presuppose* the *objective* *values* (the *expressivist* *account*; §3.2), and the *error* *theory* *misdescribes* the *moral* *discourse*.

**The *reply*** (Mackie 1977, §1; the *cognitivist* *insight*). The *moral* *discourse* *has* the *objectivist* *grammar* (the *“*X* *is* *wrong*”* *looks* like the *“*X* *is* *red*”*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *presupposition* in the *grammar*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *expressivist* *can* *explain* the *grammar* *without* the *presupposition*; the *quasi*-*realist* *project*; §3.3).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *queerness*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Parfit 2011; Enoch 2011). The *queerness* *is* *not* *a* *defect* (the *normative* *facts* are *a* *distinct* *kind* of *fact*), and the *argument* *begs* the *question* (the *naturalistic* *prejudice*).

**The *reply*** (Mackie 1977; the *naturalistic* *insight*). The *queerness* *is* the *reason* *to* *reject* the *non*-*natural* *facts* (the *science* *does* *not* *posit* them), and the *reply* *grounds* the *rejection* in the *naturalism*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.2 Expressivism

**The *thesis*** (Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, Ch. 6; Stevenson, “The Emotive Meaning of Ethical Terms,” 1937; Blackburn, *Spreading the Word*, 1984, Ch. 6; *Essays in Quasi-Realism*, 1993; Gibbard, *Wise Choices, Apt Feelings*, 1990; *Thinking How to Live*, 2003). The *moral* *sentences* *express* the *attitudes* (the *approval*, the *disapproval*, the *plan*, the *norm*), not the *propositions*, and the *moral* *judgments* are *not* *truth*-*apt* in the *ordinary* *sense*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{“Torture is wrong”} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{“Boo torture!”} \quad \text{(the *emotivist* *analysis*; Ayer)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *judgments* *motivate* the *action* (the *internalism*; Ch. 28, §2.3).} \\
2. & \text{The *beliefs* *alone* *do* *not* *motivate* (the *Humean* *theory*; Ch. 28, §2.2).} \\
3. & \text{The *moral* *judgments* *must* be the *conative* *states* (the *desires*, the *attitudes*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *judgments* *express* the *attitudes* (the *expressivism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *motivational* *force* of the *moral* *judgment* (the *internalism*), and it *fits* the *naturalism* (the *attitudes* are *natural* *states*; Ch. 25, §2), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *not* *posited*).

**The *Frege*-*Geach* *problem*** (Geach, “Assertion,” 1965; the *central* *objection*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* of a *sentence* is *constant* across the *contexts* (the *compositionality*; Ch. 31, §4).} \\
2. & \text{The *sentence* “torture is wrong” *occurs* in the *unasserted* *contexts* (“If torture is wrong, then …”; “Either torture is wrong or …”; “S believes that torture is wrong”).} \\
3. & \text{The *expressivist* *analysis* (“Boo torture!”) *does* not *fit* the *unasserted* *contexts* (the “Boo” is a *force*, not a *content*, and the *force* is *not* *composable*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *expressivism* is *false* (the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *problem* is the *most* *serious* *objection* to the *expressivism*, and it *generated* the *contemporary* *debates* (the *minimalism* about *truth*, the *hybrid* *expressivism*, the *quasi*-*realism*; §3.3).

**The *replies***.

**(i) The *minimalist* *reply*** (Blackburn 1984, Ch. 6; 1993, Ch. 3). The *truth* is *minimal* (the *T*-*schema*; Ch. 32, §5.3), and the *moral* *sentences* are *truth*-*apt* in the *minimal* *sense* (the *disquotational* *truth*), and the *expressivism* is *compatible* with the *minimal* *truth*, and the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem* *dissolves* (the *content* is the *minimal* *truth* *condition, and the *force* is the *expression* of the *attitude*).

**The *objection***. The *minimalism* is *too* *thin* (it *does* not *explain* the *moral* *disagreement*; the *realist* *content* is *lost*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *higher*-*order* *attitude* *reply*** (Blackburn 1984, Ch. 6; Gibbard 1990, Ch. 3). The *moral* *sentence* *expresses* the *higher*-*order* *attitude* (the *attitude* *toward* the *attitudes*; the *plan* *about* the *plans*), and the *higher*-*order* *attitude* *composes* (the *sentences* *express* the *attitudes* *toward* the *attitudes*), and the *composition* *is* *possible*.

**The *objection***. The *higher*-*order* *attitudes* are *not* the *ordinary* *moral* *attitudes* (the *ordinary* *moral* *judgment* is *first*-*order*, not *higher*-*order*), and the *reply* *changes* the *subject*, and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *hybrid* *expressivism*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Schroeder, *Being For*, 2008; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *sentence* *expresses* the *belief* *plus* the *attitude* (the *hybrid* *state*), and the *belief* *provides* the *content* (the *truth* *condition*), and the *attitude* *provides* the *motivation*, and the *hybrid* *composes*.

**The *objection***. The *hybrid* is *not* the *pure* *expressivism* (the *belief* *is* the *cognitivist* *element*), and the *reply* *concedes* the *cognitivism*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *hybrid* *is* a *form* of *cognitivism* with the *conative* *addition*; Ch. 25, §3.3).

### 3.3 Quasi-realism

**The *thesis*** (Blackburn, *Essays in Quasi-Realism*, 1993; *Ruling Passions*, 1998). The *expressivism* *can* *earn* the *right* to the *realist* *discourse* (the *truth*, the *objectivity*, the *moral* *facts*), and the *quasi*-*realism* *is* the *project* of *deriving* the *realist* *appearance* from the *expressivist* *basis*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Expressivism} \ \xrightarrow{\text{the quasi-realist project}} \ \text{Realist discourse (the truth, the objectivity)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *expressivism* *grounds* the *moral* *judgments* in the *attitudes* (the *non*-*cognitivism*).} \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *discourse* *uses* the *realist* *notions* (the *truth*, the *objectivity*, the *facts*).} \\
3. & \text{The *realist* *notions* *can* be *earned* (the *minimal* *truth*, the *attitude* *to* the *attitudes*, the *quasi*-*realist* *construction*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *quasi*-*realism* *vindicates* the *realist* *discourse* *on* the *expressivist* *basis* (the *quasi*-*realist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *project* *preserves* the *naturalism* (the *attitudes*) and the *motivational* *force* (the *internalism*), and it *explains* the *realist* *appearance* (the *moral* *discourse*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *facts* are *constructed*, not *discovered*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *truth*’* *objection*. The *quasi*-*realism* *does* not *provide* the *robust* *truth* (the *minimal* *truth* is *not* the *realist* *truth*), and the *moral* *discourse* *appears* *realist* only *in* a *thin* *sense*, and the *robust* *realism* is *lost*.

**The *reply*** (Blackburn 1998, Ch. 6). The *thin* *truth* is *sufficient* for the *practice* (the *moral* *debate* requires only the *minimal* *truth*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *practice*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objectivity*’* *objection*. The *quasi*-*realism* *makes* the *moral* *truth* *depend* on the *attitudes* (the *subjectivism*), and the *objectivity* *is* *lost* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *relative* to the *attitudes*).

**The *reply*** (Blackburn 1998, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *objectivity* is *constituted* by the *intersubjective* *attitudes* (the *shared* *norms*, the *critical* *practice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *practice*, and the *reply* is *contested* (the *no*-*fact* *of* *the* *matter*; Ch. 13, §3).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *simplicity*’* *objection*. The *quasi*-*realism* *is* a *complex* *project* (the *derivation* of the *realist* *appearance* from the *expressivist* *basis*), and the *complexity* *is* the *cost*, and the *realism* is *simpler* (the *positing* of the *moral* *facts*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *complexity* is *justified* by the *naturalism* (the *queerness* *is* *avoided*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *complexity* in the *explanatory* *power*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.4 The state of the anti-realism

**The *assessment***. The *anti*-*realism* is *defensible* in the *error* *theory* and the *expressivism* forms, and the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem* is the *central* *challenge*, and the *quasi*-*realism* is the *most* *developed* *response*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Error theory (Mackie)** | The naturalism; the cognitivism | The self-undermining |
| **Expressivism (Ayer)** | The motivation | The Frege-Geach |
| **Quasi-realism (Blackburn)** | The realist appearance | The truth; the objectivity |
| **Hybrid expressivism** | The composition | The cognitivism |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *anti*-*realism*: the *error* *theory* is the *most* *defensible* *cognitivist* form (the *naturalism* *insight*), and the *expressivism* is the *most* *defensible* *non*-*cognitivist* form (the *motivation* *insight*), and the *quasi*-*realism* is the *most* *developed* *reconciliation*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem* and the *self*-*undermining* *objection* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Constructivism

### 4.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Korsgaard, *The Sources of Normativity*, 1996; *Self-Constitution*, 2009; Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, 1971; Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, 1998; Street, “Constructivism about Reasons,” 2008). The *moral* *facts* are *constituted* by the *procedure* (the *rational* *agency*, the *contract*, the *practical* *reason*), and the *moral* *truths* are *not* *discovered* *independently* of the *procedure*, and the *procedure* *grounds* the *normative* *authority*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral fact} \ = \ \text{the *outcome* of the *constructive* *procedure*}
$$

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *procedure* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Kantian** | The *categorical* *imperative* (the *universalizability*) | Korsgaard |
| **Contractualist** | The *agreement* among the *reasonable* (the *veil* of *ignorance*) | Rawls; Scanlon |
| **Humean** | The *practical* *standpoint* (the *desires*, the *interests*) | Street |

### 4.2 Kantian constructivism

**The *thesis*** (Korsgaard 1996, Ch. 3; 2009, Ch. 2). The *normative* *authority* is *grounded* in the *reflective* *structure* of *agency* (the *agent* *must* *act* *on the *maxims*, and the *maxims* *must* be *universalizable*), and the *categorical* *imperative* is *constitutive* of *agency* (the *agent* *cannot* *act* *without* the *norm*, and the *norm* *is* the *source* of the *obligation*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Maxim} \ m \ \text{is *permissible*} \ \text{iff} \ \text{the *universalization* of } m \text{ is *consistent* (the *CI* *procedure*)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agency* *requires* the *self}-*legislation* (the *autonomy*; the *Kantian* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *self}-*legislation* *requires* the *universalizable* *maxims* (the *CI* *procedure*).} \\
3. & \text{The *universalizability* *constitutes* the *normative* *authority* (the *constitutive* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *facts* are *constituted* by the *agency* (the *Kantian* *constructivism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *agency* (the *autonomy*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *normative* *facts* are *constituted* by the *agency*, not *discovered* *independently*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *moral* *reasoning* is the *universalization*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *universalization*’* *objection* (Hegel, *Philosophy of Right*, 1821, §135; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *CI* *procedure* is *empty* (it *does* not *yield* the *determinate* *norms*; the *different* *maxims* *can* be *universalized*), and the *procedure* *fails* to *ground* the *morality*.

**The *reply*** (Korsgaard 1996, Ch. 4; O’Neill, *Constructions of Reason*, 1989, Ch. 5). The *CI* *yields* the *norms* when *combined* with the *empirical* *premises* (the *contradiction* in the *conception*, the *contradiction* in the *will*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *norms* in the *procedure* + the *facts*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *empty* *formalism* *objection*; Ch. 45, §2.2).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *authority*’* *objection*. The *constitutive* *norms* of *agency* are *not* the *moral* *norms* (the *agent* *can* *act* *on* the *non*-*moral* *norms*), and the *inference* from the *agency* to the *morality* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Korsgaard 2009, Ch. 1). The *agency* *requires* the *unified* *will* (the *self*-*constitution*), and the *unified* *will* *requires* the *moral* *norms*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *morality* in the *agency*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *agent*’* *objection* (Ch. 27, §5). The *agent* *is* *not* the *unified* *self* (the *divided* *mind*, the *unconscious*, the *situation*), and the *constitutive* *account* *presupposes* the *unified* *agency*, and the *presupposition* is *contested*.

### 4.3 Contractualism

**The *thesis*** (Rawls 1971, §§3–4; Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5). The *moral* *facts* are *constituted* by the *agreement* among the *reasonable* (the *contract*), and the *norms* are *justified* if they *cannot* be *reasonably* *rejected* (the *Scanlonian* *formula*).

**The *structure*** (the *Scanlonian* *formula*).

$$
\text{Act} \ A \ \text{is *wrong*} \ \text{iff} \ A \ \text{is *forbidden* by the *principles* that *no one* could *reasonably* *reject* (the *contractualist* *test*)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *morality* *requires* the *justification* to the *others* (the *contractualist* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *justification* *requires* the *principles* that *no one* could *reasonably* *reject* (the *Scanlonian* *formula*).} \\
3. & \text{The *principles* *constitute* the *moral* *facts* (the *constructivist* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *facts* are *constituted* by the *agreement* (the *contractualism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *morality* in the *justification* (the *relational* *insight*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *facts* are *constituted* by the *agreement*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *moral* *reasoning* is the *justification* *to* the *others*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *agreement*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *agreement* *requires* the *parties* (the *contractors*), and the *parties* *have* the *interests*, and the *interests* *shape* the *agreement*, and the *agreement* is *not* *neutral* (the *power* *relations*; the *feminist* *critique*; Ch. 47, §2).

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5; Rawls 1971, §24). The *parties* are *idealized* (the *veil* of *ignorance*, the *reasonable*), and the *idealization* *removes* the *power* *relations*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *neutrality* in the *idealization*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *idealization* *is* *not* *neutral*; the *choice* of the *idealization* is *contested*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *determinacy*’* *objection*. The *reasonable* *rejection* is *not* *determinate* (the *different* *conceptions* of the *reasonable* *yield* the *different* *norms*), and the *procedure* *under*-*determines* the *morality*.

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5). The *reasonable* is *defined* by the *justification* (the *pro tanto* *reasons*, the *burden* of the *judgment*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *determinacy* in the *structure* of the *reasons*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *authority*’* *objection*. The *agreement* *grounds* the *coordination* (the *convention*), not the *moral* *authority* (the *obligation*), and the *normative* *force* is *not* *grounded* in the *agreement*.

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 4). The *moral* *authority* is *grounded* in the *value* of the *justification* (the *“*we*”* *as* the *source* of the *reasons*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *authority* in the *relational* *value*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.4 Humean constructivism

**The *thesis*** (Street, “Constructivism about Reasons,” 2008; “Coming to Terms with Contingency,” 2009). The *normative* *reasons* are *constructed* from the *desires* and the *interests* (the *Humean* *standpoint*), and the *moral* *facts* are *relative* to the *practical* *standpoint* (the *agent*’s *desires*), and the *normative* *force* is *grounded* in the *desires*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Reason} \ r \ \text{for} \ A \ \text{to} \ \phi \ = \ \text{r *serves* } A\text{’s *desires*} \ (\text{the *Humean* *standpoint*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *reasons* *serve* the *desires* (the *Humean* *insight*; Ch. 28, §2.3).} \\
2. & \text{The *desires* *vary* across the *agents* (the *subjectivity*).} \\
3. & \text{The *reasons* *vary* with the *desires* (the *relativity*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *reasons* are *constructed* from the *desires* (the *Humean* *constructivism*), and the *morality* is *constructed* from the *desires* (the *constructivist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *reasons* in the *desires* (the *naturalism*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *facts* are *constructed* from the *natural* *states*), and it *fits* the *Humean* *theory* of *motivation* (Ch. 28, §2.2).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *morality*’* *objection*. The *Humean* *constructivism* *grounds* the *reasons* in the *desires*, and the *desires* *can* be *selfish*, and the *morality* is *not* *grounded* (the *amoralist* *can* *have* the *no* *moral* *reasons*), and the *account* *collapses* into the *subjectivism* (Ch. 28, §2.4).

**The *reply*** (Street 2009, §4). The *desires* are *constrained* by the *social* *practice* (the *intersubjective* *desires*, the *moral* *education*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *morality* in the *social* *desires*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *authority* *objection*; the *relativism*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objectivity*’* *objection*. The *Humean* *constructivism* *makes* the *morality* *relative* to the *desires*, and the *objectivity* *is* *lost* (the *moral* *facts* are *relative* to the *agents*).

**The *reply*** (Street 2009, §6). The *objectivity* is *grounded* in the *shared* *desires* (the *human* *nature*, the *social* *practice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *shared* *desires*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.5 The source of normative authority

**The *question***. *Why* *be* *moral*? (The *“*why* be* moral*?”* *question*; Ch. 46, §3.2.)

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *ground* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Realism** | The *moral* *facts* (the *independent* *authority*) | The *queerness* |
| **Constructivism** | The *procedure* (the *agency*, the *agreement*) | The *neutrality*; the *relativity* |
| **Expressivism** | The *attitudes* (the *desire*, the *plan*) | The *Frege-Geach*; the *objectivity* |
| **Error theory** | The *no* *authority* (the *authority* is *illusory*) | The *self*-*undermining* |

**The *argument***. The *normative* *authority* is *grounded* in the *procedure* (the *constructivist* *insight*), and the *procedure* *is* *constitutive* of the *agency* (the *Kantian* *insight*), and the *agency* *is* *the* *source* of the *authority* (the *Kantian* *insight*), and the *argument* *is* *the* *most* *defensible* *account* *of* the *authority* in the *constructivist* *tradition*.

**The *significance***. The *argument* *connects* the *constructivism* to the *agency* (Ch. 27) and to the *practical* *reason* (Ch. 28), and it *grounds* the *authority* in the *practice*, and it *leaves* the *question* of the *neutrality* (the *procedure*’s *content*) *contested*.

### 4.6 The state of the constructivism

**The *assessment***. The *constructivism* is *influential*, and the *most* *defensible* *form* is the *Kantian* *constructivism* (the *agency* *grounds* the *authority*), and the *Humean* *constructivism* is *promising* and *contested*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Kantian** | The authority | The empty formalism |
| **Contractualist** | The justification | The no-agreement |
| **Humean** | The naturalism | The no-morality |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Kantian* *constructivism*: the *normative* *authority* is *grounded* in the *agency* (the *Kantian* *insight*), and the *procedure* *yields* the *norms* *only* with the *empirical* *premises* (the *no*-*empty* *insight*), and the *procedure* *is* *constrained* by the *intersubjective* *practice* (the *contractualist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *neutrality* and the *determinacy* are *contested*.

## 5. Moral Disagreement and the Companions in Guilt

### 5.1 The disagreement data

**The *data***. |---|---|---|

| **Women’s rights** | The *equality* *vs.* the *subordination* | The *resolved* (the *contemporary* *consensus*) |

### 5.2 The argument from disagreement

**The *argument*** (the *anti*-*realist* *reconstruction*; Mackie 1977, §1; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *debunking* *argument*; Ch. 3, §5.2).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *disagreement* is *pervasive*, *durable*, and *deep* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *disagreement* is *best* *explained* by the *no*-*moral*-*facts* *hypothesis* (the *anti*-*realist* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *realism* *cannot* *explain* the *disagreement* (the *realist* *weakness*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *facts* *do* *not* *exist* (the *anti*-*realist* *conclusion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *grounds* the *anti*-*realism* in the *empirical* *data* (the *disagreement*), and it *fits* the *naturalism* (the *moral* *beliefs* *are* *explained* by the *social* *facts*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *facts* are *not* *posited*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*convergence*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *moral* *progress*). The *moral* *history* *shows* the *convergence* (the *abolition* of *slavery*, the *universal* *human* *rights*, the *women*’s *rights*), and the *convergence* *is* *best* *explained* by the *realism* (the *discovery* of the *moral* *facts*), and the *disagreement* is *transient* (the *no*-*convergence* *objection* *fails*).

**The *reply*** (Mackie 1977, §1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *convergence* is *not* *real* (the *contemporary* *moral* *disagreement* is *still* *pervasive*; the *different* *societies* *have* the *different* *codes*), and the *convergence* *is* *explained* by the *social* *facts* (the *globalization*, the *power*), and the *reply* *contests* the *data*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection* (the *epistemic* *account* of the *disagreement*; Ch. 11, §3; §6.5). The *disagreement* is *explained* by the *complexity* of the *moral* *questions* (the *hard* *cases*, the *uncertainty*), not by the *no*-*facts*, and the *realism* *can* *explain* the *disagreement* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *hard* *to* *know*), and the *argument* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *debunking* *argument*; Joyce 2001, Ch. 6; Street 2006, §6). The *complexity* *explanation* *presupposes* the *truth*-*tracking* *process*, and the *debunking* *argument* *shows* that the *process* is *not* *truth*-*tracking* (the *evolution*, the *socialization*), and the *argument* *survives*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *depth*’* *objection*. The *moral* *disagreement* is *not* *deeper* than the *disagreement* in the *other* *domains* (the *philosophy*, the *mathematics*, the *science*), and the *moral* *disagreement* *does* not *support* the *anti*-*realism* *more* than the *other* *disagreements* support the *anti*-*realism* in the *other* *domains*.

**The *reply***. The *moral* *disagreement* *is* *deeper* (it *involves* the *fundamental* *values*, not *only* the *facts*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *depth* in the *empirical* *data*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 The companions in guilt

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Mackie 1977, §1; the *companions-in-guilt* *argument*; the *contemporary* *literature*; Cuneo, *The Normative Web*, 2007; Rowland, *The Normative Web*, 2016; Ch. 43, §1.4). The *arguments* against the *moral* *realism* (the *queerness*, the *disagreement*, the *debunking*) *apply* *equally* to the *other* *normative* *domains* (the *epistemic* *normativity*, the *mathematical* *realism*, the *modal* *realism*), and the *other* *domains* are *not* *refuted* (the *science* *presupposes* the *epistemic* *norms*, the *mathematics* *presupposes* the *mathematical* *facts*, the *modality* *presupposes* the *modal* *facts*), and the *argument* is *too* *strong* (it *refutes* the *domains* *that* the *anti*-*realist* *accepts*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *argument* } A \text{ (the *queerness*, the *disagreement*) *applies* to the *moral* *realism*} \ (\text{the *anti}-*realist* \text{ *premise}).} \\
2. & \text{The *argument* } A \text{ *applies* *equally* to the *epistemic* *normativity*, the *mathematical* *realism*, the *modal* *realism*} \ (\text{the *structural* *insight}).} \\
3. & \text{The *epistemic* *normativity*, the *mathematical* *realism*, the *modal* *realism* are *not* *refuted*} \ (\text{the *realist* \text{ *premise}).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *argument* } A \text{ *fails* (it is too *strong*), and the *moral* *realism* is *not* *refuted* by the *argument* } A \ (\text{the *companions} \text{ *insight}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *shows* that the *anti*-*realist* *argument* is *too* *strong* (it *refutes* the *domains* that the *anti*-*realist* *accepts*), and it *supports* the *moral* *realism* (the *companions* *insight*), and it *fits* the *structural* *realism* (the *normative* *facts* are *analogous* to the *mathematical* *facts*; Ch. 43, §1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *companion*’* *objection*. The *moral* *realism* *is* *disanalogous* to the *mathematical* *realism* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *essentially* *motivating*, the *mathematical* *facts* are *not*, and the *queerness* *objection* *applies* to the *former* and *not* the *latter*), and the *argument* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Cuneo 2007, Ch. 4). The *epistemic* *normativity* *is* *essentially* *motivating* (the *epistemic* *reasons* *motivate* the *beliefs*), and the *mathematical* *facts* *also* *have* the *normative* *role* (the *mathematical* *reasons* *guide* the *proof*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *analogy* in the *normative* *role*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *refutation*’* *objection*. The *companions* in *guilt* *argument* *shows* that the *anti*-*realist* *argument* *fails*, and it *does* not *show* that the *moral* *realism* *is* *true* (the *argument* *is* *comparative*, and the *moral* *realism* *may* be *the* *least* *defensible* *realism*), and the *argument* *does* *not* *establish* the *realism*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *argument* is *comparative* (it *establishes* the *parity*), and the *parity* *is* *sufficient* (the *anti*-*realist* *cannot* *use* the *argument* *against* the *morality* *without* *using* it *against* the *domains* *that* *she* *accepts*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *parity* in the *argument*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *similarity*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *critique*; the *essays* in *Companions in Guilt* (ed. the contemporary literature)). The *domains* *are* *not* *relevantly* *similar* (the *mathematical* *disagreement* *is* *local*, and the *moral* *disagreement* *is* *global*, and the *debunking* *applies* to the *morality* and *not* the *mathematics*), and the *argument* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *similarity* *is* *the* *normative* *structure* (the *reasons*, the *norms*, the *practical* *deliberation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *similarity* in the *normative* *role*. The *reply* is *contested*.

## 6. Moral Epistemology

### 6.1 The moral intuition

**The *thesis*** (Ross, *The Right and the Good*, 1930, Ch. 2; Audi, *The Good in the Right*, 2004, Ch. 1; Shafer-Landau 2003, Ch. 10; Ch. 9, §4; Ch. 44, §2.4). The *moral* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *self*-*evidence*), and the *intuitions* *ground* the *fundamental* *moral* *principles*, and the *intuitions* are *fallible* (the *revision* *by* the *reflective* *equilibrium*; Ch. 3, §4).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral knowledge} \ = \ \text{the *self*-*evident* *intuition*} \ (\text{the *a* *priori* *grasp*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *fundamental* *moral* *principles* are *not* *derived* from the *empirical* *facts* (the *is*/*ought* *gap*; §1.2).} \\
2. & \text{The *principles* are *known* *a* *priori* (the *intuition*).} \\
3. & \text{The *a* *priori* *knowledge* *requires* the *intuition* (the *faculty*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *intuition* *grounds* the *principles* (the *intuitionism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *moral* *knowledge* in the *a* *priori* (the *intuition*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *moral* *reasoning* *appeals* to the *intuitions*), and it *avoids* the *empiricist* *reduction* (the *is*/*ought* *gap*).

**The *objections*** (Ch. 9, §6; Ch. 44, §5).

**(i) The *‘*no* *faculty*’* *objection*. The *intuition* *has* *no* *sensory* *organ* (the *intuition* is *a* *mystery*), and the *faculty* is *not* *independently* *identified*, and the *account* is *obscure*.

**The *reply*** (Audi 2004, Ch. 1). The *intuition* is *analogous* to the *perception* (it is the *a* *priori* *grasp*, and it is *identified* by its *role*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *intuition* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*disagreement*’* *objection* (Ch. 9, §6; Ch. 11, §3). The *moral* *intuitions* *disagree* (the *different* *cultures*, the *different* *traditions*), and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *claim* to *knowledge* (the *calibration* *problem*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *intuitionism*; Audi 2004, Ch. 6; the *reflective* *equilibrium*; Ch. 3, §4). The *intuitions* are *fallible* (the *disagreement* is *real*, and the *calibration* is *required*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *fallibility* in the *practice* (the *reflective* *equilibrium*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *intuitions* are the *default* *evidence*, *defeasible* by the *argument*).

**(iii) The *‘*debunking*’* *objection* (Ch. 3, §5; §5.2). The *moral* *intuitions* are *explained* by the *evolution* (the *non*-*truth*-*tracking* *process*), and the *explanation* *undermines* the *justification* (the *argument* from the *selection*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *companions* in *guilt*; §5.3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *debunking* *applies* to the *specific* *moral* *beliefs* (the *moral* *norms* that *were* *adaptive*), and it *does* not *apply* to the *fundamental* *moral* *principles* (the *moral* *truths* are *a* *priori*, and the *access* is the *rational* *insight*, not the *evolutionary* *process*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *two* *levels* (the *specific* *beliefs* vs. the *fundamental* *principles*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.2 The moral perception

**The *thesis*** (McDowell, “Virtue and Reason,” 1979; Blum, *Moral Perception and Particularity*, 1994; Audi 2013; Ch. 30, §2.4). The *moral* *properties* are *perceived* (the *cruelty* is *seen* in the *action*, the *generosity* in the *gesture*), and the *perception* is *not* the *inference* from the *non*-*moral* *facts*, and the *perception* *grounds* the *moral* *knowledge*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral knowledge} \ = \ \text{the *perception* of the *moral* *properties*} \ (\text{the *moral* *perception*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *properties* are *experienced* (the *cruelty*, the *kindness*; Ch. 30, §2).} \\
2. & \text{The *experience* is *perceptual* (the *non*-*inferential*, the *immediacy*; Ch. 5, §6.3).} \\
3. & \text{The *perception* *grounds* the *knowledge* (the *perceptual* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *knowledge* is the *perception* (the *moral* *perception* *insight*), and the *perception* *grounds* the *particular* *judgments* (the *particularist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *moral* *knowledge* in the *experience* (the *phenomenology*), and it *fits* the *particular* *judgments* (the *situation* *perception*), and it *avoids* the *abstract* *principles* (the *particularist* *insight*; Ch. 45, §5).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *property*’* *objection*. The *moral* *properties* are *not* *perceived* (the *perception* *presents* the *non*-*moral* *facts*, and the *moral* *judgment* is the *interpretation*), and the *perception* *does* not *ground* the *knowledge*.

**The *reply*** (McDowell 1979; Audi 2013, Ch. 2). The *perception* *presents* the *moral* *properties* (the *trained* *perception*, the *virtue* *perception*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *perception* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objectivity*’* *objection*. The *perception* is *shaped* by the *values* (the *theory*-*ladenness*; Ch. 44, §6.4), and the *objectivity* is *lost* (the *perception* *presents* the *values* *that* the *perceiver* *has*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *perception* is *fallible* (the *perceptual* *error*; the *moral* *blindness*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *calibration* (the *reflective* *equilibrium*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *access*’* *objection*. The *perception* is *not* *the* *a* *priori* *access* (the *intuition*), and the *perception* *requires* the *experience*, and the *experience* *is* *empirical*, and the *a* *priori* *is* *lost*.

**The *reply*** (Audi 2013, Ch. 3). The *perception* is *the* *a* *priori* *grasp* of the *particular* *moral* *reasons* (the *particular* *a* *priori*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *universal* *a* *priori* from the *particular*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The moral testimony

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Hills, “Moral Testimony and Moral Epistemology,” 2009; Ch. 11, §1; the *moral* *testimony* *debate*). The *moral* *knowledge* *can* be *transmitted* by the *testimony* (the *moral* *expert* *tells* us *what* *to* *do*), and the *testimony* *raises* the *question* of the *autonomy* (the *moral* *judgment* *requires* the *understanding*; Ch. 11, §2.4).

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *testimony* *is* *possible*** | The *moral* *knowledge* *can* be *acquired* by the *testimony* | Jones 1999; Sliwa 2012 |
| **The *testimony* *is* *limited*** | The *moral* *knowledge* *can* be *acquired*, but *not* the *moral* *understanding* | Hills 2009 |
| **The *testimony* *is* *impossible*** | The *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* | Wolff 1970; the *autonomy* *tradition* |

**The *argument* (the *autonomy* *argument***; Ch. 11, §2.4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *moral* *understanding* (the *grasp* of *why* the *action* is *wrong*).} \\
2. & \text{The *understanding* is *not* *transmitted* by the *testimony* (it *requires* the *achievement* of the *agent*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *knowledge* *cannot* be *acquired* *solely* from the *testimony* (the *autonomy* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *connects* the *moral* *epistemology* to the *testimony* (Ch. 11) and to the *autonomy* (Ch. 45, §3), and it *raises* the *question* of the *moral* *expertise* (the *no*-*expertise* *worry*; §6.5).

### 6.4 The debunking challenge

**The *thesis*** (Ch. 3, §5; Joyce, *The Evolution of Morality*, 2006; Joyce, *The Myth of Morality*, 2001, Ch. 6; Street, “A Darwinian Dilemma for Realist Theories of Value,” 2006). The *moral* *beliefs* are *explained* by the *evolution* (the *adaptive* *function*), and the *explanation* *is* *not* *truth*-*tracking* (the *evolution* *selected* the *moral* *beliefs* for their *social* *function*, not their *truth*), and the *explanation* *undermines* the *moral* *realism* (the *debunking* *argument*).

**The *argument*** (the *evolutionary* *debunking*; Street 2006, §4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *beliefs* are *explained* by the *evolutionary* *process* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *process* *is* *not* *truth*-*tracking* with respect to the *moral* *facts* (the *evolutionary* *insight*: the *selection* *favors* the *beliefs* that *promote* the *cooperation*, not the *beliefs* that *track* the *moral* *truth*).} \\
3. & \text{The *beliefs* that result from a *non*-*truth*-*tracking* *process* are *not* *justified* (the *epistemic* *premise*; Ch. 3, §5.2).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *realism* is *undermined* (the *debunking* *conclusion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *grounds* the *anti*-*realism* in the *empirical* *science* (the *evolution*), and it *avoids* the *queerness* (the *argument* *does* *not* *require* the *queerness*), and it *fits* the *naturalism*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *non*-*tracking*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *realist* *reply*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *evolutionary* *process* *is* *truth*-*tracking* *at* the *general* *level* (the *beliefs* about the *cooperation*, the *harm*, the *reciprocity* *are* *true*; the *moral* *truths* *are* *the* *norms* that *promote* the *flourishing*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *tracking* in the *naturalistic* *realism* (the *Cornell* *realism*; §2.3). The *reply* is *contested* (the *evolution* *does* not *track* the *non*-*natural* *facts*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *undermining*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *justification* *survives* the *debunking* (the *beliefs* *can* be *justified* *independently*; the *reflective* *equilibrium* *justifies* the *beliefs*, and the *evolution* *explains* the *origin*, not the *justification*), and the *argument* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Joyce 2006, Ch. 6; Street 2006, §6). The *independent* *justification* is *not* *available* (the *reflective* *equilibrium* *presupposes* the *intuitions* that the *evolution* *explains*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *undermining* in the *genetic* *fallacy* (the *origin* *matters* when the *process* is *not* *tracking*; Ch. 3, §5.2). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *companions* in *guilt* *objection* (§5.3). The *debunking* *applies* *equally* to the *epistemic* *norms* (the *evolution* *explains* the *epistemic* *beliefs*), and the *epistemic* *norms* are *not* *undermined*, and the *argument* is *too* *strong*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *epistemic* *norms* *are* *different*). The *epistemic* *norms* *are* *constitutive* of the *inquiry* (the *truth*-*tracking* *is* the *function* of the *epistemic* *beliefs*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *two*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.5 The moral expertise

**The *question***. Is there a *moral* *expertise*? (The *moral* *expert*, the *moral* *testimony*, the *moral* *education*.)

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *expertise* *is* *possible*** | The *moral* *expertise* *exists* (the *virtue*, the *judgment*, the *training*) | the *contemporary* *literature* (the *virtue* *tradition*) |
| **The *expertise* *is* *limited*** | The *moral* *expertise* *exists*, but it is *not* *transmissible* (the *understanding*; §6.3) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **The *expertise* *is* *impossible*** | The *moral* *judgment* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* (the *autonomy*) | the *autonomy* *tradition* |

**The *argument* (the *no*-*expertise* *worry***; Ch. 11, §6.5).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *disagreement* *persists* among the *experts* (the *philosophers*, the *theologians*), and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *expertise* (the *epistemic* *peer* *disagreement*; Ch. 11, §3).} \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* (the *autonomy*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *expertise* is *not* *a* *genuine* *expertise* (the *no*-*expertise* *insight*), and the *moral* *testimony* is *not* *authoritative* (the *autonomy* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *connects* the *moral* *epistemology* to the *disagreement* (Ch. 9, §6) and to the *autonomy* (Ch. 45, §3), and it *grounds* the *skepticism* *about* the *moral* *expertise*, and it *raises* the *question* of the *moral* *education* (the *transmission* of the *understanding*).

### 6.6 The state of the moral epistemology

**The *assessment***. The *moral* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *perception*), and the *testimony* is *limited* (the *autonomy*), and the *debunking* is *serious*, and the *expertise* is *contested*.

| The *source* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Intuition** | The a priori | The no-faculty; the disagreement |
| **Perception** | The particular | The no-property |
| **Testimony** | The transmission | The autonomy |
| **Debunking** | The science | The companions in guilt |
| **Expertise** | The practice | The disagreement |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *intuitionism*: the *moral* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition* *insight*), and the *intuitions* are *fallible* (the *disagreement* *insight*), and the *perception* *grounds* the *particular* *judgments* (the *perceptual* *insight*), and the *testimony* *transmits* the *knowledge* but *not* the *understanding* (the *autonomy* *insight*), and the *debunking* *argument* *is* *serious* but *not* *decisive* (the *debunking* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *disagreement* and the *debunking* *remain* *contested*.

## 7. Thick Ethical Concepts

### 7.1 The distinction

**The *thesis*** (Williams 1985, Ch. 7; Putnam 2002, Ch. 2; Ch. 13, §5.4; Ch. 44, §1.5). The *thick* *ethical* *concepts* (the *cruel*, the *courageous*, the *generous*, the *brutal*, the *lewd*, the *discreet*) are *both* *descriptive* and *evaluative*, and the *thin* *concepts* (the *good*, the *right*, the *obligatory*) are *purely* *evaluative*.

**The *distinction***.

| The *kind* | The *concept* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Thin** | The *purely* *evaluative* | The *good*, the *right*, the *ought* |
| **Thick** | The *descriptive* *plus* the *evaluative* | The *cruel*, the *courageous*, the *generous* |
| **Descriptive** | The *purely* *descriptive* | The *red*, the *square*, the *heavy* |

**The *significance***. The *thick* *concepts* *challenge* the *fact/value* *dichotomy* (the *evaluation* is *entangled* with the *description*), and they *challenge* the *metaethical* *distinction* (the *evaluative* *content* is *not* *purely* *prescriptive*), and they *connect* the *metaethics* to the *normative* *ethics* (Ch. 45) and to the *social* *theory* (Ch. 11, §4).

### 7.2 The entanglement

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *entanglement* *thesis*). The *thick* *concepts* *cannot* be *analyzed* into the *descriptive* *component* and the *evaluative* *component*, and the *concepts* *are* *essentially* *entangled* (the *description* *and* the *evaluation* are *inseparable*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *thick* *concept*} C \text{ *describes*} F \text{ and *evaluates*} E, \text{ and the *description* and the *evaluation* are *coextensive* (the *“*cruel*”* *is* the *action* *that* *is* *wrong* *in* the *specific* *way*).} \\
2. & \text{The *analysis* *requires* the *separation*} (C = F + E), \text{ but the *separation* *is* *not* *available*} (the *description* *of* F \text{ *without*} E \text{ *does* not *capture*} C; the *evaluation*} E \text{ *without*} F \text{ *is* the *thin* *concept*, not*} C). \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *thick* *concepts* *are* *entangled* (the *entanglement* *insight*), and the *analysis* *into* the *fact* *plus* the *value* *fails*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *thesis* *captures* the *phenomenology* of the *thick* *judgments* (the *“*that* *is* *cruel*”* *is* *not* *the* *description* *plus* *the* *attitude*, it is a *single* *judgment*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *moral* *reasoning* uses the *thick* *concepts*), and it *avoids* the *dichotomy* (the *fact*/*value* *dichotomy* is *too* *sharp*).

### 7.3 The “who’s to say” objection

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *relativist* *objection*). The *thick* *concepts* *vary* *across* the *cultures* (the *concept* of the *honor*, the *chastity*, the *modesty*), and the *variation* *suggests* that the *thick* *concepts* are *cultural* *constructions*, and the *objectivity* is *lost*.

**The *objection*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *thick* *concepts* *vary* across the *cultures* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *variation* *suggests* that the *concepts* are the *cultural* *constructions* (the *relativist* *insight*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *thick* *concepts* are *not* *objective* (the *relativist* *conclusion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *structural* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *entanglement* *insight*). The *evaluative* *structure* is *universal* (the *harm*, the *cooperation*, the *care*; the *evolutionary* *insight*), and the *content* *varies* *with* the *culture* (the *social* *construction*), and the *objectivity* is *grounded* in the *structure* (the *universal* *evaluative* *structure*), and the *variation* *does* not *undermine* the *structure*.

**The *reply*** *is* *defensible* (the *universal* *structure* *plus* the *variation* *of* *the* *content*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *variation*’* *response*. The *variation* is *less* than the *relativist* *claims* (the *deep* *moral* *structure* *is* *universal*; the *superficial* *variation* is *the* *application*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *universality* in the *deep* *structure*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *superficial* *variation* is *real*, and the *deep* *structure* is *hard* to *identify*).

**(iii) The *cultural* *response*. The *variation* is *explained* by the *culture* (the *socialization*, the *power*), and it *does* not *undermine* the *objectivity* (the *cultural* *variation* is *compatible* with the *objective* *norms*; the *moral* *progress* *corrects* the *cultural* *variation*).

**The *reply*** *is* *defensible* (the *moral* *progress* *insight*; §5.2).

### 7.4 The state of the thick concepts

**The *assessment***. The *thick* *concepts* are *real*, and the *entanglement* is *real*, and the *objectivity* is *contested*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Entanglement** | The phenomenology | The no-analysis |
| **Separation** | The analysis | The no-phenomenology |
| **No-variation** | The universal structure | The superficial variation |
| **Cultural** | The variation | The objectivity |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *entanglement*: the *thick* *concepts* are *entities* (the *description* *and* the *evaluation* *are* *entangled*) (the *entanglement* *insight*), and the *structure* is *universal* (the *universal* *insight*), and the *content* *varies* (the *cultural* *insight*), and the *objectivity* is *grounded* in the *structure* (the *realist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *variation* *is* *real* and the *structure* is *hard* to *identify*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *logical* *gap* is *real*** (the *descriptive* *premises* *do* not *entail* the *normative* *conclusion*), and the *metaphysical* *gap* is *not* *the* *logical* *gap* (the *distinction* is the *key*).
- **The *naturalistic* *fallacy*** is *distinct* from the *is*/*ought* *gap*, and the *open* *question* *argument* is *the* *most* *influential* *argument* *for* the *non*-*naturalism*, and it *faces* the *‘*proves* *too* *much*’* *objection*.
- **The *moral* *realism*** is *defensible* in the *non*-*naturalist* and the *Cornell* forms, and the *queerness* and the *access* *objections* are *serious*, and *neither* the *realism* *nor* the *anti*-*realism* is *decisive*.
- **The *error* *theory*** (Mackie) *preserves* the *cognitivism* and the *avoids* the *queerness*, and it *faces* the *self*-*undermining* *objection*.
- **The *expressivism*** (Ayer, Blackburn, Gibbard) *captures* the *motivational* *force*, and it *faces* the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem*, and the *quasi*-*realism* is the *most* *developed* *response*.
- **The *constructivism*** (Kant, Rawls, Scanlon, Street) *grounds* the *normative* *authority* in the *procedure*, and the *Kantian* *constructivism* is the *most* *defensible* form, and the *empty* *formalism* and the *neutrality* are *serious* *objections*.
- **The *disagreement*** is *real*, and the *debunking* *argument* is *serious*, and the *companions* in *guilt* *argument* is the *strongest* *defense* of the *realism*, and the *argument* is *not* *decisive*.
- **The *moral* *knowledge*** is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *perception*), and the *intuitions* are *fallible*, and the *testimony* is *limited* (the *autonomy*), and the *debunking* *argument* is *serious*.
- **The *thick* *concepts*** are *real* and *entangled*, and the *variation* is *real*, and the *objectivity* is *contested*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of the *moral* *facts*.** The *non*-*naturalism*, the *Cornell* *realism*, the *constructivism*.
- **The *semantics* of the *moral* *judgments*.** The *cognitivism*, the *expressivism*, the *quasi*-*realism*.
- **The *epistemology* of the *morality*.** The *intuition*, the *perception*, the *testimony*, the *expertise*.
- **The *disagreement* and the *debunking*.** The *companions* in *guilt*, the *evolutionary* *debunking*.
- **The *thick* *concepts*.** The *entanglement*, the *objectivity*.
- **The *source* of the *normative* *authority*.** The *agency*, the *agreement*, the *desires*.

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* between the *fact*/*value*, the *naturalistic* *fallacy*, the *thick*/*thin*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *Moral* *Twin* *Earth*, the *open* *question*, the *experience* *machine* (Ch. 48, §2), the *trolley* (Ch. 45, §2) — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *deontic* *logic*, the *preference* *structures*, the *Bayesian* *decision* *theory* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Genealogy and debunking** (§5 of Ch. 3) is the *central* *challenge* to the *moral* *knowledge*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: metaethics is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

## Summary

- **The *fact/value* *gap*** *names* the *logical* *gap* (the *no*-*ought*-*from*-*is* *thesis*) and the *metaphysical* *gap* (the *normative* *facts* ≠ the *descriptive* *facts*), and the *distinction* is the *key* to the *metaethics*.
- **The *naturalistic* *fallacy*** (Moore) is the *semantic* *thesis* (the *good* is *not* *definable* in the *natural* *terms*), and the *open* *question* *argument* is the *most* *influential* *argument* *for* the *non*-*naturalism*, and it *faces* the *‘*proves* *too* *much*’* *objection*.
- **The *thick* *ethical* *concepts*** (the *cruel*, the *courageous*) are *both* *descriptive* and *evaluative*, and they *challenge* the *sharp* *fact/value* *dichotomy*, and the *entanglement* *thesis* is the *most* *defensible* *account*.
- **The *moral* *realism*** (the *non*-*naturalism*, the *Cornell* *realism*, the *robust* *realism*) is *defensible*, and the *queerness* and the *access* *objections* are *serious*, and the *arguments* (the *phenomenology*, the *truth*-*aptness*, the *deliberative* *indispensability*, the *moral* *progress*, the *companions* in *guilt*) are *convergent* but *not* *decisive*.
- **The *anti*-*realism*** (the *error* *theory*, the *expressivism*, the *quasi*-*realism*) is *defensible*, and the *Frege*-*Geach* *problem* is the *central* *challenge* to the *expressivism*, and the *quasi*-*realism* is the *most* *developed* *response*.
- **The *constructivism*** (the *Kantian*, the *contractualist*, the *Humean*) *grounds* the *normative* *authority* in the *procedure*, and the *Kantian* *constructivism* is the *most* *defensible* form, and the *empty* *formalism* and the *neutrality* are *serious* *objections*.
- **The *moral* *disagreement*** is *real* (the *argument* from *relativity*), and the *debunking* *argument* is *serious* (the *evolutionary* *insight*), and the *companions* in *guilt* *argument* is the *strongest* *defense* of the *realism* (the *parity* *insight*), and the *arguments* are *not* *decisive*.
- **The *moral* *epistemology*** is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *perception*), and the *intuitions* are *fallible*, and the *testimony* is *limited* (the *autonomy*), and the *debunking* *argument* is *serious*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *non*-*naturalist* *realism* *combined* with a *qualified* *intuitionism* and a *qualified* *entanglement*: the *moral* *facts* are *sui* *generis* (the *irreducibility* *insight*), the *access* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition* *insight*), the *thick* *concepts* are *entangled* (the *entanglement* *insight*), and the *realism* *survives* the *objections* at a *cost* (the *queerness*, the *access*, the *disagreement*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *disagreement*, the *debunking*, and the *queerness* *remain* *unresolved*.

> *A closing note.* This chapter has followed the *metaethics* — the *fact/value* *gap*, the *naturalistic* *fallacy*, the *moral* *realism*, the *anti*-*realism*, the *constructivism*, the *disagreement*, the *debunking*, the *moral* *epistemology*, and the *thick* *concepts* — and it has arrived at a *conclusion* that is less a *theory* than a *diagnosis*: the *moral* *realism* is *defensible*, and the *anti*-*realism* is *defensible*, and the *arguments* are *convergent* and *contested*, and the *debate* is *unresolved* — not because the *philosophy* has *failed*, but because the *questions* are *hard*, and the *difficulty* is the *condition* of the *subject*.

> *And a bridge.* The next chapter turns to the *normative* *ethics* — the *theories* of the *good*, the *right*, and the *virtuous*. If the *metaethics* is the *semantics* and the *metaphysics* of the *moral* *judgment*, the *normative* *ethics* is the *content* of the *judgment*, and the *two* are *connected*: the *metaethical* *position* *constrains* the *normative* *theory* (the *expressivist* *cannot* *offer* the *realist* *foundations*, the *realist* *must* *defend* the *moral* *facts*, the *constructivist* *must* *specify* the *procedure*), and the **normative** *theory* *tests* the *metaethical* *position* (the *theory* must *yield* the *determinate* *norms*, and the *specific* *judgments* *test* the *principles*). The *next* *chapter* *examines* the *normative* *ethics*, and it *asks* the *question* that the *metaethics* *presupposes*: *what* *is* the *right* *action*, and *why*?
