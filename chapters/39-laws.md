# Chapter 39 — Laws, Capacities, and the Structure of Science

> “The law of gravitation is not a statement about what happens; it is a statement about what must happen, and the ‘must’ is the whole of the difference between a law and a coincidence.”
> — a formulation of the necessitarian intuition, after Armstrong

> “The laws of physics *lie*. They are statements about idealised systems, and no idealised system exists.”
> — Nancy Cartwright, *How the Laws of Physics Lie* (1983), the title, in a sentence

> “The special sciences do not need laws. They need mechanisms, and the mechanisms are local, contingent, and historically situated.”
> — the mechanist’s slogan, after Machamer, Darden, and Craver

---

Chapter 38 examined *explanation*, and it *presupposed* the *generalizations* that the *explanations* *use*: the **laws**. This chapter examines the *laws* — and the *concept* of law is the *anvil* on which the *philosophy* of *science* has been *forged*. It is also the *concept* that has been *most* *eroded* over the *last* *fifty* years: the *strict* *laws* of the *classical* *tradition* are *rare* outside *fundamental* *physics*, the *special* *sciences* operate with *hedged* *generalizations*, the *models* that *science* uses are *fictions*, and the *values* that *science* is supposed to *exclude* turn out to *shape* the *inquiry* at every *level*.

The *chapter’s* *thesis* is that **there is no such thing as *the* law of nature**. There are *three* *notions* — the *regularity*, the *necessitation*, the *disposition* — and each *captures* a *different* *aspect* of the *scientific* *practice*, and *no* *single* *notion* *captures* the *whole*. The *consequence* is that the *structure* of *science* is *not* a *hierarchy* grounded in a *fundamental* *law* but a *family* of *strategies* — *regularity*-*tracking*, *mechanism*-*describing*, *capacity*-*exploiting*, *model*-*building* — and the *pluralism* is *not* a *defect* but the *condition* of the *scientific* *success*.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 examines the *nature* of *laws* and the *three* *families*. §2 examines the *ceteris* *paribus* *problem* and the *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences*. §3 examines the *natural* *kinds* and the *projectibility*. §4 examines the *reduction* and the *emergence* in the *scientific* *practice*. §5 examines the *models*, the *idealization*, and the *fiction*. §6 examines the *values* in *science*. §7 examines the *interdisciplinarity* and the *constraints*. §8 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. The Nature of Laws

### 1.1 The three families

**The *question***. What *is* a *law* *of* *nature*?

**The *families***.

| The *family* | The *claim* | The *paradigm* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regularity** | The laws are the *regularities* (the *constant* *conjunctions*) that *belong* to the *best* *system* of the *theory* | The *ideal* *gas* *law*; the *Boyle* *law* | Hume; Mill; Ramsey; Lewis |
| **Necessitation** | The laws are *relations* of *necessitation* between *universals* (the *properties* *necessitate* the *regularity*) | The *gravitational* *law* (the *N*-relation) | Armstrong; Dretske; Tooley |
| **Disposition** | The laws are the *manifestations* of the *essences* of the *properties* (the *powers*; Ch. 18, §6) | The *charge* *law* (the *power* to *repel*) | Bird; Ellis; Mumford |

**The *significance***. The *three* *families* *capture* the *three* *intuitions* about the *laws*: the *laws* are *regular* (the *regularity* *intuition*), the *laws* are *necessary* (the *necessitation* *intuition*), the *laws* are *efficacious* (the *disposition* *intuition*). The *three* *intuitions* are *individually* *plausible*, and the *families* *disagree* about which *intuition* is *fundamental*.

### 1.2 The regularity account

**The *thesis***. The *laws* are the *generalizations* that are *true* and *part* of the *best* *system* of the *world* — the *system* that *balances* *simplicity* and *strength* (the *Ramsey*–*Lewis* *account*; Ch. 18, §7.1).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Law}(\phi) \quad \text{iff} \quad \phi \text{ is an axiom (or theorem) of the best system} \ \mathcal{B}
$$

**The *argument*** (Lewis 1973, 1986, 1994; Ramsey 1928; Mill 1843).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The laws are the generalizations that support the counterfactuals and the explanations.} \\
2. & \text{The support is explained by the systematic role of the generalization in the best system.} \\
3. & \text{The systematic role is constituted by the simplicity and the strength of the system.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The laws are the regularities of the best system (the regularity account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *necessity* (the *laws* are *regularities*, and the *regularities* are *not* *additional* *entities*), and it *fits* the *Humean* *supervenience* (the *laws* *supervene* on the *occurrent* *facts*), and it *explains* the *counterfactual* *support* (the *best* *system* *supports* the *counterfactuals* *by* the *systematic* *role*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*governance*’* *objection*** (Armstrong 1983; Maudlin 2007, Ch. 1). The *laws* *govern* the *events* (they *determine* what *happens*), and the *regularities* *do* not *govern* (they are *descriptions* of what *happens*), and the *account* *loses* the *governance*.

**The *reply*** (Lewis 1983, 1986; Loewer 1996). The *governance* is a *metaphor* (the *laws* are *descriptions* of the *patterns*, and the *patterns* are *all* there is), and the *reply* *dissolves* the *governance* *intuition*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *simplicity*’* *objection* (van Fraassen 1989, Ch. 3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *simplicity* is *not* *well*-*defined* (the *different* *languages* *yield* the *different* *simplest* *systems*), and the *account* *is* *language*-*dependent* (the *choice* of the *language* *determines* the *laws*), and the *dependence* *undermines* the *objectivity*.

**The *reply*** (Lewis 1983, 1994). The *simplicity* is *objective* *enough* (the *different* *languages* *yield* the *same* *laws* *if* the *languages* *are* *intertranslatable*), and the *reply* *restricts* the *languages* to the *natural* ones. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*uninstantiated* *laws*’* *objection* (Ch. 15, §4.4; Armstrong 1983, Ch. 5; Tooley 1977). The *laws* *can* *be* *uninstantiated* (the *law* “all $F$s are $G$” *holds* *even* *if* *no* $F$ *exists*), and the *regularity* *account* *cannot* *accommodate* the *uninstantiated* *laws* (the *regularity* is *vacuously* *true*, and the *laws* are *not* *vacuously* *true*). The *objection* *supports* the *necessitarian* *account*.

**The *reply*** (Lewis 1994). The *uninstantiated* *laws* are *not* *laws* (they are *consequences* of the *generalizations* *about* the *properties*, not the *regularities*), and the *reply* *restricts* the *laws* to the *instantiated* ones. The *reply* *is* *contested*.

### 1.3 The necessitarian account

**The *thesis*** (Armstrong, *What Is a Law of Nature?*, 1983; Dretske, “Laws of Nature,” 1977; Tooley, “The Nature of Laws,” 1977). The *laws* are *relations* of *necessitation* between *universals*: the *law* “all $F$s are $G$” is the *relation* $N(F, G)$ (Ch. 18, §7.2).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Law}(F, G) \quad \text{iff} \quad N(F, G)
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The laws support the counterfactuals (the law “all $F$s are $G$” supports “if $x$ were $F$, $x$ would be $G$”).} \\
2. & \text{The support requires the necessity (the regularity alone does not support the counterfactuals).} \\
3. & \text{The necessity requires the relation of necessitation between the universals.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The laws are the relations of necessitation (the necessitarian account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *counterfactual* *support* and the *governance*, and it *fits* the *realism* about the *universals* (Ch. 15, §4.2).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*inference*’* *objection* (van Fraassen, *Laws and Symmetry*, 1989, Ch. 5). The *relation* $N(F, G)$ *does* not *explain* the *regularity* (the *relation* is a *primitive*, and the *regularity* *follows* from the *relation* only *if* we *add* the *principle* that $N$ *necessitates* the *regularity*, and the *principle* is *not* *explained*) — the *account* is *circular*.

**The *reply*** (Armstrong 1983, Ch. 6). The *relation* $N$ *is* the *necessitation* (the *principle* is *analytic* of the *relation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *relation* in the *nature* of the *universals*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*identification*’* *objection* (van Fraassen 1989, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *relation* $N(F, G)$ *has* a *direction* (the *F* *necessitates* the *G*), and the *direction* is *not* *given* by the *regularity*, and the *account* *cannot* *identify* the *direction*.

**The *reply*** (Armstrong 1983, Ch. 6). The *direction* is *given* by the *nature* of the *universals* (the *F* is *essentially* the *property* that *necessitates* the *G*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *direction* in the *essence* (Ch. 16, §3). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*quidditism*’* *objection* (Lewis 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *universals* *have* *intrinsic* *natures* (the *quiddities*) that *are* *independent* of their *causal* *roles*, and the *independence* *makes* the *laws* *contingent* (the *same* *universals* *could* *have* *different* *laws*), and the *contingency* *undermines* the *necessity*.

**The *reply*** (Armstrong 1997, Ch. 5; Bird 2007). The *universals* *have* their *causal* *roles* *essentially* (the *quidditism* is *false*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *necessity* in the *essences*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.4 The dispositionalist account

**The *thesis*** (Bird, *Nature’s Metaphysics*, 2007; Ellis, *Scientific Essentialism*, 2001; Mumford, *Laws in Nature*, 2004). The *laws* are *grounded* in the *essences* of the *dispositional* *properties*, and the *properties* are *powers* (Ch. 18, §6.2), and the *laws* are the *manifestations* of the *powers*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Law}(F, G) \quad \text{iff} \quad \text{the essence of } F \text{ necessitates } G
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The properties are powers (the causal roles are constitutive of the properties; Ch. 18, §6.2).} \\
2. & \text{The powers necessitate their manifestations (the power is essentially the power to produce the effect).} \\
3. & \text{The laws are the manifestations of the powers (the laws follow from the essences).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The laws are grounded in the essences (the dispositionalist account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *necessity* in the *essences* (the *metaphysical* *necessity*; Ch. 16, §3.4), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *N*-relation (the *necessitation* is the *essence*), and it *fits* the *scientific* *essentialism* (the *laws* are the *consequences* of the *natures* of the *properties*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *categorical* *basis*’* *objection* (Ch. 18, §6.2; Armstrong 1999; the *contemporary* *literature*). If *all* the *properties* are *powers* and *the* *powers* *have* *no* *categorical* *basis*, there is *no* *ground* for the *powers* (the *powers* are *powers* *to* *produce* *nothing*), and the *objection* *undermines* the *account*.

**The *reply*** (Bird 2007, Ch. 5). The *powers* *are* the *ground* (the *manifestation* is *real*, and the *manifestation* is *the* *effect*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *powers* in the *structure* (the *“*power* *structure”*). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*circularity*’* *objection*. The *properties* are *defined* by the *laws*, and the *laws* are *defined* by the *properties* (the *circle*), and the *account* *is* *circular*.

**The *reply*** (Bird 2007, Ch. 3). The *circle* is *virtuous* (the *structure* is the *explanation*, and the *structure* is the *essence*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *essence* in the *structural* *realism* (Ch. 19, §6.3). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*conceivability*’* *objection* (Lewis 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *laws* are *conceivably* *different* (the *world* *could* *have* *the* *same* *properties* *and* *different* *laws*), and the *conceivability* *supports* the *contingency* of the *laws*, and the *contingency* *undermines* the *necessity*.

**The *reply*** (Bird 2007, Ch. 6; Ch. 16, §6.2). The *conceivability* is *not* a *reliable* *guide* to the *possibility* (the *essences* *are* *discovered* *a* *posteriori*), and the *reply* *rejects* the *CP* *thesis* for the *dispositions*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.5 The state of the laws debate

**The *assessment***. The *three* *families* *capture* the *three* *intuitions*, and the *choice* is *unresolved*.

| The *family* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Regularity** | Simple; naturalistic | The governance; the necessity |
| **Necessitation** | The necessity; the governance | The inference; the identification |
| **Disposition** | The necessity; the causal power | The categorical basis; the circularity |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *dispositionalism*: the *laws* are *grounded* in the *essences* of the *properties* (the *dispositionalist* *insight*), and the *essences* *include* the *categorical* *basis* (the *structural* *insight*; Ch. 19, §6.3), and the *regularities* are the *manifestations* (the *Humean* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *essence* is *contested*.

## 2. Ceteris Paribus Laws and the Autonomy of the Special Sciences

### 2.1 The problem

**The *datum***. The *laws* of the *special* *sciences* are *hedged*: “*ceteris* *paribus*, if an organism is deprived of food, it dies,” “*ceteris* *paribus*, if the price rises, the demand falls,” “*ceteris* *paribus*, if the gene is silenced, the protein is not expressed.”

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences state the generalizations with the ceteris paribus clauses (the practice).} \\
2. & \text{The clauses are not eliminable (the conditions are not specifiable in advance, and the formulation is open-ended).} \\
3. & \text{The laws (in the strict sense) cannot contain the clauses (the clauses are indefinite, and the laws are universal).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The special sciences lack the laws (the ceteris paribus problem).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *problem* *threatens* the *scientific* *status* of the *special* *sciences* (the *biology*, the *psychology*, the *economics*), and it *threatens* the *D-N* *model* of *explanation* (Ch. 38, §1), and it *threatens* the *unity* of *science* (the *reduction* of the *special* *sciences* to *physics*).

### 2.2 The “lost” ceteris paribus

**The *thesis*** (Earman & Roberts, “Ceteris Paribus Lost,” 2002; Earman, Roberts & Smith, “Ceteris Paribus Lost,” 2002). The *ceteris* *paribus* *clauses* are *either* *vacuous* (they *say* nothing, and the *law* is *not* a *law*) or *uneliminable* (the *clause* *specifies* the *conditions*, and the *conditions* are *not* *specifiable*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The ceteris paribus clause says “all other things being equal”.} \\
2. & \text{The clause is either specified (the conditions are listed) or unspecified.} \\
3. & \text{If specified, the clause is reducible to the strict law (the law with the conditions).} \\
4. & \text{If unspecified, the clause is vacuous (the law says nothing definite).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The ceteris paribus laws are either strict laws (and usually false) or not laws (the dilemma).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *special* *sciences* *lack* the *laws* (the *dilemma*), and the *autonomy* *must* be *grounded* *elsewhere* (the *mechanisms*, the *models*, the *capacities*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*pragmatic*’* *response* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Mitchell 1997, 2003). The *ceteris* *paribus* *clauses* are *pragmatic* (they *encode* the *practical* *constraints* and the *different* *degrees* of *stability*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *clauses* in the *practice*, and the *reply* *accepts* the *pluralism*.

**The *reply***. The *pragmatism* is *too* *weak* (it *does* not *distinguish* the *legitimate* *laws* from the *mere* *rules* of *thumb*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*mechanism*’* *response* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Machamer, Darden & Craver 2000; Ch. 38, §4). The *special* *sciences* *do* not *need* the *laws* (the *mechanisms* *explain*, and the *generalizations* are the *descriptions* of the *mechanisms*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *dilemma*, and the *reply* *accepts* the *mechanistic* *alternative*.

**The *reply***. The *mechanistic* *alternative* is *promising* (it *fits* the *practice*), and the *reply* *is* *contested* (the *laws* *remain* *important* in *the* *physics*).

### 2.3 Fodor and the autonomy

**The *thesis*** (Fodor, “Special Sciences,” 1974; Ch. 21, §7.3). The *special* *sciences* are *autonomous* because the *properties* are *multiply* *realizable*, and the *multiple* *realizability* *blocks* the *reduction* to *physics*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences classify the events by the properties that are multiply realizable (the monetary exchange, the belief, the gene).} \\
2. & \text{The multiple realizability blocks the type reduction (Ch. 23, §3.2).} \\
3. & \text{The explanations of the special sciences are causal at their own level.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The special sciences are autonomous (the autonomy thesis).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *fits* the *practice* (the *special* *sciences* *explain* at their *own* *level*), and it *avoids* the *reduction*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *autonomy*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Kim 1992; Ch. 23, §5.2). The *multiple* *realizability* is *not* *sufficient* for the *autonomy* (the *realization* is *asymmetric*, and the *higher* *level* *depends* on the *lower*), and the *autonomy* is *merely* *epistemic*.

**The *reply***. The *autonomy* is *epistemic* and *methodological* (the *explanations* *require* the *higher*-*level* *concepts*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *autonomy* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *laws*’* *objection* (the *ceteris* *paribus* *problem*). The *special* *sciences* *lack* the *strict* *laws*, and the *autonomy* *is* *grounded* in the *laws*, and the *lack* *undermines* the *autonomy*.

**The *reply*** (the *mechanist* *response*; Ch. 38, §4). The *autonomy* is *grounded* in the *mechanisms* (not the *laws*), and the *reply* *replaces* the *laws* with the *mechanisms*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.4 Cartwright and the laws that lie

**The *thesis*** (Cartwright, *How the Laws of Physics Lie*, 1983; *Nature’s Capacities and Their Measurement*, 1989; *The Dappled World*, 1999). The *laws* of *physics* are *not* *true* (they are *idealizations*), and the *scientific* *explanations* are *local* and *context*-*dependent* (the *capacities*), and the *unity* of *science* is *a* *myth*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The laws of physics (the Newtonian law, the Coulomb law) are idealizations (they hold only in the absence of the other forces).} \\
2. & \text{The idealizations do not describe the actual systems (the actual systems have the multiple forces, and the laws are false of them).} \\
3. & \text{The scientific explanations use the capacities (the tendencies that are real but not strict laws).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The laws of physics lie (the thesis), and the science explains by the capacities (the capacities account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *fits* the *practice* (the *physics* *uses* the *idealizations*, and the *special* *sciences* *use* the *local* *capacities*), and it *avoids* the *unrealistic* *demand* for the *strict* *laws*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *capacities*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Psillos 2002, Ch. 5). The *capacities* are *mysterious* (they are *dispositions* that *are* *not* *grounded* in the *laws*), and the *reply* *is* *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *unity*’* *objection*. The *account* *abandons* the *unity* of *science* (the *different* *domains* *have* the *different* *capacities*, and the *domains* are *not* *unified*), and the *consequence* *undermines* the *realism*.

**The *reply*** (Cartwright 1999, Ch. 1). The *unity* is *a* *myth* (the *science* is *a* *patchwork* of the *local* *models*), and the *reply* *accepts* the *disunity* (the *“*dappled* *world”*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.5 The state of the ceteris paribus debate

**The *assessment***. The *ceteris* *paribus* *problem* *threatens* the *special* *sciences*, and the *responses* are *all* *contested*.

| The *response* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Pragmatic (Mitchell)** | Fits the practice | The no-distinction |
| **Mechanistic (MDC)** | Explains without laws | The laws remain |
| **Autonomy (Fodor)** | Multiple realizability | The exclusion |
| **Capacities (Cartwright)** | Fits the idealization | The no-capacities |
| **Pluralism** | Fits the disciplines | The unity |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism*: the *special* *sciences* *use* the *different* *generalizations* (the *ceteris* *paribus* *laws*, the *mechanisms*, the *capacities*), and the *generalizations* are *real* at their *level* (the *pluralist* *insight*), and the *generalizations* *depend* on the *lower* *levels* (the *physicalist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *unity* is *not* *available*.

## 3. Natural Kinds and Projectibility

### 3.1 The projectibility problem

**The *problem***. The *inductions* *project* the *properties* (the *observed* $F$s *are* $G$; the *next* $F$ *will* be $G$), and the *projection* *requires* the *predicates* to be *projectible* (Ch. 10, §3).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The inductions project the predicates (the “green” projects, the “grue” does not; Ch. 10, §3.1).} \\
2. & \text{The projection requires the predicates to carve nature at its joints (the natural kinds; Ch. 15, §5.1).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The natural kinds are the ground of the projectibility (the natural kinds thesis).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *natural* *kinds* *connect* the *induction* (Ch. 10) to the *properties* (Ch. 15) and to the *laws* (§1), and the *connection* *grounds* the *scientific* *classification*.

### 3.2 Boyd's homeostatic property clusters

**The *thesis*** (Boyd, “Homeostasis, Species, and Higher Taxa,” 1999; “What Realism Implies and What It Does Not,” 1989). The *natural* *kinds* are *homeostatic* *property* *clusters* (the *HPC*): the *cluster* of the *properties* is *held* *together* by the *mechanisms* (the *homeostatic* *processes*), and the *kind* is *defined* by the *cluster* (not by a *single* *essence*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Kind}(K) \quad \text{iff} \quad K \text{ is a cluster of properties held together by a mechanism} \ M
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The biological kinds (the species, the taxa) do not have the single essences (the members vary, and the boundaries are vague).} \\
2. & \text{The kinds have the clusters of the properties that co-occur (the homeostatic mechanisms maintain the clusters).} \\
3. & \text{The clusters ground the inductions and the projections (the properties co-vary because of the mechanisms).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The natural kinds are the homeostatic property clusters (the HPC account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *fits* the *biological* *kinds* (the *species* *are* *real* *kinds*, and they *are* *not* *essentialist*), and it *avoids* the *rigid* *essentialism* (Ch. 15, §5.3), and it *fits* the *pluralism* (the *different* *mechanisms* *define* the *different* *kinds*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *mechanism*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Ereshefsky & Matthen 2005). The *mechanisms* *are* *not* *always* *available* (the *chemical* *kinds* *have* the *microstructure*, and the *microstructure* is *not* a *homeostatic* *mechanism*), and the *account* *does* not *cover* the *physical* *kinds*.

**The *reply*** (Boyd 1999). The *mechanism* *is* *the* *causal* *structure* (the *microstructure* *is* the *mechanism*), and the *reply* *extends* the *account* to the *physical* *kinds*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *boundary*’* *objection*. The *HPC* *kinds* *have* the *vague* *boundaries* (the *cluster* *admits* the *degrees*), and the *vagueness* *undermines* the *classification* (Ch. 33, §3).

**The *reply*** (Boyd 1999; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *vagueness* is *real* (the *kinds* *are* *vague*, and the *science* *accepts* the *vagueness*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *classification* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.3 The species problem

**The *problem***. The *species* *concept* *fragments* into the *multiple* *concepts* (the *biological* *species* *concept*, the *phylogenetic* *species* *concept*, the *ecological* *species* *concept*, the *morphological* *species* *concept*), and the *fragmentation* *raises* the *question* of *which* *concept* is the *right* *one* (Ch. 42, §3).

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Monism** | There is *one* *correct* *species* *concept* | Mayr (1942); the *biological* *species* *concept* |
| **Pluralism** | There are *many* *equally* *legitimate* *concepts* | Kitcher (1984); Dupré (1993) |
| **Realism** | The *species* are *real* *individuals* (the *historical* *entities*) | Ghiselin (1974); Hull (1978) |
| **Nominalism** | The *species* are *conventional* | the *nominalist* *tradition* |

**The *argument* (the *pluralism*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The species concepts capture the different biological patterns (the interbreeding, the phylogeny, the ecological niche).} \\
2. & \text{The patterns are real (they are not conventional), and the patterns cross-cut one another.} \\
3. & \text{The concepts are not reducible to a single concept.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The species pluralism (the pluralist insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *species* *problem* *tests* the *natural* *kinds* *debate*: the *kinds* of the *special* *sciences* are *real* but *plural* (Ch. 21, §11), and the *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible* *position*.

### 3.4 Chemical kinds

**The *question***. Are the *chemical* *kinds* (the *elements*, the *compounds*) *natural* *kinds*?

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Microstructural essentialism** | The *kind* is *defined* by the *atomic* *number* (the *microstructure*) | Kripke; Putnam; the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Macroscopic** | The *kind* is *defined* by the *observable* *properties* | the *contemporary* *literature* (Needham) |
| **Hybrid** | The *kind* is *defined* by the *structure* and the *properties* | the *contemporary* *literature* (Hendry) |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The chemical kinds have the microstructures (the atomic number, the electron configuration).} \\
2. & \text{The microstructures explain the observable properties (the reactivity, the color, the melting point).} \\
3. & \text{The microstructures ground the projections (the kind supports the inductions).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The chemical kinds are grounded in the microstructures (the essentialist insight), and the properties are not reducible to the microstructure (the emergentist insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *chemistry* *tests* the *natural* *kinds* *debate* (the *microstructural* *essentialism* *fits* the *elements*, and the *isotopes* *complicate* the *picture*, and the *macroscopic* *properties* *are* *emergent*), and the *case* *supports* the *hybrid* *account*.

### 3.5 The state of the natural kinds debate

**The *assessment***. The *natural* *kinds* are *real*, and the *kinds* are *plural* and *interest*-*relative*.

| The *account* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Essentialism** | The microstructure | The isotopes; the species |
| **HPC (Boyd)** | The biological kinds | The no-mechanism |
| **Pluralism** | Fits the practice | The no-unity |
| **Nominalism** | The conventional | The projectibility |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism*: the *natural* *kinds* are *real* (the *kinds* *support* the *inductions*, and the *inductions* *require* the *kinds*), and the *kinds* are *plural* (the *different* *mechanisms* *define* the *different* *kinds*), and the *kinds* *are* *interest*-*relative* (the *classification* *depends* on the *purposes*; Ch. 21, §11), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *pluralism* *does* not *collapse* into the *relativism* (the *mechanisms* *are* *real*).

## 4. Reduction and Emergence in the Sciences

### 4.1 The classic cases

**The *cases***.

| The *case* | The *reduction* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermodynamics → statistical mechanics** | The *macro* *laws* are *derived* from the *micro* *laws* | The *partial* *reduction* (the *past* *hypothesis*; the *phase* *transitions*) |
| **Mendelian → molecular genetics** | The *gene* *concept* *reduced* to the *DNA* *sequences* | The *partial* *reduction* (the *gene* *concept* *fragments*) |
| **Chemistry → physics** | The *chemical* *laws* *derived* from the *quantum* *mechanics* | The *partial* *reduction* (the *periodic* *table* *autonomy*) |
| **Biology → physics** | The *biological* *laws* *derived* from the *physical* | The *contested* *reduction* (the *multiple* *realizability*) |

**The *significance***. The *cases* *show* that the *reduction* is *partial* (the *reduction* *succeeds* *in* the *explaining* *some* *phenomena* and *fails* for the *others*), and the *partial* *reduction* *grounds* the *pluralism*.

### 4.2 The phase transitions

**The *case*** (Wilson, “The Renormalization Group and Critical Phenomena,” 1983; Batterman, *The Devil in the Details*, 2002). The *phase* *transitions* (the *water* *boiling*, the *magnet* *losing* its *magnetization*) *exhibit* the *universality* (the *different* *systems* *share* the *same* *critical* *exponents*), and the *universality* is *explained* by the *renormalization* *group* (the *scale* *invariance*), and the *explanation* *requires* the *infinite* *limit* (the *thermodynamic* *limit*), and the *limit* *is* *not* *present* in the *finite* *systems*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The critical phenomena exhibit the universality (the same exponents across the different systems).} \\
2. & \text{The universality is explained by the renormalization group (the scale invariance, the fixed points).} \\
3. & \text{The renormalization requires the infinite limit (the thermodynamic limit), and the limit is not realized in the finite systems.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The explanation requires the idealization (the infinite limit), and the idealization is not reducible to the finite systems (the emergent insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *case* *shows* that the *reduction* *fails* (the *critical* *phenomena* *are* *emergent*, and the *emergence* *is* *explained* by the *idealized* *model*), and the *case* *supports* the *emergentist* *position* (Ch. 21, §5).

### 4.3 Molecular genetics and the gene

**The *case*** (Griffiths & Stotz, *Genetics and Philosophy*, 2013; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *gene* *concept* *fragments* (the *molecular* *gene*, the *Mendelian* *gene*, the *evolutionary* *gene*, the *regulatory* *gene*), and the *fragmentation* *undermines* the *reduction* (the *Mendelian* *gene* is *not* identical* to the *molecular* *gene*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The Mendelian gene is defined by the phenotypic effects (the inheritance pattern).} \\
2. & \text{The molecular gene is defined by the DNA sequence (the transcription, the translation).} \\
3. & \text{The two concepts diverge (the pleiotropy, the polygeny, the alternative splicing), and the divergence blocks the reduction (Ch. 21, §9.2).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The gene concept is plural (the pluralist insight), and the reduction is partial (the reduction of the explanation does not eliminate the Mendelian level).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *case* *shows* that the *reduction* *is* *partial* (the *Mendelian* *level* *remains* *explanatorily* *necessary*), and the *case* *supports* the *pluralism*.

### 4.4 The state of the reduction debate

**The *assessment***. The *reduction* is *partial* (the *classic* *cases* *show* the *successes* and the *failures*), and the *emergence* is *real* (the *critical* *phenomena*, the *phase* *transitions*).

| The *case* | The *reduction* | The *emergence* |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermodynamics** | Partial | The phase transitions |
| **Genetics** | Partial | The gene fragmentation |
| **Chemistry** | Partial | The periodic table |
| **Biology** | Contested | The multiple realizability |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism*: the *reduction* *succeeds* at the *level* of the *mechanisms* (the *mechanistic* *reduction*, Ch. 21, §4), and the *emergence* *succeeds* at the *level* of the *idealized* *models* (the *critical* *phenomena*), and the *two* are *complementary* (the *pluralist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *levels* are *not* *reducible* to a *single* *hierarchy* (Ch. 21, §8).

## 5. Models, Idealization, and Fiction

### 5.1 The semantic view

**The *thesis*** (Suppes, “A Comparison of the Meaning and Uses of Models in Mathematics and the Empirical Sciences,” 1960; van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image*, 1980, Ch. 3; Giere, *Explaining Science*, 1988). The *scientific* *theories* are *families* of *models* (the *semantic* *view*), not the *sets* of *axioms* (the *syntactic* *view*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Theory} = \text{a family of models} \ \{M_1, M_2, \ldots\}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The scientific practice uses the models (the dynamical models, the statistical models, the computational models).} \\
2. & \text{The models are not the axioms (they are structures that satisfy the axioms, and the same axioms have the many models).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The theories are the families of models (the semantic view).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *fits* the *practice* (the *models* are the *units* of the *scientific* *work*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *axioms* *without* the *models*.

### 5.2 Models as fictions

**The *thesis*** (Cartwright 1983; Giere 1988, 2006; Suárez, “Scientific Fictionalism and the Problem of Inference,” 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *models* are *fictions*: they *describe* the *idealized* *systems* that *are* *not* *real*, and the *scientific* *reasoning* *uses* the *fictions* to *represent* the *real* *systems*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The models describe the idealized systems (the frictionless plane, the ideal gas, the rational agent).} \\
2. & \text{The idealized systems are not real (the friction exists, the gas is not ideal, the agent is not rational).} \\
3. & \text{The models represent the real systems without describing them truly (the representation is fictional).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The models are fictions (the fictionalist insight).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *fits* the *practice* (the *scientists* *use* the *idealized* *models*), and it *avoids* the *false* *dilemma* (the *models* are *either* *true* or *useless*), and it *grounds* the *representation* in the *fiction*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *inference*’* *objection* (Suárez 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *fictions* *cannot* *support* the *inferences* (the *inference* from the *model* to the *world* *requires* the *truth*), and the *fictionalism* *loses* the *inference*.

**The *reply*** (Suárez 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *inference* is *not* *truth*-*preserving* (the *inference* is *material*, and the *material* *inference* *is* *grounded* in the *model* *and* the *world*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *inference* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *fiction*’* *objection*. The *models* are *not* *fictions* (they are *representations* that *abstract*, and the *abstraction* *is* *not* *a* *fiction*), and the *reply* *rejects* the *fictionalism*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 Idealization and the problem of explanation

**The *problem*** (Batterman, *The Devil in the Details*, 2002; the *contemporary* *literature*). If the *models* are *false* (the *idealizations*), how do the *models* *explain*? The *explanation* *requires* the *truth*, and the *models* are *false*, and the *explanation* *seems* *impossible*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The explanation requires the truth (the explanation is factive; Ch. 38, §6).} \\
2. & \text{The models are false (the idealizations; §5.2).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The models cannot explain (the problem).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *‘*minimal* *model*’* *response* (Batterman 2002, Ch. 3). The *explanation* *uses* the *minimal* *models* (the *models* that *contain* the *essential* *features*, and the *non*-*essential* *features* *are* *the* *fictions*), and the *minimal* *model* *explains* the *universality* (the *critical* *phenomena* *are* *explained* by the *minimal* *model*, not by the *detailed* *model*). The *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *idealized* *structure*, and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*pragmatic*’* *response* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *explanation* is *not* *factive* (the *understanding* *does* not *require* the *truth*; Ch. 38, §6.1), and the *models* *explain* by *providing* the *understanding*. The *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *understanding*, and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*structural*’* *response* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 19, §6.3). The *models* *capture* the *structure* (the *structure* is *real*, and the *content* is *hidden*), and the *explanation* *succeeds* because the *structure* is *real*. The *reply* *grounds* the *explanation* in the *structural* *realism*, and it is *contested*.

### 5.4 The state of the models debate

**The *assessment***. The *models* are *fictions* (the *fictionalist* *insight*), and the *models* *explain* (the *problem*), and the *responses* are *contested*.

| The *response* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimal model** | The universality | The no-truth |
| **Pragmatic** | The understanding | The no-factivity |
| **Structural** | The realism | The structure is contested |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *structural* *realism*: the *models* are *fictions* (the *fictionalist* *insight*), and the *models* *explain* by *capturing* the *structure* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *structure* is *real* (the *realist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *structure* is *contested*.

## 6. Values in Science

### 6.1 The value-free ideal

**The *thesis*** (Weber, “Science as a Vocation,” 1919; Nagel, *The Structure of Science*, 1961, Ch. 12; Merton, *The Sociology of Science*, 1973). The *science* *should* be *value*-*free* (the *scientific* *claims* are *objective*, and the *values* are *irrelevant* to the *justification*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Science} \ \perp \ \text{Values}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The scientific claims are justified by the evidence (the epistemic norms).} \\
2. & \text{The evidence is independent of the values (the observation, the experiment, the logic).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The science is value-free (the value-free ideal).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *ideal* *grounds* the *objectivity* of *science* (the *claims* are *assessed* by the *evidence*, not the *interests*), and it *protects* the *science* from the *ideological* *capture*.

### 6.2 The argument from inductive risk

**The *thesis*** (Rudner, “The Scientist Qua Scientist Makes Value Judgments,” 1953; Douglas, *Science, Policy, and the Value-Free Ideal*, 2009). The *acceptance* of a *scientific* *hypothesis* *depends* on the *costs* of the *error* (the *inductive* *risk*), and the *costs* are *values*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The acceptance of a hypothesis requires the decision that the evidence is sufficient.} \\
2. & \text{The decision depends on the costs of the Type I and the Type II errors (the false positive, the false negative).} \\
3. & \text{The costs are values (the health, the safety, the economic interests).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The acceptance requires the values (the inductive risk argument).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *shows* that the *values* *enter* the *science* at the *acceptance* *stage* (the *threshold* of the *evidence*), and the *entry* *is* *not* *eliminable*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *values*’* *objection*. The *acceptance* *is* *not* a *scientific* *act* (the *scientific* *act* is the *assessment* of the *evidence*, and the *acceptance* is *policy*), and the *reply* *separates* the *science* from the *policy*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *acceptance* is *part* of the *science*).

**(ii) The *‘*epistemic* *values*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Laudan 1984). The *values* *that* enter the *science* are *epistemic* (the *truth*, the *consistency*, the *predictive* *power*), and the *epistemic* *values* are *not* the *non*-*epistemic* *values* (the *political*, the *economic*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *two* *kinds*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *distinction* is *not* *sharp*).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *risk*’* *objection*. The *inductive* *risk* is *not* *always* *present* (the *pure* *mathematics*, the *basic* *physics*), and the *argument* *applies* only to the *applied* *sciences*.

**The *reply*** (Douglas 2009, Ch. 3). The *risk* is *present* in the *applied* *sciences* (the *epidemiology*, the *toxicology*, the *climate* *science*), and the *reply* *restricts* the *argument*, and the *restriction* *is* *defensible*.

### 6.3 The social objectivity

**The *thesis*** (Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge*, 1990; *The Fate of Knowledge*, 2002; Ch. 13, §5.3). The *objectivity* is *social* (the *critical* *process* of the *community*), and the *social* *conditions* are *normative* (the *venues* for the *criticism*, the *shared* *standards*, the *uptake*, the *equality*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The individual inquiry is subject to the bias (the confirmation bias, the interests).} \\
2. & \text{The community can correct the bias (if the conditions of the criticism are satisfied).} \\
3. & \text{The conditions are normative (they require the equality, the diversity, the transparency).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The objectivity is social and normative (the social account).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *social* *practice*, and it *fits* the *contemporary* *science* (the *large*-*scale* *collaboration*, the *peer* *review*), and it *avoids* the *individualist* *account*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *values*’* *objection*. The *social* *conditions* *make* the *science* *value*-*laden* (the *equality*, the *diversity* are *values*), and the *science* is *supposed* to be *value*-*free* (the *reply* *rejects* the *value*-*free* *ideal*; §6.2).

**The *reply*** (Longino 2002, Ch. 5). The *objectivity* is *compatible* with the *values* (the *values* are *constitutive* of the *inquiry*, not the *content* of the *claims*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *constitutive* *values* from the *content* *values*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objectivity*’* *objection*. The *social* *account* *relativizes* the *objectivity* to the *community* (the *different* *communities* *have* the *different* *standards*), and the *relativism* *undermines* the *objectivity*.

**The *reply*** (Longino 2002, Ch. 6). The *objectivity* is *not* the *consensus* (it is the *critical* *process*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *process*, not the *agreement*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The case studies

**The *cases***.

| The *case* | The *value* *issue* | The *lesson* |
|---|---|---|
| **Climate science** | The *costs* of the *false* *negative* (the *inaction*) vs. the *costs* of the *false* *positive* (the *over*-*regulation*) | The *inductive* *risk* *is* *inescapable* |
| **Vaccine safety** | The *costs* of the *rare* *side* *effects* vs. the *costs* of the *disease* | The *values* *shape* the *evidence* *threshold* |
| **AI** | The *biases* in the *training* *data*; the *fairness* *metrics* | The *values* *enter* the *design* |
| **Pharmaceutical research** | The *financial* *interests*; the *publication* *bias* | The *interests* *shape* the *science* |
| **The replication crisis** | The *incentives* (the *publication*, the *career*) | The *social* *conditions* *matter* |

**The *significance***. The *cases* *show* that the *values* *enter* the *science* at the *multiple* *levels* (the *agenda*, the *design*, the *interpretation*, the *acceptance*, the *communication*), and the *values* *cannot* be *eliminated* (they can be *managed*).

### 6.5 The state of the values debate

**The *assessment***. The *value*-*free* *ideal* is *untenable* (the *inductive* *risk* *argument*), and the *values* *enter* the *science* at the *multiple* *levels*, and the *management* of the *values* is *the* *central* *question*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Value-free (Weber)** | The objectivity | The inductive risk |
| **Inductive risk (Douglas)** | The practice | The no-line |
| **Social (Longino)** | The process | The relativism |
| **Epistemic values (Laudan)** | The distinction | The distinction is not sharp |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *social* *account*: the *values* *enter* the *science* (the *inductive* *risk* *insight*), and the *values* are *managed* by the *social* *conditions* (the *Longino* *insight*), and the *management* *requires* the *transparency* and the *criticism* (the *normative* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *line* between the *legitimate* and the *illegitimate* *values* is *contested*.

## 7. Interdisciplinarity and the Structure of the Constraint

### 7.1 The constraints on philosophy of mind

**The *thesis***. The *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *philosophy* of *mind* (the *multiple* *realizability*, the *special* *sciences*; Ch. 23, §3).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The philosophy of mind uses the claims of the special sciences (the psychology, the neuroscience).} \\
2. & \text{The claims presuppose the theory of the laws and the kinds (§1–§3).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The philosophy of science constrains the philosophy of mind (the constraint).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *multiple* *realizability* *argument* (Ch. 23, §3.2) *presupposes* the *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences* (Ch. 21, §7.3), and the *causal* *exclusion* *argument* (Ch. 23, §5.2) *presupposes* the *theory* of *causation* (Ch. 18, §2), and the *function* *debate* (Ch. 42, §2) *presupposes* the *theory* of the *teleology* (§1.4).

### 7.2 The constraints on the metaphysics

**The *thesis***. The *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *metaphysics* (the *laws*, the *properties*, the *causation*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The metaphysics of the laws depends on the science (the best system, the necessitation, the disposition).} \\
2. & \text{The science provides the data and the constraints (the practice, the experiment).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The philosophy of science constrains the metaphysics (the constraint).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *debate* about the *laws* (§1) *constrains* the *debate* about the *modality* (Ch. 16), and the *debate* about the *natural* *kinds* (§3) *constrains* the *debate* about the *properties* (Ch. 15), and the *debate* about the *models* (§5) *constrains* the *debate* about the *representation* (Ch. 25, §3).

### 7.3 The constraints on the ethics

**The *thesis***. The *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *ethics* (the *research* *ethics*, the *science* *policy*, the *epistemic* *injustice*; Ch. 51).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The ethics of the science depends on the theory of the values in the science (§6).} \\
2. & \text{The theory of the values depends on the theory of the inquiry (Ch. 8, §5).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The philosophy of science constrains the ethics (the constraint).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *argument* from the *inductive* *risk* (§6.2) *grounds* the *ethics* of the *science* *policy*, and the *social* *account* of the *objectivity* (§6.3) *grounds* the *epistemic* *injustice* *argument* (Ch. 11, §4), and the *debate* about the *natural* *kinds* (§3) *grounds* the *debate* about the *human* *kinds* (the *race*, the *gender*, the *disability*; Ch. 33, §7).

### 7.4 The constraints on the epistemology

**The *thesis***. The *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *epistemology* (the *testimony*, the *expertise*, the *disagreement*; Ch. 11).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The epistemology of the science depends on the theory of the testimony (the trust, the expertise; Ch. 11, §1).} \\
2. & \text{The theory of the testimony depends on the theory of the institutions (Ch. 11, §5).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The philosophy of science constrains the epistemology (the constraint).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *climate* *science* *depends* on the *trust* in the *models* and the *expertise* (Ch. 11, §1), and the *AI* *science* *depends* on the *testimony* of the *algorithms* (Ch. 11, §6), and the *replication* *crisis* *depends* on the *social* *conditions* of the *inquiry* (Ch. 11, §6.5).

### 7.5 The code of practice

**The *thesis***. The *interdisciplinary* *work* *requires* a *code* of *practice*.

**The *code***.

| The *rule* | The *demand* |
|---|---|
| **Learn the science** | The *philosophical* *claims* *must* be *informed* by the *science* (the *practice*, the *experiments*, the *models*) |
| **Respect the specialization** | The *philosophy* of the *science* *does* not *replace* the *science* (it *clarifies* the *concepts*) |
| **Avoid the imperialist** | The *philosophy* of the *science* *does* not *dictate* the *science* (the *philosophy* *follows* the *science*) |
| **Avoid the servile** | The *philosophy* of the *science* *does* not *merely* *report* the *science* (the *philosophy* *criticizes*) |
| **Be explicit about the values** | The *philosophical* *claims* *must* *acknowledge* the *values* (§6) |
| **Be explicit about the limits** | The *philosophical* *claims* *must* *acknowledge* the *limits* of the *science* and the *philosophy* |

**The *significance***. The *code* *grounds* the *interdisciplinary* *practice*, and it *avoids* the *two* *extremes* (the *imperialism* and the *servility*), and it *fits* the *method* of the *book* (Ch. 4, §4).

### 7.6 The state of the interdisciplinarity debate

**The *assessment***. The *interdisciplinarity* is *necessary* and *dangerous*, and the *code* is *the* *response*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Imperialism** | The philosophical authority | The science is ignored |
| **Servility** | The scientific authority | The philosophy is idle |
| **The code** | The balance | The code is not read |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. The *code* of *practice* (the *rules*), *combined* with the *methodological* *humility* (the *negative* *capability*; Ch. 3, §7), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *interdisciplinarity* is *hard* and *the* *code* is *not* *sufficient*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *three* *families* of *laws* are *all* *live*.** The *regularity*, the *necessitation*, and the *disposition* *capture* the *different* *intuitions*, and *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *ceteris* *paribus* *problem* is *real*.** The *special* *sciences* *lack* the *strict* *laws*, and the *responses* (the *pragmatic*, the *mechanistic*, the *capacities*) are *contested*.
- **The *natural* *kinds* are *real* and *plural*.** The *HPC* (Boyd), the *species* *pluralism*, and the *chemical* *essentialism* *ground* the *classification*, and the *kind* *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible*.
- **The *reduction* is *partial*.** The *classic* *cases* (the *thermodynamics*, the *genetics*, the *chemistry*) *show* the *successes* and the *limits*.
- **The *models* are *fictions*.** The *idealizations* *represent* the *systems* *without* *describing* them *truly*, and the *explanation* *requires* a *further* *account*.
- **The *values* *enter* the *science*.** The *inductive* *risk* *argument* is *decisive*, and the *social* *account* (Longino) *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *practice*.
- **The *interdisciplinarity* *constrains* the *philosophy*.** The *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *philosophy* of *mind*, the *metaphysics*, the *ethics*, and the *epistemology*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of the *laws*.** The *regularity*, the *necessitation*, the *disposition* — *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *ceteris* *paribus* *laws*.** Are they *laws*, and if so, *what* *kind*?
- **The *natural* *kinds*.** The *HPC*, the *essentialism*, the *pluralism* — *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *models* and the *explanation*.** How do the *false* *models* *explain*?
- **The *values* in the *science*.** The *legitimate* vs. the *illegitimate* *values*.
- **The *interdisciplinarity*.** The *balance* between the *imperialism* and the *servility*.

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* among the *regularity*, the *necessitation*, and the *disposition*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *grue* (Ch. 10, §3), the *phase* *transitions*, the *idealizations* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *HPC*, the *renormalization* *group*, the *structural* *equations* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: the philosophy of science is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regularity (Lewis)** | Laws are best-system regularities | Naturalistic; simple | The governance |
| **Necessitation (Armstrong)** | Laws are relations of universals | The necessity | The inference |
| **Disposition (Bird)** | Laws are grounded in essences | The necessity; the power | The categorical basis |
| **Pragmatic (Mitchell)** | Ceteris paribus laws are pragmatic | Fits the practice | The no-distinction |
| **Mechanistic (MDC)** | Explanation is mechanism | The special sciences | The levels; the normativity |
| **Capacities (Cartwright)** | Laws lie; capacities explain | The idealization | The no-capacities |
| **HPC (Boyd)** | Kinds are homeostatic clusters | The biological kinds | The no-mechanism |
| **Species pluralism** | Species concepts are many | Fits the practice | The no-unity |
| **Reduction** | The macro is reduced to the micro | The unification | The partial reduction |
| **Emergence** | The macro is emergent | The critical phenomena | The unexplained |
| **Fictionalism (Giere)** | Models are fictions | The idealization | The inference |
| **Inductive risk (Douglas)** | Values enter at acceptance | The practice | The no-line |
| **Social objectivity (Longino)** | Objectivity is social | The process | The relativism |
| **Code of practice** | The balance | The interdisciplinary | The code is not read |

The table is a map, not a verdict. The reader who wants to choose a position must decide which cost is acceptable — and the decision is a philosophical argument.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *metaphysics* of the *dispositions* and the *quantum* *mechanics*.** The *quantum* *mechanics* *suggests* that the *fundamental* *properties* are *dispositional* (the *propensities*, the *probabilities*; Ch. 19, §5.3), and the *suggestion* *supports* the *dispositionalist* *account* of the *laws* (§1.4), and the *connection* *constrains* the *interpretation* of the *quantum* *mechanics*.

**2. The *ceteris* *paribus* *laws* and the *philosophy* of *biology*.** The *ceteris* *paribus* *debate* (§2) *connects* the *philosophy* of *biology* (Ch. 42, §2) to the *philosophy* of *science*, and the *connection* *constrains* the *debate* about the *biological* *kinds* (§3.3) and the *biological* *explanation* (Ch. 38, §7.1).

**3. The *models* and the *science* *of* *science*.** The *model*-*based* *reasoning* (§5) *connects* the *philosophy* of *science* to the *cognitive* *science* (Ch. 30, §3), and the *science* *of* *science* (the *metascience*; the *replication* *crisis*; Ch. 11, §6.5) *connects* the *philosophy* of *science* to the *social* *epistemology*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *laws* and *kinds*.** The *Buddhist* *tradition* *developed* a *robust* *theory* of *conditionality* (*idappaccayatā*; the *“*when* *this* *exists,* *that* *comes* *to* *be*”*), and the *Nyāya* *tradition* *developed* a *realist* *theory* of *universals* and *causation* (the *jāti*, the *vyāpti*). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* to the *Humean* and the *necessitarian* *accounts* (Matilal 1986, 1998; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *ethics* of the *laws*.** The *laws* *can* *be* *harmful* (the *criminal* *laws*, the *natural* *laws* as *legitimations* of the *status* *quo*), and the *ethics* of the *laws* *connects* the *philosophy* of *science* to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §7). The *connection* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent*.

## Summary

- **The *three* *families* of *laws*** — the *regularity*, the *necessitation*, the *disposition* — *capture* the *different* *intuitions*, and *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *ceteris* *paribus* *problem*** *threatens* the *special* *sciences*, and the *responses* (the *pragmatic*, the *mechanistic*, the *capacities*, the *autonomy*) are *all* *contested*.
- **The *natural* *kinds*** are *real* and *plural*: the *HPC* (Boyd), the *species* *pluralism*, and the *chemical* *essentialism* *ground* the *classification*, and the *kind* *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible*.
- **The *reduction* is *partial***: the *thermodynamics*, the *genetics*, and the *chemistry* *show* the *successes* and the *limits* of the *reduction*, and the *emergence* (the *phase* *transitions*) is *real*.
- **The *models* are *fictions***: the *idealizations* *represent* the *systems* *without* *describing* them *truly*, and the *explanation* *requires* a *further* *account* (the *minimal* *model*, the *structural* *realism*).
- **The *values* *enter* the *science***: the *inductive* *risk* *argument* is *decisive*, and the *social* *account* (Longino) *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *practice*, and the *management* of the *values* is the *central* *question*.
- **The *interdisciplinarity* *constrains* the *philosophy***: the *philosophy* of *science* *constrains* the *philosophy* of *mind*, the *metaphysics*, the *ethics*, and the *epistemology*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *pluralism*: the *laws* are *grounded* in the *essences* (the *dispositionalist* *insight*), the *ceteris* *paribus* *generalizations* are *real* at their *level* (the *pluralist* *insight*), the *natural* *kinds* are *real* and *plural* (the *HPC* *insight*), the *reduction* is *partial* (the *mechanistic* *insight*), the *models* are *fictions* (the *fictionalist* *insight*), the *values* are *inescapable* (the *inductive* *risk* *insight*), and the *interdisciplinarity* is *constrained* by the *code* of *practice* (the *methodological* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *unity* is *not* *available*.
