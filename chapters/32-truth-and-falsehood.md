# Chapter 32 — Truth and Falsehood

> “Truth is the agreement of thought with reality.”
> — Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* (1265–74), I.16.1, articulating the correspondence intuition

> “‘What is truth?’ Pilate asks, and does not stay for an answer. Most philosophers have stayed, and have not agreed.”
> — a formulation of the predicament, in many mouths

> “‘Snow is white’ is true if and only if snow is white.”
> — Alfred Tarski, “The Semantic Conception of Truth” (1944), the *T*-schema, in its most famous instance

---

Chapter 31 examined *meaning* through the *truth*-*conditional* *tradition*, and it *presupposed* the concept of **truth** without *examining* it. The *presupposition* is *not* *innocent*: if the *meaning* of a *sentence* is its *truth* *conditions*, the *theory* of *meaning* *rests* on the *theory* of *truth*, and a *failure* at the *foundation* *propagates* through the *whole* *semantics*.

This chapter examines the foundation. The *question* — *what is it for a proposition to be true?* — is one of the *oldest* in philosophy, and it has been *transformed* in the *last* *century* by three *developments*: the *Tarskian* *formalization* of *truth* (1933), the *deflationary* *turn* (Ramsey 1927; Ayer 1936; Quine 1970; Horwich 1990), and the *truthmaker* *revival* (Armstrong 1997, 2004; Bigelow 1988). The *three* *developments* *pull* in *different* *directions*, and the *chapter*'s *thesis* is that the *pull* is *instructive*: the *deflationary* *account* *captures* the *logical* *core* of *truth* (the *T*-schema, the *generalization* *function*, the *transparency*), the *correspondence* *account* *captures* the *metaphysical* *ground* (the *truthmakers*), and *neither* *captures* the *value* of *truth* (the *normative* *dimension*).

The *thesis* is *qualified* by the *admission* that the *three* are *not* *easily* *combined*, and that the *combination* is the *most* *important* *open* *problem* in the *field*. The *chapter*'s *verdict* is that *truth* is *real* but *thin*, that the *thinness* is *compatible* with a *robust* *metaphysics* of *truthmaking*, and that *neither* *dispenses* with the *value* of *truth*, which is *irreducible* to *both*.

The chapter proceeds in nine movements. §1 states the *problem*. §2 examines *correspondence*. §3 examines *coherence*. §4 examines *pragmatism*. §5 examines *deflationism*. §6 examines *truthmaking*. §7 examines *alethic* *pluralism*. §8 examines *relativism* about *truth*. §9 examines the *value* of *truth*. §10 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

---

## 1. The Problem Stated

### 1.1 Truthbearers

**The *question***. What *is* the *bearer* of *truth*?

**The *candidates***.

| Candidate | The *example* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Sentence** | “Snow is white” | The *sentence* is *ambiguous*; the *meaning* *depends* on the *language* and the *context* |
| **Proposition** | The *content* that snow is white | The *nature* of the *proposition* is *contested* (Ch. 12, §7.3) |
| **Utterance** | The *act* of *asserting* | The *utterance* *includes* the *illocutionary* *force* (Ch. 34) |
| **Belief** | The *state* of *believing* | The *belief* *can* be *true* *without* the *sentence* |

**The *standard* *view***. The *primary* *truthbearer* is the *proposition* (the *content*), and the *sentence* and the *utterance* are *derivative* (the *sentence* *expresses* the *proposition*; the *utterance* *asserts* it).

**The *complication***. The *selection* *determines* the *theory*: if the *truthbearer* is the *sentence*, the *truth* *depends* on the *language* (the *deflationary* *view*); if the *truthbearer* is the *proposition*, the *truth* is *language*-*independent* (the *correspondence* *view*). The *difference* is *not* *verbal*.

**The *consequence***. The *question* "what is truth?" *presupposes* an *answer* to the *question* "what is *true*?", and the *two* *questions* are *not* *independent*.

### 1.2 The criteria of adequacy

**The *criteria***. A *theory* of *truth* *must* *satisfy* *five* *desiderata* (the *list* is *derived* from the *contemporary* *literature*; the *formulation* is the *author's*).

| Criterion | The *demand* |
|---|---|
| **Extension** | The theory *classifies* the *true* and the *false* *correctly* |
| **Generality** | The theory *explains* the *truth* of *any* *truth* (the *logical*, the *mathematical*, the *empirical*, the *moral*, the *modal*) |
| **T-schema** | The theory *entails* the *instances* of the *T*-schema (the *disquotation* *intuitions*) |
| **Normativity** | The theory *explains* the *value* of *truth* (why *truth* is a *goal* of *inquiry*) |
| **Substantivity** | The theory *gives* a *substantive* *account* (not a *mere* *label*) |

**The *tension***. The *criteria* *conflict*: the *deflationary* *theories* *satisfy* the *T*-schema and the *generality* but *fail* the *normativity*; the *correspondence* *theories* *satisfy* the *substantivity* but *fail* the *extension* (the *negative*, *general*, *modal* *truths*); the *pragmatist* *theories* *satisfy* the *normativity* but *fail* the *extension* (the *useful* *falsehood*).

**The *significance***. The *criteria* are the *standards* by which the *theories* are *assessed*, and the *assessment* is the *subject* of the *chapter*.

**The *significance***. The *debate* is *not* a *single* *dispute*; it is a *network* of *disputes*, and the *network* is the *subject* of the *chapter*.

## 2. The Correspondence Theory

### 2.1 Russell and Moore

**The *thesis*** (Russell, "On the Nature of Truth," 1906; *The Problems of Philosophy*, 1912, Ch. XII; Moore, "Facts and Propositions," 1927). Truth is a *relation* of *correspondence* between a *truthbearer* (a *proposition*, a *belief*) and a *fact*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ corresponds to the fact } F
$$

**Russell's *version*** (the *multiple* *relation* *theory*, 1912, Ch. XII). A *belief* is a *relation* of the *subject* to *several* *objects* (the *constituents* of the *proposition*), and the *belief* is *true* if the *objects* *are* *related* *in* the *way* the *belief* *represents* them as *related*.

**The *example***. The *belief* that *Othello* *loves* *Desdemona* is a *relation* of the *believer* to *Othello*, *Desdemona*, and *love*; the *belief* is *true* if *Othello* *does* *love* *Desdemona* (the *relation* *obtains*), and *false* if it *does* not.

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *grounds* *truth* in the *world* (the *fact*), and it *captures* the *intuition* that *truth* is *objective* (the *fact* *obtains* *independently* of the *believer*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*correspondence* *relation*’* *objection***. What *is* the *relation*? The *proposition* is *not* a *picture* of the *fact*, and the *relation* is *not* *resemblance* (the *proposition* is *abstract*, the *fact* is *concrete*). The *relation* is *mysterious*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *correspondence* *theorists*). The *relation* is *structural* (the *proposition* *shares* a *structure* with the *fact*; the *isomorphism* *account*; the *reply* is *contested*, and it *faces* the *‘*facts* *as* *descriptions*’* *problem*).

**(ii) The *‘*facts* *are* *not* *independent*’* *objection*** (Frege, "The Thought," 1918; Strawson, "Truth," 1950). The *facts* are *constituted* by *true* *propositions* (a *fact* *is* a *true* *thought*), and the *correspondence* *relation* is *circular* (the *proposition* *corresponds* to a *fact*, and the *fact* *is* the *proposition*).

**The *reply***. The *facts* are *worldly* (they are *not* *thoughts*; they are *states* of *affairs*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *fact* (the *truthmaker*) from the *truth* (the *proposition*). The *reply* *presupposes* the *truthmaker* *principle* (§6), and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *negative* *truths* *objection* (§2.2).

### 2.2 Austin and the neo-classical development

**The *thesis*** (Austin, "Truth," 1950; the *contemporary* *correspondence* *theorists*; Armstrong 1997, 2004; Bigelow 1988). Truth is the *correspondence* of a *statement* to a *state* of *affairs*, and the *state* of *affairs* is a *worldly* *entity* (a *complex* of *particulars* and *universals*; Ch. 15, §4.2).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The statement is a description (a representation of a state of affairs).} \\
2. & \text{The statement is true iff the state of affairs obtains (the state is real).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The truth is the correspondence to the obtaining state of affairs.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *grounds* the *truth* in the *worldly* *states* of *affairs* (the *states* are *real*, *complex*, and *independent* of the *statement*), and it *fits* the *truthmaker* *principle* (§6).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *negative* *truths* *objection***. "There are no unicorns" is *true*, and it *seems* to *require* a *negative* *state* of *affairs* (the *absence* of unicorns). But an *absence* is *nothing*, and *nothing* *cannot* *make* a *statement* *true* (Ch. 22, §2.3).

**The *reply*** (Armstrong 1997, Ch. 8; 2004, Ch. 7). The *truthmaker* is the *totality* *fact* (the *higher*-*order* *fact* that the *totality* of *things* *is* *such* that *no* *unicorn* *exists*), and the *reply* *posits* a *higher*-*order* *entity*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *general* *truths* *objection***. "All ravens are black" is *true*, and the *truthmaker* is the *totality* of *all* *ravens* (the *fact* *that* *each* *is* *black* *and* *that* *there* *are* *no* *others*). The *totality* *fact* is a *higher*-*order* *entity*, and the *positing* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *modal* *truths* *objection***. "There could have been a blue swan" is *true*, and the *truthmaker* is a *possible* *world* (or a *modal* *fact*). The *truthmaker* *commits* us to *modal* *realism* or *ersatzism* (Ch. 16, §2), and the *commitment* is *contested*.

**(iv) The *mathematical* *truths* *objection***. "2+2=4" is *true*, and the *truthmaker* is a *mathematical* *object* (or *structure*). The *truthmaker* *commits* us to *mathematical* *platonism* (Ch. 15, §6), and the *commitment* is *contested*.

### 2.3 The slingshot argument

**The *argument*** (Frege, *Grundgesetze*, 1893, §13; Church, "A Formulation of the Logic of Sense and Denotation," 1951; Davidson, "True to the Facts," 1969). The *argument* *shows* that *if* *sentences* *refer* to *facts*, *all* *true* *sentences* *refer* to the *same* *fact*, and the *correspondence* *theory* *collapses* (the *facts* are *not* *fine*-*grained*).

**The *argument* (the *simplified* *form*; the *assumptions* are *the* *substitutivity* of *logically* *equivalent* *sentences* *salva* *veritate*, and the *validity* of the *definite* *description* *logic*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{“Snow is white” is true.} \\
2. & \text{“Snow is white” is logically equivalent to “the unique } x \text{ such that } (x = x \wedge \text{snow is white})” = \text{“the unique } x \text{ such that } (x = x)”. \\
3. & \text{The two sentences refer to the same fact (the substitution preserves the reference, if the reference is the fact).} \\
4. & \text{The argument generalizes: any true sentence refers to the same fact (the Great Fact).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The correspondence theory cannot distinguish facts (the facts are not fine-grained).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *correspondence* *theory* *requires* a *fine*-*grained* *notion* of *fact* (the *states* of *affairs* *are* *structured*), and the *slingshot* *shows* that the *sentential* *semantics* *cannot* *provide* it (the *logical* *equivalents* *are* *substitutable*).

**The *objections* to the *slingshot***.

**(i) The *substitutivity* *assumption*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *substitution* of *logically* *equivalent* *sentences* *fails* in *intensional* *contexts* (the *belief* *contexts*; Ch. 25, §4), and the *assumption* is *false*.

**(ii) The *structural* *response*** (Armstrong 1997, Ch. 8; the *truthmaker* *theorists*). The *states* of *affairs* are *structured* (they are *composed* of *particulars* and *universals*), and the *structure* *distinguishes* the *facts* *independently* of the *sentences*. The *reply* *grounds* the *facts* in the *world*, not in the *language*, and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *deflationary* *response*** (§5). The *slingshot* *shows* that the *correspondence* *theory* *fails*, and the *failure* *supports* the *deflationary* *view* (the *truth* is *not* a *relation* to a *fact*; it is a *logical* *property*). The *response* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The state of the correspondence theory

**The *assessment***. The *correspondence* *theory* is *the* *most* *intuitive* *account*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Russell (multiple relation)** | Grounds truth in the world | The correspondence relation is mysterious |
| **Austin (statement–state of affairs)** | Fits the truthmaker principle | The negative, general, modal truths |
| **Neo-classical (Armstrong)** | Grounds truth in structured states | The slingshot; the truthmaker regress |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *correspondence*: the *truth* is *grounded* in the *worldly* *states* of *affairs* (the *truthmaker* *insight*), and the *grounding* is *not* a *simple* *relation* of *resemblance* (the *slingshot* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *negative* and *general* *truths* *require* *higher*-*order* *entities*.

## 3. The Coherence Theory

### 3.1 Bradley and Blanshard

**The *thesis*** (Bradley, *Appearance and Reality*, 1893, Chs. XV–XVI; Blanshard, *The Nature of Thought*, 1939, Chs. XXV–XXVI; the *British* *idealist* *tradition*). Truth is the *coherence* of a *proposition* with the *whole* *system* of *propositions* (the *complete* *system* *of* *reality*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ coheres with the whole system } \Sigma
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The truth of a proposition depends on its relations to other propositions (the holistic premise; Ch. 8, §3).} \\
2. & \text{The relations are constitutive of the truth (the coherence is the truth).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The truth is the coherence (the coherence theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *holistic* *character* of *inquiry* (the *truth* of a *proposition* *depends* on the *whole* *system*), and it *fits* the *idealist* *metaphysics* (the *Absolute* is the *whole*; Ch. 20, §3).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *isolation* *objection* (the *most* *serious*; Ch. 8, §3.3). A *coherent* *system* of *propositions* can be *entirely* *false* (a *well*-*written* *novel*, a *consistent* *mathematical* *fiction*), and the *coherence* *does* not *guarantee* the *truth*.

**The *reply*** (the *idealists*). The *system* is *not* *isolated*; it *includes* the *experiences* (the *sensory* *input*), and the *coherence* is *constrained* by the *input*. The *reply* *hybridizes* the *theory* (the *coherence* *plus* the *empirical* *input*; Ch. 8, §3.3), and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *plural* *systems* *objection*. There are *many* *coherent* *systems* (the *different* *paradigms*, the *different* *cultures*), and the *coherence* *does* not *select* among them (the *relativist* *consequence*; Ch. 13, §3).

**The *reply*** (Blanshard). The *systems* are *not* *equally* *coherent* (the *degree* of *coherence* *varies*, and the *most* *coherent* *is* the *true* *one*), and the *reply* *introduces* the *degree* of *coherence* (the *reply* is *contested*).

**(iii) The *definition* *objection*. What *is* *coherence*? The *notion* is *vague* (the *logical* *consistency*, the *explanatory* *connections*, the *systematic* *unity*), and the *theory* *fails* to *specify* it (Ch. 8, §3.2).

### 3.2 The ideal of a completed science

**The *thesis*** (the *pragmatist* *and* *idealist* *tradition*; Peirce, "How to Make Our Ideas Clear," 1878; the *convergence* *thesis*). The *truth* is the *ideal* *limit* of *inquiry* (the *opinion* *that* *inquiry* *would* *reach* *at* the *end* of *inquiry*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ is the opinion that inquiry would converge on}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *connection* between *truth* and *inquiry*, and it *fits* the *scientific* *practice* (the *convergence* of *inquiry*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *end* *of* *inquiry*’* *objection*. The *inquiry* *may* *never* *end* (the *human* *inquiry* is *finite*, and the *universe* *may* *end*), and the *ideal* *limit* is *unavailable*.

**The *reply***. The *ideal* is a *regulative* *ideal* (it *guides* the *inquiry* without *being* *reached*), and the *reply* *fits* the *Kantian* *framework*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*convergence* *is* *not* *truth*’* *objection*. The *convergence* *may* be a *social* *phenomenon* (the *consensus*), and the *consensus* *may* be *false* (the *scientific* *orthodoxies* that *turn* out to be *wrong*).

**The *reply***. The *convergence* is *constrained* by the *world* (the *inquiry* *aims* at the *truth*, and the *aim* *constrains* the *convergence*), and the *reply* *presupposes* the *correspondence* *theory*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.3 The state of the coherence theory

**The *assessment***. The *coherence* *theory* is *influential* in the *holistic* *tradition*, and it is *qualified* by the *isolation* *objection*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Bradley (Absolute)** | Fits the idealist metaphysics | The isolation objection |
| **Blanshard (degree)** | Fits the holistic inquiry | The degree is vague |
| **Peirce (ideal limit)** | Fits the convergence of inquiry | The end of inquiry is unavailable |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *coherence*: the *truth* is *constrained* by the *coherence* (the *holistic* *insight*), and the *coherence* *is* *not* *sufficient* (the *isolation* *objection*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *coherence* *requires* the *empirical* *input* (the *hybrid* *view*; Ch. 8, §3.3).

## 4. The Pragmatist Theory

### 4.1 Peirce and the pragmatic maxim

**The *thesis*** (Peirce, "How to Make Our Ideas Clear," 1878; *Collected Papers*, 1931–58). The *meaning* of a *concept* is its *practical* *consequences*, and the *truth* of a *proposition* is its *role* in *inquiry* (the *convergence* *thesis*; §3.2).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ is the opinion fated to be agreed upon by the community of inquirers}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *connects* the *truth* to the *practice* of *inquiry*, and it *fits* the *scientific* *practice* (the *self*-*correcting* *method*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*fated* *agreement*’* *objection*. The *fated* *agreement* *presupposes* a *deterministic* *universe* (the *agreement* *is* *fated*), and the *presupposition* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *guarantee*’* *objection*. The *agreement* *may* *never* *be* *reached*, and the *theory* *makes* the *truth* *unavailable* (the *reply* is the *regulative* *ideal*).

### 4.2 James and the expedient

**The *thesis*** (James, *Pragmatism*, 1907, Ch. VI; *The Meaning of Truth*, 1909). The *true* is the *expedient* in the way of *belief* (the *“*true* *is* *the* *name* *of* *whatever* *proves* *itself* *good* *in* the *way* of *belief*”*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ is expedient (useful, practical, satisfying) in the long run}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *practical* *dimension* of *truth* (the *beliefs* *are* *instruments* for *action*), and it *fits* the *pragmatist* *epistemology* (Ch. 10, §6).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*useful* *falsehood*’* *objection* (the *most* *serious*; Russell, "William James's Conception of Truth," 1908; *Philosophical Essays*, 1910). A *false* *belief* can be *useful* (the *belief* that the *disease* *is* *curable*, even if it is *false*), and the *theory* *implies* that the *belief* is *true*. The *consequence* is *counterintuitive* (the *truth* is *not* *identified* with the *utility*).

**The *reply*** (James 1909). The *utility* is the *long*-*run* *utility* (the *belief* that *proves* *itself* *in* the *whole* *of* *experience*), and the *reply* *distances* the *theory* from the *short*-*run* *utility*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *long*-*run* *utility* is *hard* to *specify*, and it *may* *coincide* with the *truth* only *contingently*).

**(ii) The *‘*circularity*’* *objection*. The *utility* *presupposes* the *truth* (the *belief* is *useful* *because* it is *true*), and the *theory* *is* *circular*.

**The *reply***. The *utility* *constitutes* the *truth* (the *belief* is *true* *because* it is *useful*, not *vice* *versa*), and the *reply* *is* *the* *pragmatist* *thesis*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.3 Dewey and the warranted assertibility

**The *thesis*** (Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*, 1938; *Essays in Experimental Logic*, 1916). The *truth* is *warranted* *assertibility*: a *proposition* is *true* if it is *warranted* *by* the *inquiry* (the *resolution* of a *problematic* *situation*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad p \text{ is warrantedly assertible} \text{ (in the inquiry)}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *connects* the *truth* to the *inquiry* and to the *practice*, and it *fits* the *pragmatist* *epistemology* (Ch. 8, §5).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*warrant* *is* *not* *truth*’* *objection*. The *warrant* is *defeasible* (the *warranted* *assertion* *can* be *false*), and the *theory* *identifies* the *truth* with a *defeasible* *property*.

**The *reply***. The *warrant* is *idealized* (the *warrant* *at* the *end* of *inquiry*), and the *reply* *reintroduces* the *ideal* *limit* (§3.2). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *normative* *force*’* *objection*. The *warranted* *assertibility* is *descriptive* (it *describes* the *practice*), and the *truth* is *normative* (it *prescribes* the *assertion*).

**The *reply***. The *warrant* is *normative* (it *prescribes* the *assertion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.4 The state of the pragmatist theory

**The *assessment***. The *pragmatist* *theory* is *influential*, and it is *qualified* by the *useful* *falsehood* *objection*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Peirce (convergence)** | Fits the scientific inquiry | The fated agreement |
| **James (expedient)** | Fits the practical dimension | The useful falsehood |
| **Dewey (warranted assertibility)** | Fits the inquiry | The warrant is not truth |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *pragmatism*: the *truth* is *connected* to the *inquiry* and to the *practice* (the *pragmatist* *insight*), and the *connection* is *not* an *identity* (the *truth* *constrains* the *inquiry*, not the *reverse*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *practice* *presupposes* the *truth*.

## 5. The Deflationary Turn

### 5.1 Ramsey and the redundancy theory

**The *thesis*** (Ramsey, "Facts and Propositions," 1927; Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, Ch. 5). To *say* that a *proposition* is *true* is *not* to *say* *anything* *about* it (the *word* "true" *adds* *nothing* to the *proposition*); the *assertion* of *truth* is *redundant*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(\langle p \rangle) \quad \text{iff} \quad p
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{“It is true that snow is white” says the same as “snow is white”.} \\
2. & \text{“Snow is white” says nothing about truth (it is about snow).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The word “true” is redundant (the redundancy theory).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *correspondence* *relation*, and it *fits* the *T*-schema (the *disquotation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*generalization*’* *objection* (the *most* *serious*; the *contemporary* *literature*; Horwich 1990, Ch. 1). The *word* "true" is *not* *redundant* in *general* *claims* ("Everything she said is true," "Some of his beliefs are true"), and the *redundancy* *theory* *cannot* *account* for the *generalization*.

**The *reply***. The *generalization* is a *logical* *device* (the *infinite* *conjunction* "she said that $p_1$ and $p_1$, and she said that $p_2$ and $p_2$, …"), and the *reply* *grounds* the *generalization* in the *truth* *predicate* as a *device* for *expressing* the *infinite* *conjunctions*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*opaque* *contexts*’* *objection*. The *truth* *predicate* *behaves* *differently* in *opaque* *contexts* ("Lois believes that 'Superman can fly' is true"), and the *redundancy* *theory* *cannot* *explain* the *difference*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *opaque* *contexts* *involve* the *sentence* (the *quotation*), not the *proposition*, and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *truth* *of* the *sentence* from the *truth* *of* the *proposition*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.2 Disquotationalism

**The *thesis*** (Quine, *Philosophy of Logic*, 1970, Ch. 1; "Truth," in *Pursuit of Truth*, 1990; Leeds, "Theories of Reference and Truth," 1978). The *truth* *predicate* is a *device* for *disquotation*: the *schema* "‘$p$’ is true iff $p$" *exhausts* the *meaning* of "true."

**The *structure***.

$$
\forall s\, (\text{True}(s) \leftrightarrow \text{the content of } s)
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *generalization* (the *truth* *predicate* *expresses* the *infinite* *conjunctions*), and it *avoids* the *metaphysical* *commitments* of the *correspondence* *theory*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *substantivity*’* *objection*. The *disquotationalism* *does* not *explain* *why* the *true* *sentences* are *true* (the *truth* *is* *reduced* to a *linguistic* *device*), and the *theory* *loses* the *world*.

**The *reply*** (Quine 1990, Ch. 1). The *world* is *described* by the *true* *sentences* (the *truth* *is* *immanent* to the *theory*), and the *reply* *is* *the* *naturalistic* *account* (the *truth* is *internal* to the *science*; Ch. 22, §3). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *normativity*’* *objection*. The *disquotationalism* *does* not *explain* the *value* of *truth* (the *truth* is a *mere* *device*), and the *theory* *loses* the *normative* *dimension* (§9).

**The *reply***. The *normativity* is *external* to the *truth* *predicate* (it is *grounded* in the *practice* of *inquiry*), and the *reply* *separates* the *semantics* from the *pragmatics*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 Minimalism

**The *thesis*** (Horwich, *Truth*, 1990; *Truth-Meaning-Reality*, 2010). The *truth* *predicate* is *minimal*: the *T*-schema *exhausts* its *meaning* (the *propositions* of the *form* "$\langle p \rangle$ is true iff $p$" *are* *all* the *facts* about *truth*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{The T-schema} \text{ (the equivalence schema) is the whole theory of truth}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *generalization*, the *opaque* *contexts*, and the *T*-schema, *without* the *metaphysical* *commitments* of the *correspondence* *theory*. The *theory* is the *most* *sophisticated* *deflationary* *account*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *minimalism* *does* not *explain* *why* the *truth* *is* *valuable*, *why* the *truth* *is* a *goal* of *inquiry*, or *why* the *truth* is *normative* (§9). The *objection* is *serious*, and the *reply* (the *pragmatic* *account*; the *reply* is *contested*) is *incomplete*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *correspondence*’* *objection*. The *minimalism* *does* not *capture* the *intuition* that the *true* *propositions* *correspond* to the *world* (the *truthmaker* *intuition*; §6), and the *theory* *loses* the *worldly* *ground*.

**The *reply*** (Horwich 2010, Ch. 2). The *correspondence* *intuition* is *explained* by the *T*-schema (the *proposition* is *true* *because* the *world* is *thus*-and-*so*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *correspondence* in the *schema*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.4 Prosententialism

**The *thesis*** (Grover, Camp & Belnap, "A Prosentential Theory of Truth," 1975; Brandom, *Making It Explicit*, 1994, Ch. 5). The *truth* *predicate* is a *prosentential* *device* (it *functions* like a *pronoun* for a *sentence*), and it is *not* a *predicate* of *propositions*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{“That is true”} \quad \text{functions as} \quad \text{“That” (a pronoun for the sentence)}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *avoids* the *ontological* *commitment* to *propositions* (the *truth* *predicate* is a *device*, not a *property*), and it *fits* the *inferentialist* *account* (Ch. 31, §3.4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *property*’* *objection*. The *theory* *denies* that *truth* is a *property*, and the *denial* is *contested* (the *correspondence* *intuition*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *generalization*’* *objection*. The *prosentential* *account* *struggles* with the *general* *claims* ("Everything she said is true"), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.5 The state of deflationism

**The *assessment***. The *deflationary* *theories* are the *most* *influential* *contemporary* *accounts*, and they are *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Redundancy (Ramsey)** | Simple | The generalization |
| **Disquotationalism (Quine)** | Explains generalization | The normativity; the substantivity |
| **Minimalism (Horwich)** | Systematic | The value; the correspondence |
| **Prosententialism (Grover et al.)** | Avoids propositions | The property; the generalization |

**The *most* *defensible* *version***. A *qualified* *minimalism*: the *T*-schema *exhausts* the *semantics* of "true" (the *deflationary* *insight*), and the *schema* *does* not *exhaust* the *metaphysics* of *truthmaking* (the *correspondence* *insight*) or the *normativity* of *truth* (the *pragmatist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *combination* is *unresolved*.

## 6. Truthmakers and the Metaphysics of Truth

### 6.1 The truthmaker principle

**The *thesis*** (Armstrong, *Truth and Truthmakers*, 2004; Bigelow, *The Reality of Numbers*, 1988; Mulligan, Simons & Smith, "Truth-Makers," 1984). Every *truth* has a *truthmaker*: a *thing* whose *existence* *necessitates* the *truth* of the *proposition*.

**The *structure*** (Ch. 22, §2).

$$
\forall p\, (p \to \exists x\, (x \Vdash p))
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The truth is a relation between a proposition and the world.} \\
2. & \text{The relation requires a relatum in the world (the truthmaker).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Every truth has a truthmaker.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *principle* *grounds* the *truth* in the *world*, and it *provides* a *criterion* of *ontological* *commitment* (Ch. 14, §3).

**The *objections*** (the *problem* *cases*; Ch. 22, §2.3).

| The *case* | The *challenge* | The *response* |
|---|---|---|
| **Negative** | "There are no unicorns" | The *totality* *fact* |
| **General** | "All ravens are black" | The *totality* *fact* |
| **Modal** | "There could have been a blue swan" | The *possible* *world* |
| **Mathematical** | "2+2=4" | The *mathematical* *object* |
| **Counterfactual** | "If I had left, I would have caught the train" | The *counterfactual* *fact* |

**The *significance***. The *principle* is *the* *most* *influential* *metaphysical* *account* of *truth*, and it *faces* the *problem* *cases*.

### 6.2 Modest truthmaker theory

**The *thesis*** (Lewis, "Truthmaking and Difference-Making," 2001; Milne, "Not Every Truth Has a Truthmaker," 2005; MacBride, "Truthmakers," 2005). *Some* *truths* have *truthmakers*, but *not* *all* (the *principle* is *restricted* to the *positive*, *atomic* *truths*).

**The *argument***. The *maximalist* *principle* *generates* the *problem* *cases*, and the *problem* *cases* are *not* *solved* by the *proposed* *truthmakers* (the *truthmakers* are *ad* *hoc*). The *modest* *principle* *avoids* the *problems* and *preserves* the *insight* (the *positive* *truths* are *made* *true*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *arbitrary* *restriction* *objection*. Why *should* the *positive* *truths* have *truthmakers* and the *negative* *truths* not? The *restriction* *seems* *ad* *hoc*.

**(ii) The *explanatory* *impotence* *objection*. If *some* *truths* *do* not *have* *truthmakers*, the *principle* *cannot* *ground* the *truth* *of* those *truths*, and the *question* "what makes *them* true?" *returns*.

**The *reply*** (Lewis 2001). The *truthmaker* *principle* is a *form* of the *difference*-*making* *intuition* (Ch. 18, §2), and the *intuition* *does* not *require* a *special* *entity* for every *truth*. The *truthmaker* is the *thing* whose *difference* *makes* the *truth* *true*, and the *difference* can be *structural*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 Truthmaking and grounding

**The *distinction***.

| Relation | The *relata* | The *direction* |
|---|---|---|
| **Truthmaking** | Entity → Proposition | World → Truth |
| **Grounding** | Fact → Fact | More basic → Less basic |

**The *connection***. The *truthmaker* *grounds* the *truth* (the *state* of *affairs* *grounds* the *proposition*'s *being* *true*), and the *two* *relations* are *aspects* of a *single* *structure* (Ch. 22, §2.5).

**The *significance***. The *distinction* *clarifies* the *truthmaker* *debate*: the *maximalist* is *committed* to a *truthmaker* for every *truth*, and the *modest* *theorist* is *committed* to a *ground* for every *truth* (the *ground* may be *structural*, not *entity*-*based*).

### 6.4 The state of the truthmaker debate

**The *assessment***. The *truthmaker* *principle* is *influential*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *maximalism*: the *truthmakers* are *structural* (the *totality* *facts*, the *structural* *facts*), and the *positing* of the *higher*-*order* *entities* is *defensible* *in* the *absence* of a *better* *alternative*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *higher*-*order* *entities* is *contested*.

## 7. Alethic Pluralism and the Shape of the Debate

### 7.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Wright, *Truth and Objectivity*, 1992; *Saving the Differences*, 2003; Lynch, *Truth in Context*, 1998; *True to Life*, 2004). There are *several* *kinds* of *truth* (the *scientific*, the *moral*, the *mathematical*, the *aesthetic*), and the *kinds* *differ* in their *nature* (the *correspondence* for the *empirical*, the *coherence* for the *mathematical*, the *warrant* for the *moral*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Truth}_{\text{empirical}} = \text{correspondence}; \quad \text{Truth}_{\text{moral}} = \text{warrant}; \quad \text{Truth}_{\text{mathematical}} = \text{coherence}
$$

**The *argument* (Lynch 2004, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The empirical truths correspond to the world (the truthmaker intuition).} \\
2. & \text{The moral truths depend on the warrant (the moral truth is not a correspondence to a moral fact; the realism is contested; Ch. 44, §2).} \\
3. & \text{The mathematical truths depend on the coherence of the axioms (the structure; Ch. 15, §7).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The truth is plural (the alethic pluralism).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *view* *accommodates* the *different* *domains* *without* *reducing* them to a *single* *model*, and it *fits* the *practice* of the *different* *sciences*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*disunity*’* *objection*. If the *truth* is *plural*, what *unifies* the *kinds*? The *word* "true" *would* be *ambiguous* (the *equivocation*; Ch. 2, §2.3), and the *unity* of the *concept* is *lost*.

**The *reply*** (Lynch 2004, Ch. 2). The *kinds* are *unified* by a *functional* *role* (the *truth* is *the* *property* that *plays* the *truth* *role*: the *aim* of *inquiry*, the *norm* of *assertion*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *unity* in the *function*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *difference*’* *objection*. The *different* *domains* *may* *differ* in *epistemology* (the *warrant*) *without* *differing* in *truth* (the *correspondence* is *universal*). The *objection* *supports* the *monism*.

**The *reply*** (Wright 1992, Ch. 4). The *differences* in the *epistemic* *constraints* *track* the *differences* in the *truth* (the *truth* *is* *epistemically* *constrained* in the *moral* *domain*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *pluralism* in the *epistemic* *differences*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.2 The inflationism/deflationism axis

**The *distinction***. The *debate* *between* *inflationism* (the *truth* is a *substantive* *property*) and *deflationism* (the *truth* is a *thin* *device*) is the *central* *axis* of the *contemporary* *debate* (Ch. 22, §3; §5).

**The *mapping***.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflationism** | Truth is a substantive property (correspondence) | Armstrong; the *correspondence* *theorists* |
| **Deflationism** | Truth is a thin logical device | Horwich; Quine; the *minimalists* |
| **Pluralism** | Truth is substantive but plural | Wright; Lynch |
| **Primitivism** | Truth is primitive and indefinable | Davidson (1996); the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *significance***. The *axis* *structures* the *debate*, and the *choice* *among* the *positions* *determines* the *theory* of *meaning* (Ch. 31, §3.2), the *theory* of *inquiry* (Ch. 10, §4), and the *theory* of *value* (§9).

### 7.3 Davidson's primitivism

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, "The Folly of Trying to Define Truth," 1996; *Truth, Language, and History*, 2005). The *truth* is *primitive* and *indefinable* (the *attempt* to *define* it *fails*, and the *failure* *shows* that the *concept* is *fundamental*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The definitions of truth (the correspondence, the coherence, the pragmatist) all fail (the objections are decisive).} \\
2. & \text{The failure shows that the truth is not definable (the primitive concept).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The truth is primitive (the primitivism).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *view* *avoids* the *failures* of the *definitions*, and it *fits* the *Tarskian* *account* (the *truth* is *defined* *for* a *language*, but not *for* the *concept*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *primitivism* *does* not *explain* the *truth* (it *labels* it *primitive*), and the *theory* *is* *uninformative*.

**The *reply***. The *explanation* is *not* *required* (the *primitive* *concepts* are *the* *foundations* of the *theory*), and the *reply* *fits* the *foundationalist* *tradition* (Ch. 8, §2). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *normativity*’* *objection*. The *primitivism* *does* not *explain* the *value* of *truth* (§9).

### 7.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment***. The *alethic* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *pluralism*: the *truth* is *thin* at the *level* of the *concept* (the *deflationary* *insight*), *substantive* at the *level* of the *truthmakers* (the *correspondence* *insight*), and *plural* at the *level* of the *domains* (the *pluralist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *combination* is *unresolved*.

## 8. Relativism about Truth

### 8.1 The varieties

**The *distinction***. The *relativism* about *truth* is *not* a *single* *position*, and the *varieties* *differ* in their *relata*.

| Variety | The *claim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Alethic relativism** | The *truth* is *relative* to a *framework* | The *conceptual* *schemes* |
| **Epistemic relativism** | The *justification* is *relative* to an *epistemic* *system* | The *scientific* *paradigms* (Ch. 13, §3) |
| **Assessment relativism** | The *truth* is *relative* to the *assessor* | The *taste* *predicates* (MacFarlane 2014) |
| **Cultural relativism** | The *truth* is *relative* to a *culture* | The *moral* *norms* |

**The *significance***. The *varieties* are *distinct*, and the *arguments* for and *against* *differ* *across* the *varieties* (Ch. 13, §3).

### 8.2 MacFarlane's assessment sensitivity

**The *thesis*** (MacFarlane, *Assessment Sensitivity*, 2014; "The Assessment Sensitivity of Knowledge Attributions," 2005). The *truth* of a *sentence* is *relative* to the *context* of the *assessor* (the *person* *evaluating* the *claim*), not *only* to the *context* of the *speaker*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{True}(p, c_s, c_a) \quad \text{(the truth depends on the speaker's context } c_s \text{ and the assessor's context } c_a)
$$

**The *argument* (MacFarlane 2014, Ch. 3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The faultless disagreement (two people disagree about a taste predicate, and both are right from their own perspective).} \\
2. & \text{The faultless disagreement is explained by the assessment sensitivity (the truth depends on the assessor).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The truth is assessment-sensitive (for the taste predicates).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *faultless* *disagreement* (a *central* *datum* of the *taste* *debate*), and it *fits* the *retraction* *data* (the *speaker* *can* *retract* a *claim* *without* *implying* that the *original* *claim* was *false*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*metaphysical* *oddity*’* *objection*. The *truth* *depends* on the *assessor*, and the *dependence* is *metaphysically* *odd* (the *truth* *is* *not* *a* *property* of the *world* but a *relation* to the *assessor*).

**The *reply***. The *dependence* is *semantic* (the *truth* *predicate* is *assessment*-*sensitive*, like "tasty"), and the *reply* *fits* the *relativist* *semantics*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *metaphysical* *status* of the *assessment*-*sensitivity* is *unclear*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *disagreement*’* *objection*. If the *truth* *depends* on the *assessor*, the *parties* *do* not *really* *disagree* (they *talk* *past* each other), and the *consequence* *undermines* the *intuition* of *disagreement*.

**The *reply***. The *disagreement* is *genuine* (the *parties* *disagree* *about* the *taste* *predicate*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *disagreement* in the *assessment* *sensitivity*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 8.3 The self-refutation objection

**The *argument*** (the *standard* *objection*; Ch. 13, §4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The relativist claims that the truth is relative.} \\
2. & \text{The claim is itself a truth claim (the relativist asserts it as true).} \\
3. & \text{If the truth is relative, the claim is true only relative to a framework.} \\
4. & \text{The claim cannot bind the opponent (the opponent can reject the framework).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The relativism is self-undermining (the argument is contested; Ch. 13, §4).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *replies***.

**(i) The *indexical* *reply* (Ch. 13, §4.2). The *relativist* *claim* is *indexical* ("true for me"), and the *claim* is *not* *contradicted* by the *disagreement*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *meta*-*level* *reply* (Ch. 13, §4.3). The *relativist* *claim* is a *meta*-*claim*, and the *meta*-*claim* is *assessed* by the *meta*-*standards*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 8.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment***. The *relativism* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *anti*-*relativism*: the *truth* is *objective* (the *relativism* *fails*), and the *objectivity* is *compatible* with the *context*-*sensitivity* of *some* *predicates* (the *taste*, the *knowledge*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *line* *between* the *context*-*sensitivity* and the *relativism* is *contested*.

## 9. The Value of Truth

### 9.1 The question

**The *question***. *Why* is *truth* *valuable*? *Why* is the *truth* a *goal* of *inquiry*, and *why* is *falsehood* a *defect*?

**The *data***. The *truth* is *valuable* in *three* *ways*.

| The *way* | The *claim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrumental** | The *truth* is *valuable* *because* it *leads* to *success* | The *true* *belief* *about* the *bridge* |
| **Intrinsic** | The *truth* is *valuable* *for* its *own* *sake* | The *curiosity*, the *understanding* |
| **Normative** | The *truth* is *valuable* *because* it is the *norm* of *assertion* (the *truth* *is* what we *ought* to *believe* and *assert*) | The *truthfulness*, the *honesty* |

**The *significance***. The *three* *values* are *distinct*, and the *theory* of *truth* *must* *explain* them (or *explain* them *away*).

### 9.2 The swamping objection

**The *argument*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *analogue* of the *value* of *knowledge* *problem*; Ch. 7, §7.2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The truth is valuable because it leads to success (the instrumental value).} \\
2. & \text{If the truth is valuable only instrumentally, the value is exhausted by the success.} \\
3. & \text{The success can be achieved without the truth (the useful falsehood), and the truth can fail to achieve the success (the inconvenient truth).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The instrumental value does not explain the distinctive value of truth (the swamping).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *instrumental* *account* *fails* to *explain* *why* the *truth* is *valuable* *in* *itself*, and the *theory* *needs* an *intrinsic* or *normative* *account*.

### 9.3 The accounts

**The *accounts***.

| Account | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrumentalism** | Truth is valuable as a means to success | The *pragmatist* *tradition* |
| **Intrinsic** | Truth is valuable for its own sake (the *understanding*, the *curiosity*) | The *Aristotelian* *tradition*; Lynch (2004) |
| **Normative** | Truth is the norm of assertion (the *truth* is what we *ought* to believe) | The *contemporary* *literature* (the *truth* *norm*; Ch. 7, §4.2) |
| **Constitutive** | Truth is valuable because it is constitutive of inquiry (the *aim* of the *practice*) | The *contemporary* *literature* (the *aim* *of* *inquiry*) |

**The *normative* *account*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *truth*-*norm*; Ch. 7, §4.2). The *truth* is valuable *because* it is the *norm* of *assertion* and *belief* (the *truth* is what we *ought* to *believe*, and the *norm* is *constitutive* of the *practice* of *inquiry*). The *account* *grounds* the *value* in the *practice*, and it *fits* the *contemporary* *literature* (the *truth*-*norm*).

**The *intrinsic* *account*** (Lynch, *True to Life*, 2004, Ch. 6). The *truth* is valuable *because* it is *constitutive* of *understanding* (the *understanding* *requires* the *truth*, and the *understanding* is *intrinsically* *valuable*). The *account* *grounds* the *value* in the *understanding*, and it *fits* the *Aristotelian* *tradition*.

### 9.4 The connection to inquiry

**The *thesis*** (Price, "Truth as Convenient Friction," 2003; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *truth* is valuable *because* it *provides* the *friction* that *makes* *inquiry* *possible* (the *truth* is the *constraint* that *corrects* the *beliefs*, and the *correction* is *constitutive* of the *practice*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The inquiry requires a constraint (the beliefs must be corrected by the world).} \\
2. & \text{The constraint is the truth (the true beliefs survive the correction, and the false beliefs are revised).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The truth is valuable because it provides the friction (the constraint of inquiry).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *connects* the *value* of *truth* to the *practice* of *inquiry*, and it *fits* the *pragmatist* *tradition*.

**The *objection***. The *account* *makes* the *truth* *instrumental* (the *friction* is a *means* to the *inquiry*), and the *account* *swamps* the *value* (§9.2). The *reply* (the *friction* is *constitutive* of the *practice*, and the *practice* is *intrinsically* *valuable*) is *contested*.

### 9.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment***. The *value* of *truth* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *normativism*: the *truth* is *valuable* *because* it is the *norm* of *assertion* and *belief* (the *normative* *insight*), and the *norm* is *constitutive* of the *practice* of *inquiry* (the *constitutive* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *intrinsic* *value* of the *truth* is *not* *fully* *explained*.

## 10. The State of the Debate

### 10.1 What is settled

- **The *truthbearer* is the *proposition* (the *content*), and the *sentence* and the *utterance* are *derivative*.
- **The *correspondence* *theory* is *intuitive*, and it *faces* the *slingshot*, the *negative* *truths*, and the *general* *truths*.
- **The *coherence* *theory* *captures* the *holistic* *character* of *inquiry*, and it *faces* the *isolation* *objection*.
- **The *pragmatist* *theory* *connects* the *truth* to the *practice*, and it *faces* the *useful* *falsehood* *objection*.
- **The *deflationary* *theories* are the *most* *influential* *contemporary* *accounts*, and they *face* the *normativity* and the *substantivity* *objections*.
- **The *truthmaker* *principle* is *the* *most* *influential* *metaphysical* *account* of *truth*, and it *faces* the *problem* *cases*.
- **The *alethic* *pluralism* is *promising*, and it *faces* the *disunity* *objection*.
- **The *relativism* about *truth* is *influential* in the *taste* and *knowledge* *debates*, and it *faces* the *self*-*refutation* *objection*.
- **The *value* of *truth* is *real*, and the *instrumental* *account* is *incomplete*.

### 10.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *truth*.** Is it *substantive* (correspondence), *thin* (deflation), *plural*, or *primitive*?
- **The *truthmaker* *principle*.** Is it *maximalist* or *modest*?
- **The *alethic* *pluralism*.** Is the *truth* *plural*, and what *unifies* the *kinds*?
- **The *relativism*.** Is the *truth* *assessment*-*sensitive*, and *for* *which* *domains*?
- **The *value* of *truth*.** Is the *truth* *intrinsically* *valuable*, and *why*?
- **The *connection* between *truth* and *meaning*.** Does the *truth*-*conditional* *semantics* *require* a *substantive* *theory* of *truth* (Ch. 31, §3.2)?

### 10.3 The methodological lesson

The *chapter* is a *case* *study* in the *method* of *Part* I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *truthbearers*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *slingshot*, the *isolation*, the *useful* *falsehood* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *Tarskian* *T*-schema, the *truthmaker* *principle*, the *assessment* sensitivity — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *truth* is a *field* in *transition*. The *classical* *theories* are *untenable* in their *pure* forms; the *deflationary* *theories* are *dominant* but *incomplete*; the *truthmaker* *account* is *influential* but *contested*; the *alethic* *pluralism* and the *relativism* are *promising* but *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled* *answer*, but a *sharper* *understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 10.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correspondence** | Truth is correspondence to fact | Intuitive; objective | The slingshot; the negative truths |
| **Coherence** | Truth is coherence with the system | Holistic | The isolation objection |
| **Pragmatism** | Truth is the expedient in belief | Fits the practice | The useful falsehood |
| **Redundancy** | "True" is redundant | Simple | The generalization |
| **Disquotationalism** | "True" is a disquotational device | Explains the generalization | The normativity |
| **Minimalism** | The T-schema exhausts truth | Systematic | The value; the correspondence |
| **Prosententialism** | "True" is a prosentence | Avoids propositions | The property |
| **Truthmaker** | Every truth has a truthmaker | Metaphysically robust | The problem cases |
| **Alethic pluralism** | Truth is plural | Fits the domains | The disunity |
| **Relativism** | Truth is assessment-sensitive | Fits the taste debate | Self-refutation |
| **Primitivism** | Truth is primitive | Avoids the failures | Unexplanatory |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *formal* *theories* of *truth*.** The *Tarskian* *account* (Tarski 1933, 1944) *defines* *truth* *for* a *formal* *language*, and the *definition* *faces* the *liar* *paradox* (Ch. 33, §1) and the *limitative* *theorems* (the *undefinability* of *arithmetic* *truth* in *arithmetic*). The *contemporary* *literature* (the *axiomatic* *theories* of *truth*, the *fixed*-*point* *theories*) is *technical* and *rich*, and it *connects* the *philosophy* of *truth* to the *mathematical* *logic*.

**2. The *truth*-*norm* *debate*.** The *norm* of *assertion* (the *truth*-*norm*; Williamson 2000, Ch. 11; Ch. 7, §4.2) is *contested* (the *knowledge*-*norm*, the *justification*-*norm*, the *reasonable*-*norm*). The *debate* *connects* the *theory* of *truth* to the *theory* of *assertion* (Ch. 34) and to the *theory* of *knowledge* (Ch. 7).

**3. The *truth* *and* *the* *sciences*.** The *scientific* *realism* *debate* (Ch. 40, §5) *presupposes* a *theory* of *truth* (the *approximate* *truth*, the *structural* *truth*), and the *theory* is *contested*. The *connection* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *central* to the *philosophy* of *science*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *truth*.** The *Buddhist* *tradition* *developed* the *two*-*truths* *theory* (the *conventional* *truth* and the *ultimate* *truth*; the *saṃvṛti*/*paramārtha* *distinction*), and the *theory* *anticipates* the *alethic* *pluralism*. The *Nyāya* *tradition* *defended* a *correspondence* *theory* (*pramā* as the *correspondence* to the *object*). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *ethics* of *truth*.** The *truth* is *valuable* not *only* *epistemically* but *ethically* (the *truthfulness*, the *honesty*, the *trust*), and the *ethics* of *truth* is *central* to the *political* *epistemology* (Ch. 11, §6) and to the *ethics* of *belief* (Ch. 4, §4.5). The *connection* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent*.

## Summary

- **The *problem* of *truth* *presupposes* the *answer* to the *question* "what is *true*?" (the *truthbearer*), and the *propositions* are the *primary* *truthbearers*.
- **The *correspondence* *theory*** (Russell, Austin, Armstrong) *grounds* the *truth* in the *world*, and it *faces* the *slingshot*, the *negative*, *general*, and *modal* *truths*.
- **The *coherence* *theory*** (Bradley, Blanshard) *captures* the *holistic* *character* of *inquiry*, and it *faces* the *isolation* *objection*.
- **The *pragmatist* *theory*** (Peirce, James, Dewey) *connects* the *truth* to the *practice*, and it *faces* the *useful* *falsehood* *objection*.
- **The *deflationary* *theories*** (Ramsey, Ayer, Quine, Horwich, Grover et al.) *treat* the *truth* as a *thin* *logical* *device*, and they *face* the *normativity* and the *generalization* *objections*.
- **The *truthmaker* *principle*** (Armstrong, Bigelow) *grounds* the *truth* in the *worldly* *entities*, and it *faces* the *problem* *cases* (negative, general, modal, mathematical).
- **The *alethic* *pluralism*** (Wright, Lynch) *holds* that the *truth* is *plural* (different *kinds* for different *domains*), and it *faces* the *disunity* *objection*.
- **The *relativism* about *truth*** (MacFarlane) *holds* that the *truth* is *assessment*-*sensitive* (for *some* *domains*), and it *faces* the *self*-*refutation* *objection*.
- **The *value* of *truth*** is *real* and *irreducible* to the *instrumental* *value*; the *normative* and *intrinsic* *accounts* are *promising* but *incomplete*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *combination*: the *thin* *concept* (the *deflationary* *insight*), the *worldly* *ground* (the *truthmaker* *insight*), and the *normative* *value* (the *pragmatist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *combination* is *unresolved*.
