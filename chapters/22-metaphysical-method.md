# Chapter 22 — Metaphysical Method, Realism, and Truthmakers

> “The question of realism is not a question about what there is; it is a question about the relation between what there is and what we can know.”
> — a formulation of the semantic anti-realist’s starting point, after Dummett

> “To be is to be the truthmaker of a true proposition — or it is nothing at all.”
> — a maximalist slogan, in the spirit of Armstrong (2004)

> “The only way to answer the question of what there is is to answer the question of what we ought to say there is; and the second question has no answer that is independent of our purposes.”
> — a deflationist slogan, in the spirit of Carnap and Thomasson

---

Part III has surveyed the terrain: existence (Ch. 14), universals (Ch. 15), modality (Ch. 16), time (Ch. 17), causation (Ch. 18), matter (Ch. 19), idealism (Ch. 20), and levels (Ch. 21). At every stage, the debate was conducted within a *framework* that was *not itself examined*: the framework of **metaphysical realism** — the assumption that there is a way the world is, independently of how we describe it, and that the task of metaphysics is to say what that way is.

This chapter examines the framework. It is the *meta*-chapter of Part III, and it is the *most* reflexive chapter in the book. It asks: what *is* the realist/anti-realist dispute? What are the *standards* by which metaphysical theories are assessed? Is metaphysics a *substantive* inquiry, or a *verbal* dispute? And what *code of practice* should the metaphysician follow?

The chapter has a distinctive shape, because the territory has been *contested twice* — by the logical positivists, who sought to *eliminate* metaphysics, and by the *deflationists*, who seek to *domesticate* it. The chapter’s thesis is that *neither* elimination *nor* domestication is *adequate*: metaphysics is a *substantive* inquiry, but its *standards* are *plural*, *contested*, and *not algorithmic*. The *honest* metaphysician is one who *knows* this, and who *practices* accordingly.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 disentangles the *three axes* of the realism/anti-realism debate. §2 examines *truthmaker theory*, the most influential *methodological* tool in contemporary metaphysics. §3 examines *ontological deflationism*, the most *serious* challenge to the *substantive* conception. §4 examines *conceptual engineering* and *revisionary metaphysics*, the *constructive* response to the challenge. §5 examines the *problem of disagreement*. §6 examines the *constraints* on metaphysical theorizing. §7 offers a *code of practice*. §8 gives an honest verdict and closes Part III.

## 1. The Realism/Anti-Realism Axis

### 1.1 The confusion of axes

The *realism* debate is *notoriously* confused, and the *confusion* is *structural*: the word “realism” is used for *at least* three *distinct* theses, and the *theses* are *logically independent*.

| Axis | The *question* | The *thesis* |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphysical realism** | Is there a mind-independent reality? | Yes: the world exists independently of our representations |
| **Semantic realism** | Is truth independent of our capacity to know? | Yes: truth is *verification-transcendent* |
| **Epistemic realism** | Is there a *single* correct theory of the world? | Yes: there is a fact of the matter, and it is not relative to a framework |

**The *axes* are *independent*.** One can be a *metaphysical* realist while *denying* semantic realism (the *epistemic* anti-realist, who holds that the world is *independent* but *truth* is *not*); one can be a *semantic* realist while *denying* epistemic realism (the *relativist*, who holds that truth is *verification-transcendent* but *relative* to a framework); and so on.

**The *consequence*.** Most of the *heat* in the realism debate comes from the *conflation* of the axes. The *anti-realist* is *often* a *semantic* anti-realist who is *charged* with *metaphysical* idealism; the *metaphysical* realist is *often* *charged* with *epistemic* dogmatism. The *charges* are *unfair*, and the *unfairness* is the *first* thing to clear.

### 1.2 Metaphysical realism

**The *thesis*.** There is a *way* the world *is*, *independently* of *how* we *represent* it, and the *task* of *inquiry* is to *discover* it.

**The *statement*.** The *view* *goes* *back* to *Plato* and *Aristotle*, and it is the *default* *position* of *common sense* and *science*. The *contemporary* *defenders*: Armstrong (1983), Lewis (1984, 1986), Sider (2011).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The scientific image of the world is independent of the observer.} \\
2. & \text{The independence requires a mind-independent reality.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{There is a mind-independent reality.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *epistemic* *access* *problem***. If the *reality* is *independent*, how do we *know* *about* it? The *answer* (perception, science, evolution) is *contested* (Ch. 6; Ch. 13).

**(ii) The *conceptual* *scheme* *problem***. The *reality* is *always* *described* *under* a *conceptual* *scheme*, and the *scheme* is *not* *given* by the *world* (Ch. 13, §3). The *objection* *threatens* the *claim* that the *description* is *uniquely* *correct*.

**(iii) The *“just* *more* *theory”* *problem***. Any *defense* of the *view* is *itself* a *theory*, and the *theory* *presupposes* the *view* (the *self-undermining* *worry*; §3.3).

### 1.3 Semantic realism and Dummett

**The *thesis*.** The *most* *sophisticated* *form* of the realism debate is *semantic*, and it is due to Michael Dummett (“Realism,” 1963; “Truth,” 1959; *The Logical Basis of Metaphysics*, 1991).

**The *core* *distinction***. The *semantic* *realist* holds that *truth* is **verification-transcendent**: a proposition can be *true* even if *no* *one* *could* *ever* *verify* it. The *semantic* *anti-realist* holds that *truth* is **verification-immanent**: a proposition is *true* only if it *could* be *verified* (or *warrantedly* *asserted*).

**The *formal* *statement***. Let $Tp$ = “$p$ is true”; $Vp$ = “$p$ is verifiable.” The *anti-realist* *principle*:

$$
(\mathrm{VR}) \quad \forall p\, (Tp \to \Diamond Vp)
$$

**The *bivalence* *principle***:

$$
(\mathrm{BIV}) \quad \forall p\, (Tp \vee T\neg p)
$$

**The *anti-realist* *argument***. The *anti-realist* *denies* *bivalence* for *undecidable* *statements* (statements about the *remote* *past*, about *infinite* *totalities*, about *other* *minds*). The *argument* *rests* on a *theory* of *meaning*: to *know* the *meaning* of a *sentence* is to *know* the *conditions* *under* which it is *assertible*; if the *conditions* are *verification*-conditions, then the *meaning* *cannot* *outrun* the *verification*.

**The *argument* *stated***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{To know the meaning of a sentence is to know its use (the manifestation requirement).} \\
2. & \text{The use is constituted by the conditions under which the sentence is asserted.} \\
3. & \text{The assertion conditions are verification-conditions.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The truth conditions cannot outrun the verification-conditions.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *connects* the *semantics* of *truth* to the *epistemology* of *understanding*, and it *explains* why the *anti-realist* *positions* (intuitionism in mathematics; verificationism in the philosophy of science) are *systematic*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *falsity* *of* *premise* 1**. The *manifestation* *requirement* is *contested*: a speaker can *understand* a *sentence* *without* being *able* to *manifest* the *conditions* *of* its *truth* (the *“modesty”* *objection*; Dummett himself worried about this).

**(ii) The *mathematical* *objection***. The *intuitionist* *program* in mathematics *rejects* *bivalence*, and the *results* (the *intuitionist* *logic*, the *Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov* *interpretation*) are *mathematically* *coherent* but *philosophically* *contested* (Ch. 43, §2).

**(iii) The *“realism* *is* *irresistible”* *objection***. The *anti-realist* *position* *seems* to *imply* that *the* *past* *is* *not* *real* *until* *someone* *remembers* it, and the *implication* is *absurd* (the *“logical* *argument”* *against* *anti-realism*; the *“no* *past*”* *objection*).

### 1.4 Epistemic anti-realism

**The *thesis*.** There is *no* *single* *correct* *description* of the *world*; the *description* is *relative* to a *framework*, a *community*, or a *purpose* (Ch. 13, §3).

**The *statement*.** Kuhn (1962), Rorty (1979), Goodman (*Ways of Worldmaking*, 1978), Putnam (*Reason, Truth and History*, 1981, *internal* *realism*), the *sociology* of *science* (Ch. 13, §3.3).

**The *argument*.** The *argument* is the *relativist* *argument* of Ch. 13, §3.5: the *descriptions* are *relative* to a *framework*, and the *frameworks* are *not* *adjudicable* by *framework*-*neutral* *standards*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *slides* from *plurality* to *relativism* (Ch. 13, §3.5), and the *slide* is *unjustified*.

### 1.5 The map

**The *table*.**

| Position | Metaphysical realism | Semantic realism | Epistemic realism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classical realism** (Armstrong, Lewis) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Internal realism** (Putnam, later) | Yes (but framework-relative) | Yes | No |
| **Semantic anti-realism** (Dummett) | Yes | No | Yes |
| **Kuhnian constructivism** | No (or agnostic) | No | No |
| **Idealism** (Ch. 20) | No | No | No |
| **Deflationism** (Carnap, Thomasson) | Yes (trivially) | Yes | Yes (but *trivial*) |

**The *lesson*.** The *realism* *debate* is *not* a *single* *dispute*; it is a *network* of *disputes*, and the *network* is *underdeveloped* (the *axes* have not been *systematically* *investigated*). The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *realism*: metaphysical realism *plus* semantic realism *plus* a *fallibilist* *epistemology* — the *position* of Chs. 13–14, with the *qualifications* of §3.

## 2. Truthmaker Theory

### 2.1 The principle

**The *principle***. Every *truth* has a *truthmaker*: a *thing* whose *existence* *necessitates* the *truth* of the *proposition*.

**The *formal* *statement***. Let $x \Vdash p$ mean “$x$ *makes* $p$ *true*”; then:

$$
(\mathrm{TP}) \quad \forall p\, (p \to \exists x\, (x \Vdash p))
$$

**The *truthmaking* *relation***. Armstrong (*Truth and Truthmakers*, 2004; following Mulligan, Simons & Smith 1984) *defines* the *relation* as *cross-categorial* *necessitation*:

$$
(\mathrm{TM}) \quad x \Vdash p \quad \text{iff} \quad \Box(\exists y (y = x) \to p)
$$

In words: $x$ *makes* $p$ *true* iff the *existence* of $x$ *necessitates* the *truth* of $p$.

**The *paradigm*.** The *truth* “the rose is red” is *made* *true* by the *state* of *affairs* *of* the *rose* *being* *red*. The *state* of *affairs* is a *complex* *entity* — a *particular* *instantiating* a *universal* (Ch. 15, §4.2).

**The *virtue*.** The *principle* *connects* *ontology* to *truth*: it *tells* us *what* *must* *exist* for our *claims* to be *true*, and it *constrains* our *ontology* (Ch. 14, §3).

**The *cost*.** The *principle* is *strong*: it *commits* us to *truthmakers* for *every* *truth*, and the *commitment* *generates* *problems* for *negative*, *general*, *modal*, *mathematical*, and *counterfactual* *truths*.

### 2.2 Maximalism

**The *thesis***. *Every* *truth* has a *truthmaker*. The *view* is *maximalism*, and its *defenders* are Armstrong (1997, 2004), Bigelow (*The Reality of Numbers*, 1988), and Heil (*From an Ontological Point of View*, 2003).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Truth is a relation between a proposition and the world.} \\
2. & \text{The relation requires a relatum in the world (the truthmaker).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Every truth has a truthmaker.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtues*.** (i) The *view* *captures* the *intuition* that *truths* are *true* *in* *virtue* *of* *something*; (ii) the *view* *provides* a *single* *criterion* of *ontological* *commitment*; (iii) the *view* *fits* the *correspondence* *theory* of *truth* (Ch. 32, §1).

### 2.3 The problem cases

The *maximalist* *faces* *five* *problem* *cases*.

**(i) Negative existentials.** “There are no unicorns” is *true*, and it *seems* to *require* a *truthmaker* that *is* the *absence* of unicorns. But *absences* are *not* *entities* — they are *nothing* — and *nothing* *cannot* *make* a *proposition* *true*.

**The *responses*.** (a) *Totality* *facts* (Armstrong 1997, 2004): the *truthmaker* is a *higher-order* *fact* — the *fact* *that* the *totality* of *things* *is* *such* that *no* *unicorn* *is* *among* *them*. (b) *Absences* as *entities* (the *“negative* *truthmakers”* *view*; Beebee, Dodd). (c) The *rejection* of the *maximalist* *principle* for *negative* *truths* (the *modest* *view*; §2.4).

**(ii) General truths.** “All ravens are black” is *true*, and it *requires* a *truthmaker* that *is* *the* *totality* of all *ravens* (the *fact* *that* *each* *raven* *is* *black* *and* *that* *there* *are* *no* *other* *ravens*). The *totality* *facts* are *complex* *higher-order* *entities* (Armstrong 1997, Ch. 8), and the *entities* are *contested*.

**(iii) Modal truths.** “There could have been a blue swan” is *true*, and it *requires* a *truthmaker* that is *a* *possible* *world* (or a *modal* *fact*). The *truthmaker* *commits* us to *modal* *realism* (Lewis) or *ersatzism* (Ch. 16, §2), and the *commitment* is *contested*.

**(iv) Mathematical truths.** “$2+2=4$” is *true*, and it *requires* a *truthmaker* that is *a* *mathematical* *object* (or *structure*). The *truthmaker* *commits* us to *mathematical* *platonism* (Ch. 15, §6), and the *commitment* is *contested*.

**(v) Counterfactuals.** “If I had left earlier, I would have caught the train” is *true*, and it *requires* a *truthmaker* that is a *counterfactual* *fact*. The *truthmaker* is *contested* (Ch. 37, §3).

### 2.4 Modest truthmaker theory

**The *thesis***. *Some* *truths* have *truthmakers*, but *not* *all*. The *principle* is *restricted* to *positive*, *atomic* *truths*, and the *other* *truths* are *made* *true* by *the* *absence* of *truthmakers* *for* their *negations*, or *by* *the* *structure* of the *world*, or *by* *nothing*.

**The *statement*.** The *modest* *view* is *defended* by Lewis (“Truthmaking and Difference-Making,” 2001), Milne (“Not Every Truth Has a Truthmaker,” 2005), and MacBride (“Truthmakers,” 2005).

**The *argument*.** The *maximalist* *principle* *generates* *problems* (the *five* *cases*), and the *problems* *are* *not* *solved* by the *proposed* *truthmakers* (they are *ad* *hoc*). The *modest* *principle* *avoids* the *problems* and *preserves* the *insight* (the *positive* *truths* are *made* *true*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *arbitrary* *restriction***. Why *should* *positive* *atomic* *truths* have *truthmakers* and *negative* *truths* *not*? The *restriction* *seems* *ad* *hoc*.

**(ii) The *explanatory* *impotence***. If *some* *truths* *do* not *have* *truthmakers*, the *principle* *cannot* *ground* the *truth* *of* *those* *truths*, and the *question* “what makes *them* true?” *returns*.

**(iii) The *“difference-making”* *objection***. Lewis (2001) *argues* that the *truthmaker* *principle* *is* *a* *form* of *the* *difference-making* *intuition* (Ch. 18, §2), and the *intuition* *does* not *require* a *special* *entity* for every *truth*. The *truthmaker* is *the* *thing* whose *difference* *makes* the *truth* *true*, and the *difference* can be *structural*.

### 2.5 Truthmaking and grounding

**The *distinction***. *Truthmaking* is a *relation* between an *entity* and a *proposition*; *grounding* (Ch. 16, §3.3) is a *relation* between *facts*. The *two* *relations* *overlap* but are *distinct*.

| Relation | The *relata* | The *direction* | The *paradigm* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Truthmaking** | Entity → Proposition | World → Truth | The rose makes “the rose is red” true |
| **Grounding** | Fact → Fact | More basic → Less basic | The rose’s being red grounds the truth of “the rose is red” |

**The *connection***. The *truthmaker* *grounds* the *truth*: the *state* of *affairs* *grounds* the *proposition*’s *being* *true*. The *two* *relations* *are* *aspects* of a *single* *structure* (the *world*’s *grounding* the *truth* of *propositions*).

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *clarifies* the *truthmaker* *debate*: the *maximalist* is *committed* to a *truthmaker* for *every* *truth*, and the *modest* *theorist* is *committed* to a *ground* for *every* *truth* (the *ground* may be *structural*, not *entity*-*based*).

## 3. Ontological Deflationism

### 3.1 Carnap's framework

**The *thesis*** (Carnap, “Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology,” 1950). Ontological *questions* are *either* **internal** to a *framework* or **external** to it.

**The *distinction*.**

| Kind | The *question* | The *answer* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | “Are there numbers?” (within the framework of arithmetic) | “Yes, by the rules of the framework” | *Trivial*; *analytic* |
| **External** | “Are there *really* numbers?” (apart from any framework) | “The question is *practical*: which framework should we adopt?” | *Pragmatic*; *not* *theoretical* |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The internal questions are answered by the rules of the framework.} \\
2. & \text{The external questions have no theoretical answer (there is no framework-neutral standard).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Ontological questions are either trivial or pragmatic.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *dissolves* the *ontological* *debates* (the *platonism/nominalism* *debate*, Ch. 15, §6) by *relocating* them to *pragmatic* *questions* *about* *frameworks*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *proves* *too* *much*: it *dissolves* *every* *ontological* *question*, including the *question* of *whether* *there* *are* *ordinary* *objects*. (The *reply*: the *ordinary* *objects* are *internal* to the *ordinary* *framework*, and the *internal* *question* is *trivial*; the *reply* *accepts* the *consequence*, and the *acceptance* is *contested*.)

### 3.2 Easy ontology and quantifier variance

**The *theses*.**

**Easy ontology** (Thomasson, *Ordinary Objects*, 2007; *Ontology Made Easy*, 2015). The *ontological* *questions* are *easy*: they are *answered* by *conceptual* *analysis* *plus* *empirical* *facts*. “Are there tables?” is *answered* by the *concept* of a *table* *plus* the *fact* that *there* *are* *things* *that* *satisfy* it. There is *no* *deep* *metaphysical* *question* *beneath* the *conceptual* *question*.

**Quantifier variance** (Hirsch, *Dividing Reality*, 1993; *Quantifier Variance and Realism*, 2011). The *quantifier* “there is” is *not* *univocal*: *different* *languages* *use* *different* *quantifier* *meanings*, and the *ontological* *debates* are *verbal* (the *parties* *use* *different* *quantifiers*).

**The *argument* *for* *easy* *ontology***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The concept of a table specifies the conditions under which something counts as a table.} \\
2. & \text{The empirical facts determine whether the conditions are satisfied.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The question “are there tables?” is answered by conceptual analysis plus empirical facts.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *makes* *ontology* *accessible*: the *answers* are *available* to *ordinary* *inquiry*, and the *mystery* of the *ontological* *debate* *dissolves*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *“deep* *ontology”* *objection*** (Sider, *Writing the Book of the World*, 2011, Ch. 3). The *easy* *ontologist* *confuses* the *question* of *what* *our* *concepts* *count* *as* *existing* with the *question* of *what* *fundamentally* *exists*. The *two* *questions* are *distinct*, and the *second* is *not* *easy*.

**(ii) The *“quantifier* *variance”* *objection*** (Sider 2011; Eklund 2008). If the *quantifier* *varies* *across* *languages*, *which* *language* is *the* *language* of *ontology*? The *answer* (there is *no* *privileged* *language*) *undermines* the *objectivity* of *ontology*.

**(iii) The *“just* *more* *theory”* *objection***. The *deflationist* *position* is *itself* a *theory*, and the *theory* *presupposes* the *framework*-*neutrality* it *denies* (the *self-undermining* *worry*).

### 3.3 The “just more metaphysics” objection

**The *objection***. The *deflationist* *proposes* a *theory* of *what* *ontology* *is* (a *meta*-*ontology*), and the *theory* is *itself* a *metaphysical* *theory*. The *proposal* *is* *“just* *more* *metaphysics”*, and it is *subject* to the *same* *demands* (consistency, explanatory power, parsimony) as the *first*-*order* *theories* it *rejects*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The deflationist theory makes claims about the nature of ontology.} \\
2. & \text{The claims are metaphysical (they concern the structure of reality and language).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The deflationist theory is itself a form of metaphysics.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *deflationist* *cannot* *escape* *metaphysics* by *declaring* it *verbal*; the *declaration* is *itself* a *metaphysical* *position*. The *“just* *more* *metaphysics”* *objection* is *the* *strongest* *challenge* to the *deflationist* *program*, and it is *unresolved*.

**The *reply***. The *deflationist* *accepts* the *charge* and *argues* that the *meta*-*metaphysics* is *a* *different* *kind* of *inquiry* — *conceptual*, *not* *substantive* — and the *kind* *distinction* *is* *defended* by *the* *“conceptual* *analysis”* *tradition* (Ch. 3, §1). The *reply* *is* *contested*, and the *controversy* *is* *the* *debate*.

### 3.4 Sider's ontologese

**The *response*** (Sider, “Ontological Realism,” 2009; *Writing the Book of the World*, 2011). The *realist* *response* to *deflationism* is to *introduce* a *privileged* *quantifier* — the *quantifier* of **Ontologese** — that *carves* *nature* *at* its *joints*.

**The *thesis*.** There is a *fundamental* *structure* to *reality*, and the *structure* is *described* by *the* *language* whose *quantifiers* and *predicates* *carve* *nature* *at* its *joints*. The *deflationist* *debates* are *about* the *peripheral* *languages*; the *real* *question* is *what* *Ontologese* *says*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Some descriptions of the world are more fundamental than others (the structure thesis; Ch. 19, §6.3).} \\
2. & \text{The fundamental description uses the privileged quantifier and predicates.} \\
3. & \text{The deflationist debates ignore the distinction.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The realist position is vindicated by the structure of Ontologese.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *preserves* the *objectivity* of *ontology*, and it *gives* a *precise* *sense* to the *question* “what is *fundamental*?”

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *mystery* of *Ontologese***. How do we *identify* the *privileged* *quantifier*? The *answer* (the *“joint*-*carving”* *criterion*) is *contested*, and the *criterion* *presupposes* the *structure* it is *meant* to *identify*.

**(ii) The *circularity* *objection***. The *defense* of the *structure* *presupposes* the *structure*, and the *circle* *is* *not* *broken*.

**(iii) The *“no* *fact* *of* *the* *matter”* *objection***. The *question* of *which* *quantifier* is *privileged* is *itself* an *ontological* *question*, and the *answer* is *not* *given* by the *structure* *thesis*. The *debate* *continues*.

### 3.5 The state of the meta-debate

**The *table*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carnapian deflationism** | Ontological questions are internal or pragmatic | Dissolves the debates | Proves too much |
| **Easy ontology** | Ontological questions are easy | Accessible | Confuses concept and fundamentality |
| **Quantifier variance** | Ontological debates are verbal | Pluralist | Undermines objectivity |
| **Sider's ontologese** | There is a privileged quantifier | Preserves objectivity | The privileged quantifier is mysterious |
| **Ontological realism** | Ontology is substantive | Fits the practice | The “just more theory” objection |

**The *state*.** The *meta*-*debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *the* *most* *fundamental* *debate* in *contemporary* *metaphysics*. The *choice* *between* *realism* and *deflationism* is *not* *settled* by the *first*-*order* *theories*, and the *meta*-*theories* are *themselves* *contested*.

**The *position* of this book.** A *qualified* *realism* (§1.5): the *ontological* *questions* are *substantive* (the *deflationist* *dissolves* *too* *much*), but the *substantive* *answers* are *plural* and *not* *uniquely* *determined* by the *evidence* (§6). The *qualified* *realism* *accepts* the *“just* *more* *metaphysics”* *objection* and *practices* *metaphysics* with *awareness* of the *objection*.

## 4. Conceptual Engineering and Revisionary Metaphysics

### 4.1 Descriptive and ameliorative

**The *distinction*** (Haslanger, “Gender and Race: (What) Are They? (What) Do We Want Them to Be?”, 2000; *Resisting Reality*, 2012).

| Project | The *question* | The *criterion* |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | What *is* the *concept* (or *kind*) we *actually* have? | *Extensional* *adequacy* to *pre-theoretic* *intuition* |
| **Ameliorative** | What *concept* (or *kind*) *should* we *have*, *given* our *purposes*? | *Fitness* *for* the *purpose*; *normative* *argument* |

**The *argument*.** The *descriptive* *question* *presupposes* a *purpose* (whether *acknowledged* or not), and the *ameliorative* *question* is *prior*. The *analysis* of *gender* (or *race*, or *disability*) *should* *start* from the *question* of *what* *we* *want* the *concept* *to* *do*.

**The *consequence*.** Metaphysics is *not* *purely* *descriptive*; it is *also* *normative*, and the *normative* *dimension* is *inescapable*.

**The *objection*.** The *ameliorative* *project* *changes* the *subject*: the *new* *concept* is *not* the *old* one, and the *analysis* is *revisionary*, not *descriptive*.

**The *reply*.** The *change* is *the* *point*: the *old* *concept* *served* *interests* *that* *should* *not* be *served*, and the *revision* is a *normative* *achievement* (Ch. 11, §4).

### 4.2 Sider's structure

**The *thesis*** (Sider, *Writing the Book of the World*, 2011). The *world* *has* a *structure* — a *privileged* *description* — and the *task* of *metaphysics* is to *discover* it.

**The *apparatus***.

| Concept | The *definition* | The *role* |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | The *privileged* *description* of the *world* | The *goal* of *metaphysics* |
| **Joint-carving** | The *predicates* that *carve* *nature* *at* its *joints* | The *criterion* of *structure* |
| **Fundamentality** | The *entities* that are *not* *grounded* in *others* | The *content* of the *structure* |
| **Ideology** | The *primitive* *vocabulary* of the *theory* | The *cost* of the *theory* (Ch. 14, §4.2) |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The sciences aim at a privileged description (the fundamental theory).} \\
2. & \text{Metaphysics aims at the same description.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Metaphysics is the search for structure.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *gives* *metaphysics* a *clear* *goal*, and it *connects* *metaphysics* to *science*.

**The *objection*.** The *structure* is *mysterious*: what *is* the *privileged* *description*, and how do we *know* it? (The *reply*: the *structure* is *identified* by *joint*-*carving*, and *joint*-*carving* is *identified* by *theoretical* *virtues*; the *reply* *is* *circular*; §3.4.)

### 4.3 Parfit's climbing

**The *thesis*** (Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984; *On What Matters*, 2011). The *task* of *philosophy* is to *climb* *toward* the *truth*: to *discover* the *best* *description* of *reality*, *even* *if* it *requires* *revision* of *ordinary* *belief*.

**The *metaphor***. The *philosopher* is *climbing* a *mountain*: the *summit* is the *truth*, and the *climb* is the *process* of *revision* — *discarding* *false* *beliefs*, *refining* *concepts*, *accepting* *conclusions* *that* *are* *counterintuitive*.

**The *argument*.** The *intuitive* *beliefs* are *not* *reliable* (Ch. 3, §2.4), and the *revisionary* *conclusions* may be *true* *despite* their *counterintuitiveness*. The *task* is to *follow* the *argument* *where* it *leads*.

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *takes* the *arguments* *seriously*, and it *avoids* the *conservatism* of the *descriptive* *project*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *has* *no* *stopping* *rule*: if *any* *belief* can be *revised*, the *revisionary* *process* *can* *continue* *indefinitely*. (The *reply*: the *stopping* *rule* is *reflective* *equilibrium* (Ch. 3, §4); the *reply* *is* *contested*.)

### 4.4 The costs

**The *costs* of *revisionary* *metaphysics***.

1. **Changing the subject.** The *revisionary* *theory* *may* *not* *answer* the *original* *question*; it *answers* a *different* *question* (the *“change* *of* *subject”* *objection*).
2. **The *normative* *burden***. The *revision* *requires* a *normative* *argument* (why *should* we *revise*?), and the *argument* is *not* *given* by the *metaphysics*.
3. **The *loss* of *intuitions***. The *intuitions* are *data* (Ch. 3, §2), and the *revision* *discards* the *data*. The *revisionist* *owes* an *explanation* of *why* the *data* *are* *misleading*.
4. **The *political* *dimension***. The *revision* *may* *serve* *interests* (the *“whose* *concepts?”* *question*; Ch. 33, §5), and the *interests* *must* be *acknowledged*.

**The *balance*.** The *revisionary* *project* is *necessary* (the *descriptive* *project* *presupposes* the *ameliorative* *question*) and *dangerous* (the *revision* *can* *change* the *subject* *without* *argument*). The *honest* *position* is a *hybrid*: *describe* the *concept*, *evaluate* its *fitness*, and *revise* *only* with a *normative* *argument*.

## 5. The Problem of Metaphysical Disagreement

### 5.1 The data

**The *observation*.** Metaphysics is *characterized* by *pervasive*, *durable* *disagreement*: the *debates* of Chs. 14–21 have *persisted* for *centuries*, and the *parties* are *equally* *intelligent*, *informed*, and *careful*.

**The *examples*.** The *platonism/nominalism* *debate* (Ch. 15), the *presentism/eternalism* *debate* (Ch. 17), the *regularity/counterfactual/process* *debate* (Ch. 18), the *realism/idealism* *debate* (Ch. 20), the *reduction/emergence* *debate* (Ch. 21).

**The *question*.** What *does* the *disagreement* *show*?

### 5.2 The meta-induction

**The *argument*** (Chalmers, “Why Isn’t There More Progress in Philosophy?”, 2015).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Philosophy has unresolved disagreement for centuries.} \\
2. & \text{If a discipline has unresolved disagreement for centuries, the disagreement is evidence that the questions are hard or the methods are weak.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Philosophical confidence should be calibrated to the disagreement.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *meta-induction* *is* *evidence* for *humility* (Ch. 4, §4.6), and the *humility* is *compatible* with *continued* *inquiry*. The *induction* *does* not *show* that *the* *questions* *are* *unanswerable*; it *shows* that the *answers* *are* *hard*.

**The *objections*.** (i) The *meta-induction* *proves* *too* *much* (it *undermines* the *confidence* in *itself*; Ch. 3, §5.5). (ii) The *disagreement* is *not* *uniform*: some *debates* *have* *converged* (the *theory* of *descriptions*, the *logic* of *modality*), and the *convergence* *is* *evidence* of *progress*.

### 5.3 Verbal disputes and the diagnostic method

**The *method*** (Chalmers, “Verbal Disputes,” 2011). To *determine* whether a *disagreement* is *verbal*, *ask* what the *parties* would *say* if they *agreed* about the *words*.

**The *procedure*.**

1. **Identify** the *disputed* *terms*.
2. **Specify** the *candidate* *meanings*.
3. **Determine** whether the *parties* *agree* on the *facts* *given* the *meanings*.
4. **Determine** whether the *disagreement* *survives* the *specification*.

**The *application* to metaphysics.** Some *metaphysical* *debates* *are* *partly* *verbal* (the *“are* *tables* *real?”* *debate* *may* *turn* on the *meaning* of “real”); others are *substantive* (the *“is* *the* *mental* *causally* *efficacious?”* *debate* *turns* on *facts* *about* *causation*).

**The *consequence*.** The *method* *does* not *settle* the *debates*; it *classifies* them. The *classification* is a *first* *step* toward *resolution*, and it is *indispensable*.

### 5.4 The three positions

**The *positions***.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphysical pessimism** | The *debates* *are* *unresolvable*; metaphysics is *not* a *science* | van Inwagen (“We’re Right. They’re Wrong.”, 2006) — *ironically*; the *“no* *progress”* *view* |
| **Metaphysical optimism** | The *debates* *are* *resolvable*; progress is *slow* | Chalmers (2015); the *“climbing”* *view* |
| **Deflationism** | The *debates* *are* *verbal*; the *resolution* is *diagnostic* | Carnap; Hirsch; Thomasson |

**The *argument* *for* *pessimism***. The *history* of *metaphysics* *shows* *no* *convergence*, and the *absence* of *convergence* is *evidence* of *insolubility*.

**The *argument* *for* *optimism***. The *history* *shows* *partial* *convergence* (the *logical* *achievements* of the *twentieth* *century*; the *clarity* of the *contemporary* *debates*), and the *partial* *convergence* is *evidence* of *progress*.

**The *argument* *for* *deflationism***. The *debates* *persist* *because* they are *verbal*, and the *diagnostic* *method* *dissolves* them.

**The *verdict*.** The *three* *positions* are *all* *live*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *diagnosis* of *particular* *debates*. The *book’s* *position* is a *qualified* *optimism*: *some* *debates* *are* *verbal* (and *should* be *diagnosed*), *some* are *substantive* (and *should* be *pursued*), and the *distinction* is *itself* a *metaphysical* *question*.

## 6. Constraints on Metaphysics

### 6.1 Science

**The *constraint*.** Metaphysics *should* be *consistent* with the *best* *science* of the *day*.

**The *argument*.** Science is the *most* *reliable* *form* of *inquiry*, and the *metaphysical* *theories* that *contradict* *science* are *unlikely* to be *true* (the *naturalistic* *constraint*; Ch. 1, §4).

**The *problem*.** The *constraint* *presupposes* that *science* *gives* us *metaphysics*-*relevant* *results*, and the *presupposition* is *contested* (the *science* *presupposes* *metaphysics*; Ch. 1, §4; Ch. 53, §3).

### 6.2 Common sense

**The *constraint*.** Metaphysics *should* be *consistent* with *common* *sense*.

**The *argument*.** Common sense is *reliable* (Ch. 6, §6.1), and the *metaphysical* *theories* that *deny* *common* *sense* (idealism, eliminativism) are *unlikely* to be *true*.

**The *problem*.** Common sense is *not* *infallible* (the *history* of *science* is a *history* of *refutations* of *common* *sense*), and the *constraint* *must* be *calibrated* to the *evidence* (Ch. 4, §3.4).

### 6.3 Ideology

**The *constraint*.** Metaphysics *should* be *aware* of its *political* *and* *ideological* *dimension*.

**The *argument*** (the *feminist* and *decolonial* *critiques*; Ch. 11, §4; Ch. 13, §5.2). The *metaphysical* *theories* *encode* *interests* (the *essentialism* about *gender*, the *atomism* about *society*, the *individualism* about *mind*), and the *interests* *must* be *acknowledged*.

**The *problem*.** The *critique* *can* *slide* into *relativism* (Ch. 13, §3), and the *slide* *must* be *resisted* (the *critique* *presupposes* the *objectivity* of *the* *interests*).

### 6.4 Parsimony and coherence

**The *constraint*.** Metaphysics *should* be *parsimonious* (Ch. 14, §4) and *coherent* (Ch. 3, §4).

**The *argument*.** The *criteria* of *theory* *choice* (Ch. 4, §3) *apply* to *metaphysics*, and the *criteria* *are* the *standards* of *assessment*.

**The *problem*.** The *criteria* *conflict* (Ch. 4, §3.2), and the *conflict* *is* *not* *resolved* by an *algorithm*.

### 6.5 The no-algorithm problem

**The *thesis*.** There is *no* *algorithm* for *metaphysics*. The *criteria* (science, common sense, ideology, parsimony, coherence) *conflict*, and the *resolution* is a *judgment*, not a *calculation*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The criteria conflict (the cases show it).} \\
2. & \text{No algorithm resolves the conflict (the criteria are not commensurable).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The resolution is a judgment.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** Metaphysics is a *skill* (Ch. 4, §5.6), not a *procedure*. The *skill* is *acquired* by *practice*, and the *practice* is the *subject* of §7.

## 7. A Code of Practice

**The *code***. The *chapter* *closes* with a *code* of *practice* for the *metaphysician* — *seven* *rules*, *derived* from *the* *chapter*.

**(1) Specify the axis.** *Before* *arguing* *for* or *against* *realism*, *specify* *which* *realism* (metaphysical, semantic, epistemic). The *confusion* of the *axes* is the *commonest* *error* in the *debate*.

**(2) State the truthmaker.** *For* *every* *claim* *about* *what* *exists*, *state* *what* *makes* it *true* — or *state* *that* *you* *deny* the *truthmaker* *principle* and *why*.

**(3) Acknowledge the deflationist challenge.** *For* *every* *ontological* *claim*, *ask* *whether* the *question* *is* *internal* or *external*, *and* *address* the *challenge* *rather* than *ignoring* it.

**(4) Declare the project.** *State* *whether* *you* are *describing* a *concept* or *revising* it, and *give* the *normative* *argument* for the *revision*.

**(5) Calibrate the confidence.** *State* the *strength* of the *evidence*, *acknowledge* the *disagreement*, and *avoid* *overclaiming* (Ch. 4, §4.1).

**(6) Specify the constraints.** *State* *which* *constraints* (science, common sense, ideology, parsimony) *you* are *applying*, and *how* *they* *interact*.

**(7) Seek the strongest objection.** *Write* *out* *the* *best* *objection* *to* *your* *position* (Ch. 4, §5.2), *and* *do* not *answer* it *until* *you* have *stated* it *fully*.

**The *rationale*.** The *code* is *not* a *guarantee* of *truth*; it is a *discipline* of *honesty*. The *metaphysician* who *follows* it is *less* *likely* to *be* *fooled* — by *others* or by *herself*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The realism debate has three axes.** The *confusion* of the *axes* is the *central* *error*, and the *disentanglement* is the *first* *achievement* of the *chapter*.
- **Dummett's semantic anti-realism is a systematic position.** The *view* *connects* *truth* to *verification*, and it is *contested* but *coherent*.
- **Truthmaker theory is the most influential methodological tool in contemporary metaphysics.** The *maximalist* *version* *faces* *five* *problem* *cases*, and the *modest* *version* *restricts* the *principle*.
- **Ontological deflationism is a serious challenge.** The *Carnapian* *distinction* is *powerful*, and the *“just* *more* *metaphysics”* *objection* is *the* *strongest* *reply*.
- **Conceptual engineering is a real research program.** The *ameliorative* *turn* *makes* *metaphysics* *normative* as well as *descriptive*, and the *normative* *dimension* is *inescapable*.
- **Metaphysical disagreement is pervasive.** The *meta-induction* *supports* *humility*, and the *verbal-dispute* *method* *classifies* the *debates*.
- **Metaphysics has no algorithm.** The *criteria* *conflict*, and the *resolution* is a *judgment*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *reality* of the *privileged* *quantifier*. Is *Ontologese* *real*, or is it a *fiction*?
- **The *status* of *deflationism*. Is the *deflationist* *theory* *itself* a *form* of *metaphysics*?
- **The *nature* of *structure*. Is the *structure* a *real* *feature* of the *world*, or a *projection* of our *interests*?
- **The *resolution* of the *disagreement*. Is *progress* *possible*, and what *would* it *look* *like*?
- **The *relation* between *metaphysics* and *science*. Is metaphysics *continuous* with *science*, or *autonomous*?

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The *chapter* is a *case* *study* in the *method* of *Part* I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* among the *axes*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *truthmaker* *cases*, the *verbal-dispute* *cases* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *truthmaker* *principle*, the *quantifier* *variance* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The *honest* verdict: the *meta*-*debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *the* *most* *fundamental* *debate* in *contemporary* *metaphysics*. The *choice* *between* *realism* and *deflationism* is *not* *settled*, and the *meta*-*theories* are *themselves* *contested*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what *philosophical* *progress* looks like — not a *settled* *answer*, but a *sharper* *understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metaphysical realism** | There is a mind-independent reality | Fits common sense and science | The access problem |
| **Semantic realism** | Truth is verification-transcendent | Fits the semantics of truth | The manifestation requirement |
| **Semantic anti-realism** | Truth is verification-immanent | Connects truth to understanding | The “no past” objection |
| **Epistemic anti-realism** | No single correct description | Fits the plurality of frameworks | Slides into relativism |
| **Truthmaker maximalism** | Every truth has a truthmaker | Systematic; fits correspondence | The five problem cases |
| **Truthmaker modesty** | Only some truths have truthmakers | Avoids the problem cases | The arbitrary restriction |
| **Carnapian deflationism** | Ontological questions are internal or pragmatic | Dissolves the debates | Proves too much |
| **Easy ontology** | Ontological questions are easy | Accessible | Confuses concept and fundamentality |
| **Quantifier variance** | Ontological debates are verbal | Pluralist | Undermines objectivity |
| **Sider's ontologese** | There is a privileged quantifier | Preserves objectivity | The privileged quantifier is mysterious |
| **Conceptual engineering** | Metaphysics is normative | Fits the ameliorative turn | Changes the subject |
| **Pessimism** | Metaphysical debates are unresolvable | Honest about the history | Too pessimistic |
| **Optimism** | Progress is slow but real | Fits the partial convergence | Too optimistic |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

- **Sider's *Ontologese*** is the *realist* *response*: the *privileged* *quantifier* *carves* *nature* *at* its *joints*. The *response* *preserves* *objectivity*, but the *privileged* *quantifier* is *mysterious*.
- **Conceptual engineering** (Haslanger) *makes* *metaphysics* *normative* as well as *descriptive*, and the *ameliorative* *question* is *prior*. The *revisionary* *project* is *necessary* and *dangerous*.
- **Metaphysical disagreement** is *pervasive*, and the *meta-induction* *supports* *humility*. The *verbal-dispute* *method* *classifies* the *debates*, and the *three* *positions* (pessimism, optimism, deflationism) are *all* *live*.
- **The constraints** on *metaphysics* — science, common sense, ideology, parsimony, coherence — *conflict*, and there is *no* *algorithm*. The *resolution* is a *judgment*, and the *judgment* is a *skill*.
- **The code of practice** — *seven* *rules* — is the *discipline* of *honesty* that the *chapter* *recommends*.
- **Part III ends** with a *hinge*: the *recognition* that *metaphysics* is *possible* *despite* the *deflationist* *challenge*, and that *reality* is *real* *despite* the *disagreement*.
