# Chapter 24 — Consciousness and Qualia

> “Consciousness is the having of perceptions, thoughts, and feelings; awareness. … It is what makes the mind a mind.”
> — the Cartesian intuition, restated in the idiom of contemporary philosophy of mind

> “It is undeniable that some organisms are subjects of experience. But the question of how it is that these systems are subjects of experience is perplexing.”
> — Thomas Nagel, “What Is It Like to Be a Bat?” (1974)

> “The hard problem is the problem of experience. … It is the problem of why and how the physical processes give rise to experience.”
> — David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* (1996), Ch. 1

---

Part IV has been the *largest gap* in Russell’s book. Russell devoted no chapter to consciousness; his *Problems of Philosophy* treats the mind only at the edges — in the discussion of self-knowledge (Ch. V), and in the argument for other minds. The *omission* is *not* an oversight: in 1912, the mind–body problem *seemed* to many philosophers to be *soluble* by the methods of *introspective psychology*, and the *phenomenal* character of experience did not yet *seem* to be a *distinct* problem.

Chapter 23 examined the *relation* between the mental and the physical; this chapter examines the *nature* of the *mental* that is *most* resistant to the relation. The *subject* is **consciousness** — the *having* of experience — and the *question* is the *most* *difficult* in *philosophy*: **what is it, and how does it fit into the natural world?**

The chapter has a *distinctive* shape, because the *territory* has been *re-contested* three times in the last fifty years. The *first* contest was *Chalmers’s*: the *distinction* between the *easy* and *hard* problems, and the *hard* problem’s *irreducibility*. The *second* was the *illusionist* *response*: the *denial* that the *hard* problem *exists*. The *third* — and the *most* *recent* — is the *meta-problem*: the *explanation* of why the *hard* problem *seems* *hard*, which *may* *dissolve* the *problem* *without* *solving* it.

The chapter’s *thesis* is that the *hard* problem is *real* — the *phenomenal* character of experience is *not* *explained* by any *current* *theory* — but that the *meta-problem* is *tractable*, and that its *solution* is the *most* *promising* *route* to *progress*. The *thesis* is *qualified* by the *admission* that the *hard* problem *may* be *dissolved* rather than *solved*, and that the *dissolution* is *not* a *failure*.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 distinguishes the *varieties* of consciousness. §2 states the *hard* problem and the *explanatory* *gap*. §3 examines the *knowledge* arguments (Mary, zombies, absent qualia). §4 examines the *theories*. §5 examines the *meta-problem*. §6 examines the *attribution* problem (animals, infants, AI). §7 examines the *contemplative* and *cross-cultural* data. §8 gives an *honest* verdict.

---

## 1. The Varieties of Consciousness

### 1.1 Four concepts

The *word* “consciousness” *names* *at least* *four* *distinct* *concepts*, and the *distinction* is the *first* thing to *clear*.

| Kind | The *definition* | The *paradigm* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creature consciousness** | The *organism* is *awake* and *responsive* | A *dog* is conscious; a *stone* is not | the *common-sense* view |
| **Phenomenal consciousness** | The *subjective* *character* of *experience* — “what it is like” | The *taste* of coffee; the *redness* of red | Nagel (1974); Block (1995) |
| **Access consciousness** | The *availability* of *information* to *reasoning*, *control*, and *report* | The *content* of *working memory* | Baars (1988); Block (1995) |
| **Self-consciousness** | The *awareness* of *oneself* as a *subject* | The *thought* “I am thinking” | Descartes; Ch. 26 |

**The *distinctions* *matter*.** The *four* *kinds* are *not* *independent* (they are *correlated* in *normal* *experience*), but they are *distinct*: an *organism* can be *creature*-conscious *without* being *self*-conscious (a *dog*); a *state* can be *access*-conscious *without* being *phenomenally* *conscious* (the *“blindsight”* *patient* who *reports* a *stimulus* *without* *seeing* it); a *state* can be *phenomenally* *conscious* *without* being *access*-conscious (the *“overflow”* *debate*; §1.3).

**The *significance*.** The *hard* problem is the problem of *phenomenal* consciousness — the *“what it is like”* — and *not* the problem of the *other* *three* *kinds*, which are *tractable* (or *nearly* so) by *cognitive* *science*.

### 1.2 The “what it is like” formulation

**The *formulation*** (Nagel 1974). A *state* is *phenomenally* *conscious* if there is *something* it is *like* to be in it — for the *subject*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\text{State } S \text{ is *phenomenally conscious* iff } \exists x\, (\text{there is something it is like for } x \text{ to be in } S)
$$

**The *examples*.** The *taste* of coffee, the *smell* of a rose, the *feeling* of *pain*, the *visual* *experience* of *red*, the *emotional* *experience* of *grief*.

**The *virtue*.** The *formulation* *captures* the *subjective* *character* of *experience* *without* *committing* to a *particular* *theory* of *what* it is.

**The *limitation*.** The *formulation* is *negative* (it *tells* us *what* is *distinctive* about *experience*, not *what* it *is*), and it *leaves* the *nature* of the *subjective* *character* *unexplained*.

### 1.3 The phenomenal/access distinction

**The *distinction*** (Block, “On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness,” 1995). *Phenomenal* consciousness (P-consciousness) is *experience*; *access* consciousness (A-consciousness) is the *availability* of *information* to *reasoning*, *control*, and *report*.

**The *independence*.** The *two* are *conceptually* *independent*: a *state* can be P-conscious without being A-conscious (the *“overflow”* *case*), and a *state* can be A-conscious without being P-conscious (the *“blindsight”* *case*, the *“zombie”* *case*).

**The *overflow* *debate*** (Block 2007; the *critique* in Cohen & Dennett 2011). Block *argues* that *phenomenal* *experience* *overflows* *access*: the *visual* *field* *contains* *more* *information* than the *subject* can *report* or *cognitively* *access* (the *“sperling”* *experiments*; the *richness* of *experience*). The *critique* (Cohen & Dennett) *argues* that the *richness* is *not* *evidence* of *phenomenal* *overflow*; it is *evidence* of *access* to a *representation* that is *quickly* *forgotten*.

**The *significance*.** The *debate* is *contested*, and it *bears* on the *theories* of *consciousness*: if *phenomenal* *experience* *overflows* *access*, the *global* *workspace* *theories* (which *identify* *consciousness* with *access*) are *false*.

## 2. The Hard Problem and the Explanatory Gap

### 2.1 Chalmers's distinction

**The *thesis*** (Chalmers, “Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness,” 1995; *The Conscious Mind*, 1996).

**The *easy* *problems*.** The *problems* of *consciousness* that are *tractable* by *cognitive* *science*: *discrimination*, *integration*, *report*, *control*, *attention*, *memory*, *learning*. The *problems* are *easy* in the *sense* that they are *explanable* by *functional* *mechanisms*.

**The *hard* *problem*.** The *problem* of *experience*: *why* is there *something* it is *like* to be in a *mental* *state*? *Why* does *information* *processing* *give* *rise* to *subjective* *experience*?

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *easy* *problems* are *explained* by *functional* *mechanisms* (the *functionalist* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *functional* *mechanisms* do *not* *explain* *why* there is *something* it is *like* to be in the *state* (the *explanatory* *gap*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *hard* *problem* is *not* *reducible* to the *easy* *problems*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *physicalist* *theories* of *mind* (Ch. 23) *explain* the *functional* *role* of *consciousness* but *not* the *phenomenal* *character*. The *hard* *problem* is the *residue* that the *theories* *leave*.

### 2.2 The explanatory gap

**The *thesis*** (Levine, “Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap,” 1983). Even if *physicalism* is *true*, there is an *explanatory* *gap*: we *cannot* *explain* *why* the *physical* *states* *give* *rise* to the *phenomenal* *states*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *identity* between *pain* and *C-fiber* *firing* is *contingent* (it is *discovered* *a* *posteriori*).} \\
2. & \text{The *identity* does *not* *explain* *why* *pain* *feels* the *way* it *does*.} \\
3. & \text{The *explanation* *requires* a *conceptual* *connection* *between* the *physical* and the *phenomenal*.} \\
4. & \text{The *connection* is *not* *available* (the *concepts* are *distinct*).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The *explanatory* *gap* is *real*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *distinction* *from* the *hard* *problem*.** The *hard* *problem* is *ontological* (the *phenomenal* *is* *not* *reducible*); the *explanatory* *gap* is *epistemic* (we *cannot* *explain* the *phenomenal* *even* *if* it is *reducible*). The *two* are *connected* (the *gap* *supports* the *irreducibility*), but they are *distinct*.

**The *significance*.** The *gap* is *not* a *proof* of *dualism*; it is a *challenge* to the *physicalist* *account*. The *challenge* is *met* by the *phenomenal* *concept* *strategy* (§4.6), and the *meeting* is *contested*.

### 2.3 The structure of the hard problem

**The *structure***. The *hard* *problem* has *three* *components*.

| Component | The *question* |
|---|---|
| **The *existence* *question*** | Why is there *any* *experience* at all? |
| **The *character* *question*** | Why does *this* *experience* *feel* *this* *way*? |
| **The *distribution* *question*** | Which *systems* *have* *experience*? |

**The *existence* *question*.** Why is the *universe* *not* *dark* — why is there *any* *subjective* *experience* *rather* than *none*? The *question* is the *most* *metaphysical*, and it is the *least* *tractable*.

**The *character* *question*.** Why does *red* *look* *red* and not *blue*? Why does *pain* *feel* *bad*? The *question* is the *most* *phenomenological*, and it is the *most* *discussed*.

**The *distribution* *question*.** Are *animals* *conscious*? Are *infants*? Are *AI* *systems*? The *question* is the *most* *practical*, and it is the *subject* of §6.

**The *consequence*.** The *hard* *problem* is *not* a *single* *question*; it is a *family* of *questions*, and the *answers* *may* *differ* across the *family*. The *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*, and the *clarity* is *the* *contribution* of the *chapter*.

## 3. The Knowledge Arguments

### 3.1 Mary's room

**The *argument*** (Jackson, “Epiphenomenal Qualia,” 1982; “What Mary Didn’t Know,” 1986).

**The *scenario*.** Mary is a *brilliant* *neuroscientist* who has *lived* her *entire* *life* in a *black*-and-*white* *room*. She *knows* *everything* *physical* *there* is to *know* *about* *colour* *vision*: the *wavelengths*, the *retinal* *processes*, the *neural* *pathways*, the *behavioural* *dispositions*. One *day*, she *leaves* the *room* and *sees* a *red* *rose* *for* the *first* *time*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Mary *knows* *everything* *physical* *about* *colour* *vision* *before* *leaving* the *room*.} \\
2. & \text{When she *leaves*, she *learns* *something* *new*: *what* *red* *looks* *like*.} \\
3. & \text{If she *learns* *something* *new*, the *new* *knowledge* is *not* *physical* *knowledge* (it is *not* *included* in the *physical* *facts* *she* *already* *knew*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There are *non-physical* *facts* (the *phenomenal* *facts*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** The *argument* is *an* *argument* *against* *physicalism*: if Mary *learns* a *new* *fact*, the *fact* is *not* *physical*, and *physicalism* is *false*.

### 3.2 The replies

**The *replies* are *three*.

**(i) The *ability* *hypothesis*** (Lewis, “What Experience Teaches,” 1988; Nemirow, “Physicalism and the Cognitive Role of Acquaintance,” 1990). Mary *does* not *learn* a *new* *fact*; she *learns* a *new* *ability* — the *ability* to *recognise*, *imagine*, and *remember* *red* *experiences*. The *knowledge* is *know-how*, not *know-that*, and *know-how* is *not* a *fact*.

**The *objection*.** The *ability* *hypothesis* *does* not *capture* the *phenomenology*: Mary *seems* to *learn* *what* *red* *is* *like*, not *merely* *how* to *do* something. (The *reply*: the *seeming* is *itself* a *form* of *know-how*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(ii) The *phenomenal* *concept* *strategy*** (Loar, “Phenomenal States,” 1990; Papineau, *Thinking about Consciousness*, 2002; Chalmers, “The Content of Phenomenal Concepts,” 2003). Mary *learns* a *new* *concept* of an *old* *fact*: she *acquires* the *phenomenal* *concept* of *red*, which *refers* to the *same* *physical* *property* as the *physical* *concept*, but *under* a *first-person* *mode* of *presentation*. The *fact* is *physical*; the *concept* is *new*; and the *knowledge* is *new* *without* being *knowledge* of a *new* *fact*.

**The *objection*.** The *strategy* *assumes* that the *phenomenal* *concept* *refers* to a *physical* *property*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *knowledge* *argument* *denies*. (The *reply*: the *strategy* is *not* *question-begging*; it *offers* a *theory* of *what* the *phenomenal* *concept* *refers* to, and the *theory* is *defended* *independently*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(iii) The *“old* *fact,* *new* *way”* *response*** (the *general* *form* of the *strategy*). The *knowledge* is *new* *because* the *mode* of *presentation* is *new*, not *because* the *fact* is *new*. The *response* is *a* *form* of the *phenomenal* *concept* *strategy*, and it is the *most* *defensible* *reply*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *knowledge* *argument* is *the* *most* *discussed* *argument* in the *philosophy* of *mind*, and it is *not* *decisive*. The *replies* are *available*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *theory* of *phenomenal* *concepts* (§4.6).

### 3.3 The zombie argument

**The *argument*** (Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind*, 1996, Ch. 3).

**The *scenario*.** A *zombie* is a *being* that is *physically* *identical* to a *human* *being* — the *same* *brain* *states*, the *same* *behaviour*, the *same* *functional* *organisation* — but *without* *conscious* *experience*. There is *nothing* it is *like* to be a *zombie*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{It is *conceivable* that there be a *zombie* (a *physical* *duplicate* *without* *consciousness*).} \\
2. & \text{If it is *conceivable*, it is *metaphysically* *possible* (the *conceivability–possibility* *thesis*; Ch. 16, §6.2).} \\
3. & \text{If it is *metaphysically* *possible*, *physicalism* is *false* (the *physical* *facts* do *not* *entail* the *phenomenal* *facts*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Physicalism is *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *structure*.** The *argument* is a *modal* *argument*, and its *validity* *depends* on the *conceivability–possibility* *thesis*, which is *contested* (Ch. 16, §6.2).

**The *replies*.**

**(i) The *type-A* *materialist* *reply***. The *zombie* is *not* *conceivable*; the *conceivability* is an *illusion* *generated* by our *ignorance* of the *physical* *facts* (the *“phenomenal* *concept”* *strategy*; §4.6).

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *denies* the *phenomenology* of *conceivability*; it *seems* *obvious* that the *zombie* is *conceivable*.

**(ii) The *type-B* *materialist* *reply***. The *zombie* is *conceivable* but *not* *possible*: the *conceivability* is *a* *priori*, and the *possibility* is *a* *posteriori* (the *Kripkean* *response*; Ch. 9, §3.3). The *zombie* is *like* “water is not H₂O”: *conceivable* but *metaphysically* *impossible*.

**The *objection*.** The *analogy* *fails*: the *water–H₂O* *case* *involves* a *contingent* *identity*, while the *zombie* *involves* a *necessary* *identity* (if *physicalism* is *true*). The *reply* *is* *contested*.

**(iii) The *type-C* *materialist* *reply***. The *zombie* is *not* *conceivable*, and the *physical* *facts* *entail* the *phenomenal* *facts* *a* *priori* (the *a* *priori* *entailment* *thesis*). The *reply* is *the* *most* *demanding*, and it is *defended* by *Jackson* (1998) and *Chalmers* (2003) *in* *different* *forms*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *zombie* *argument* is the *most* *influential* *argument* for *property* *dualism* (Ch. 23, §7), and it is *not* *decisive*. The *choice* *among* the *replies* *is* the *choice* *among* the *theories* of *consciousness*.

### 3.4 Absent qualia

**The *argument*** (Block, “Troubles with Functionalism,” 1978). A *system* can *have* the *functional* *organisation* of a *human* *mind* *without* *having* *phenomenal* *experience* — the *“absent* *qualia”* *case*.

**The *scenario*.** The *“Chinese* *nation”*: the *population* of *China* *simulates* the *functional* *organisation* of a *human* *brain*, using *radios* to *communicate*. The *system* *has* the *functional* *organisation*; does it have *experience*?

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *functional* *organisation* is *sufficient* for *consciousness* (the *functionalist* *premise*; Ch. 23, §4).} \\
2. & \text{The *Chinese* *nation* *has* the *functional* *organisation* but *no* *phenomenal* *experience*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Functionalism is *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** The *argument* *supports* the *phenomenal*/*access* *distinction*: *access* consciousness is *functional*, and *phenomenal* consciousness is *not*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *intuition* *objection*. The *intuition* that the *Chinese* *nation* has *no* *experience* is *contested*; the *system* *might* have *experience* (the *“system* *reply”*; Ch. 23, §4.4).

**(ii) The *correlation* *objection*. The *argument* *assumes* that the *functional* *organisation* is *not* *sufficient* for *experience*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *functionalist* *denies*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *argument* is *a* *form* of the *zombie* *argument*, and it *faces* the *same* *controversies*.

## 4. The Theories

### 4.1 Higher-order theories

**The *thesis***. A *mental* *state* is *conscious* iff it is the *object* of a *higher-order* *representation* — a *representation* *of* the *state*.

**The *statement*.** Armstrong, *A Materialist Theory of the Mind* (1968); Lycan, *Consciousness and Experience* (1996); Rosenthal, *Consciousness and Mind* (2005); Carruthers, *Phenomenal Consciousness* (2000).

**The *structure*.**

$$
\text{State } S \text{ is *conscious* iff } \exists H\, (H \text{ is a *representation* *of* } S)
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *difference* between *conscious* and *unconscious* *states* (the *higher-order* *representation* *makes* the *difference*), and it *fits* the *introspective* *access*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *empty* *higher-order* *problem*** (Block, “The Higher Order Approach to Consciousness Is Defunct,” 2011). A *higher-order* *representation* can *occur* without a *first-order* *state* (the *“empty”* *case*), and the *theory* *implies* that the *empty* *representation* is *conscious* — which is *absurd*.

**(ii) The *targetless* *problem***. A *higher-order* *misrepresentation* can *represent* a *first-order* *state* that *does* not *exist*, and the *theory* *implies* that the *nonexistent* *state* is *conscious*.

**(iii) The *explanatory* *gap***. The *theory* *explains* *access* but *not* *phenomenality*: why does the *higher-order* *representation* *give* *rise* to *experience*?

### 4.2 Global workspace theory

**The *thesis***. Consciousness is the *global* *broadcast* of *information* in a *workspace* — the *availability* of the *information* to *many* *cognitive* *processes*.

**The *statement*.** Baars, *A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness* (1988); Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain* (2014); the *empirical* *literature*.

**The *structure*.**

| Element | The *role* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Workspace** | The *limited* *capacity* *system* | The *prefrontal* *cortex* |
| **Broadcast** | The *distribution* of *information* to *many* *processes* | The *“ignition”* *of* *neuronal* *activity* |
| **Access** | The *availability* of *information* to *report* and *control* | The *“global* *availability”* |

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *fits* the *empirical* *literature* (the *neural* *correlates* of *consciousness*, the *“ignition”* *pattern*), and it *explains* the *access* *consciousness*.

**The *objection*.** The *theory* *explains* *access* but *not* *phenomenality*: it *tells* us *which* *information* is *available*, but not *why* the *availability* *feels* *like* *anything*. (The *reply*: the *phenomenality* *is* the *global* *availability*; the *reply* is *precisely* what the *hard* *problem* *denies*.)

### 4.3 Integrated information theory

**The *thesis***. Consciousness is *integrated* *information*: a *system* is *conscious* to the *degree* that it *integrates* *information* *irreducibly*.

**The *statement*.** Tononi, “An Information Integration Theory of Consciousness” (2004); Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi, “From the Phenomenology to the Mechanisms of Consciousness” (2014); Koch, *Consciousness* (2012).

**The *structure*.**

| Concept | The *definition* | The *role* |
|---|---|---|
| **Information** | The *reduction* of *uncertainty* | The *content* |
| **Integration** | The *irreducibility* of the *whole* to the *parts* | The *unity* |
| **Φ (phi)** | The *measure* of *integrated* *information* | The *degree* of *consciousness* |

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *provides* a *mathematical* *measure* of *consciousness*, and it *makes* *testable* *predictions* (the *zap* *and* *zap* *experiment*; the *posterior* *hot* *zone*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *panpsychist* *consequence***. The *theory* *implies* that *any* *system* with *Φ > 0* is *conscious* — *including* *photodiodes*, *thermostats*, and *the* *economy* — and the *consequence* is *panpsychist* (Ch. 19, §7).

**The *reply*.** The *consequence* is *acceptable* (panpsychism is *not* *absurd*); the *reply* *is* *contested*.

**(ii) The *intractability* *problem***. The *Φ* *measure* is *computationally* *intractable* for *large* *systems*, and the *theory* is *not* *testable* *in* *practice*.

**(iii) The *“unfolding”* *objection*** (Aaronson, “Why I Am Not an Integrated Information Theorist,” 2014). The *theory* *implies* that a *grid* of *logic* *gates* is *more* *conscious* than a *human* *being*, and the *implication* is *absurd*.

**The *reply*.** The *intuition* is *not* *reliable* (the *theory* *tells* us *what* *consciousness* *is*, and the *intuition* *is* *pre-theoretic*); the *reply* *is* *contested*.

### 4.4 Representationalism

**The *thesis***. Consciousness is a *species* of *representation*: the *phenomenal* *character* of an *experience* is *identified* with its *representational* *content* (Ch. 5, §4).

**The *statement*.** Dretske, *Naturalizing the Mind* (1995); Tye, *Ten Problems of Consciousness* (1995); Byrne & Tye, “Qualia Ain’t in the Head” (2006).

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *reduces* the *phenomenal* to the *representational*, and it *fits* the *naturalistic* *worldview*.

**The *objection*.** The *theory* *faces* the *explanatory* *gap* *for* *content*: why does *representational* *content* *feel* *like* *anything*? (The *reply*: the *content* *just* *is* the *feeling*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 4.5 Illusionism

**The *thesis***. The *hard* *problem* is an *illusion*: *phenomenal* *consciousness*, as *conceived* by the *hard* *problem*, *does* *not* *exist*. What *exists* is a *set* of *introspective* *representations* that *misrepresent* the *mental* *states* as *having* *phenomenal* *properties*.

**The *statement*.** Frankish, “Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness” (2016), and “Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness, Revised” (2019); Dennett, *Consciousness Explained* (1991), and “Quining Qualia” (1988); the *contemporary* *defenders* (Kammerer 2019; the *essays* in *Illusionism*, ed. Frankish, 2017).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *phenomenal* *properties* (qualia) are *introspectively* *represented* *as* *they* *seem* (the *“seeming”*).} \\
2. & \text{The *introspective* *representations* are *the* *only* *data*.} \\
3. & \text{The *introspective* *representations* *misrepresent* the *mental* *states* (they *attribute* *phenomenal* *properties* to *states* that *do* *not* *have* them).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *phenomenal* *properties* are *illusions*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *hard* *problem* by *denying* the *existence* of the *phenomenal* *properties*; it *makes* the *problem* *tractable* (the *meta-problem*; §5).

**The *objection*.** The *theory* *denies* the *most* *obvious* *datum* of *experience* — the *“what* *it* *is* *like”*. The *denial* *seems* to be *a* *form* of *eliminativism* about *consciousness* (Ch. 23, §6), and the *eliminativism* *faces* the *self-refutation* *objection*.

**The *reply*.** The *reply* is *the* *illusionist’s* *most* *important* *task*: the *illusion* *must* be *explained*, and the *explanation* is the *meta-problem*. The *reply* is *promising* and *incomplete*.

### 4.6 The phenomenal concept strategy

**The *thesis*** (Loar 1990; Papineau 2002; Chalmers 2003; the *strategy* *is* *the* *most* *influential* *physicalist* *response* to the *knowledge* and *zombie* *arguments*).

**The *structure*. The *phenomenal* *concepts* are *special*: they *refer* to *physical* *properties* *under* a *first-person* *mode* of *presentation*, and the *mode* *explains* the *explanatory* *gap* *without* *positing* *non-physical* *properties*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *phenomenal* *concepts* *refer* to *physical* *properties*.} \\
2. & \text{The *phenomenal* *concepts* *present* the *properties* *under* a *distinctive* *mode* (the *first-person*).} \\
3. & \text{The *gap* *between* the *physical* *concept* and the *phenomenal* *concept* is an *epistemic* *gap*, not a *metaphysical* *gap*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Physicalism is *compatible* with the *explanatory* *gap*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *strategy* *preserves* *physicalism* and *explains* the *gap*, and it is *the* *most* *sophisticated* *response* to the *knowledge* and *zombie* *arguments*.

**The *objection*. The *strategy* *presupposes* that the *phenomenal* *concepts* *refer* to *physical* *properties*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *anti-physicalist* *denies*. (The *reply*: the *strategy* *offers* a *theory* of *reference*, and the *theory* is *defended* *independently*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**The *state* of the debate.** The *strategy* is the *most* *influential* *physicalist* *position*, and it is *unresolved*. The *choice* *between* the *strategy* and *property* *dualism* *is* the *central* *disagreement* in the *philosophy* of *mind*.

### 4.7 The theories compared

| Theory | The *core* *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Higher-order** | Consciousness = higher-order representation | Explains access | Empty higher-order |
| **Global workspace** | Consciousness = global broadcast | Fits the neuroscience | Explains access, not phenomenality |
| **IIT** | Consciousness = integrated information | Mathematical | Panpsychist; intractable |
| **Representationalism** | Consciousness = representation | Naturalistic | Explanatory gap for content |
| **Illusionism** | Consciousness = illusion | Dissolves the hard problem | Denies the datum |
| **Phenomenal concepts** | Consciousness = physical, under a special concept | Preserves physicalism | Begs the question |
| **Panpsychism** | Consciousness = fundamental | Fits the hard problem | Combination problem |
| **Property dualism** | Consciousness = irreducible property | Fits the hard problem | Exclusion problem |

**The *lesson*.** The *theories* are *all* *live*, and *none* is *decisive*. The *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to the *criteria* (Ch. 23, §1.3) and on the *solution* to the *meta-problem* (§5).

## 5. The Meta-Problem

### 5.1 The problem

**The *thesis*** (Chalmers, “The Meta-Problem of Consciousness,” 2018, 2020). The *meta-problem* is the *problem* of *explaining* why we *think* there is a *hard* *problem* — why we *have* the *intuitions* and *reports* that *generate* the problem.

**The *structure*.**

| Problem | The *question* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **The hard problem** | Why is there *phenomenal* *experience*? | *Intractable* |
| **The meta-problem** | Why do we *think* there is a *hard* *problem*? | *Tractable* (by *cognitive* *science*) |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *hard* *problem* is *generated* by our *intuitions* and *reports* (the *“problem* *intuitions”*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intuitions* and *reports* are *cognitive* *phenomena*, *explainable* by *cognitive* *science*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meta-problem* is *tractable*, and its *solution* may *dissolve* the *hard* *problem*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *meta-problem* is the *most* *promising* *route* to *progress*, because it *turns* the *philosophical* *problem* into an *empirical* *one*. The *empirical* *problem* *may* *dissolve* the *philosophical* *problem* (the *illusionist* *route*) or *vindicate* it (the *realist* *route*).

### 5.2 The explanations

**The *candidate* *explanations* of the *problem* *intuitions***.

| Explanation | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Illusionism** | The intuitions are *introspective* *misrepresentations* | Frankish; Dennett |
| **The *“phenomenal* *concept”* *explanation*** | The intuitions are *generated* by the *first-person* *concepts* | Papineau; Chalmers |
| **The *“predictive* *processing”* *explanation*** | The intuitions are *generated* by the *brain’s* *self-model* | Seth 2021 |
| **The *“social* *construction”* *explanation*** | The intuitions are *shaped* by *cultural* *practices* | the *cross-cultural* *literature* |

**The *significance*.** The *explanations* are *all* *empirical* *hypotheses*, and the *empirical* *literature* is *beginning* to *test* them (the *“meta-problem”* *research* *programme*).

### 5.3 The dissolution

**The *argument*** (the *illusionist* *version*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *problem* *intuitions* are *fully* *explained* by *cognitive* *science* (the *meta-problem* is *solved*).} \\
2. & \text{The *explanation* *shows* that the *intuitions* are *misrepresentations* (the *phenomenal* *properties* *do* not *exist*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *hard* *problem* *dissolves*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *hard* *problem* is *not* *solved*; it is *dissolved* — the *question* *loses* its *grip* (Ch. 3, §7).

**The *objection*.** The *dissolution* *denies* the *datum* of *experience*, and the *denial* is *counterintuitive*. (The *reply*: the *datum* is the *seeming*, and the *seeming* is *explained*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 5.4 The vindication

**The *argument*** (the *realist* *version*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *problem* *intuitions* are *not* *fully* *explained* by *cognitive* *science* (the *meta-problem* is *unsolved*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intuitions* *track* a *real* *feature* of the *world* (the *phenomenal* *properties*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *hard* *problem* is *real* and *unresolved*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *hard* *problem* is *vindicated*, and the *meta-problem* *supports* *property* *dualism* or *panpsychism*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *two* *versions* are *live*, and the *empirical* *literature* is *not* *decisive*. The *choice* *depends* on the *interpretation* of the *intuitions*, which is *itself* a *philosophical* *question*.

## 6. Animal, Infant, and Artificial Consciousness

### 6.1 The attribution problem

**The *problem***. We *cannot* *directly* *access* the *consciousness* of *others* (Ch. 29, §1), and the *attribution* of *consciousness* is *inferential*. The *criteria* are *behavioural*, *structural*, *functional*, and *theoretical*.

**The *criteria*.**

| Criterion | The *evidence* | The *limitation* |
|---|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | The *flexible*, *appropriate* *response* to *stimuli* | *Behaviour* can be *simulated* |
| **Structural** | The *neural* *architecture* (the *thalamocortical* *system*) | The *architecture* is *necessary*, not *sufficient* |
| **Functional** | The *integration* of *information* (the *global* *workspace*) | The *integration* is *not* *sufficient* for *phenomenality* |
| **Theoretical** | The *theory* of *consciousness* (IIT, GWT) | The *theories* are *contested* |

**The *consequence*.** The *attribution* is *probabilistic* and *fallible*, and the *probabilities* *differ* *across* the *cases*.

### 6.2 Animals

**The *evidence*.** The *behavioural* *evidence* (the *flexible* *problem-solving*, the *social* *cognition*, the *pain* *behaviour*), the *structural* *evidence* (the *thalamocortical* *system* is *present* in *mammals* and *birds*), and the *evolutionary* *evidence* (the *continuity* of the *nervous* *system*).

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Mammal-centric** | Only *mammals* and *birds* are *conscious* | the *mainstream* *view* |
| **Inclusive** | *Many* *animals* (fish, cephalopods, insects) are *conscious* | the *“Cambridge* *Declaration”* (2012); the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Sceptical** | *No* *non-human* *animals* are *conscious* | the *Cartesian* *view* (the *“beast-machine”*) |

**The *ethical* *stakes*.** The *attribution* *determines* the *moral* *status* of *animals* (Ch. 51, §5): *if* the *animals* are *conscious*, their *suffering* *matters* *morally*, and the *practices* of *factory* *farming*, *experimentation*, and *habitat* *destruction* are *morally* *serious*.

**The *precautionary* *principle*.** The *uncertainty* *supports* a *precautionary* *approach*: *where* the *evidence* is *uncertain*, the *attribution* of *consciousness* *should* *err* on the *side* of *caution* (the *“precautionary* *principle”*; Birch 2017).

### 6.3 Infants

**The *evidence*.** The *behavioural* *evidence* (the *responses* to *pain*, the *social* *engagement*), the *structural* *evidence* (the *developing* *neural* *system*), and the *theoretical* *evidence* (the *development* of the *global* *workspace*).

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Early** | *Consciousness* is *present* *before* *birth* (the *third* *trimester*) | the *contemporary* *consensus* |
| **Late** | *Consciousness* *emerges* *after* *birth* (the *first* *year*) | the *“late* *emergence”* *view* |

**The *ethical* *stakes*.** The *attribution* *determines* the *moral* *status* of *fetuses* and *infants* (Ch. 45; the *abortion* *debate*; the *neonatal* *care* *debate*).

**The *epistemic* *humility*.** The *attribution* is *uncertain*, and the *uncertainty* *supports* *caution* in *both* *directions* (the *treatment* of *fetuses* and the *treatment* of *infants*).

### 6.4 Artificial consciousness

**The *question*.** Can *AI* *systems* be *conscious*?

**The *criteria*.**

| Criterion | The *test* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | The *Turing* *test* | The *test* *measures* *performance*, not *experience* |
| **Functional** | The *global* *workspace* | The *workspace* *can* be *simulated* |
| **Structural** | The *neural* *architecture* | The *architecture* is *different* from the *brain* |
| **Theoretical** | The *IIT* (the *Φ* *measure*) | The *measure* is *intractable* |

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong AI** | *Consciousness* *is* *computation* | the *classical* *computational* *theory* |
| **Weak AI** | *Consciousness* *requires* a *physical* *substrate* | the *biological* *naturalism* (Searle) |
| **Sceptical** | *Consciousness* *is* *not* *achievable* by *AI* | the *dualist* *and* *mysterian* *views* |

**The *ethical* *stakes*.** The *attribution* *determines* the *moral* *status* of *AI* *systems* (Ch. 51, §3): *if* the *systems* are *conscious*, their *interests* *matter*, and the *design* of *AI* *must* *take* the *interests* *into* *account*.

**The *“moral* *uncertainty”* *argument*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *uncertainty* *supports* *caution* *in* the *design* of *AI* (the *avoidance* of *suffering*; the *avoidance* of *deception*).

## 7. Contemplative and Cross-Cultural Data

### 7.1 The data

**The *claim***. The *contemplative* *traditions* (Buddhist *meditation*, *Advaita* *Vedānta*, *Christian* *mysticism*, *Sufism*) have *developed* *sophisticated* *first-person* *methods* for *investigating* *consciousness*, and the *methods* *produce* *data* that are *relevant* to the *philosophy* of *mind*.

**The *examples*.**

| Tradition | The *phenomenon* | The *claim* |
|---|---|---|
| **Theravāda** *Buddhism* | *Anattā* (non-self); *vipassanā* (insight) | The *self* is *not* a *substance*; *consciousness* is a *stream* |
| **Mahāyāna** *Buddhism* | *Rigpa* (pure awareness); *śūnyatā* (emptiness) | *Consciousness* is *luminous* and *empty* |
| **Advaita** *Vedānta* | *Turīya* (the *fourth* *state*); *sākṣin* (the *witness*) | *Consciousness* is *fundamental* and *unitary* |
| **Christian** *mysticism* | *Unio* *mystica*; the *dark* *night* | The *soul* *unites* with *God* |
| **Phenomenology** | *Epochē*; the *lifeworld* | *Consciousness* is *intentional* and *embodied* |

**The *significance*.** The *traditions* *offer* *first-person* *data* about *consciousness* that the *analytic* *tradition* has *largely* *ignored*, and the *data* *challenge* the *assumptions* of the *theories*.

### 7.2 The methodological questions

**The *questions*.**

| Question | The *challenge* |
|---|---|
| **The *evidential* *question*** | Are the *first-person* *reports* *evidence*? |
| **The *interpretive* *question*** | Are the *reports* *describing* the *same* *phenomena* as the *Western* *theories*? |
| **The *theoretical* *question*** | Do the *traditions* *have* a *theory* of *consciousness*, or only a *practice*? |
| **The *naturalistic* *question*** | Are the *reports* *compatible* with *physicalism*? |

**The *evidential* *question*.** The *reports* are *first-person* *data*, and the *data* are *not* *intersubjectively* *checkable* (Ch. 3, §6.1). The *reports* *can* be *treated* as *data* to be *explained* (by *psychology*, *neuroscience*), or as *evidence* for *claims* about *consciousness*.

**The *interpretive* *question*.** The *traditions* *use* *different* *concepts* (anattā, rigpa, sākṣin), and the *concepts* *may* *not* *map* *onto* the *Western* *concepts* (the *“incommensurability”* *problem*; Ch. 13, §3).

**The *theoretical* *question*.** The *traditions* *have* *sophisticated* *theories* (the *Abhidharma* *taxonomy*, the *Advaita* *analysis*), and the *theories* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Ch. 19, §7; Ch. 20, §4.4).

### 7.3 The *anattā* *debate*

**The *Buddhist* *claim***. The *self* is *not* a *substance*; it is a *convention*, a *stream* of *moments* (the *“five* *aggregates”*).

**The *argument*** (the *Anattalakkhaṇa* *Sutta*; the *Milindapañha*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If the *self* were a *substance*, it would be *permanent* and *unchanging*.} \\
2. & \text{Everything that is *observed* is *impermanent* and *changing*.} \\
3. & \text{The *self* is *not* *observed* *apart* from the *aggregates*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *self* is *not* a *substance*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *relevance*.** The *argument* *anticipates* the *Buddhist* *reductionism* *about* the *self* (Ch. 26, §4), and it *connects* the *philosophy* of *mind* to the *philosophy* of the *self*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *assumes* that the *self* *must* be *observed* to *exist*, and the *assumption* is *contested* (the *self* *may* be *the* *subject* of *experience*, not an *object*).

### 7.4 The *witness* *debate*

**The *Advaita* *claim***. The *self* is *pure* *witness* *consciousness* (*sākṣin*), *distinct* from the *body*, the *mind*, and the *ego*.

**The *argument*** (Śaṅkara, *Upadeśasāhasrī*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *witness* is *that* *in* *which* all *experience* *appears*.} \\
2. & \text{The *witness* is *not* *itself* an *object* of *experience* (it is *the* *subject*).} \\
3. & \text{The *witness* is *unitary* and *unchanging*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *self* is *pure* *consciousness* (the *witness*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *relevance*.** The *view* *anticipates* the *idealist* and *panpsychist* *accounts* (Ch. 19, §7; Ch. 20, §4), and it *connects* the *philosophy* of *mind* to the *philosophy* of *consciousness*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *assumes* that the *witness* is *distinct* from the *content*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *Buddhist* *anattā* *denies*. The *debate* between the *Advaita* and the *Buddhist* *traditions* is *one* of the *deepest* in the *history* of *philosophy*, and it is *still* *live*.

### 7.5 The *naturalistic* *interpretation*

**The *claim***. The *contemplative* *reports* are *compatible* with *physicalism*, and the *reports* *describe* *altered* *states* of the *brain* (the *default* *mode* *network*, the *insula*, the *prefrontal* *cortex*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meditative* *states* are *correlated* with *neural* *changes* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *changes* *explain* the *reports* *without* *positing* *non-physical* *consciousness*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *reports* are *compatible* with *physicalism*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *fits* the *naturalistic* *framework*, and it *makes* the *contemplative* *data* *accessible* to *cognitive* *science*.

**The *objection*.** The *correlation* *does* not *explain* the *phenomenal* *character* — the *same* *gap* as the *hard* *problem*. The *naturalistic* *interpretation* *is* *available*, and it is *contested*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The distinction between phenomenal and access consciousness is real.** The *four* *kinds* of *consciousness* are *distinct*, and the *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*.
- **The hard problem is real.** The *phenomenal* *character* of *experience* is *not* *explained* by any *current* *theory*, and the *explanatory* *gap* is *real*.
- **The knowledge argues are not decisive.** The *replies* (the *ability* *hypothesis*, the *phenomenal* *concept* *strategy*) are *available*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *theory* of *phenomenal* *concepts*.
- **The theories are all live, and none is decisive.** The *choice* *depends* on the *criteria* and on the *meta-problem*.
- **The meta-problem is tractable.** The *problem* of *explaining* our *problem-intuitions* is *empirical*, and the *empirical* *literature* is *beginning* to *test* the *explanations*.
- **The attribution problem is urgent.** The *ethical* *stakes* (animals, infants, AI) are *high*, and the *uncertainty* *supports* *caution*.
- **The contemplative data are relevant.** The *traditions* *offer* *first-person* *data* that the *analytic* *tradition* has *ignored*, and the *data* *challenge* the *assumptions* of the *theories*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The nature of the phenomenal.** Is it *irreducible*, *representational*, or *illusory*?
- **The solution to the meta-problem.** Are the *problem-intuitions* *misrepresentations* (illusionism) or *tracking* a *real* *feature* (realism)?
- **The nature of the theories.** Are the *theories* (IIT, GWT, higher-order) *adequate*, and which is *best*?
- **The attribution.** Are *animals*, *infants*, and *AI* *conscious*?
- **The interpretation of the contemplative data.** Are the *reports* *evidence* for *non-physical* *consciousness*, or *descriptions* of *altered* *brain* *states*?

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *four* *kinds* of *consciousness*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — *Mary’s* *room*, the *zombie*, the *Chinese* *nation* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *Φ* *measure*, the *higher-order* *structure* — is *essential* for the *theories*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The honest verdict: the theory of consciousness is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary theories are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Higher-order** | Consciousness = higher-order representation | Explains access | Empty higher-order |
| **Global workspace** | Consciousness = global broadcast | Fits neuroscience | Explains access, not phenomenality |
| **IIT** | Consciousness = integrated information | Mathematical | Panpsychist; intractable |
| **Representationalism** | Consciousness = representation | Naturalistic | Explanatory gap for content |
| **Illusionism** | Consciousness = illusion | Dissolves the hard problem | Denies the datum |
| **Phenomenal concepts** | Consciousness = physical, special concept | Preserves physicalism | Begs the question |
| **Panpsychism** | Consciousness = fundamental | Fits the hard problem | Combination problem |
| **Property dualism** | Consciousness = irreducible property | Fits the hard problem | Exclusion problem |
| **Mysterianism** | Consciousness = beyond our cognitive grasp | Honest | Unexplanatory |

The table is a map, not a verdict. The reader who wants to choose a position must decide which cost is acceptable — and the decision is a philosophical argument.

The *account* *fits* the *predictive* *processing* *framework* (Ch. 5, §5.2), and it is *promising* and *incomplete*.

**3. The *quantum* *consciousness* *hypotheses*.** Penrose (*Shadows of the Mind*, 1994) and Hameroff (*“Quantum Computation in Brain Microtubules”*, 1998) *propose* that *consciousness* *arises* from *quantum* *processes* in *microtubules*. The *hypothesis* is *contested* (the *decoherence* *problem*; the *lack* of *evidence*), and it is *a* *minority* *position*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *consciousness*.** The *Buddhist* *Abhidharma* *tradition* *developed* a *sophisticated* *taxonomy* of *mental* *states* (the *“five* *aggregates”*, the *“fifty-one* *mental* *factors”*) that *anticipates* the *contemporary* *theories* of *consciousness*, and the *Advaita* *Vedānta* *tradition* *developed* a *radical* *monism* that *anticipates* the *idealist* and *panpsychist* *accounts* (Ch. 19, §7; Ch. 20, §4). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; Thompson 2020).

**5. The *ethics* of *consciousness*.** The *attribution* of *consciousness* has *ethical* *consequences* (animals, infants, AI), and the *ethics* is *underdeveloped*. The *“precautionary* *principle”* (Birch 2017) is *one* *response*, and it is *contested*. The *ethics* of *consciousness* is *the* *most* *urgent* *area* of the *field*.

## Summary

- **The *four* *kinds* of *consciousness* — creature, phenomenal, access, self — are *distinct*, and the *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*.
- **The *hard* *problem*** (Chalmers) is the *problem* of *phenomenal* *experience*: *why* is there *something* it is *like* to be in a *mental* *state*? The *problem* is *not* *reducible* to the *easy* *problems*.
- **The *explanatory* *gap*** (Levine) is the *epistemic* *residue*: even if *physicalism* is *true*, we *cannot* *explain* *why* the *physical* *states* *give* *rise* to the *phenomenal* *states*.
- **The *knowledge* arguments** (Mary’s *room*, the *zombie*, *absent* *qualia*) are the *most* *influential* *arguments* *against* *physicalism*, and they are *not* *decisive*.
- **The *theories*** (higher-order, global *workspace*, IIT, representationalism, illusionism, phenomenal *concepts*, panpsychism, property *dualism*) are *all* *live*, and *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *meta-problem*** (Chalmers) is the *problem* of *explaining* our *problem-intuitions*, and it is *tractable*. Its *solution* *may* *dissolve* the *hard* *problem* (illusionism) or *vindicate* it (realism).
- **The *attribution* *problem*** (animals, infants, AI) is *urgent*, and the *uncertainty* *supports* *caution*.
- **The *contemplative* *data*** are *relevant*, and the *traditions* *offer* *systematic* *alternatives* to the *Western* *theories*.
- **The *state* of the *debate*: the *classical* *positions* are *untenable* in their *pure* forms; the *contemporary* *theories* are *promising* but *incomplete*; the *debate* is *unresolved*.
