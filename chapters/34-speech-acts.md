# Chapter 34 — Speech Acts, Presupposition, and Pragmatics

> “It was for too long the assumption of philosophers that the business of a ‘statement’ can only be to ‘describe’ some state of affairs, or to ‘state some fact’, which it must do either truly or falsely. … But now, in recent years, … we have come to realize that many utterances which look like statements are either not intended at all, or only intended in part, to record or impart straightforward information about the facts.”
> — J. L. Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962), Lecture I

> “The presence of a conversational implicature must be capable of being worked out. … The hearer must be able to calculate it, and the fact that it is calculable is what distinguishes it from a mere association.”
> — H. P. Grice, “Logic and Conversation” (1975)

> “To say something is to do something — to assert, to warn, to promise, to christen. The philosophy of language that forgets this is a philosophy of a language no one speaks.”
> — a formulation of the pragmatic turn, after Austin and Searle

---

Chapter 33 ended with *paradox* — the point at which the *concepts* of *semantics* break down. This chapter approaches the *same* *territory* from a *different* *direction*: not from the *content* of the utterance but from the *act* of uttering it.

The *shift* is *decisive*, and it was *Austin*'s. The *tradition* that *preceded* him treated language as a *system* of *representations* — strings of symbols with *truth conditions*. Austin *observed* that *most* of what we *do* with language is *not* *representation*. We *promise*, *warn*, *name*, *marry*, *bet*, *order*, *apologize*, *insult*, and *insinuate*. These *acts* are *not* *assessable* as *true* or *false* in the first instance; they are *assessable* as *felicitous* or *infelicitous*, *appropriate* or *inappropriate*, *binding* or *void*. And the *conditions* of *felicity* are *social*, *institutional*, and *normative* — they are the point at which *language* meets *power*.

The chapter's *thesis* is that the *pragmatic* *dimension* of language is *not* a *peripheral* *addition* to the *semantic* *core* but a *constitutive* *dimension* of the *same* phenomenon; that the *semantics/pragmatics* *boundary* is *real* but *contested*; that *presupposition* reveals the *normative* *background* against which every *utterance* is *made*; and that the *speech-act* *framework*, *properly* *developed*, is the *most* *powerful* *tool* that *philosophy* has for understanding *language* as a *social* *practice*.

The chapter proceeds in nine movements. §1 examines the *performative* and its *discovery*. §2 examines the *threefold* *distinction*. §3 examines *Searle*'s *taxonomy* and the *constitutive* *rules*. §4 examines *Grice* and *implicature*. §5 examines the *semantics/pragmatics* *boundary*. §6 examines *presupposition* and *accommodation*. §7 examines *non-literal* speech. §8 examines *performative* *contradiction*. §9 examines *language* and *power*. §10 gives an *honest* *verdict* and *closes* **Part V**.

---

## 1. The Performative and Its Discovery

### 1.1 The distinction

**The *datum***. Austin begins with an *observation* about a class of *utterances* that *resist* the *true/false* *assessment*.

**The *examples***.

| Utterance | What it does | The *assessment* |
|---|---|---|
| “I name this ship the *Queen Elizabeth*” | It *names* the ship | *Felicitous* or *infelicitous* |
| “I promise to pay you five pounds” | It *creates* an obligation | *Binding* or *void* |
| “I bet you sixpence it will rain tomorrow” | It *places* a bet | *Effective* or *ineffective* |
| “I pronounce you husband and wife” | It *marries* the couple | *Valid* or *invalid* |
| “I apologize for the delay” | It *apologizes* | *Sincere* or *insincere* |

**The *thesis***. The *utterances* are **performative**: in *saying* the *words*, the *speaker* *does* the *act*, and the *act* is *not* a *description* of the *act* (the *utterance* does *not* *describe* a *naming*; it *is* the *naming*).

**The *contrast***. The **constative** *utterances* are *descriptions* (true or false), and the **performative** *utterances* are *actions* (felicitous or infelicitous). The *distinction* is *grammatical* (the *first-person* *present* *indicative* *active*, the *“hereby”* *construction*), and it is *explicit* in the *performative* *form*.

**The *significance***. The *discovery* *undermines* the *descriptive* *fallacy* — the *assumption* (the *positivist* *assumption*; Ch. 9, §2.3) that the *only* *meaningful* *utterances* are *descriptions* — and it *opens* the *field* of *pragmatics*.

### 1.2 Felicity conditions

**The *thesis***. The *performative* *utterance* *succeeds* (is *felicitous*) only if *certain* *conditions* *obtain*.

**The *conditions*** (Austin 1962, Lecture II, pp. 14–15).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(\mathrm{A.1}) & \text{There is an *accepted* *conventional* *procedure* *having* a *certain* *effect.} \\
(\mathrm{A.2}) & \text{The *circumstances* *are* *appropriate* to the *procedure*.} \\
(\mathrm{B.1}) & \text{The *procedure* is *executed* *correctly* *by* *all* *participants*.} \\
(\mathrm{B.2}) & \text{The *procedure* is *executed* *completely*.} \\
(\Gamma.1) & \text{The *participants* *have* the *requisite* *thoughts*, *feelings*, and *intentions*.} \\
(\Gamma.2) & \text{The *participants* *conduct* *themselves* *accordingly* *afterwards*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *taxonomy* of *infelicities***.

| The *kind* | The *condition* *violated* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Misinvocation** | A.1, A.2 | The *ship-naming* by someone *not* *authorized*; the *baptism* in a *country* that *does* not *recognize* the *church* |
| **Misexecution** | B.1, B.2 | The *marriage* *ceremony* with the *wrong* *words*; the *bet* that is *not* *completed* |
| **Abuse** | Γ.1, Γ.2 | The *insincere* *promise*; the *apology* without *regret*; the *warning* without *belief* |

**The *distinction***. The *misfires* (the *misinvocation* and the *misexecution*) make the *act* *void* (the *act* does *not* *take* *place*); the *abuses* make the *act* *defective* (the *act* *takes* *place*, but it is *insincere* or *inconsistent*).

**The *significance***. The *conditions* are *normative* and *social* (they *depend* on the *conventions*, the *institutions*, and the *community*), and the *normativity* is the *first* *sign* of the *connection* between *language* and *power* (§9).

### 1.3 The collapse of the distinction

**The *argument*** (Austin 1962, Lectures VIII–XI). The *distinction* between the *constative* and the *performative* *collapses*, because the *constative* *utterances* are *also* *assessable* by *felicity* *conditions* (the *assertion* *requires* the *sincerity*, the *evidence*, the *authority*), and the *performative* *utterances* are *also* *assessable* by *truth*-*like* *conditions* (the *promise* can be *described* *truly* or *falsely* as a *promise*, and the *assertion* *performed* by a *performative* *form* — “I state that $p$” — is *true* or *false* in *virtue* of $p$).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *constatives* are *assessed* by the *felicity* *conditions* (the *assertion* has its *own* *norms*).} \\
2. & \text{The *performatives* are *assessed* by the *truth*-*like* *conditions* (the *act* can be *described* *truly* or *falsely*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *distinction* *collapses* (the *performative*/*constative* *dichotomy* is *not* *fundamental*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. Austin *replaces* the *dichotomy* with the *threefold* *distinction* (the *locutionary*, the *illocutionary*, the *perlocutionary*; §2), and the *replacement* is the *foundation* of the *speech-act* *theory*.

**The *significance***. Every *utterance* is *both* a *representation* (it has *content*) *and* an *action* (it has *force*), and the *two* *dimensions* are *distinct* but *inseparable*. The *distinction* is the *central* *thesis* of the *chapter*.

## 2. The Threefold Distinction

### 2.1 The structure

**The *thesis*** (Austin 1962, Lecture VIII, pp. 94–108). The *total* *speech* *act* is *analyzed* into *three* *acts*.

| The *act* | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Locutionary** | The *act* of *saying* *something* (the *production* of a *meaningful* *utterance*) | The *utterance* of the *sounds*, the *words*, the *sentence* |
| **Illocutionary** | The *act* *done* *in* *saying* (the *force* of the *utterance*) | The *assertion*, the *warning*, the *promise*, the *order* |
| **Perlocutionary** | The *act* *done* *by* *saying* (the *effect* of the *utterance*) | The *persuasion*, the *alarm*, the *amusement* |

**The *locutionary* *act*** is *further* *subdivided* (Austin 1962, p. 92).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Phonetic act} & \text{The *production* of the *sounds}. \\
\text{Phatic act} & \text{The *production* of *words* in a *grammar}. \\
\text{Rhetic act} & \text{The *production* of a *sentence* *with* a *sense* and a *reference}. \\
\end{array}
$$

**The *illocutionary* *force*** is *indicated* by the *mood* (the *imperative*, the *interrogative*, the *indicative*), the *performative* *prefix* (“I promise”), the *intonation*, and the *context*.

**The *perlocutionary* *effect*** is *not* *conventional* (it is *causal*): the *warning* is *illocutionary* (it is *complete* when the *hearer* *understands* the *force*), and the *alarm* is *perlocutionary* (it is *complete* when the *hearer* is *frightened*).

### 2.2 The formal distinction

**The *structure* (the *Searlean* *formalization*; Searle 1969, Ch. 2).

$$
\text{Speech act} = F(p)
$$

where $F$ is the *illocutionary* *force* and $p$ is the *propositional* *content*. The *same* *content* *can* *occur* *with* *different* *forces*:

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Promise}: & F_{\text{promise}}(p) \\
\text{Prediction}: & F_{\text{predict}}(p) \\
\text{Warning}: & F_{\text{warn}}(p) \\
\text{Threat}: & F_{\text{threat}}(p)
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *content* is *not* *sufficient* to *determine* the *act* (the *same* *content* *can* be *promised*, *predicted*, *warned*, *threatened*), and the *force* is *not* *reducible* to the *content* (the *force* is *constituted* by the *conventions* and the *intentions*).

### 2.3 Uptake and the illocutionary/perlocutionary boundary

**The *question***. Is the *illocutionary* *act* *complete* when the *hearer* *understands* it (the *uptake*), or *independently* of the *hearer*?

**Austin's *requirement*** (1962, p. 117). The *illocutionary* *act* *requires* the *uptake* (the *hearer*'s *recognition* of the *force*). The *order* is *given* only if the *hearer* *recognizes* it as an *order*.

**The *objection***. The *uptake* *requirement* makes the *illocutionary* *act* *depend* on the *hearer*, and the *dependence* *blurs* the *distinction* between the *illocutionary* (the *act* *done* *in* *saying*) and the *perlocutionary* (the *act* *done* *by* *saying*).

**The *reply*** (Searle 1969, p. 47). The *uptake* is the *recognition* of the *force* (the *hearer* *understands* that the *speaker* *is* *ordering*), and the *recognition* is *not* the *obedience* (the *perlocutionary* *effect*). The *reply* *distinguishes* the *understanding* from the *effect*, and it is *defensible*.

**The *significance***. The *uptake* *requirement* is the *point* at which the *speech-act* *theory* *connects* to the *epistemology* *of* *testimony* (the *testimony* *requires* the *hearer*'s *recognition*; Ch. 11, §1) and to the *social* *theory* *of* *power* (the *silencing* *is* the *failure* of *uptake*; §9.2).

## 3. Searle: The Taxonomy and the Constitutive Rules

### 3.1 The taxonomy

**The *thesis*** (Searle, “A Taxonomy of Illocutionary Acts,” 1975/1979; *Speech Acts*, 1969, Ch. 3). The *illocutionary* *acts* *divide* into *five* *categories*, and the *categories* are *defined* by the *direction* *of* *fit* (Ch. 28, §3.1) and the *psychological* *state* (the *sincerity* *condition*).

**The *taxonomy***.

| The *category* | The *direction* *of* *fit* | The *sincerity* | The *examples* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assertives** | Word → world | *Belief* | *Statements*, *descriptions*, *predictions*, *assertions* |
| **Directives** | World → word | *Desire* | *Requests*, *orders*, *questions*, *advice* |
| **Commissives** | World → word | *Intention* | *Promises*, *offers*, *threats*, *vows* |
| **Expressives** | *None* (the *presupposition* of the *fit*) | *The* *expressed* *state* | *Apologies*, *thanks*, *congratulations*, *condolences* |
| **Declarations** | *Both* (the *word* → *world* and the *world* → *word*) | *None* (the *institutional* *position*) | *Marrying*, *firing*, *declaring* *war*, *resigning* |

**The *structure***. The *categories* are *defined* by *four* *conditions* (the *propositional* *content*, the *preparatory*, the *sincerity*, the *essential*).

**The *significance***. The *taxonomy* *grounds* the *speech-act* *theory* in the *direction* *of* *fit* (Ch. 28, §3.1) and the *institutions* (the *declarations*), and it *provides* a *systematic* *framework* for the *comparative* *study* of the *speech* *acts* *across* the *cultures*.

### 3.2 Constitutive rules

**The *thesis*** (Searle, *Speech Acts*, 1969, Ch. 2; *The Construction of Social Reality*, 1995). The *institutions* are *constituted* by *rules* of the *form*:

$$
X \ \text{counts as} \ Y \ \text{in} \ C
$$

**The *distinction***.

| The *kind* | The *form* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulative** | “Do $X$” or “If $Y$, do $X$” | The *traffic* *laws* |
| **Constitutive** | “$X$ counts as $Y$ in $C$” | The *rules* of *chess*, the *rules* of *marriage* |

**The *argument***. The *regulative* *rules* *regulate* a *pre-existing* *activity*; the *constitutive* *rules* *create* the *possibility* of the *activity* (the *chess* *pieces* are *constituted* by the *rules*, and the *rules* *create* the *game*). The *speech* *acts* (the *promises*, the *marriages*) are *constituted* by the *constitutive* *rules*, and the *rules* *create* the *deontic* *powers* (the *obligations*, the *rights*, the *commitments*).

**The *significance***. The *constitutive* *rules* *connect* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *social* *ontology* (Ch. 25, §7.3), and the *connection* is the *foundation* of the *institutional* *reality* (the *money*, the *property*, the *government*).

### 3.3 The institutional reality

**The *thesis*** (Searle 1995, Ch. 2). The *institutional* *facts* are *constituted* by the *collective* *intentionality* (Ch. 25, §7.1) *plus* the *constitutive* *rules*, and the *facts* *have* *deontic* *powers* (the *rights*, the *obligations*, the *permissions*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Institutional fact} = \text{collective intentionality} + X \text{ counts as } Y \text{ in } C
$$

**The *examples***.

| The *fact* | The *constitutive* *rule* | The *deontic* *power* |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | *This paper counts as money in the economy* | The *purchase*, the *debt*, the *credit* |
| **Marriage** | *This ceremony counts as marriage in the community* | The *rights*, the *obligations*, the *inheritance* |
| **Property** | *This land counts as owned in the legal system* | The *exclusion*, the *transfer*, the *use* |
| **Government** | *This assembly counts as the legislature in the state* | The *legislation*, the *taxation*, the *coercion* |

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *ontology* of the *social* *world* (the *institutions* are *real*, *objective*, and *mind-dependent*), and it *fits* the *speech-act* *theory* (the *institutions* are *constituted* by the *declarations*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *collective* *intentionality*’* *objection*. The *theory* *presupposes* the *collective* *intentionality* (Ch. 25, §7.1), and the *notion* is *contested* (the *reductionist* *accounts*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *normativity*’* *objection*. The *theory* *explains* the *constitutive* *rules* but not the *normative* *force* (the *obligation* to *follow* the *rules*), and the *normativity* is *contested* (Ch. 44, §4).

**(iii) The *‘*political*’* *objection*. The *theory* *legitimates* the *status* *quo* (the *institutions* are *real*, and the *existing* *institutions* are *legitimate*), and the *legitimation* is *contested* (Ch. 47, §7).

### 3.4 The *taxonomy* *objections*

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *exhaustiveness***. Some *acts* *fit* *multiple* *categories* (“I warn you” is a *directive* and an *assertive*; “I promise” is a *commissive* and an *expressive*), and the *taxonomy* *is* *not* *exhaustive*.

**The *reply***. The *categories* are *prototypes* (the *acts* *can* be *mixed*), and the *reply* *qualifies* the *taxonomy*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *cross-cultural* *objection*. The *taxonomy* *is* *based* on the *English* *verbs* (the *“*promise*”*, *“*warn*”*, *“*order*”*), and the *categories* *may* *not* *generalize* *across* the *languages* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reply* (the *categories* are *universal*, and the *verbs* are *language*-*specific*) is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *declarations*’* *objection*. The *declarations* *require* the *institutional* *position*, and the *position* *is* *constituted* by the *collective* *acceptance*, and the *acceptance* *is* *not* a *speech* *act* (it is a *social* *fact*). The *objection* *threatens* the *foundation* of the *institutional* *reality*.

## 4. Grice and Implicature

### 4.1 Meaning-nn

**The *thesis*** (Grice, “Meaning,” 1957; *Studies in the Way of Words*, 1989, Ch. 14). The **non-natural** *meaning* (the *meaning-nn*) is *defined* by the *speaker*'s *reflexive* *intention*.

**The *analysis***.

$$
U \ \text{meant}_{\text{nn}} \ \text{something by} \ x \quad \text{iff} \quad U \ \text{intended} \ x \ \text{to produce some effect in an audience by means of the recognition of that intention}
$$

**The *structure***. The *intention* is *reflexive*: the *speaker* *intends* the *hearer* to *recognize* the *intention*, and the *recognition* is the *means* by which the *effect* is *produced*.

**The *virtue***. The *analysis* *explains* the *difference* between the *natural* *meaning* (the *smoke* *means* *fire*, and the *meaning* is *causal*) and the *non-natural* *meaning* (the *utterance* *means* something, and the *meaning* is *intentional*), and it *grounds* the *meaning* in the *communicative* *intention*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *reflexive* *intention*’* *objection*. The *speaker* *can* *mean* something *without* the *reflexive* *intention* (the *sincere* *assertion* *does* not *require* the *hearer*'s *recognition*), and the *analysis* *is* *too* *intellectual*.

**The *reply***. The *reflexive* *intention* is *implicit* in the *communicative* *practice*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *analysis* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *convention*’* *objection*. The *meaning-nn* *is* *not* *conventional* (the *speaker* *can* *mean* something by a *non*-*conventional* *sign*), and the *analysis* *loses* the *linguistic* *meaning* (the *conventional* *meaning* of the *sentence*).

**The *reply***. The *linguistic* *meaning* is a *species* of the *meaning-nn* (the *convention* *is* the *regular* *means* of *recognition*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *linguistic* *meaning* in the *communicative* *intention*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.2 The cooperative principle and the maxims

**The *thesis*** (Grice, “Logic and Conversation,” 1975; *Studies*, Ch. 2). The *conversation* is *governed* by the **Cooperative Principle** (the *CP*), and the *principle* *generates* the **maxims**.

**The CP***.

> “Make your conversational contribution such as is required, at the stage at which it occurs, by the accepted purpose or direction of the talk exchange in which you are engaged.”

**The *maxims***.

| The *maxim* | The *sub*-*maxims* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Quantity** | Be as informative as required; do not be more informative | “Some of the students passed” (implying not all) |
| **Quality** | Do not say what you believe false; do not say what you lack evidence for | The *ironic* *utterance* (flouting quality) |
| **Relation** | Be relevant | “I am out of petrol” / “There is a garage around the corner” |
| **Manner** | Be perspicuous; avoid obscurity, ambiguity, prolixity; be orderly | The *deliberate* *obscurity* |

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *explains* the *gap* between the *literal* *meaning* and the *communicated* *content*, and it *grounds* the *communication* in the *rational* *cooperation*.

### 4.3 Implicature

**The *thesis***. An **implicature** is a *proposition* that is *communicated* *without* being *entailed* by the *literal* *content*, and the *implicature* is *inferred* from the *maxims*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Speaker} \ S \ \text{implicates} \ q \ \text{by} \ \text{uttering} \ p \quad \text{iff} \quad S \ \text{believes} \ q, \ \text{and} \ q \ \text{is required} \ \text{by} \ \text{the CP}
$$

**The *examples***.

**(i) The *recommendation* *letter***. “He has beautiful handwriting” (the *implicature*: he is *not* *good* at *philosophy*). The *maxim* *of* *quantity* (the *letter* is *less* *informative* than *required*), and the *inference* is that the *speaker* *has* *nothing* *better* to *say*.

**(ii) The *garage* *case***. A: “I am out of petrol.” B: “There is a garage around the corner.” The *implicature*: the *garage* is *open* and *sells* *petrol*. The *maxim* *of* *relation* (the *relevance* of the *garage* to the *petrol*).

**(iii) The *scalar* *implicature***. “Some of the students passed” (the *implicature*: not all). The *maxim* *of* *quantity* (the *speaker* *would* *say* “all” if *all* *passed*), and the *inference* is the *negation* of the *stronger* *term*.

**The *properties***.

| The *property* | The *definition* | The *test* |
|---|---|---|
| **Calculability** | The *implicature* can be *derived* from the *content*, the *CP*, and the *context* | The *reconstruction* of the *inference* |
| **Cancellability** | The *implicature* can be *canceled* without *contradiction* | “Some passed, and in fact all did” |
| **Non-detachability** | The *implicature* *survives* the *substitution* of *synonyms* | “A few passed” (the *same* *implicature*) |
| **Determinacy** | The *implicature* is *determined* by the *context* | The *contested* *property* |

**The *significance***. The *implicature* is the *central* *notion* of *pragmatics*, and it *explains* the *gap* between the *semantic* *content* and the *communicative* *intent*.

## 5. The Semantics/Pragmatics Boundary

### 5.1 Grice's saying/implicating distinction

**The *thesis*** (Grice 1975, §2). The *saying* is the *literal* *content* (the *truth* *conditions*), and the *implicating* is the *pragmatic* *content* (the *communicated* *intent*), and the *two* are *distinct*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Utterance} = \text{What is said} \ (\text{semantics}) + \text{What is implicated} \ (\text{pragmatics})
$$

**The *virtue***. The *distinction* *preserves* the *truth*-*conditional* *semantics* (Ch. 31, §3.2), and it *grounds* the *pragmatics* in the *rational* *cooperation*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*unarticulated* *constituents*’* *objection* (Perry, “Thought without Representation,” 1986; Recanati, *Literal Meaning*, 2004). The *literal* *content* *depends* on the *context* (the *“*It is raining*”* *requires* a *location*, the *“*She is tall*”* *requires* a *comparison* *class*), and the *dependence* *blurs* the *saying/implicating* *distinction*.

**The *reply***. The *literal* *content* *includes* the *context*-*dependent* *elements* (the *indexicals*, the *demonstratives*; Ch. 12, §4), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *context*-*sensitivity* from the *pragmatic* *enrichment*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*free* *enrichment*’* *objection* (Recanati 2004). The *speaker* *can* *enrich* the *literal* *content* *freely* (the *“*The ham sandwich is waiting for his bill*”* *requires* the *enrichment* of the *sandwich* to a *person*), and the *enrichment* *is* *not* *an* *implicature* (it is part of *what* is *said*).

**The *reply***. The *enrichment* is *pragmatic* but *not* *implicature* (the *“*pragmatic* *intrusion*”*; the *reply* *introduces* a *third* *level*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.2 Relevance theory

**The *thesis*** (Sperber & Wilson, *Relevance: Communication and Cognition*, 1986; 2nd ed., 1995). The *communication* is *governed* by the *principle* *of* *relevance* (the *cognitive* *effect* *relative* to the *processing* *effort*), and the *implicature* is a *by*-*product* of the *relevance* *maximization*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Relevance} = \frac{\text{Cognitive effect}}{\text{Processing effort}}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *unifies* the *maxims* (the *four* *maxims* are *reducible* to the *relevance*), and it *explains* the *online* *processing* (the *psychological* *plausibility*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *independent* *test*’* *objection*. The *relevance* is *defined* by the *effect* and the *effort*, and the *two* are *not* *independently* *measurable*. The *theory* is *circular*.

**The *reply***. The *relevance* is a *theoretical* *notion* (the *effect* and the *effort* are *defined* *within* the *theory*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *theory* in the *empirical* *predictions*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *social* *dimension*’* *objection*. The *theory* *ignores* the *social* *norms* (the *politeness*, the *power*, the *institutions*; §9), and the *communication* is *not* *only* *relevance* *maximization*.

**The *reply***. The *social* *norms* are *part* of the *context* (the *relevance* *depends* on the *norms*), and the *reply* *integrates* the *social* *dimension* into the *theory*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 Semantic minimalism and contextualism

**The *debate***. The *semantics/pragmatics* *boundary* is *contested*, and the *debate* *opposes* the *minimalism* (the *semantics* is *narrow*, and the *pragmatics* is *broad*) to the *contextualism* (the *semantics* is *context*-*sensitive*, and the *pragmatics* is *pervasive*).

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic minimalism** | There is a *minimal* *proposition* (the *literal* *content*) | Cappelen & Lepore, *Insensitive Semantics* (2005) |
| **Contextualism** | The *truth* *conditions* *depend* on the *context* (the *free* *enrichment*) | Recanati (2004); Travis (1985) |
| **Relativism** | The *truth* *depends* on the *assessor* (the *assessment* *sensitivity*; Ch. 32, §8.2) | MacFarlane (2014) |
| **Relevance theory** | The *content* is *inferred* by the *relevance* *maximization* | Sperber & Wilson (1986) |

**The *stakes***. The *boundary* *determines* the *theory* of *meaning* (Ch. 31, §3.2), the *theory* of *truth* (Ch. 32, §5), and the *theory* of *communication* (the *pragmatics*).

**The *state* of the debate***. The *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *contextualism*: the *literal* *content* *depends* on the *context* (the *contextualist* *insight*), and the *context*-*dependence* is *constrained* by the *conventions* (the *minimalist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *boundary* is *not* *sharp*.

## 6. Presupposition and Accommodation

### 6.1 The phenomenon

**The *datum***. A **presupposition** is a *proposition* that is *taken* *for* *granted* by the *utterance*, *rather* than *asserted*.

**The *examples***.

| The *utterance* | The *presupposition* |
|---|---|
| “The King of France is bald” | There is a King of France |
| “Have you stopped beating your wife?” | You have been beating your wife |
| “John regrets that he lied” | John lied |
| “It was Mary who broke the vase” | Someone broke the vase |

**The *test***. The *presupposition* *survives* the *negation* (the *“*The King of France is not bald*”* *still* *presupposes* the *King*), the *questioning*, the *modals*, and the *conditionals* (the *“*projection*”*; §6.2).

### 6.2 The projection problem

**The *problem***. The *presupposition* *projects* *through* *some* *embeddings* (the *negation*, the *questions*, the *modals*) but *not* *through* *others* (the *conditionals*, the *conjunctions*, the *disjunctions*).

**The *data***.

| The *embedding* | The *presupposition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Negation** | *Projects* | “The King of France is not bald” (still presupposes) |
| **Question** | *Projects* | “Is the King of France bald?” (still presupposes) |
| **Modal** | *Projects* | “The King of France might be bald” (still presupposes) |
| **Conditional** | *Canceled* | “If there is a King of France, the King of France is bald” (no presupposition) |
| **Conjunction** | *Canceled* | “There is a King of France, and the King of France is bald” (no presupposition) |

**The *theories***.

| The *theory* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic** | The *presupposition* is a *semantic* *relation* (the *failure* *makes* the *sentence* *neither* *true* *nor* *false*) | Strawson (1950); the *classical* *view* |
| **Pragmatic** | The *presupposition* is a *pragmatic* *constraint* (the *common* *ground*; the *accommodation*) | Stalnaker (1974); Lewis (1979); Karttunen (1974) |
| **Dynamic** | The *presupposition* is a *constraint* on the *context* *change* (the *update* *semantics*) | Heim (1982); Kamp (1981) |
| **Projection** | The *presupposition* *projects* by a *rule* (the *“*plugs*”*, *“*holes*”*, *“*filters*”*) | Karttunen (1973) |

**The *significance***. The *projection* *problem* is *one* of the *deepest* *problems* in the *pragmatics*, and it *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *dynamic* *semantics* and to the *theory* of *truth* (the *failure* of *bivalence*; Ch. 32, §2).

### 6.3 Stalnaker's common ground

**The *thesis*** (Stalnaker, “Pragmatic Presuppositions,” 1974; “Assertion,” 1978). The *presuppositions* are the *propositions* that the *participants* *take* *for* *granted* (the *common* *ground*), and the *assertion* *updates* the *common* *ground*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Common ground} \ \to \ \text{Assertion} \ \to \ \text{Updated common ground}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *grounds* the *presupposition* in the *social* *practice* (the *common* *knowledge*), and it *fits* the *dynamic* *semantics*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *common* *knowledge*’* *objection*. The *common* *ground* *requires* the *common* *knowledge* (the *mutual* *knowledge*; Ch. 25, §7.2), and the *mutual* *knowledge* *is* a *regress* (the *“*I know that you know that I know …*”*). The *objection* is *contested* (the *mutual* *knowledge* *can* be *defined* by the *fixed* *point*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *common* *ground* is a *primitive* (the *fixed* *point* of the *mutual* *knowledge*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *common* *ground* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *accommodation*’* *objection*. The *presuppositions* *can* be *accommodated* (the *hearer* *adjusts* the *common* *ground* to *fit* the *presupposition*; Lewis 1979), and the *accommodation* *is* *not* *explained* by the *theory*.

**The *reply*** (Lewis 1979). The *accommodation* is *driven* by the *maxim* *of* *accommodation* (the *hearer* *accepts* the *presupposition* *unless* it is *rejected*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *accommodation* in the *pragmatic* *principle*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 Presupposition and bivalence

**The *argument***. The *semantic* *presupposition* *fails* (the *King* *does* not *exist*), and the *sentence* is *neither* *true* *nor* *false* (the *failure* of *bivalence*; Ch. 12, §2.4).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Presupposition failure} \ \to \ \text{Truth-value gap} \ \to \ \text{No bivalence}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *connects* the *presupposition* *theory* to the *semantics* of *truth* (Ch. 32, §5.2) and to the *theory* of *definite* *descriptions* (Ch. 12, §2.4).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Russellian* *reply*. The *sentence* is *false* (not *truth*-*valueless*), because the *existential* *conjunct* *fails* (Ch. 12, §2.2). The *reply* *preserves* the *bivalence*, and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *pragmatic* *reply*. The *presupposition* is a *pragmatic* *constraint* (the *common* *ground*), and the *failure* *does* not *affect* the *truth* *value*; the *sentence* is *false* (or *true*) *independently* of the *presupposition*. The *reply* *preserves* the *bivalence*, and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *dynamic* *reply*. The *presupposition* *constrains* the *context* *change*, and the *failure* *prevents* the *update*; the *sentence* is *not* *evaluated* *for* *truth* (it *fails* to *make* a *claim*). The *reply* *abandons* the *bivalence* (the *sentence* *is* *neither* *true* *nor* *false*), and it is *contested*.

## 7. Non-Literal Speech

### 7.1 Metaphor

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, “What Metaphors Mean,” 1978; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *metaphor* is *not* a *matter* of *meaning* (the *metaphorical* *sentence* *has* *only* the *literal* *meaning*); the *metaphor* is a *use* of the *sentence* that *makes* the *hearer* *see* an *aspect* of the *world*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *metaphorical* *sentence* *has* the *literal* *meaning* (the *words* *mean* what they *mean*).} \\
2. & \text{The *metaphor* *is* the *use* of the *sentence* (the *speaker* *uses* the *literal* *content* to *produce* an *insight*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *metaphor* *has* *no* *special* *meaning* (the *“*no* *paraphrase*”* *thesis*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *captures* the *open*-*ended* *character* of *metaphor* (the *metaphor* *cannot* be *paraphrased* *without* *loss*), and it *fits* the *aesthetic* *theory* of *metaphor* (the *metaphor* *shows* *rather* than *says*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *truth* *value*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; Camp 2008; Hills 2017). The *metaphor* *can* be *true* or *false* (“No man is an island” is *true*; “The sun is a golden coin” is *false*), and the *truth* *value* *requires* a *content* (the *metaphorical* *content*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *truth* *value* is *derived* from the *literal* *content* (the *metaphor* *is* *true* *if* the *literal* *comparison* is *apt*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *literal* *content*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *cognitive* *content*’* *objection*. The *metaphor* is *not* a *mere* *ornament* (it *conveys* a *cognitive* *content*), and the *theory* *loses* the *content*.

**The *reply***. The *content* is *the* *insight* *produced* by the *use*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *content* in the *cognitive* *effect*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *embedding* *objection* (Camp 2008). The *metaphor* *embeds* in *conditionals*, *negations*, and *propositional* *attitudes* (“If Juliet is the sun, then she is hot”), and the *embedding* *requires* a *content* (the *metaphorical* *content* *composes*).

**The *reply***. The *embedding* *is* a *problem* for *Davidson*'s *theory*, and the *reply* *requires* a *semantic* *account* of *metaphor* (the *“*metaphorical* *content*”*; the *reply* is *contested*).

### 7.2 Irony

**The *thesis***. The *irony* is the *act* of *saying* something *while* *meaning* the *opposite*, and the *irony* *depends* on the *hearer*'s *recognition* of the *speaker*'s *intention*.

**The *theories***.

| The *theory* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Gricean** | Irony *flouts* the *maxim* of *quality* (the *speaker* *says* what she *believes* *false*, and the *hearer* *infers* the *opposite*) | Grice (1975) |
| **Echoic** | Irony is *echoic* (the *speaker* *echoes* a *claim* *she* *does* not *endorse*) | Wilson & Sperber (1992) |
| **Pretense** | Irony is a *pretense* (the *speaker* *pretends* to *assert*) | Clark & Gerrig (1984) |

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *flouting*’* *objection*. The *irony* *does* not *require* the *flouting* (the *speaker* can be *ironic* *without* *violating* the *quality*), and the *Gricean* *account* is *too* *narrow*.

**The *reply*** (the *echoic* *account*). The *irony* is *echoic* (the *speaker* *echoes* a *claim* *without* *endorsing* it), and the *reply* *grounds* the *irony* in the *echoic* *use*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *pretense*’* *objection*. The *irony* is *not* a *pretense* (the *speaker* is *sincere* *in* the *irony*), and the *pretense* *account* *misses* the *sincerity*.

**The *reply*** (the *pretense* *account*). The *pretense* *is* the *form* of the *irony* (the *speaker* *pretends* to *believe* the *claim*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *irony* in the *pretense*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.3 Hyperbole and understatement

**The *thesis***. The *hyperbole* (the *exaggeration*) and the *understatement* (the *litotes*) are *scalar* *departures* from the *literal* *content*, and the *departures* are *governed* by the *maxim* of *quality* (the *speaker* *does* not *believe* the *literal* *content*, and the *hearer* *infers* the *intended* *content*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Hyperbole} = \text{The literal content is} \ \text{weaker/stronger} \ \text{than the intended content}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *unifies* the *hyperbole* and the *understatement* with the *irony* (the *scalar* *departure*), and it *fits* the *Gricean* *framework*.

**The *objections***. The *hyperbole* *embeds* *like* the *metaphor* (the *“*If I have told you a million times, then …*”*), and the *embedding* *requires* a *content* (the *account* is *contested*).

### 7.4 Slurs and dogwhistles

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Hom 2008; Camp 2013; Anderson & Lepore 2013). The *slurs* *encode* a *derogatory* *content* (the *“*expressive*”* *dimension*), and the *content* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *descriptive* *content*.

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic** | The *slur* *encodes* a *derogatory* *content* *semantically* | Hom (2008) |
| **Pragmatic** | The *slur* *encodes* a *derogatory* *content* *pragmatically* (the *use* is *derogatory*) | Anderson & Lepore (2013) |
| **Expressivist** | The *slur* *expresses* an *attitude* (not a *content*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Performative** | The *slur* *performs* a *derogatory* *act* (a *speech* *act*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *significance***. The *slur* *debate* *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *ethics* *of* *language* (the *harm* of *slurs*), and it *supports* the *performative* *account* (the *slur* *is* an *act* of *subordination*; §9).

**The *dogwhistles*** (Saul, “Dogwhistles, Political Manipulation, and Philosophy of Language,” 2018).

| The *kind* | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Overt** | The *dogwhistle* *is* *recognized* by the *audience* (the *deniability* is *plausible*) | The *“*inner city*”* *code* |
| **Covert** | The *dogwhistle* is *not* *recognized* by the *audience* (the *speaker* *exploits* the *ignorance*) | The *“*welfare queen*”* *code* |

**The *significance***. The *dogwhistles* *show* that the *implicature* *can* be *weaponized* (the *speaker* *communicates* the *derogatory* *content* *without* *being* *held* *accountable*), and the *phenomenon* *connects* the *pragmatics* to the *politics* (Ch. 11, §6.2).

### 7.5 The continuum

**The *thesis***. The *literal*/*figurative* *distinction* is a *continuum*, not a *dichotomy*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Literal} \quad \to \quad \text{Loose use} \quad \to \quad \text{Metonymy} \quad \to \quad \text{Metaphor} \quad \to \quad \text{Hyperbole} \quad \to \quad \text{Irony}
$$

**The *significance***. The *continuum* *undermines* the *literal* *core* (the *“*literal*”* *is* a *limiting* *case*), and it *supports* the *contextualist* *account* of *meaning* (§5.3).

## 8. Performative Contradiction

### 8.1 The notion

**The *thesis*** (Apel, *Transformation der Philosophie*, 1973; Habermas, *Theorie des kommunikativen Handelns*, 1981; the *contemporary* *literature*). A **performative contradiction** is an *utterance* whose *content* *contradicts* the *presuppositions* of the *act* of *uttering* it.

**The *examples***.

| The *utterance* | The *contradicted* *presupposition* | The *act* |
|---|---|---|
| “I do not exist” | The *existence* of the *speaker* (the *indexical* *reference*) | The *assertion* |
| “All speech is meaningless” | The *meaningfulness* of the *speech* (the *assertion*) | The *assertion* |
| “You should not argue” | The *rational* *argumentation* (the *assertion* *is* an *argument*) | The *assertion* |
| “There are no reasons” | The *reason*-*giving* (the *assertion* *gives* a *reason*) | The *assertion* |

**The *structure***. The *content* $\neg p$ *contradicts* the *presupposition* $p$ of the *act* (the *assertion* *presupposes* the *truth* *norms*, the *sincerity*, the *authority*, the *intelligibility*), and the *contradiction* is *performative* (the *act* *contradicts* the *content*).

**The *significance***. The *argument* *grounds* the *critique* of *skepticism* (Ch. 6, §3.3) and of *relativism* (Ch. 13, §4), and it *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *epistemology* and to the *ethics* of *discourse*.

### 8.2 The presuppositions of assertion

**The *thesis***. The *assertion* *presupposes* *four* *conditions* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *Habermasian* *account*).

| The *condition* | The *claim* | The *violation* |
|---|---|---|
| **Truth** | The *speaker* *aims* at the *truth* (the *truth*-*norm*; Ch. 32, §9.3) | The *lie*, the *bullshit*, the *dogwhistle* |
| **Sincerity** | The *speaker* *believes* the *content* | The *insincerity*, the *hypocrisy* |
| **Authority** | The *speaker* *is* *entitled* to *assert* (the *warrant*, the *expertise*) | The *mansplaining*, the *overreach* |
| **Intelligibility** | The *content* is *communicable* (the *shared* *concepts*, the *language*) | The *nonsense*, the *gibberish* |

**The *argument***. The *presuppositions* are *constitutive* of the *act* (the *assertion* without the *truth*-*aim* is not an *assertion*), and the *content* that *denies* the *presupposition* *undermines* the *act* *that* *denies* it.

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *grounds* the *critique* in the *structure* of the *speech* *act*, and it *avoids* the *circularity* of the *content*-*based* *refutation* (the *self*-*refutation*; Ch. 13, §4).

### 8.3 The objections

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *logical* *contradiction*’* *objection*. The *performative* *contradiction* is *not* a *logical* *contradiction* (the *content* and the *presupposition* *can* *both* be *true* in a *possible* *world*), and the *force* of the *argument* is *dialectical*, not *deductive*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *argument* is *dialectical* (it *targets* the *practice* of *assertion*, not the *proposition*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *force* in the *normative* *structure* of the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *presupposition*’* *objection*. The *presuppositions* are *conventional* (they are *not* *universal*; the *different* *communities* *have* *different* *norms*), and the *argument* *depends* on the *contested* *norms*.

**The *reply***. The *norms* are *transcendental* (they are the *conditions* of the *possibility* of the *practice*; Ch. 1, §2), and the *reply* *grounds* the *universality* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *practical* *force*’* *objection*. The *performative* *contradiction* *does* *not* *prevent* the *speaker* from *acting* (the *speaker* can *continue* to *assert*), and the *argument* *does* not *refute* the *position*.

**The *reply***. The *argument* *undermines* the *justification* (the *speaker* cannot *consistently* *defend* the *position*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *force* in the *practice* of *justification*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 8.4 The abuse of the argument

**The *problem***. The *performative* *contradiction* is *a* *powerful* *rhetorical* *device*, and it is *often* *abused* (the *charge* is *used* to *dismiss* the *opponent* *without* *engaging* the *argument*).

**The *criteria***. The *charge* is *legitimate* only if (i) the *presupposition* is *genuinely* *constitutive* of the *act*, (ii) the *content* *genuinely* *contradicts* it, and (iii) the *argument* is *not* a *substitute* for the *substantive* *engagement*.

**The *significance***. The *criteria* *ground* the *ethics* of the *argument* (Ch. 4, §4), and the *criteria* *connect* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *ethics* of *discourse*.

## 9. Language and Power

### 9.1 The authority condition

**The *thesis***. The *speech* *acts* *require* the *speaker* to be in a *position* of *authority* (the *A.2* *condition*; §1.2), and the *authority* is *social* and *institutional*.

**The *examples***.

| The *act* | The *authority* | The *failure* |
|---|---|---|
| **Marrying** | The *priest*, the *registrar* | The *random* *person* (“I pronounce you …”) |
| **Sentencing** | The *judge* | The *bystander* (“I sentence you …”) |
| **Promising** | The *agent* (the *autonomy*, the *capacity*) | The *child*, the *coerced* *person* |
| **Naming** | The *parent*, the *captain*, the *authority* | The *bystander* |

**The *argument***. The *authority* *condition* *shows* that the *speech* *acts* are *not* *merely* *linguistic* (they are *social* *acts*), and the *distribution* of *authority* is *not* *equal* (the *marginalized* *groups* *have* *less* *authority*).

**The *significance***. The *condition* *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *social* *theory* (the *power*, the *institutions*), and it *grounds* the *critique* of the *silencing* (§9.2).

### 9.2 Silencing and illocutionary disablement

**The *thesis*** (Langton, “Speech Acts and Unspeakable Acts,” 1993; Hornsby & Langton, “Free Speech and Illocution,” 1998). The *speech* *acts* can be *disabled* (the *utterance* *fails* to *be* the *act*) by the *social* *conditions*, and the *disablement* is a *form* of *silencing*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Utterance} \ \to \ \text{Illocutionary act} \ \to \ \text{Silencing (the act is disabled)}
$$

**The *examples***.

**(i) The *refusal***. The *woman* *says* “no” (the *refusal*), and the *hearer* *does* not *recognize* the *utterance* *as* a *refusal* (the *hearer* *hears* the *“*no*”* as a *“*yes*”*), and the *refusal* *fails* *to* *be* an *act* (the *illocutionary* *disablement*).

**(ii) The *pornography***. The *pornography* *performs* the *act* of *subordination* (it *ranks* the *women* *as* *inferior*, it *legitimates* the *violence*), and the *act* *requires* the *authority* (the *authority* *is* *conferred* by the *social* *acceptance*).

**(iii) The *hate* *speech***. The *hate* *speech* *performs* the *act* of *subordination* (it *ranks* the *group* *as* *inferior*), and the *act* *requires* the *authority* (the *authority* *is* *conferred* by the *social* *acceptance*).

**The *virtue***. The *theory* *grounds* the *harm* of the *speech* *in* the *illocutionary* *act* (the *speech* *does* something, and the *doing* is *harmful*), and it *avoids* the *“*mere* *words”* *defense*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *authority*’* *objection*. The *pornography* and the *hate* *speech* *do* not *have* the *authority* (the *state* *does* not *authorize* them), and the *theory* *over*-*extends* the *authority* *condition*.

**The *reply*** (Langton 1993). The *authority* *is* *conferred* by the *social* *acceptance* (the *informal* *authority*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *formal* (the *legal*) from the *informal* (the *social*) *authority*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *causation*’* *objection*. The *speech* *act* *theory* *explains* the *meaning* of the *speech*, not the *causation* of the *harm* (the *harm* *requires* a *causal* *explanation*).

**The *reply*** (McGowan, *Just Words*, 2019). The *speech* *acts* are *causal* (the *illocutionary* *acts* *change* the *social* *facts*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *harm* in the *exercitive* *acts* (the *acts* that *change* the *permissions* and the *obligations*). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*free* *speech*’* *objection*. The *regulation* of the *harmful* *speech* *conflicts* with the *free* *speech* (Ch. 47, §4), and the *theory* *licenses* the *censorship*.

**The *reply***. The *free* *speech* *is* *not* *absolute* (the *harm* *principle*; Ch. 47, §4), and the *reply* *balances* the *free* *speech* against the *harm*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 9.3 Testimonial and hermeneutical injustice

**The *connection***. The *epistemic* *injustice* (Ch. 11, §4) *is* a *speech-act* *phenomenon*.

| The *injustice* | The *speech-act* *failure* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Testimonial** | The *credibility* *deficit* (the *uptake* *fails*; the *testimony* *is* *not* *accepted*) | The *woman*'s *testimony* *discounted* |
| **Hermeneutical** | The *conceptual* *gap* (the *speech* *act* *cannot* *be* *performed*; §7.5) | The *harassment* *without* the *concept* |

**The *significance***. The *injustice* *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *epistemic* *injustice* *literature*, and the *connection* *shows* that the *speech* *acts* are *epistemic* and *political*, not *merely* *linguistic*.

### 9.4 Legal performatives

**The *thesis***. The *law* *is* a *system* of *constitutive* *rules* (the *“*X counts as Y in C*”*), and the *legal* *acts* (the *contracts*, the *wills*, the *sentences*) *are* *speech* *acts* *constituted* by the *legal* *institutions*.

**The *structure***.

| The *legal* *act* | The *constitutive* *rule* | The *deontic* *power* |
|---|---|---|
| **Contract** | The *offer* + the *acceptance* counts as a *contract* | The *obligations*, the *remedies* |
| **Will** | The *declaration* + the *formalities* counts as a *will* | The *inheritance*, the *execution* |
| **Sentence** | The *judge*'s *pronouncement* counts as a *sentence* | The *punishment*, the *liberty* |
| **Marriage** | The *ceremony* counts as a *marriage* | The *rights*, the *obligations* |

**The *significance***. The *legal* *performatives* *show* that the *speech-act* *theory* *grounds* the *legal* *ontology* (the *facts* are *constituted* by the *acts*), and the *theory* *connects* the *philosophy* of *language* to the *philosophy* of *law* (Ch. 47, §7).

### 9.5 The *‘*just words*’* *defense*

**The *thesis***. The *defense* *“*these are just words*”* *is* *false* (the *words* *do* things), and the *speech-act* *theory* *refutes* the *defense*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *words* *perform* *acts* (the *promises*, the *orders*, the *subordinations*).} \\
2. & \text{The *acts* *change* the *social* *world* (the *obligations*, the *permissions*, the *rankings*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *words* are *not* *“*just* *words*”* (they *have* *effects*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *refutation* *grounds* the *ethics* of *language* (the *responsibility* for the *speech* *acts*), and it *connects* the *speech-act* *theory* to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §4).

## 10. The State of the Debate

### 10.1 What is settled

- **The performative/constative dichotomy collapses**, and the *illocutionary* force is a *distinct* *dimension* of the utterance.
- **The illocutionary/perlocutionary distinction is real**, and the *uptake* is *required* for the *illocutionary* act.
- **Searle's taxonomy is influential**, and the *constitutive* rules ground the *institutional* reality.
- **Grice's implicature is the central notion of pragmatics**, and the *cooperative* principle grounds the *communication*.
- **The semantics/pragmatics boundary is contested**, and the *contextualist* and *minimalist* positions are both *live*.
- **Presupposition is a normative background**, and the *projection* *problem* is *unresolved*.
- **Non-literal speech is a continuum**, and the *metaphor*, *irony*, and *slurs* are *central* to the *pragmatics*.
- **The performative contradiction is a real argument**, and it grounds the *critique* of skepticism and relativism.
- **The speech acts are social and political**, and the *authority* condition grounds the *silencing* and the *epistemic* *injustice*.

### 10.2 What is open

- **The *semantics/pragmatics* boundary** — the *minimalism*, the *contextualism*, the *relevance* theory.
- **The *nature* of *presupposition* — the *semantic*, the *pragmatic*, the *dynamic*.
- **The *projection* *problem* — the *rules*, the *filters*, the *plugs*.
- **The *nature* of *metaphor* — the *Davidsonian* (no content), the *semantic* (content), the *pragmatic*.
- **The *performative* contradiction** — the *dialectical* force and its *abuse*.
- **The *speech-act* *theory* of *power** — the *authority*, the *silencing*, the *harm*.

### 10.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *locutionary*, the *illocutionary*, and the *perlocutionary*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *performative* cases, the *presupposition* *projection*, the *irony* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *felicity* conditions, the *CP*, the *dynamic* semantics — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: the theory of speech acts is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the empirical and political dimensions are transforming the debate. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

### 10.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Austin** | The performative/constative distinction | Fits the practice | The distinction collapses |
| **Searle** | The taxonomy and the constitutive rules | Systematic; institutional | The exhaustiveness |
| **Grice** | The implicature and the CP | Fits the communication | The boundary |
| **Relevance theory** | The relevance maximization | Unifies the maxims | The circularity |
| **Semantic minimalism** | The minimal proposition | Preserves compositionality | The free enrichment |
| **Contextualism** | The truth conditions depend on the context | Fits the free enrichment | The instability |
| **Stalnaker** | The common ground and the accommodation | Dynamic; social | The common knowledge |
| **Davidson** | The metaphor is not meaning | Fits the open-endedness | The embedding |
| **Habermas** | The performative contradiction | Grounds the critique | The abuse |
| **Langton** | The silencing and the illocutionary disablement | Grounds the harm | The authority |
| **McGowan** | The exercitive acts | Grounds the social change | The causation |

The table is a map, not a verdict. The reader who wants to choose a position must decide which cost is acceptable — and the decision is a philosophical argument.

## Coda: The End of Part V

Part V began with *meaning* (Ch. 31) and *truth* (Ch. 32), and it ends with *speech acts* (this chapter). The *arc* is a *shift* from the *content* of language to the *practice* of language — from the *sentence* to the *act*, from the *representation* to the *interaction*, from the *semantics* to the *pragmatics*.

The *result* is *not* a *system*. It is a *map* of the *options*, a *set* of *distinctions*, and a *repertoire* of *arguments* — and, above all, a *recognition* that language is *not* a *neutral* *medium* but a *social* *practice* with *norms*, *institutions*, and *powers*.

### What Part V established

- **Meaning is a family of notions** — sense, reference, use, inferential role (Ch. 31).
- **Truth is real but thin**, and its *thinness* is compatible with a *robust* metaphysics (Ch. 32).
- **Paradox is real**, and the *solutions* are *all* *costly* (Ch. 33).
- **Speech is action**, and the *acts* are *constituted* by *social* *norms* (this chapter).

### What Part VI will do

Part VI turns to *science*, *explanation*, and *mathematics* — the *domains* where the *language* meets the *world* under the *most* *rigorous* *constraints*.

The *connection* is *direct*. The *speech* acts are the *vehicle* of *scientific* *communication* (the *assertion* is the *norm* of the *scientific* *paper*, the *constitutive* *rules* are the *norms* of the *institution* of *science*), and the *pragmatics* of *science* (the *testimony*, the *trust*, the *authority*; Ch. 11) is the *social* *dimension* of the *scientific* *inquiry*. The *next* *chapter* *turns* to *formal* *logic* — the *skeleton* of the *language* — and it *asks* the *question* that the *speech-act* *theory* *presupposes*: *what* *is* *it* for an *argument* to be *valid*?

### The hinge

The *chapter* — and *Part V* — *ends* with a *hinge*. The *hinge* is the *recognition* that the *language* is *not* a *mirror* of the *world* but a *practice* of *acting* in the *world*, and that the *philosophy* of *language* is the *philosophy* of *action* (Ch. 27–28) *applied* to the *linguistic* *acts*.

**The *Austin* *formula***, *applied* to the *medium*:

> “The words are not the thing, but the saying of them is a doing of it.”

**The *closing* *thought*. The *philosophy* of *language* is *not* a *branch* of *philosophy*; it is the *place* where the *metaphysics* (the *content*), the *epistemology* (the *truth*), the *ethics* (the *norms*), and the *politics* (the *power*) *meet*. The *next* *part* *begins* from *this* *meeting*, and it *asks* *what* *logic* *must* *be* *like* for the *meeting* to be *possible*.

## Summary

- **Austin** discovered the *performative* (the *utterances* that *do* things), and the *felicity* conditions (the *A.1*, *A.2*, *B.1*, *B.2*, *Γ.1*, *Γ.2*). The *performative/constative* *dichotomy* *collapses*, and the *threefold* *distinction* (the *locutionary*, the *illocutionary*, the *perlocutionary*) *replaces* it.
- **Searle** *provides* the *taxonomy* (assertives, directives, commissives, expressives, declarations) and the *constitutive* rules (*X counts as Y in C*), and the *theory* *grounds* the *institutional* *reality*.
- **Grice** *provides* the *meaning-nn* (the *reflexive* *intention*), the *cooperative* principle, and the *maxims* (quality, quantity, relation, manner), and the *implicature* is the *central* notion of *pragmatics*.
- **The semantics/pragmatics boundary** is *contested*: the *minimalism*, the *contextualism*, the *relevance* theory, and the *relativism* are *all* *live*.
- **Presupposition** is a *normative* background (the *common* *ground*), and the *projection* problem is *unresolved*.
- **Non-literal speech** (metaphor, irony, hyperbole, slurs, dogwhistles) is a *continuum*, and the *pragmatic* accounts are *contested*.
- **The performative contradiction** *grounds* the *critique* of skepticism and relativism, and the *abuse* of the argument is a *risk*.
- **Language and power** are *entangled*: the *authority* condition, the *silencing*, the *epistemic* injustice, and the *legal* performatives *show* that the *speech* acts are *social* and *political*.
- **The most defensible position** is a *qualified* *contextualism* *combined* with a *speech-act* *theory* of *power*, *qualified* by the *admission* of the *contested* boundary and the *incomplete* accounts.
