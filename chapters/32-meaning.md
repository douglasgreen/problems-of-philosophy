# Chapter 31 — Meaning, Reference, and Understanding

> “The word ‘meaning’ is one of the most obscure and contested words in the language, and the theories of meaning are as numerous as the theories of everything else.”
> — a formulation of the predicament, in the spirit of Quine

> “The reference of a proper name is the object itself which we designate by its means; the idea, which we have in that case, is wholly subjective; in between lies the sense, which is indeed no longer subjective like the idea, but is yet not the object itself.”
> — Gottlob Frege, “On Sense and Reference” (1892)

> “Don’t ask for the meaning; ask for the use.”
> — Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953), §43 (paraphrased)

---

Part IV examined the mind, and it *presupposed* a concept that it did not *explain*: the concept of **content**. Minds *represent*; beliefs *have* *propositional* *content*; experiences *are* *of* objects; words *mean* things. The *content* was the *glue* that held the mind together, and it was *taken* *for* *granted*.

Part V examines the *medium* in which the content is *articulated*: **language**. The *medium* is not *neutral*. It *shapes* the *content* it *carries* (the *Sapir*-*Whorf* *hypothesis*, the *contemporary* *debate*), and it is the *object* of the *independent* *inquiry* of *linguistics*, *logic*, and *philosophy*. The *philosophy* of *language* is the *foundation* of the *philosophy* of *mind*, and the *reversal* of the *order* is the *distinctive* *move* of the *analytic* *tradition*.

The chapter's *thesis* is that the *question* "what is meaning?" is *not* a *single* *question* but a *family* of *questions*, and that the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *truth*-*conditional* *semantics* *combined* with a *use*-*theoretic* and *inferentialist* *account* of *understanding*. The *position* *acknowledges* that the *compositionality* of *meaning* is *real* and *explains* the *productivity* of *language*, that *vagueness* is *real* and *resists* the *classical* *semantics*, and that *meaning* is *normative* in a way no *purely* *descriptive* *account* *captures*.

The chapter proceeds in seven movements. §1 examines the *triangle* of *meaning* and its *problems*. §2 examines *Frege*'s *sense*/*reference* *distinction*. §3 examines the *four* *families* of *theory* of *meaning*. §4 examines *compositionality* and *productivity*. §5 examines *vagueness* and the *sorites*. §6 examines *meaning* and *normativity*. §7 examines *meaning* and the *world*.

---

## 1. The Triangle of Meaning

### 1.1 The semantic triangle

**The *thesis*** (Ogden & Richards, *The Meaning of Meaning*, 1923, p. 11; the *classical* *formulation*). The *meaning* of a *word* is *analysed* by the *triangle* of *sign*, *thought*, and *object*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Sign (word)} \ \to \ \text{Thought (reference)} \ \to \ \text{Object (referent)}
$$

The *sign* *expresses* the *thought*; the *thought* *refers* to the *object*; the *sign* and the *object* are *connected* *only* *through* the *thought* (the *dotted* *line*).

**The *virtue*.** The *triangle* *captures* the *three* *relata* of *meaning* (the *linguistic*, the *mental*, the *worldly*), and it *avoids* the *crude* *identification* of *meaning* with the *object* (the *Millian* *view*; §2.1).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *psychologism* *objection*** (Frege, *Foundations of Arithmetic*, 1884, §26; *Review of Philosophy of Arithmetic*, 1894). The *triangle* *makes* *meaning* *depend* on the *private* *mental* *state* of the *speaker*, and the *privacy* *undermines* the *objectivity* of *meaning* (two speakers with *different* *ideas* would mean *different* *things* by the *same* *word*).

**The *reply***. The *thought* is *shared* (the *senses* are *public*; §2.2), and the *reply* *presupposes* the *distinction* between the *sense* and the *idea*. The *reply* is *contested*, and it is *precisely* what the *Fregean* *argument* *establishes*.

**(ii) The *triangle* *is* *not* *enough***. The *triangle* *tells* us *which* *relata* are *involved*, not *what* the *meaning* *relation* *is*. The *relation* *between* the *sign* and the *thought* (the *expression*), the *relation* *between* the *thought* and the *object* (the *reference*), and the *relation* *between* the *sign* and the *object* (the *denotation*) are *all* *distinct*, and *none* is *explained* by the *triangle*.

**The *reply***. The *triangle* is a *heuristic*, not a *theory*. The *theory* *requires* a *further* *account* (the *truth*-*conditional* *semantics*; the *use*-*theoretic* *account*; §3).

### 1.2 Mill and the referential theory

**The *thesis*** (Mill, *A System of Logic*, 1843, I.ii.5). The *meaning* of a *proper* *name* is the *object* it *refers* to.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(\text{name } n) = \text{referent}(n)
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *referential* *function* of *names*, and it *fits* the *ordinary* *intuition* ("Socrates" *means* Socrates).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *empty*-*name* *objection***. Empty *names* ("Sherlock Holmes," "Vulcan") *mean* *something*, and they *have* *no* *referent*. The *Mililian* *theory* *cannot* *explain* their *meaning* (Ch. 25, §6).

**The *reply***. The *empty* *names* *are* *meaningful* in a *derivative* *sense* (the *pretence*; Ch. 49, §4), and the *reply* *restricts* the *theory* to *referring* *names*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *informative*-*identity* *objection*** (§2.1). "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is *informative*, and "Hesperus is Hesperus" is *trivial*, and the *difference* *cannot* be *explained* if the *meaning* is the *referent*.

**The *reply***. The *difference* is *pragmatic*, not *semantic* (the *Millian* *reply*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *Fregean* *argument*; §2.2).

### 1.3 The description theory

**The *thesis*** (Russell, "On Denoting," 1905; *The Problems of Philosophy*, 1912, Ch. V; Frege, *Foundations*, 1884, §62, in a *different* *form*). The *meaning* of a *name* is a *description* (or a *cluster* of *descriptions*) that *picks* out the *object*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(\text{name } n) = \text{the description } \varphi \text{ such that } \text{referent}(n) = \text{the unique } x \text{ satisfying } \varphi
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *empty* *names* (the *description* *may* *fail* to *be* *satisfied*; Ch. 12, §2), and it *fits* the *ordinary* *practice* of *identifying* the *referent*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *Kripkean* *objection*** (Ch. 12, §3). The *modal* and *epistemic* *arguments* *show* that *names* are *rigid* and *descriptions* are *not*, and the *identification* is *false*.

**(ii) The *cluster* *objection*** (Searle, "Proper Names," 1958). The *description* *may* *not* *be* *unique* (the *cluster* *of* *descriptions* *fails* to *pick* out the *object*), and the *theory* *must* *appeal* to a *cluster*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *triangle* and its *descendants* are *all* *inadequate* in their *pure* forms, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *hybrid*: the *meaning* *involves* a *referent* (the *Millian* *insight*), a *mode* of *presentation* (the *Fregean* *insight*), and a *use* (the *Wittgensteinian* *insight*; §3.3), and the *three* *are* *distinct* *components* of a *single* *notion*.

## 2. Frege's Sense and Reference

### 2.1 The problem of informative identity

**The *datum*. "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is *informative* (it *tells* us *something* *new*), and "Hesperus is Hesperus" is *trivial* (it *tells* us *nothing*).

**The *argument*** (Frege, "On Sense and Reference," 1892).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If the *meaning* of a *name* is its *referent*, "Hesperus is Phosphorus" and "Hesperus is Hesperus" *mean* the *same* (the *same* *object*). \\
2. & \text{The *two* *sentences* *have* *different* *cognitive* *significance* (the *first* is *informative*, the *second* is *trivial*). \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is *not* the *referent* (the *Millian* *theory* is *false*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion***. The *meaning* *must* *include* a *component* *distinct* from the *referent*, and the *component* is the *sense*.

### 2.2 The sense/reference distinction

**The *distinction***.

| Concept | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference** (Bedeutung) | The *object* the expression *refers* to | The planet Venus |
| **Sense** (Sinn) | The *mode* of *presentation* of the *object* | "The morning star"; "the evening star" |

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Expression} \ \to \ \text{Sense} \ \to \ \text{Reference}
$$

**The *theses*.

**(i) The *sense* is *public***. The *sense* is *not* a *private* *idea* (the *psychologism* *objection*), and it is *available* to all *competent* *speakers* of the *language*.

**(ii) The *sense* *determines* the *reference***. The *sense* is a *mode* of *presentation*, and the *mode* *determines* *which* *object* is *presented* (the *reference* is a *function* of the *sense* and the *world*).

**(iii) The *sense* is *not* the *object***. The *sense* of "Hesperus" is not Venus; it is the *way* Venus is presented. The *senses* are *objective* but *not* *worldly* (they are "in between" the *idea* and the *object*; the *famous* *passage* in the epigraph).

**The *virtue*.** The *distinction* *explains* the *informative* *identity* (the *senses* are *different*, the *reference* is *the same*), and it *grounds* the *objectivity* of *meaning* (the *senses* are *public*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *nature* of the *sense* *is* *mysterious***. What *is* a *sense*? It is *not* an *object* (it is *not* *located* in *space* or *time*), *not* a *mental* *state* (it is *not* *private*), and *not* a *mere* *convention* (it *determines* the *reference*). The *category* is *unclear*, and the *mystery* is *the* *central* *problem* of the *Fregean* *tradition*.

**The *reply* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *sense* is a *mode* of *presentation* (a *procedure* for *identifying* the *reference*, or a *property* of the *referent*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *sense* in the *cognitive* *economy*. The *reply* is *contested* (§3.4).

**(ii) The *compositionality* *problem***. The *senses* *combine* into *complex* *senses* (the *sense* of "the morning star is bright" is a *complex* *sense*), and the *combination* *must* *respect* the *structure* of the *sentence*. The *reply* (the *Fregean* *principle*) is *contested* (§4).

**(iii) The *opacity* *problem***. In *belief* *contexts*, the *substitution* of *co*-*referential* *terms* *fails* ("Lois believes that Superman can fly" is *true*, and "Lois believes that Clark Kent can fly" is *false*), and the *failure* *shows* that the *sense* is *not* *reducible* to the *reference*. The *reply* (the *intensional* *semantics*) is *contested* (Ch. 32, §2; Ch. 25, §4).

### 2.3 The context principle

**The *thesis*** (Frege, *Foundations of Arithmetic*, 1884, §62; §106). A *word* *has* *meaning* only in the *context* of a *sentence* (the *“context* *principle”*).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(\text{word } w) = \text{the contribution of } w \text{ to the meaning of sentences containing } w
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* of a *word* is *not* a *self*-standing *entity* (it is not a *mental* *image*, an *object*, or a *sense* *in* *isolation*).} \\
2. & \text{The *word* *functions* in a *sentence* (it *contributes* to the *sentence*'s *content*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is *constituted* by the *contribution* (the *context* *principle*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *principle* *avoids* the *psychological* *account* of *meaning*, and it *grounds* the *compositional* *semantics* (the *meaning* of the *whole* is the *function* of the *meanings* of the *parts*; §4).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *holism* *objection***. The *principle* *makes* the *meaning* of a *word* *depend* on the *whole* *sentence*, and the *dependence* *leads* to a *holism* (the *meaning* of a *sentence* *depends* on the *language*; the *meaning* of a *word* *depends* on the *sentences*). The *holism* is *threatening* (§4.4).

**The *reply***. The *meaning* is *compositional* (the *word* *contributes* to the *sentence*, and the *contribution* is *stable* across *sentences*), and the *reply* *qualifies* the *principle*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *sense*/*reference* *distinction* is *indispensable*, and the *Fregean* *semantics* is *influential*, but the *nature* of the *sense* is *unresolved*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Millianism** | Meaning = reference | Simple; extensional | The informative identity |
| **Fregeanism** | Meaning = sense + reference | Explains cognitive significance | The nature of the sense |
| **Neo-Fregeanism** | Sense as a cognitive role | Fits the contemporary literature | The role is vague |
| **Two-dimensionalism** | Sense as a primary intension | Technical; precise | The primary intension is contested |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *Fregeanism*: the *meaning* *involves* a *mode* of *presentation* (the *sense*), and the *mode* is *grounded* in the *cognitive* *role* of the *expression*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *nature* of the *role* is *contested*.

## 3. Theories of Meaning

### 3.1 Verificationism

**The *thesis*** (the *logical* *positivists*; Schlick, "The Turning Point in Philosophy," 1930; Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936, Ch. 1). The *meaning* of a *sentence* is the *method* of its *verification* (the *empirical* *conditions* *that* would *confirm* or *disconfirm* it).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(p) = \text{the set of experiences that would verify } p
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *grounds* *meaning* in the *empirical* *evidence*, and it *avoids* the *metaphysical* *speculation* (the *positivist* *programme*; Ch. 9, §2.3).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *self*-*refutation* *objection***. The *verification* *principle* is itself *neither* *analytic* *nor* *empirically* *verifiable*, and the *principle* *undermines* *itself* (Ch. 9, §2.3).

**The *reply***. The *principle* is a *proposal* (a *convention*), not a *thesis* (the *Carnapian* *reply*), and the *reply* *abandons* the *claim* to *truth*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*many* *methods*’* *objection***. A *sentence* *can* be *verified* *in* *many* *ways* (the *dispositions*, the *instruments*), and the *meaning* is *not* *determined* by the *method*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *verification*’* *objection***. The *universal* *statements* (the *laws* of *physics*) *cannot* be *conclusively* *verified*, and the *theory* *makes* them *meaningless* (or *reduces* them to *their* *instances*). The *objection* *undermines* the *theory*.

### 3.2 Truth-conditional semantics

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, "Truth and Meaning," 1967; *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation*, 1984). The *meaning* of a *sentence* is its *truth* *conditions*: to *know* the *meaning* is to *know* *what* *the* *world* *would* *have* *to* *be* *like* for the *sentence* to be *true*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(p) = \text{the truth conditions of } p
$$

**The *formalism*** (the *Tarskian* *truth* *definition*; Tarski, "The Concept of Truth in Formalized Languages," 1933; Ch. 32, §1).

$$
\forall s\, (\text{True}(s) \leftrightarrow p) \quad \text{(the *T*-schema, for each sentence } s \text{ with content } p)
$$

**The *argument*** (Davidson 1967, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* of a *sentence* is *what* a *competent* *speaker* *knows* when she *understands* it.} \\
2. & \text{The *knowledge* is *propositional* (the *speaker* *knows* the *conditions* *under* *which* the *sentence* is *true*).} \\
3. & \text{The *conditions* are *specifiable* by a *truth* *definition* (the *Tarskian* *form*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *truth* *conditions* (the *truth*-*conditional* *semantics*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *grounds* *meaning* in *truth* (an *objective* *notion*; Ch. 32), and it *provides* a *compositional* *semantics* (the *meaning* of the *whole* is *built* from the *meanings* of the *parts*; §4).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*understanding* *is* *not* *truth*’* *objection*** (Dummett, "What Is a Theory of Meaning? (I)," 1975). The *truth* *conditions* are *verification*-*transcendent* (Ch. 22, §1.3), and the *speaker* *cannot* *manifest* the *knowledge* of the *conditions* (the *manifestation* *requirement*). The *objection* *supports* the *verificationist* *or* *use*-*theoretic* *account*.

**The *reply***. The *manifestation* *requirement* is *contested*, and the *speaker* *can* *manifest* the *knowledge* *implicitly* (in the *practice* of *assertion* and *inference*). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *truth* *conditions*’* *objection***. Some *sentences* *have* *no* *truth* *conditions* (the *expressive* *sentences*, the *performatives*; Ch. 34), and the *theory* *excludes* them.

**The *reply***. The *expressives* and *performatives* *have* *truth* *conditions* in a *broad* *sense* (the *conditions* of *felicity*; Ch. 34, §1), and the *reply* *extends* the *theory*.

### 3.3 Use-theoretic semantics

**The *thesis*** (Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations*, 1953, §43; the *later* *Wittgenstein*; the *ordinary*-*language* *tradition*; Ch. 3, §6.2). The *meaning* of a *word* is its *use* in the *language*-*game*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(w) = \text{the use of } w \text{ in the language-game}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* of a *word* is *not* an *entity* (an *object*, an *image*, a *sense*), but a *practice*.} \\
2. & \text{The *practice* is the *use* (the *rule*-*governed* *employment* of the *word*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *use* (the *use*-*theoretic* *semantics*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *sense* and the *truth* *conditions*, and it *fits* the *diversity* of *language* *games* (the *commands*, the *promises*, the *questions*; Ch. 34).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*use* *is* *too* *broad*’* *objection***. The *use* of a *word* *includes* its *phonology*, its *syntax*, its *pragmatics*, and its *social* *role*, and the *notion* *does* not *isolate* the *meaning*.

**The *reply*.** The *meaningful* *use* is the *normative* *use* (the *rule*-*governed* *use*; §6), and the *reply* *qualifies* the *theory*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *compositionality*’* *objection***. The *use* *theory* *does* not *explain* the *compositionality* of *meaning* (the *meaning* of a *sentence* is a *function* of the *meanings* of the *words*), and the *theory* *fails* the *productivity* *test* (§4).

**The *reply* (the *use*-*theoretic* *account*; the *‘*meaning* *as* *use*’* *tradition*). The *use* *includes* the *compositional* *rules* (the *rules* that *determine* the *meaning* of a *complex* *expression* from the *meanings* of the *parts*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *compositionality* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*language* *is* *normative*’* *objection***. The *use* *theory* *describes* the *practice* but not the *norm* (the *correctness* *conditions*; §6), and the *theory* *misses* the *normativity* of *meaning*.

**The *reply* (the *inferentialist* *account*; §3.4). The *use* is *normative* (it is *governed* by *rules*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *normativity* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.4 Inferentialism

**The *thesis*** (Brandom, *Making It Explicit*, 1994; *Articulating Reasons*, 2000; Sellars, "Inference and Meaning," 1953). The *meaning* of an *expression* is its *inferential* *role*: the *expression* *means* what it *does* because of the *inferences* it *licenses* and the *inferences* in which it *figures*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(e) = \text{the inferential role of } e
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* is *normative* (it *determines* what *follows* from an *assertion*, what *contradicts* it, what *supports* it).} \\
2. & \text{The *normative* *structure* is *inferential* (the *relations* of *entailment*, *incompatibility*, and *support*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is the *inferential* *role* (the *inferentialism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *normativity* of *meaning*, and it *grounds* the *meaning* in the *social* *practices* of *giving* and *asking* for *reasons* (the *“*game* *of* *giving* *and* *asking* *for* *reasons”*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*holism*’* *objection*** (Fodor & Lepore, *Holism: A Shopper’s Guide*, 1992; Ch. 25, §3.2). The *inferential* *role* *depends* on the *whole* *system* of *beliefs*, and the *dependence* *leads* to a *holism* (any *change* in *belief* *changes* the *meaning* of *every* *expression*).

**The *reply***. The *inferential* *role* *includes* the *structural* *features* (the *logical* *constants* are *defined* by their *roles*, and the *roles* are *stable*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *stable* *core* from the *variable* *periphery*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *reference*’* *objection***. The *inferential* *role* *does* not *explain* the *reference* to the *world* (the *expression* *means* an *object*, not *only* a *role*), and the *theory* *loses* the *world*.

**The *reply*** (Brandom 1994, Ch. 5). The *reference* is *constituted* by the *inferential* *role* (the *object* is *what* the *inferences* *track*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *reference* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *theories* of *meaning* are *all* *live*, and *none* is *decisive*.

| Theory | The *core* *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verificationism** | Meaning = method of verification | Fits the empirical evidence | Self-refutation |
| **Truth-conditional** | Meaning = truth conditions | Objective; compositional | Verifications-transcendence |
| **Use-theoretic** | Meaning = use | Fits the diversity of language games | Compositionality |
| **Inferentialist** | Meaning = inferential role | Captures normativity | Holism; reference |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *truth*-*conditional* *semantics* *combined* with an *inferentialist* *account* of *understanding*: the *truth* *conditions* *provide* the *extension* of the *meaning* (the *worldly* *component*), and the *inferential* *role* *provides* the *sense* (the *cognitive* *component*), and the *two* *are* *complementary* *aspects* of a *single* *notion*.

## 4. Compositionality and Productivity

### 4.1 The principle

**The *thesis*** (Frege, *Foundations*, 1884, §46; the *compositionality* *principle*). The *meaning* of a *complex* *expression* is *determined* by the *meanings* of its *parts* and the *manner* of their *combination*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Meaning}(e_1 \ldots e_n) = F(\text{Meaning}(e_1), \ldots, \text{Meaning}(e_n), \text{structure})
$$

**The *argument*** (the *productivity* *argument*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *competent* *speaker* *understands* *sentences* *she* *has* *never* *heard* (the *productivity* *of* *language*).} \\
2. & \text{The *understanding* *requires* that the *meaning* of the *sentence* *be* *derived* from the *meanings* of the *words* (the *compositionality*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is *compositional*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *principle* *explains* the *productivity* (the *infinite* *number* of *sentences* from a *finite* *vocabulary*) and *systematicity* (the *understanding* of *one* *sentence* *entails* the *understanding* of *related* *sentences*).

### 4.2 The challenges

**The *challenges* are *many*, and they are *instructive***.

**(i) The *idiom* *challenge***. The *meaning* of an *idiom* ("kick the bucket") is *not* a *function* of the *meanings* of its *parts*, and the *compositionality* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *idiom* is a *lexical* *item* (a *single* *unit*), and the *compositionality* *applies* to the *sentence* (the *idiom* *contributes* its *meaning*). The *reply* *relativizes* the *principle* to the *level* (the *word*, the *idiom*, the *sentence*).

**(ii) The *ambiguity* *challenge***. The *sentence* "Visiting relatives can be boring" is *ambiguous*, and the *meaning* is *not* *determined* by the *meanings* of the *words* (the *syntactic* *structure* *differs*).

**The *reply***. The *ambiguity* is *syntactic* (the *two* *structures* *have* *different* *meanings*), and the *compositionality* *applies* to *each* *structure* *separately*. The *reply* *relativizes* the *principle* to the *parse*.

**(iii) The *context* *sensitivity* *challenge***. The *meaning* of an *indexical* ("I," "now," "here") *depends* on the *context* (Ch. 12, §4), and the *compositionality* *fails* unless the *context* is *included* in the *meaning*.

**The *reply*** (Kaplan 1977/1989). The *meaning* *includes* the *character* (the *rule* that *determines* the *content* from the *context*), and the *content* is *compositional*. The *reply* *grounds* the *compositionality* in the *character*, and it is *contested*.

**(iv) The *attitude* *reports* *challenge***. The *belief* *reports* are *opaque* (§2.2), and the *substitution* of *co*-*referential* *terms* *fails*, and the *compositionality* *fails* (the *meaning* of the *report* *depends* on the *mode* of *presentation*, not the *reference*).

**The *reply*** (the *intensional* *semantics*). The *compositionality* *applies* to the *senses* (the *intensions*), not the *references* (the *extensions*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *compositionality* in the *intensional* *semantics*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.3 The language of thought

**The *thesis*** (Fodor, *The Language of Thought*, 1975; *LOT 2*, 2008; Ch. 25, §5.4). The *compositionality* of *thought* *requires* a *language*-like *medium* (the *language* of *thought*, LOT), and the *natural* *language* *inherits* its *compositionality* from the LOT.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Thought is *compositional* (the *productivity* *argument*; §4.1).} \\
2. & \text{The *compositionality* *requires* a *medium* *with* *syntax* and *semantics* (the *language*-*like* *medium*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Thought has a *language* of *thought* (the LOT).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *compositionality*, and it *grounds* the *natural* *language* *in* the *cognitive* *architecture*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*infinite* *regress*’* *objection*** (Dennett, *Brainstorms*, 1978; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *understanding* of the *LOT* *requires* a *homunculus* *who* *reads* the *LOT*, and the *homunculus* *requires* a *further* *LOT*, and the *regress* is *infinite*. (The *reply*: the *homunculus* *is* *eliminated* by the *functional* *analysis*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(ii) The *‘*empirical*’* *objection***. The *LOT* is a *theoretical* *postulate* without *direct* *evidence*, and the *empirical* *literature* (the *mental* *imagery*; Ch. 25, §5.2) *challenges* it. The *objection* is *contested*.

### 4.4 The holism challenge

**The *problem***. The *compositionality* *requires* *stable* *meanings* for the *parts*, and the *holism* (the *meaning* *depends* on the *whole* *system*) *threatens* the *stability*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *compositionality* *requires* that the *meaning* of a *word* be *stable* across *sentences*.} \\
2. & \text{The *holism* *makes* the *meaning* of a *word* *depend* on the *whole* *system* of *beliefs*.} \\
3. & \text{The *dependence* *makes* the *meaning* *unstable* (any *change* in *belief* *changes* the *meaning*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *holism* and the *compositionality* are *incompatible*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.

**(i) The *‘*molecular*’* *response*** (Dummett 1975; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* *depends* on a *molecular* *set* of *inferences* (not the *whole* *system*), and the *molecular* *dependence* *preserves* the *stability*.

**(ii) The *‘*two*-*factor*’* *response*** (Block 1986; Ch. 25, §3.3). The *meaning* has a *narrow* *component* (the *conceptual* *role*, which is *holistic*) and a *wide* *component* (the *reference*, which is *stable*), and the *compositionality* *applies* to the *wide* *component*.

**(iii) The *‘*externalist*’* *response*** (Putnam 1975; Kripke 1980; Ch. 25, §4). The *meaning* *depends* on the *external* *reference* (the *causal* *chain*), and the *externality* *preserves* the *stability* (the *reference* does *not* *change* when the *beliefs* *change*).

**The *state* of the debate.** The *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *compositionality*: the *meaning* is *compositional* at the *level* of the *reference* and the *logical* *structure*, and *holistic* at the *level* of the *conceptual* *role*, and the *two* *levels* are *distinct* *aspects* of a *single* *notion*.

## 5. Vagueness and the Sorites

### 5.1 The sorites paradox

**The *paradox*** (Eubulides, 4th c. BCE; the *heap* *paradox*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{1 grain of sand is not a heap (the *base* *case*).} \\
2. & \text{If } n \text{ grains are not a heap, then } n+1 \text{ grains are not a heap (the *induction* *step*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{No number of grains is a heap (the *conclusion*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *structure*.** The *argument* is *valid* (the *induction* *step* *is* *a* *universal* *conditional*), and the *conclusion* is *false* (there are heaps). The *premises* are *individually* *plausible* (the *base* *case* is *certain*, and the *induction* *step* is *intuitive*). The *sorites* *is* a *paradox* *because* the *three* *are* *incompatible*.

**The *challenge*.** A *satisfactory* *semantics* *must* *reject* one of the *three* (the *validity*, the *base* *case*, the *induction* *step*), and the *rejection* *requires* a *theory* of *vagueness*.

### 5.2 Epistemicism

**The *thesis*** (Williamson, *Vagueness*, 1994; Sorensen, *Blindspots*, 1988; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *vague* *predicates* *have* *sharp* *boundaries*, and the *boundaries* *are* *unknowable* (the *vagueness* is *epistemic*, not *metaphysical*).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{There is a sharp } n \text{ such that } n \text{ grains are not a heap and } n+1 \text{ are}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *principle* of *bivalence* (every *proposition* is *true* or *false*) *applies* to the *vague* *predicates* (the *classical* *logic*).} \\
2. & \text{The *principle* *requires* a *sharp* *boundary* (the *predicate* *is* *either* *true* or *false* of *each* *number*).} \\
3. & \text{The *boundary* is *not* *knowable* (the *margin* of *error*: if we are *at* the *boundary*, we *cannot* *know* *which* *side* *we* *are* on).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *vagueness* is *epistemic* (the *boundary* *exists*, but we *cannot* *know* it).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *preserves* the *classical* *logic* and the *bivalence*, and it *explains* the *sorites* (the *induction* *step* is *false*, but *not* *knowably* *false*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*no* *fact* *of* *the* *matter*’* *objection***. The *epistemicist* *posits* a *sharp* *boundary* *that* *nothing* *in* *the* *world* *determines*, and the *positing* is *mysterious* (what *makes* the *boundary* *be* *here* rather than *there*?).

**The *reply***. The *boundary* is *determined* by the *meaning* of the *predicate* (the *convention*, the *use*), and the *determination* *is* *not* *mysterious* (it is *the* *same* *kind* of *determination* as the *meaning* of *any* *word*). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*the* *boundary* *is* *arbitrary*’* *objection***. The *boundary* *seems* *arbitrary* (the *predicate* *is* *vague* *because* the *boundary* *is* *not* *determinate*), and the *epistemicist* *denies* the *seeming*.

**The *reply***. The *seeming* is *explained* by the *epistemic* *limits* (we *cannot* *know* the *boundary*), and the *explanation* *is* *defensible*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.3 Supervaluationism

**The *thesis*** (Fine, "Vagueness, Truth and Logic," 1975; Keefe, *Theories of Vagueness*, 2000; the *contemporary* *literature*). A *sentence* is *true* iff it is *true* on *all* the *admissible* *precisifications* (the *ways* of *making* the *vague* *predicate* *precise*), and *false* iff *false* on *all* *precisifications*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{True}(p) \quad \text{iff} \quad \forall \text{ precisifications } \pi\, (\text{True}_\pi(p))
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *vagueness* is a *deficiency* of *the* *language*, not of *the* *world*.} \\
2. & \text{The *deficiency* is *modelled* by the *admissible* *precisifications*.} \\
3. & \text{The *truth* is *the* *super*-*truth* (the *truth* on all the *precisifications*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *vagueness* is *handled* by the *supervaluation* (the *supervaluationism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *preserves* the *classical* *logic* *for* the *determinate* *cases*, and it *explains* the *sorites* (the *induction* *step* is *not* *super*-*true*, and the *base* *case* is *super*-*true*, and the *conclusion* *fails*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*higher*-*order* *vagueness*’* *objection***. The *precisifications* *are* *themselves* *vague* (the *boundary* *of* the *admissible* *precisifications* is *vague*), and the *theory* *requires* a *further* *level* of *precisifications* (the *higher*-*order* *vagueness*). The *objection* *is* *serious*.

**The *reply***. The *higher*-*order* *vagueness* is *handled* by a *hierarchy* of *precisifications*, and the *hierarchy* *terminates* in the *indeterminate* (the *reply* is *contested*).

**(ii) The *‘*truth* *is* *not* *super*-*truth*’* *objection***. The *supervaluation* *makes* the *truth* *depend* on the *admissible* *precisifications*, and the *admissibility* is *not* *determined* by the *world*. The *objection* *is* *contested*.

### 5.4 Degree theories

**The *thesis*** (Machina, "Truth, Belief, and Vagueness," 1976; Smith, *Vagueness and Degrees of Truth*, 2008). The *truth* *is* a *matter* of *degree* (the *sentence* *is* *true* *to* a *degree* $\in [0,1]$), and the *vagueness* is *modelled* by the *degree* of *truth*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{True}(p) = d \quad \text{for } d \in [0,1]
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *graded* *character* of *vagueness* (the *heap* *is* *more* *of* a *heap* *the* *more* *grains*), and it *avoids* the *sharp* *boundary*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*no* *degree* *of* *truth*’* *objection***. The *degrees* of *truth* are *mysterious* (what *is* a *degree* of *truth*?), and the *theory* *replaces* the *vague* *predicate* with a *precise* *function* (the *degree*), and the *replacement* *does* not *explain* the *vagueness*.

**(ii) The *‘*logical* *connectives*’* *objection***. The *degree* *theory* *must* *define* the *logical* *connectives* (the *conjunction*, the *negation*) *for* the *degrees*, and the *definitions* are *contested* (the *Łukasiewicz* *negation*, the *Zadeh* *negation*).

### 5.5 Contextualism

**The *thesis*** (Kamp, "Two Theories about Adjectives," 1975; Raffman, *Vagueness without Paradox*, 1994; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *vagueness* is *context*-*dependent*: the *extension* of the *vague* *predicate* *changes* with the *context* (the *conversational* *score*), and the *sorites* *is* *explained* by the *shifting* *standard*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Extension}(\text{heap}) = f(\text{context})
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *context*-*sensitivity* of the *vague* *predicates*, and it *explains* the *sorites* (the *induction* *step* *changes* the *context*, and the *change* *makes* the *step* *false* in the *new* *context*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*no* *sharp* *boundary*’* *objection***. The *theory* *still* *requires* a *boundary* (in *each* *context*), and the *boundary* is *arbitrary*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *higher*-*order* *vagueness*’* *objection***. The *context* *itself* is *vague*, and the *theory* *requires* a *further* *account* of the *context*.

### 5.6 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *theories* of *vagueness* are *all* *live*, and *none* is *decisive*.

| Theory | The *core* *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epistemicism** | The boundary is sharp but unknowable | Preserves classical logic | The boundary is mysterious |
| **Supervaluationism** | Truth is truth on all precisifications | Preserves classical logic | Higher-order vagueness |
| **Degree** | Truth is a matter of degree | Captures gradedness | The degrees are mysterious |
| **Contextualism** | The extension depends on the context | Captures context-sensitivity | The context is vague |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *epistemicism*: the *vagueness* is a *feature* of the *language* (not the *world*), and the *boundaries* are *sharp* but *unknowable*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *epistemicist* *explanation* of the *boundary* is *incomplete*.

## 6. Meaning and Normativity

### 6.1 The normativity of meaning

**The *datum*.** The *meaning* is *normative*: to *mean* something by a *word* is to be *correct* or *incorrect* in its *use* (the *correctness* is *not* *reducible* to the *regularity* of the *use*).

**The *argument*** (Kripke, *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language*, 1982; the *contemporary* *literature*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* *determines* the *correct* *use* (the *extension* of the *predicate*).} \\
2. & \text{The *correctness* is *normative* (it *prescribes* the *use*, not merely *describes* it).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *meaning* is *normative* (the *normativity* is *constitutive* of *meaning*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *grounds* the *normativity* of *meaning* in the *correctness* *conditions*, and it *fits* the *ordinary* *practice* of *correction* and *justification*.

### 6.2 Kripke's Wittgenstein

**The *argument*** (Kripke 1982, the *rule*-*following* *paradox*). The *paradox* is *stated* by *Kripke* (as an *interpretation* of *Wittgenstein*; the *interpretation* is *contested*), and it is *one* of the *most* *discussed* *arguments* in the *philosophy* of *language*.

**The *scenario***. The *agent* has *used* the *word* "+" *in* the *past*, and the *use* *has* *always* *been* *consistent* with the *finite* *number* of *cases* (the *agent* *has* *never* *added* *numbers* *larger* than 57). The *question*: *what* *makes* it *true* that the *agent* *means* *addition* (and not an *alternative* *function*, "quaddition," which *agrees* with *addition* on the *past* *cases* and *differs* on the *future* *ones*)?

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *meaning* *must* be *determined* by the *past* *use* (the *past* *cases*, the *dispositions*, the *mental* *states*).} \\
2. & \text{The *past* *use* is *finite*, and it is *consistent* with *both* *addition* and *quaddition*.} \\
3. & \text{The *mental* *states* are *also* *finite*, and they *do* not *determine* the *future* *use*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There is *no* *fact* of the *matter* about *what* the *agent* *means* (the *meaning* *skepticism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *skeptical* *solution*** (Kripke 1982, Ch. 3). The *skeptic* *is* *correct* (there is *no* *fact* of the *matter*), and the *solution* is *pragmatic*: the *meaning* *attributions* are *justified* by their *role* in the *social* *practice* (the *community*'s *agreement* in *judgments*; the *“*assertibility*”* *conditions*).

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *challenges* the *classical* *conception* of *meaning* (the *meaning* as a *determinate* *fact*), and the *skeptical* *solution* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *social* *practice*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*primitivism*’* *objection*** (McGinn, *Wittgenstein on Meaning*, 1984; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* is a *primitive* *fact* (it is *not* *reducible* to the *use*), and the *skeptic* *is* *wrong* (there *is* a *fact* of the matter). The *reply* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *primitive* *intentionality*, and it is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*dispositionalism*’* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *meaning* is *determined* by the *dispositions* (the *agent* *would* *answer* *in* a *certain* *way*), and the *skeptic* *is* *wrong*. The *reply* *grounds* the *meaning* in the *dispositions*, and it is *contested* (the *dispositions* *are* *finite*, and the *future* *use* *is* *infinite*).

**(iii) The *‘*normativity*’* *objection*** (Boghossian, "The Rule-Following Considerations," 1989). The *dispositions* *are* *descriptive* (they *predict* the *use*, not *prescribe* it), and the *normativity* *is* *lost*. The *objection* *supports* the *normativist* *account* (the *meaning* *is* *constituted* by the *norms*).

### 6.3 The normativist accounts

**The *positions***.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Primitivism** | Meaning is a primitive normative fact | McGinn; the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Communitarianism** | Meaning is constituted by the community's norms | Kripke; the *skeptical* *solution* |
| **Inferentialism** | Meaning is constituted by the inferential norms | Brandom; Ch. 31, §3.4 |
| **Teleosemantics** | Meaning is constituted by the biological norms | Millikan; Ch. 25, §2.3 |

**The *argument* (the *normativist* *view*).** The *meaning* *is* the *normative* *role* (the *inferences* *licensed*, the *assertions* *permitted*), and the *normative* *role* *grounds* the *correctness* *conditions* (the *theory* of *meaning* *is* the *theory* of the *norms*).

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *normativity* of *meaning*, and it *fits* the *social* *practice* of *correction* and *justification*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*circularity*’* *objection***. The *normative* *role* *presupposes* the *meaning* (the *norms* *are* *formulated* in a *language*, and the *language* *has* *meaning*), and the *account* *is* *circular*.

**The *reply***. The *norms* are *constituted* by the *practice* (the *social* *sanctions*, the *corrections*), and the *practice* *does* not *presuppose* the *meaning* (it *constitutes* it). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *determinacy*’* *objection***. The *norms* *underdetermine* the *meaning* (the *norms* *are* *many*, and the *choice* is *not* *unique*), and the *account* *fails* the *determinacy* *test*.

**The *reply***. The *norms* are *determined* by the *practice* (the *community*'s *agreement*), and the *determination* *is* *defeasible* but *real*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *normativity* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *normativism*: the *meaning* *is* *normative* (the *correctness* *conditions* are *constitutive*), and the *norms* are *grounded* in the *social* *practice* (the *inferential* *roles*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *determination* of the *norms* is *contested*.

## 7. Meaning and the World

### 7.1 The linguistic turn

**The *thesis*** (the *analytic* *tradition*; the *linguistic* *turn*). The *philosophical* *questions* are *questions* about *language*, and the *analysis* of *language* is the *method* of *philosophy*.

**The *argument*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *tradition* *from* Frege *to* the *later* *Wittgenstein*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *philosophical* *problems* are *generated* by the *misuse* of *language* (the *Wittgensteinian* *diagnosis*).} \\
2. & \text{The *misuse* is *corrected* by the *analysis* of *language* (the *method*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *philosophy* is the *analysis* of *language* (the *linguistic* *turn*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *turn* *grounds* the *philosophical* *method* in the *linguistic* *analysis*, and it *fits* the *practice* of the *analytic* *tradition* (Ch. 3, §6.2).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*linguistic* *idealism*’* *objection***. The *turn* *reduces* the *philosophy* to the *analysis* of *language*, and the *reduction* *loses* the *world* (the *philosophical* *questions* are *about* the *world*, not *only* about the *words*).

**The *reply***. The *language* is *about* the *world* (the *meaning* *involves* the *reference*; §2), and the *analysis* *recovers* the *world* (the *reply* is *contested*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *progress*’* *objection***. The *linguistic* *turn* *has* *not* *solved* the *philosophical* *problems* (the *analysis* *has* *produced* *distinctions* but *not* *answers*), and the *turn* *is* *a* *methodological* *detour*.

**The *reply***. The *progress* is *the* *clarification* (the *distinctions* *are* *the* *progress*; Ch. 1, §5), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.2 The Sapir–Whorf hypothesis

**The *thesis*** (Sapir, "The Status of Linguistics as a Science," 1929; Whorf, *Language, Thought, and Reality*, 1956). The *language* *shapes* the *thought* (the *linguistic* *relativity* *hypothesis*; the *strong* *version*: the *language* *determines* the *thought*).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Language} \ \to \ \text{Thought} \quad \text{(the *strong* *version*)}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *linguistic* *categories* (the *grammar*, the *vocabulary*) *differ* across *languages*.} \\
2. & \text{The *categories* *shape* the *conceptual* *categories* (the *perception*, the *memory*, the *classification*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *language* *shapes* the *thought* (the *linguistic* *relativity*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *challenges* the *universalist* *conception* of *thought*, and it *fits* the *empirical* *literature* (the *colour* *terms*, the *spatial* *frames* of *reference*, the *number* *words*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*weak* *version*’* *objection***. The *strong* *version* (the *language* *determines* the *thought*) is *false* (the *thought* *can* *exist* *without* the *language*; the *pre*-*linguistic* *infants*, the *animals*; Ch. 25, §5), and the *weak* *version* (the *language* *influences* the *thought*) is *defensible* but *unexciting*.

**The *reply***. The *weak* *version* is *sufficient* for the *philosophical* *claim* (the *language* *shapes* the *thought*), and the *reply* *qualifies* the *thesis*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *experiment*’* *objection***. The *hypothesis* is *hard* to *test* (the *confounds*: the *culture*, the *environment*), and the *empirical* *evidence* is *contested* (the *replication* *crisis*; Ch. 3, §6.3).

### 7.3 Natural language metaphysics

**The *thesis***. The *structure* of *natural* *language* *encodes* a *metaphysics* (the *count*/*mass* *distinction*, the *natural* *kind* *terms*, the *event* *structure*), and the *metaphysics* *constrains* the *philosophical* *ontology* (Ch. 14).

**The *structure*.

| The *linguistic* *category* | The *metaphysical* *commitment* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Count nouns** | Discrete objects | "Three apples" |
| **Mass nouns** | Continuous stuff | "Some water" |
| **Natural kind terms** | Essential kinds | "Water is H₂O" |
| **Event verbs** | Events with participants | "She broke the window" |
| **Tense** | Temporal reality | "He will come" |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *linguistic* *categories* are *systematic* (the *count*/*mass* *distinction*, the *kind* *terms*).} \\
2. & \text{The *categories* *encode* a *metaphysics* (the *objects*, the *kinds*, the *events*).} \\
3. & \text{The *metaphysics* *constrains* the *ontology* (the *philosophical* *theory* *must* *accommodate* the *categories*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *natural* *language* *encodes* a *metaphysics* (the *natural* *language* *metaphysics*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *connects* the *philosophy* *of* *language* to the *metaphysics*, and it *fits* the *linguistic* *literature* (the *semantics* of *mass* *terms*, the *natural* *kind* *terms*; Ch. 15, §5.1).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*no* *metaphysics*’* *objection***. The *linguistic* *categories* are *conventional* (the *languages* *differ*), and the *categories* *do* not *encode* a *metaphysics* (the *metaphysics* is *independent* of the *language*).

**The *reply***. The *categories* *encode* the *metaphysics* of the *community* (the *language* *reflects* the *ontology*), and the *reply* *relativizes* the *metaphysics* to the *language*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*linguistic* *idealism*’* *objection***. The *view* *makes* the *metaphysics* *depend* on the *language*, and the *dependence* *undermines* the *realism* (the *world* is *independent* of the *language*).

**The *reply***. The *language* *encodes* a *metaphysics* *about* the *world* (the *metaphysics* *is* *about* the *world*, not *constituted* by the *language*), and the *reply* *preserves* the *realism*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *meaning*–*world* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *realism*: the *language* *encodes* a *metaphysics* (the *natural* *language* *metaphysics*), and the *metaphysics* is *about* the *world* (the *realist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *linguistic* *categories* *vary* and *shape* the *metaphysical* *commitments*.

## Summary

- **The *triangle* of *meaning*** (Ogden & Richards) *captures* the *three* *relata* (the *sign*, the *thought*, the *object*), and it *fails* to *explain* the *relations* (the *psychologism* *objection*, the *Fregean* *critique*).
- **Frege's *sense*/*reference* *distinction*** *explains* the *informative* *identity*, and it *raises* the *mystery* of the *sense* (the *mode* of *presentation*).
- **The *four* *families* of *theory* of *meaning*** — *verificationism*, *truth*-*conditional*, *use*-*theoretic*, *inferentialism* — are *all* *live*, and *none* is *decisive*.
- **The *compositionality* *principle*** *explains* the *productivity* and *systematicity* of *language*, and it *faces* the *idiom*, *ambiguity*, *context*-*sensitivity*, and *attitude* *reports* *challenges*.
- **The *sorites* *paradox*** *challenges* the *classical* *semantics*, and the *theories* of *vagueness* (epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree, contextualism) are *all* *contested*.
- **The *normativity* of *meaning*** is *constitutive*, and the *rule*-*following* *paradox* (Kripke's Wittgenstein) *challenges* the *determinacy* of *meaning*.
- **The *meaning*–*world* *relation*** is *contested*: the *linguistic* *turn*, the *Sapir*-*Whorf* *hypothesis*, and the *natural* *language* *metaphysics* *connect* the *language* to the *world*, and the *connection* is *qualified* by the *admission* of the *conventional* and *variable* *character* of the *linguistic* *categories*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *truth*-*conditional* *semantics* *combined* with an *inferentialist* *account* of *understanding*, *qualified* by the *admission* of the *vagueness*, the *normativity*, and the *linguistic* *relativity*.
