# Chapter 20 — Idealism, Monism, and the Mind-First Options

> “All the choir of heaven and furniture of the earth — in a word, all those bodies which compose the mighty frame of the world — have not any subsistence without a mind.”
> — George Berkeley, *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge* (1710), §6

> “Though we cannot know things as they are in themselves, we can nonetheless know them as appearances, and the world of appearance is empirically real.”
> — Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787), Bxxvi (paraphrased)

> “The whole universe is one vast, single, indivisible consciousness.”
> — a slogan of objective idealism, variously formulated

---

Chapter 19 ended with an *admission* and a *question*. The *admission*: physics describes the *structure* of the world, not its *intrinsic* *nature*; the *question*: what *is* the *intrinsic* *nature* of the physical? The *chapter* examined the *physicalist* and *structuralist* *answers*, and it *noted* that *panpsychism* is *one* *option* — but it *deferred* the *mind-first* *options* to *this* *chapter*.

This chapter takes up the *debt*. The *mind-first* *options* — **idealism**, **neutral monism**, **dual-aspect monism**, **cosmopsychism** — are the *oldest* *answers* to the *question* of the *intrinsic* *nature*, and they are the *options* that Russell, in *The Problems of Philosophy*, Chapter IV, treated as *refuted*. One of the *tasks* of this chapter is to *give* them the *hearing* that Russell *denied* them.

The chapter has a *polemical* *edge*, and it should be *acknowledged* at the outset. The *twentieth* *century* *dismissed* idealism as *obviously* *false* — a *curiosity* of the *pre-Kantian* *past*, refuted by Moore and Russell and *superseded* by the *scientific* *worldview*. The *dismissal* was *partly* a *response* to the *absolute* *idealism* of the *British* *Hegelians* (against whom Moore and Russell *revolted*), and it was *partly* a *philosophical* *prejudice*. The *contemporary* *revival* of idealism — in the *analytic* tradition (Foster, Chalmers, Goff, Kastrup), in the *philosophy* of *physics* (the *consciousness-causes-collapse* *interpretations*, the *quantum* *informational* *ontologies*), and in the *comparative* *literature* (the *Yogācāra* *tradition*) — is a *sign* that the *dismissal* was *premature*.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 examines Berkeley's *master* *argument* and the *veil* of *perception*. §2 examines Kant's *transcendental* *idealism*, the *most* *influential* *version* of the *view*. §3 examines the *absolute* *idealisms* — Bradley and McTaggart — and the *dissolution* of *relations*. §4 examines the *contemporary* *idealisms* — objective idealism, cosmopsychism, and the analytic revival. §5 examines *neutral* *monism* and *dual-aspect* *monism*, the *most* *defensible* *alternatives* to both *realism* and *idealism*. §6 examines the *coherence* of *mind-independent* *reality*, the *central* *issue* between the *two* *sides*. §7 asks the *hard-nosed* *question*: what would *settle* the dispute? §8 gives an honest verdict.

## 1. Berkeley’s Master Argument and the Veil of Perception

### 1.1 The thesis

Berkeley’s *immaterialism* is the *most* *radical* and *most* *systematic* *form* of *idealism* in the *Western* *tradition*. Its *thesis* is *compressed* into the *slogan*:

> ***Esse est percipi*** — to be is to be perceived.
>
> (*Principles*, §3)

**The *structure*.**

1. The *only* things we *perceive* are **ideas** (sensations, perceptions, mental contents).
2. An *idea* *cannot* exist *unperceived* (its *esse* is *percipi*).
3. The *material* *substance* is *posited* as the *support* of the *ideas*, but the *positing* is *unmotivated* and *unintelligible*.
4. Therefore there *is* no *material* *substance* — only *minds* and *their* *ideas*.

**The *positive* *alternative*.** The *continuity* of *unperceived* *objects* is *grounded* in the *perception* of *God*. When I *leave* the *room* and the *table* is *unperceived* by *me*, it is *still* *perceived* by *God* — and *therefore* it *continues* to *exist*. The *view* *preserves* the *common-sense* *belief* in the *continuity* of *objects*, and it *explains* the *regularity* of *experience* without *positing* *matter*.

**The *motive*.** Berkeley’s *target* is *not* *only* the *materialist*; it is also the *skeptic*. If we *perceive* only *ideas*, then (i) the *materialist* *cannot* *explain* how we *know* the *external* *world*, and (ii) the *skeptic* *wins*. Berkeley’s *idealism* *dissolves* the *problem* by *denying* that there is an *external* *world* *beyond* the *ideas*.

### 1.2 The master argument

The *master* *argument* is stated in the *Principles* (§22–23) and *developed* in the *Three* *Dialogues* (1713). It is *Berkeley’s* *central* *argument*, and it is *one* of the *most* *discussed* *arguments* in the *history* of *philosophy*.

**The *argument* (as Berkeley states it).**

> “If we can conceive it possible for any mixture or combination of qualities, or any sensible object whatever, to exist without the mind, then I will grant it actually to be so. … But this is impossible. … For what is the object but something that is perceived? … To conceive of a thing existing without the mind is to conceive of it as perceived; which is a contradiction.”

**The *reconstruction*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{To *conceive* of an object is to have an *idea* of it (Berkeley's *idealism* about conception).} \\
2. & \text{Every *idea* is *perceived* (the *esse* *est* *percipi* principle).} \\
3. & \text{To *conceive* of an *object* existing *unperceived* is to have an *idea* of an *object* that is *not* perceived.} \\
4. & \text{But the *idea* *is* perceived (by the *conceiver*), and therefore the *object* *is* perceived.} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{It is *impossible* to conceive of an *unperceived* object.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** The *very* *attempt* to *conceive* of a *mind-independent* *object* *refutes* *itself*: the *object* is *conceived*, and *therefore* it is *perceived*, and *therefore* it is *not* *mind-independent*.

**The *structure* of the *trick*.** The *argument* *trades* on an *equivocation* between (i) the *act* of *conceiving* (which is *mental*) and (ii) the *content* of the *conception* (which is *what* is *conceived*). The *act* is *perception*-like; the *content* *need* *not* be.

### 1.3 The objection: conception is not perception

The *standard* *objection*, due to *Moore* (“The Refutation of Idealism,” 1903), *Russell* (*Problems*, Ch. IV), and *every* *textbook* *since*, is that the *master* *argument* *confuses* the *act* with the *content*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{To *conceive* of an *object* is to *have* a *mental* *act* *directed* at the *object*.} \\
2. & \text{The *mental* *act* is *mine*, and it is *perceived* (by introspection).} \\
3. & \text{The *object* of the *act* is *what* the *act* is *about*; it is *not* the *act* itself.} \\
4. & \text{The *object* can be *unperceived* even though the *act* is *perceived*.} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The *master* *argument* is *invalid*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *analogy*.** I can *think* about *Julius* *Caesar* without *Caesar* being *present*; the *thought* is *mine*, but *Caesar* is *not*. In the *same* *way*, I can *conceive* of a *tree* *existing* *unperceived*: the *conceiving* is *mine*, but the *tree* *conceived* *need* *not* be *perceived*.

**Berkeley’s *reply*** (*Three* *Dialogues*, *Third* *Dialogue*). The *objection* *assumes* that *conceiving* is a *relation* *to* an *object* *independent* of the *conceiving*. But *Berkeley* *denies* the *assumption*: on his *view*, the *object* *just* *is* the *content* of the *conceiving*, and the *content* *cannot* be *separated* from the *act*. The *reply* *is* *circular* if the *question* is *precisely* whether the *object* can be *independent*; it *reasserts* the *thesis* rather than *arguing* for it.

### 1.4 The likeness principle

Berkeley’s *second* *argument* is the **likeness* *principle*** (*Principles*, §8).

**The *principle*.** “An *idea* can be *like* nothing but an *idea*.”

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{We *perceive* only *ideas*.} \\
2. & \text{If the *ideas* *resemble* *material* *objects*, the *resemblance* *must* be *between* an *idea* and a *material* *object*.} \\
3. & \text{But an *idea* can *resemble* only *another* *idea* (the *likeness* *principle*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *ideas* *cannot* *resemble* *material* *objects*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** The *representationalist* *view* — that *ideas* *represent* *material* *objects* *by* *resembling* them — is *incoherent*. The *view* *leaves* the *material* *object* *unrepresented*, and the *material* *object* *becomes* an *idle* *posit*.

**The *significance*.** The *likeness* *principle* is *Berkeley’s* *strongest* *argument*, and it is *the* *argument* that *anticipates* the *contemporary* *critique* of the *representationalist* *theory* of *perception* (Ch. 5, §4). If *perception* is *representation*, the *representation* *cannot* be *explained* by *resemblance* to a *non-representational* *object*; the *representation* is *constituted* by its *content*, and the *content* is *not* a *copy*.

### 1.5 The veil of perception

The *veil* of *perception* is the *epistemic* *consequence* of the *representationalist* *picture*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{We *perceive* only *ideas* (the *epistemic* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *ideas* *represent* *material* *objects* (the *representationalist* *premise*).} \\
3. & \text{The *representation* is *inferred* from the *ideas*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{We *cannot* *know* whether the *material* *objects* *exist*.}
\end{array}
$$

**Berkeley’s *response*.** The *veil* *dissolves* if we *deny* *premise* 2. The *ideas* *are* the *objects*; there is *no* *further* *object* *behind* them. The *world* *is* the *ideas*, and the *knowledge* of the *world* is *direct* (it is the *knowledge* of the *ideas*).

**The *cost*.** The *response* *dissolves* the *veil* by *dissolving* the *world*: the *material* *object* is *gone*, and the *common-sense* *belief* in *mind-independent* *objects* is *denied*. (Berkeley *argues* that the *belief* *survives*: the *objects* are *still* *real*, *public*, and *continuous* — they are *God’s* *ideas*, not ours. The *argument* *depends* on the *theological* *premise*, and the *premise* is *contested*.)

### 1.6 The assessment

**The *strengths*.**

1. **The *argument* *against* *material* *substance* is *powerful*.** The *notion* of a *substratum* *supporting* *ideas* is *unintelligible* (Ch. 19, §2.2), and Berkeley’s *critique* *anticipates* the *contemporary* *critique* of the *bare* *particular*.
2. **The *dissolution* of the *veil* is *elegant*.** The *idealist* *avoids* the *skeptical* *consequence* of *representationalism* by *denying* the *representationalist* *picture*.
3. **The *argument* is *phenomenologically* *grounded*.** The *starting* *point* is the *direct* *experience* of *ideas*, and the *experience* is *indubitable*.

**The *weaknesses*.**

1. **The *master* *argument* is *invalid*.** The *conception*/*perception* *confusion* is *decisive*.
2. **The *likeness* *principle* is *contested*.** The *contemporary* *representationalist* *denies* that *resemblance* is the *representation* *relation*; the *content* is *structural*, not *pictorial* (Ch. 5, §4).
3. **The *theological* *premise* is *contested*.** The *continuity* of *unperceived* *objects* *requires* *God*, and the *argument* *for* *God* is *not* *given*.
4. **The *common-sense* *objection*.** The *view* *denies* the *ordinary* *belief* in *mind-independent* *objects*, and the *denial* *is* *counterintuitive* (the *Moore* *objection*; Ch. 6, §6.1).

**The *verdict*.** Berkeley’s *idealism* is *not* *refuted*, but it is *not* *established*. It is a *coherent* *position* with *serious* *costs*. The *Russell* *treatment* of *idealism* (Ch. IV) is *unfair*: Russell *rejects* the *view* *without* *engaging* the *strongest* *arguments*, and the *rejection* is *rhetorical* *rather* than *philosophical*.

## 2. Kant’s Transcendental Idealism

### 2.1 The Copernican revolution

Kant’s *transcendental* *idealism* is the *most* *influential* *version* of *idealism* in the *modern* *tradition*, and it is *also* the *most* *misunderstood*.

**The *Copernican* *revolution*** (*Critique* *of* *Pure* *Reason*, Bxvi).

> “Hitherto it has been assumed that all our knowledge must conform to objects. … We must therefore make trial whether we may not have more success … if we suppose that objects must conform to our knowledge.”

**The *thesis*.** The *objects* of *experience* *conform* to the *forms* of *our* *cognition* — *space*, *time*, and the *categories* — and *therefore* we *can* have *a* *priori* *knowledge* of *them*.

**The *structure*.**

| Element | What it is | The *role* |
|---|---|---|
| **Forms of intuition** | Space and time | The *a* *priori* *forms* of *sensory* *experience* |
| **Categories** | Substance, causality, etc. | The *a* *priori* *concepts* of the *understanding* |
| **Phenomena** | The *world* as it *appears* | The *object* of *experience* |
| **Noumena** | The *world* as it is *in* *itself* | The *object* of *thought* (not *experience*) |

**The *consequence*.** The *world* of *experience* is *constructed* by the *mind*, and the *knowledge* of the *world* is *knowledge* of the *construction*. The *thing* *in* *itself* is *unknowable*.

### 2.2 Empirical realism within transcendental idealism

The *most* *important* *clarification* of *Kant’s* *view* is the *distinction* between *empirical* *realism* and *transcendental* *idealism*.

**The *thesis*.**

- **Transcendental* *idealism***: *space*, *time*, and the *categories* are *forms* of *our* *cognition*, not *features* of *things* *in* *themselves*.
- **Empirical* *realism***: *within* the *world* of *experience*, *objects* are *real*, *public*, *spatio-temporal*, and *causally* *ordered*.

**The *point*.** The *two* *theses* are *compatible*: the *world* of *experience* is *real* (it is *not* a *dream* or an *illusion*), and it is *dependent* on the *forms* of *cognition* (it is *not* the *world* *in* *itself*).

**The *structure*.**

```
            TRANSCENDENTAL IDEALISM
            (the forms of cognition)
                      |
                      v
            EMPIRICAL REALISM
            (the world of experience)
                      |
                      v
            THE THING IN ITSELF
            (unknowable)
```

**The *distinction* *from* *Berkeley*.** Kant *insists* on the *existence* of *things* *in* *themselves*; Berkeley *denies* *matter* *entirely*. Kant’s *view* is *empirically* *realist* and *transcendentally* *idealist*; Berkeley’s *view* is *empirically* and *transcendentally* *idealist*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *a* *priori* *knowledge* of *mathematics* and *physics* is *possible* (Kant's *datum*).} \\
2. & \text{The *knowledge* *requires* that *the* *objects* *conform* to *the* *forms* of *cognition*.} \\
3. & \text{The *forms* are *not* *derived* from *experience* (they are *a* *priori*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *objects* of *experience* are *constituted* by the *forms*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *explains* the *possibility* of *a* *priori* *knowledge* (Ch. 9, §3), and it *avoids* the *skeptical* *consequence* of the *veil* (empirical *realism* is *robust*).

### 2.3 The two worlds and the two aspects

**The *interpretive* *debate*. The *Critique* *can* be *read* in *two* *ways*.

| Reading | What it is | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-worlds** | Phenomena and noumena are *two* *distinct* *realms* | the *traditional* *reading* (Strawson 1966; Guyer 1987) |
| **Two-aspects** | Phenomena and noumena are *two* *ways* of *considering* the *same* *thing* | Allison (1983, 2004); the *contemporary* *consensus* |

**The *two-worlds* *reading***: the *phenomenal* *world* is a *world* of *appearances*, and the *noumenal* *world* is a *separate* *world* of *things* *in* *themselves*. The *reading* *raises* the *problem* of the *relation* between the *two* *worlds* (the *causal* *interaction*, the *correspondence*).

**The *two-aspects* *reading***: the *same* *thing* can be *considered* as an *appearance* (under the *forms* of *space*, *time*, and the *categories*) or as a *thing* *in* *itself* (apart from the *forms*). The *reading* *avoids* the *problem* of the *two* *worlds*, and it *makes* the *view* *more* *defensible*.

**The *cost*.** The *two-aspects* *reading* *is* *closer* to a *form* of *epistemic* *humility* than to a *metaphysical* *idealism*; the *view* *may* be *a* *form* of *realism* *about* the *things* *in* *themselves*, with a *restriction* on *our* *knowledge*. The *debate* is *unresolved*.

### 2.4 The neglected alternative

**The *objection*** (Feder & Meerbote 1992; the *“neglected* *alternative”*). Kant *argues* that *space* and *time* *must* be *forms* of *intuition* because *otherwise* *a* *priori* *knowledge* of *geometry* *would* be *impossible*. But the *argument* *assumes* that *a* *priori* *knowledge* *cannot* be *about* *the* *world* *in* *itself* — and *that* is *precisely* *what* the *realist* *denies*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Kant: the *a* *priori* *knowledge* of *geometry* *requires* that *space* be a *form* of *intuition*.} \\
2. & \text{The *alternative*: *space* is a *feature* of *the* *world* *in* *itself*, and the *a* *priori* *knowledge* *tracks* that *feature*.} \\
3. & \text{Kant *does* not *refute* the *alternative*; he *assumes* it is *impossible*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *argument* is *incomplete*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *neglected* *alternative* is *a* *form* of *realism* *about* *space* and *time* *combined* with *a* *priori* *knowledge*. If it is *viable*, Kant’s *transcendental* *idealism* is *not* *established*.

**Kant’s *reply*** (in the *Critique*, and in the *Prolegomena*). The *alternative* *makes* the *a* *priori* *knowledge* *contingent* on a *feature* of the *world*, and the *contingency* *undermines* the *necessity* of the *a* *priori*. The *reply* *begs* the *question* against the *alternative*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*.

### 2.5 The assessment

**The *strengths*.**

1. **The *view* *explains* the *possibility* of *a* *priori* *knowledge*, and it *fits* the *scientific* *worldview*.
2. **The *empirical* *realism* is *robust***: the *view* *avoids* the *skeptical* *consequences* of the *veil*.
3. **The *view* is *compatible* with *physicalism*** (the *phenomenal* *world* is the *physical* *world* *as* *it* *appears*, and the *thing* *in* *itself* is *not* *a* *further* *substance*).

**The *weaknesses*.**

1. **The *two-worlds* *reading* is *unstable***: the *relation* between the *phenomenal* and *noumenal* *worlds* is *mysterious*.
2. **The *neglected* *alternative* is *serious***: the *argument* *does* not *establish* the *transcendental* *idealism*.
3. **The *thing* *in* *itself* is *a* *mystery***: the *view* *posits* an *entity* *whose* *nature* is *unknowable*, and the *positing* is *unmotivated* except by the *demand* *for* a *ground*.

**The *verdict*.** Kant’s *view* is *the* *most* *sophisticated* *form* of *idealism*, and it is *not* *refuted*. The *two-aspects* *reading* is the *most* *defensible* *interpretation*, and it *makes* the *view* *a* *form* of *humility* rather than a *form* of *mind-dependence*.

## 3. Absolute Idealism and the Dissolution of Relations

### 3.1 Bradley's regress

The *most* *radical* *form* of *idealism* is the *absolute* *idealism* of the *British* *Hegelians* — F. H. Bradley and J. M. E. McTaggart. Their *central* *argument* is the **dissolution* *of* *relations***.

**The *argument* (Bradley, *Appearance and Reality*, 1893, Ch. II).**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If a relation } R \text{ relates two terms } a \text{ and } b, \text{ then } R \text{ must be *related* to } a \text{ and } b.} \\
2. & \text{The *relation* between } R \text{ and } a \text{ is itself a *further* *relation} (R'). \\
3. & \text{The *relation* between } R' \text{ and } R \text{ is a *further* *relation} (R''). \\
4. & \text{The *regress* is *infinite*.} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{Relations are *unreal* (they are *appearance*, not *reality*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *conclusion*.** Reality is *one* — the **Absolute** — and the *multiplicity* of *things* and *relations* is an *appearance*. The *Absolute* is a *single*, *undifferentiated* *whole*, and the *distinctions* *we* *draw* (between *subjects* and *objects*, *causes* and *effects*, *parts* and *wholes*) are *not* *real*.

**The *argument* *for* the *Absolute***. The *regress* *shows* that *relations* are *contradictory*, and *therefore* *unreal*. If *relations* are *unreal*, the *world* *cannot* be *a* *system* of *related* *things*; it *must* be a *single* *undifferentiated* *whole*. The *whole* is the *Absolute*.

### 3.2 The critique of the regress

**The *objection*** (Russell, “The Monistic Theory of Truth,” 1906; Moore, “The Refutation of Idealism,” 1903). The *regress* *is* *vicious* *only* if the *relation* is *itself* a *term* that *needs* to be *related* to the *other* *terms*. But the *relation* is *not* a *term*; it is the *form* of *connection*. The *regress* *assumes* that *every* *connection* *requires* a *further* *connection*, and the *assumption* is *the* *thesis* *to* be *proved*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *regress* *assumes* that *the* *relation* } R \text{ is a *term* *alongside* } a \text{ and } b. \\
2. & \text{But } R \text{ is the *relating* relation; it is *not* a *term* in the *same* *sense*.} \\
3. & \text{The *regress* is *avoided* if the *relation* is *primitive*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *regress* is *not* *decisive*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (Bradley’s). The *relation* *is* a *term* in *some* *sense* — it is *something* — and *therefore* it *needs* to be *related*. The *reply* *rests* on the *assumption* that *everything* *is* a *term*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *anti-idealist* *denies*.

**The *self-undermining* *objection***. The *argument* *uses* *relations* (the *relation* *between* *premises*, the *relation* *between* *the* *argument* and the *conclusion), and *therefore* the *argument* *undermines* *itself*. (The *reply*: the *argument* is *not* a *description* of *reality*; it is a *ladder* that is *thrown* *away* *once* *climbed*. The *reply* is the *Wittgensteinian* *response*, and it is *contested*.)

### 3.3 McTaggart: time and the Absolute

**The *view***. McTaggart’s *idealist* *system* (*The Nature of Existence*, 1921–27) *combines* the *unreality* *of* *time* (Ch. 17, §2) with the *unreality* *of* *matter* and the *reality* of *selves*.

**The *structure*.**

1. **The *unreality* of *time***. McTaggart’s *paradox* (Ch. 17, §2.1) *shows* that the *A-series* is *contradictory*, and *therefore* *unreal*.
2. **The *unreality* of *matter***. Matter is *divisible*, and the *division* leads to an *infinite* *regress*; *therefore* matter is *unreal*.
3. **The *reality* of *selves***. Selves are *not* *divisible*; they are *real*, and they are *related* by *love*.

**The *conclusion*.** Reality is a *community* of *immortal* *selves*, *related* by *love*. The *view* is a *personal* *idealism*, and it is *one* of the *most* *striking* *metaphysical* *systems* in the *history* of *philosophy*.

**The *assessment*.** The *view* is *a* *curiosity*, and it is *not* *widely* *defended*. Its *strength* is the *boldness* of the *system*; its *weakness* is the *implausibility* of the *premises* (the *unreality* *of* *time*, the *community* of *immortal* *selves*).

### 3.4 The absolute idealist legacy

**The *decline*.** Absolute idealism was *the* *dominant* *philosophy* in *Britain* and *America* in the *late* *nineteenth* *century*, and it was *swept* *away* by the *revolt* of *Moore* and *Russell* (and, in a *different* *register*, by *Wittgenstein*).

**The *reasons* *for* the *decline*.**

1. **The *revolt* *against* *the* *Absolute***. Moore and Russell *rejected* the *monism* and the *coherence* *theory* of *truth* (Ch. 32, §3), and they *replaced* them with *pluralism* and *correspondence*.
2. **The *rise* of *analysis***. The *analytic* *method* (Ch. 3, §3) *replaced* the *speculative* *method* of the *idealists*.
3. **The *political* *association***. Absolute idealism was *associated* with *German* *idealism* and, in *Britain*, with *conservatism*; the *analytic* *tradition* *positioned* *itself* as *empiricist* and *liberal*.

**The *legacy*.** The *absolute* *idealists* *posed* *questions* that the *analytic* *tradition* *still* *has* *not* *answered*: the *reality* of *relations*, the *nature* of *the* *Absolute*, the *relation* between *appearance* and *reality*. The *questions* *survive*, and the *contemporary* *revival* of *metaphysics* (Ch. 22) is, in *part*, a *return* to them.

## 4. Contemporary Idealisms

### 4.1 Objective idealism

**The *thesis*.** Reality is *mind-like* — it is *constituted* by *experience* or *consciousness* — but it is *not* the *experience* of a *single* *person*; it is *objective* *mind*.

**The *statement*.** Schelling (*System of Transcendental Idealism*, 1800); Hegel (*Phenomenology of Spirit*, 1807); Peirce (*Collected Papers*, 1931–58, the *“objective* *idealism”* *of* the *categories*); Sprigge (*The Vindication of Absolute Idealism*, 1983); Foster (*The Case for Idealism*, 1982).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *not* *given* by *physics* (Ch. 19, §1.5).} \\
2. & \text{The *only* *intrinsic* *nature* we *know* is *experience*.} \\
3. & \text{The *best* *explanation* of the *intrinsic* *nature* is that it is *experience*-like.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Reality is *objective* *mind*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *fills* the *gap* left by *structural* *realism*, and it *avoids* the *theological* *premise* of *Berkeley’s* *view*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *presupposes* that the *intrinsic* *nature* *must* be *like* *experience*, and the *presupposition* is *anthropomorphic*. (The *reply*: the *alternative* is *not* *a* *description*; it is an *admission* of *ignorance* — the *“Ramseyan* *humility”* — and the *idealist* *offers* a *description*.)

### 4.2 Cosmopsychism

**The *thesis*.** The *fundamental* *entity* is the *cosmos* — a *single* *subject* of *experience* — and the *individual* *minds* are *differentiations* *within* the *cosmic* *consciousness*.

**The *statement*.** Goff (*Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 2017), Shani (*Cosmopsychism*, 2015), Albahari (*Analytical Buddhism*, 2006), Itay Shani and others.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *universe* is a *single*, *unified* *whole* (the *structuralist* *insight*; Ch. 15, §7).} \\
2. & \text{The *whole* *has* *properties* that the *parts* *do* not *have* (the *cosmic* *consciousness*).} \\
3. & \text{The *properties* are *fundamental*, and the *parts* are *derived*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *cosmos* is the *fundamental* *experiencer*, and the *individual* *minds* are *differentiations*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *combination* *problem* (Ch. 19, §7.2) by *reversing* it: the *question* is *how* the *cosmic* *consciousness* *differentiates* into *individual* *minds*, and the *differentiation* is *derived* *from* the *whole*.

**The *objection*.** The *differentiation* *problem* is *as* *mysterious* as the *combination* *problem* (Ch. 19, §7.2). And the *view* *faces* the *“one* *subject*” *problem*: if there is *only* *one* *subject*, the *individual* *minds* are *not* *real* *subjects*, and the *view* *collapses* *into* a *form* of *monism* that *denies* the *reality* of *individual* *experience*.

### 4.3 The analytic revival

**The *phenomenon*.** The *last* *two* *decades* have *seen* a *revival* of *idealism* in the *analytic* *tradition*. The *revival* is *driven* by:

1. **The *hard* *problem*** (Ch. 24, §2): the *failure* of *physicalist* *accounts* to *explain* *consciousness*.
2. **The *structural* *realist* *conclusion*** (Ch. 19, §1.5): physics *describes* *structure*, not *intrinsic* *nature*.
3. **The *combination* *problem*** (Ch. 19, §7.2): the *panpsychist* *account* *relocates* the *mystery*.

**The *figures*.** Foster (*The Case for Idealism*, 1982; *A World for Us*, 2008); Chalmers (*The Conscious Mind*, 1996, Ch. 8 — *not* an *idealist*, but *sympathetic* to the *“it* *from* *bit”* *view*); Kastrup (*Why Materialism Is Baloney*, 2014; *The Idea of the World*, 2019); Adams (*“Idealism* *Vindicated”*, 2007).

**The *arguments*.**

**(i) The *intrinsic* *nature* *argument*** (Foster 1982, Ch. 4; Kastrup 2019, Ch. 4). The *physical* *world* *has* an *intrinsic* *nature* *beyond* its *structure*, and the *only* *intrinsic* *nature* we *know* is *experience*; therefore the *intrinsic* *nature* is *experience*.

**(ii) The *“it* *from* *bit”* *argument***. The *universe* is *fundamentally* *informational*, and *information* *requires* a *mind* to *interpret* it. The *view* is *idealist* in *spirit*, and it is *associated* with *Wheeler* (1989), *Chalmers* (1996), and *Kastrup* (2019).

**(iii) The *Occam’s* *razor* *argument*** (Kastrup 2019, Ch. 4). Idealism *posits* *only* *mind*, while *physicalism* *posits* *mind* *and* *matter*; the *idealist* *ontology* is *simpler*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *anthropomorphism* *objection***. The *argument* *presupposes* that the *intrinsic* *nature* must be *like* *experience*, and the *presupposition* is *unjustified*.

**(ii) The *parsimony* *objection***. The *idealist* *posits* *God* (Berkeley) or a *cosmic* *mind* (cosmopsychism), and the *positing* may be *more* *profligate* than the *physicalist* *ontology*.

**(iii) The *empirical* *objection***. Idealism and physicalism are *empirically* *equivalent* (they *make* the *same* *predictions*), and the *choice* *between* them is *underdetermined* by *evidence*.

**(iv) The *common-sense* *objection***. The *view* *denies* the *ordinary* *belief* in a *mind-independent* *world*, and the *denial* is *counterintuitive*.

### 4.4 Yogācāra and the comparative perspective

**The *tradition*.** The *Yogācāra* (*“consciousness-only”*) *school* of *Indian* *Buddhism* — *Maitreya*, *Asaṅga*, *Vasubandhu* (4th c. CE) — developed a *systematic* *idealism* that *anticipates* the *contemporary* *arguments*.

**The *thesis*.** The *external* *world* is *not* *independent* of *consciousness*; the *apparent* *objects* are *constructions* of the *mind* (*vijñapti*).

**The *arguments*.**

1. **The *dream* *argument***. The *objects* of *dreams* are *not* *external*, yet they *appear* *as* *external*; the *waking* *case* is *structurally* *similar*.
2. **The *momentariness* *argument***. The *objects* are *momentary*; the *continuity* is a *construction* of the *mind*.
3. **The *karma* *argument***. The *regularity* of *experience* *requires* a *ground*; the *ground* is the *storehouse* *consciousness* (*ālayavijñāna*), not *external* *matter*.

**The *significance*.** The *comparative* *perspective* *shows* that idealism is *not* a *Western* *curiosity*; it is a *systematic* *option* that *has* been *developed* in *detail* in other *traditions*. The *contemporary* *revival* *would* *benefit* from *engaging* the *tradition*, and the *engagement* is *beginning* (Albahari 2006; Thompson 2020).

## 5. Neutral Monism and Dual-Aspect Monism

### 5.1 The neutral monist thesis

**The *thesis*.** The *fundamental* *entities* are *neither* *mental* *nor* *physical*; the *mental* and the *physical* are *constructions* (or *aspects*) of a *neutral* *stuff*.

**The *statement*.** Mach (*The Analysis of Sensations*, 1886); James (*Essays in Radical Empiricism*, 1912); Russell (*The Analysis of Mind*, 1921; *The Analysis of Matter*, 1927); the *contemporary* *defenders*: Banks (*The Realistic Empiricism of Mach, James, and Russell*, 2014); Stubenberg (*Consciousness and Qualia*, 1998).

**The *structure*.**

$$
\text{Neutral stuff} \ \rightarrow \ \begin{cases} \text{Mental constructions (the subject)} \\ \text{Physical constructions (the object)} \end{cases}
$$

**Russell’s *version***. The *stuff* is *“sensibilia”* — *actual* and *possible* *sensations* — and the *physical* *objects* are *constructions* out of *sensibilia* *plus* the *laws* of *perspective*. The *mental* *objects* are *constructions* out of the *same* *stuff* *plus* the *laws* of *association* and *introspection*.

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *dualism* of *mind* and *matter*, and it *explains* the *intimate* *connection* between *experience* and the *world* (the *world* *is* *constructed* out of *experience*-like *stuff*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *“neutral* *stuff”* *is* *a* *mystery***. What *is* the *neutral* *stuff*? If it is *defined* *negatively* (neither mental nor physical), the *definition* is *uninformative*. If it is *defined* *positively* (as *sensibilia*), it is *mental* after all, and the *view* *collapses* into *idealism*.

**(ii) The *construction* *problem***. How do the *constructions* *arise* from the *neutral* *stuff*? The *view* *asserts* that they *do*, but it *does* not *explain* the *construction*.

**(iii) The *causal* *problem***. The *neutral* *stuff* is supposed to *ground* both the *mental* and the *physical*; the *causal* *relation* between the *stuff* and the *constructions* is *mysterious*.

### 5.2 Dual-aspect monism

**The *thesis*.** There is a *single* *substance* with *two* *aspects* — the *mental* and the *physical*. The *aspects* are *not* *reducible* to one another, and the *substance* is *neither* mental *nor* physical *exclusively*.

**The *statement*.** Spinoza (*Ethics*, 1677: *Deus* *sive* *Natura* has *two* *attributes*, *thought* and *extension*); the *contemporary* *defenders*: Nagel (1979, 1986), Strawson (2006), Velmans (*Understanding Consciousness*, 2009).

**The *structure*.**

$$
\text{Substance} \ \rightarrow \ \begin{cases} \text{Mental aspect (thought)} \\ \text{Physical aspect (extension)} \end{cases}
$$

**Spinoza’s *version***. There is *one* *substance* (*Deus* *sive* *Natura*), and it has *infinitely* many *attributes*; *we* *know* *two* of them — *thought* and *extension*. The *mental* and the *physical* are *two* *descriptions* of the *same* *events*.

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *interaction* *problem* (the *mental* and the *physical* are *not* *distinct* *substances*), and it *explains* the *correlation* between *mental* and *physical* *events*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *aspect* *problem***. What *is* an *aspect*? If it is a *way* *of* *describing* the *substance*, the *view* is *a* *form* of *neutral* *monism* or *a* *form* of *epistemic* *dualism*. If it is a *real* *feature*, the *view* is *a* *form* of *property* *dualism* (Ch. 23, §7).

**(ii) The *combination* *problem***. How do the *two* *aspects* *relate*? If they are *not* *causally* *connected*, the *correlation* is *mysterious*. If they *are*, the *view* *reintroduces* the *interaction* *problem*.

**(iii) The *parsimony* *objection***. The *view* *posits* *two* *aspects*, and the *positing* is *not* *economical*.

### 5.3 The neutral monist strategy

**The *strategy*.** The *neutral* *monist* *avoids* the *mind-body* *problem* by *dissolving* the *dualism*. The *mental* and the *physical* are *not* *two* *kinds* of *thing*; they are *two* *ways* of *organizing* the *same* *stuff*.

**The *strength*.** The *strategy* *fits* the *Russellian* *monist* *insight* (Ch. 19, §7.1): the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *not* *physical* *structure*, and the *neutral* *stuff* *is* the *intrinsic* *nature*. The *neutral* *monist* *offers* a *positive* *account* of the *intrinsic* *nature*: it is *neither* mental *nor* physical.

**The *weakness*.** The *positive* *account* is *vague*: the *neutral* *stuff* *is* *defined* *negatively*, and the *vagueness* is *the* *cost* of the *strategy*.

### 5.4 The assessment

**The *comparative* *table*.**

| View | Fundamental | Mental | Physical | Main problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Physicalism** | Physical | Derived | Fundamental | Hard problem |
| **Idealism** | Mental | Fundamental | Derived | Anthropomorphism |
| **Panpsychism** | Proto-mental | Derived | Derived | Combination |
| **Neutral monism** | Neutral | Construction | Construction | The neutral stuff |
| **Dual-aspect monism** | Substance | Aspect | Aspect | The aspects |

**The *verdict*.** The *mind-first* *options* — idealism, panpsychism, neutral monism, dual-aspect monism — are *all* *live*, and *all* *costly*. The *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to *parsimony*, *explanatory* power, and *intuition* (Ch. 4, §3). The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *form* of *Russellian* *monism* (the *intrinsic* *nature* is *proto-experiential*), *combined* with a *structural* *realist* *account* of the *physical* (Ch. 19, §6.3). The *combination* is *promising* and *incomplete*.

## 6. The Coherence of Mind-Independent Reality

### 6.1 The question

**The *question*.** Can we *coherently* *conceive* of a *mind-independent* *reality*?

**The *idealist* *argument*.** Any *conception* of a *mind-independent* *reality* is *our* *conception*: the *act* of *conceiving* is *mental*, and the *content* is *conditioned* by the *act*. The *“independence”* is *always* *relative* to a *representational* *act*, and the *absolute* *independence* is *inconceivable*.

**The *realist* *reply*.** The *argument* *confuses* the *act* of *conceiving* with the *content* of the *conception*. We can *represent* a *world* that no one *represents*; the *representation* is *ours*, but the *content* is *not*.

### 6.2 The structure of the argument

**The *idealist* *argument* *stated*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{To *conceive* of } X \text{ is to *represent* } X. \\
2. & \text{The *representation* is *mental*.} \\
3. & \text{If } X \text{ is *represented*, } X \text{ is *related* to a *mind*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Every *conceived* } X \text{ is *mind-related*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *realist* *reply*.** The *argument* *proves* that every *conceived* *X* is *related* to a *mind* — but the *relation* is *epistemic* (the *mind* *represents* *X*), not *constitutive* (the *mind* *creates* *X*). The *conclusion* *confuses* the *vehicle* of *representation* with the *object* of *representation*.

**The *structure* of the *reply*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *act* of *representing* is mental.} \\
2. & \text{The *content* is *what* is represented; it is *not* the *act*.} \\
3. & \text{The *content* can be a *world* that is *not* *represented* by *any* *mind*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *argument* does not establish *idealism*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection* to the *reply*.** The *reply* *assumes* that the *content* can be *separated* from the *act*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *idealist* *denies* (the *“no* *content* *without* *act”* *thesis*). The *debate* *is* *unresolved*.

### 6.3 The argument from consciousness

**The *argument*.** The *hard* *problem* of *consciousness* (Ch. 24, §2) is the *strongest* *argument* for *idealism*: the *physicalist* *accounts* *cannot* *explain* *why* *there* is *something* *it* *is* *like* to *be* a *subject*, and the *idealist* *accounts* *make* *consciousness* *fundamental*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *hard* *problem* is *unsolved* by *physicalism*.} \\
2. & \text{Idealism *makes* *consciousness* *fundamental*, and the *hard* *problem* *dissolves*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Idealism is the *best* *explanation* of the *hard* *problem*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *assumes* that the *hard* *problem* *requires* a *fundamental* *place* for *consciousness*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *physicalist* *denies* (the *“illusionist”* *and* *“mysterian”* *responses*; Ch. 24, §5). The *debate* is *unresolved*.

### 6.4 The no-miracles argument for idealism

**The *argument*.** If the *world* is *mind*, the *“fit”* between *mind* and *world* is *not* *miraculous*. If the *world* is *mind-independent*, the *fit* is a *brute* *fact*, and the *bruteness* is *unexplained*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Our *cognitive* *faculties* *fit* the *world* (we *can* *know* it).} \\
2. & \text{The *fit* is *not* *miraculous* under *idealism* (the *world* is *mind}).} \\
3. & \text{The *fit* *is* *miraculous* under *realism* (the *world* is *mind-independent*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Idealism is the *better* *explanation*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *realist* *can* *explain* the *fit* by *evolution*: the *faculties* were *selected* for *tracking* the *world*, and the *tracking* is *not* *miraculous*. (The *reply*: the *evolutionary* *explanation* *presupposes* the *causal* *closure* of the *world*, which *idealism* *denies*. The *reply* is *contested*.)

### 6.5 The state of the debate

The *coherence* of *mind-independent* *reality* is *the* *central* *issue* between *realism* and *idealism*, and it is *unresolved*. The *realist* *has* a *coherent* *account* of the *conception*; the *idealist* *has* a *powerful* *argument* from the *conditions* of *representation*; the *debate* *turns* on *whether* the *content* can be *separated* from the *act*.

## 7. What Would Settle the Dispute?

### 7.1 The empirical equivalence

**The *problem*.** Idealism and *realism* are *empirically* *equivalent*: they *make* the *same* *predictions* about *observable* *experience*. The *choice* *between* them *cannot* be *settled* by *experiment*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *observable* *data* are *the* *same* under *idealism* and *realism*.} \\
2. & \text{The *choice* between *theories* that are *empirically* *equivalent* *cannot* be settled by *evidence*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *debate* is *metaphysical*, not *empirical*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *debate* *cannot* be *settled* by the *methods* of *science*; it *requires* *philosophical* *argument*.

### 7.2 The options

**The *options***.

| Option | The *claim* | The *cost* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *metaphysical* *option** | The *debate* is *substantive*, and the *choice* is made by *theoretical* *virtues* | The *virtues* are *contested* (Ch. 4, §3) |
| **The *deflationary* *option** | The *debate* is *verbal* (the *two* *views* *describe* the *same* *facts* *differently*) | The *deflation* *undermines* the *realism/anti-realism* *debate* (Ch. 22) |
| **The *phenomenological* *option** | The *debate* is *settled* by *first-person* *data* | The *first-person* *data* are *not* *intersubjectively* *checkable* |
| **The *pragmatic* *option** | The *debate* is *settled* by *practical* *consequences* | The *consequences* are *the* *same* |

**The *position* of this book.** The *metaphysical* *option* is the *most* *defensible*: the *debate* is *substantive*, and the *choice* is made by *IBE* (Ch. 10, §6), with the *criteria* of *Ch. 4, §3*. The *deflationary* *option* is *too* *strong* (it *undermines* the *distinction* between *the* *views*), and the *phenomenological* *option* is *too* *weak* (it *does* not *provide* *intersubjective* *evidence*).

### 7.3 The criteria

**The *criteria* for *choosing* between *idealism* and *realism***.

| Criterion | Idealism | Realism |
|---|---|---|
| **Consistency** | High (no *external* *world* to *reconcile*) | High (the *world* is *independent*) |
| **Fertility** | Medium (the *revival* is *new*) | High (the *scientific* *tradition*) |
| **Simplicity** | High (one *kind* of *stuff*) | Low (two *kinds*: *mind* and *matter*) |
| **Conservatism** | Low (denies *common* *sense*) | High (fits *common* *sense*) |
| **Explanatory power** | High (the *hard* *problem*) | High (the *scientific* *worldview*) |
| **Adequacy to ordinary thought** | Low (denies *independent* *objects*) | High (fits *ordinary* *belief*) |

**The *weighting*.** The *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to *simplicity* (idealism *wins*) vs. *conservatism* and *adequacy* (realism *wins*). The *book* *does* not *settle* the *choice*; it *lays* *out* the *criteria* and *invites* the *reader* to *make* the *judgment*.

### 7.4 The *hard-nosed* *question*

**The *question*.** Is there *any* *observation* that would *count* for or against *idealism*?

**The *answer*.** There are *three* *candidates*.

**(i) The *first-person* *data***. The *phenomenology* of *experience* is *more* *intimate* with *idealism* than *realism*. If the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *experience*, the *first-person* *data* are *directly* *relevant*. The *candidate* is *contested*, because the *first-person* *data* are *not* *intersubjectively* *checkable*.

**(ii) The *quantum* *measurement* *problem***. The *consciousness-causes-collapse* *interpretation* (Wigner 1961) *predicts* a *difference* between *observed* and *unobserved* *systems*, and the *difference* is *testable* (in *principle*). The *interpretation* is *contested*, and the *experiments* are *not* *conclusive* (Ch. 19, §5).

**(iii) The *fine-tuning* *data***. If the *universe* is *fine-tuned* for *life* (Ch. 50, §2), the *fine-tuning* *can* be *explained* by *idealism* (the *universe* is *mind-like*). The *candidate* is *contested*, and the *fine-tuning* *argument* is *controversial*.

**The *conclusion*.** The *dispute* is *not* *empirically* *settled*, and the *candidates* for *empirical* *evidence* are *contested*. The *honest* *position* is that the *dispute* is *metaphysical*, and that the *choice* is made by *philosophical* *argument*, not by *experiment*.

### 7.5 The *prospects*

**The *state* of the debate.** The *mind-first* *options* are *live*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *prospects* for *settlement* are *dim*, because the *options* are *empirically* *equivalent* and the *theoretical* *virtues* *conflict*.

**The *significance*.** The *debate* is *not* *a* *curiosity*; it is *the* *deepest* *question* in *metaphysics*, and it is *connected* to *every* *other* *question* in the *book*: the *nature* of *knowledge* (Ch. 6), the *nature* of *mind* (Ch. 23), the *nature* of *matter* (Ch. 19), and the *nature* of *value* (Ch. 44).

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **Berkeley's master argument is invalid.** The *conception*/*perception* *confusion* is *decisive*, and the *argument* does *not* *establish* *immaterialism*.
- **The likeness principle is serious.** The *argument* *anticipates* the *contemporary* *critique* of *representationalism*, and it is *not* *refuted*.
- **Kant's transcendental idealism is the most sophisticated form of idealism.** The *two-aspects* *reading* is the *most* *defensible*, and the *neglected* *alternative* is a *standing* *problem*.
- **Absolute idealism is dead as a research program.** The *revolt* of *Moore* and *Russell* was *decisive*, and the *view* is *not* *widely* *defended*.
- **The contemporary revival is real.** Analytic idealism (Foster, Kastrup), cosmopsychism (Goff, Shani), and the *comparative* *studies* (Albahari, Thompson) are *active* *research* *programs*.
- **Neutral monism and dual-aspect monism are live alternatives.** The *views* *avoid* the *dualism* of *mind* and *matter*, but they *face* the *mystery* of the *neutral* *stuff* and the *aspects*.
- **The debate is empirically equivalent.** The *choice* between *idealism* and *realism* is *not* *settled* by *experiment*, and the *theoretical* *virtues* *conflict*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of the *intrinsic*.** Is it *experience*, *proto-experience*, *neutral* *stuff*, or *unknowable*?
- **The *coherence* of *mind-independent* *reality*.** Can we *conceive* of a *world* that *no* *mind* *represents*?
- **The *relation* between *idealism* and *panpsychism*.** Are they *distinct* *positions*, or *varieties* of a *single* *view*?
- **The *empirical* *evidence*.** Is there *any* *observation* that *distinguishes* *idealism* from *realism*?
- **The *status* of the *deflationary* *option*.** Is the *debate* *substantive* or *verbal*?
- **The *ethics* of *idealism*.** Does *idealism* have *ethical* *consequences* (the *value* of *nature*, the *relation* to the *world*)?

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a *case* *study* in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *act* and the *content* of *conception*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *unperceived* *tree*, the *dream*, the *brain* in a *vat* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *logical* *structure* of the *master* *argument*, the *Bradley* *regress* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *idealism* is a *field* in *transition*. The *classical* *versions* are *untenable*; the *contemporary* *versions* are *promising* but *incomplete*; the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled answer*, but a *sharper understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Berkeley** | *Esse est percipi* | Direct; dissolves the veil | Master argument invalid |
| **Kant** | Transcendental idealism | Explains a priori knowledge | Neglected alternative |
| **Bradley** | Relations are unreal; the Absolute is one | Bold; systematic | Regress is contestable |
| **McTaggart** | Reality is a community of selves | Personal; systematic | Implausible premises |
| **Objective idealism** | Reality is mind-like | Fills the structuralist gap | Anthropomorphism |
| **Cosmopsychism** | The cosmos is one subject | Avoids the combination problem | Differentiation problem |
| **Analytic idealism** | The world is a mental construction | Parsimonious; solves the hard problem | Empirical equivalence |
| **Neutral monism** | The fundamental is neither mental nor physical | Avoids dualism | The neutral stuff is a mystery |
| **Dual-aspect monism** | One substance, two aspects | Explains the correlation | The aspects are mysterious |
| **Realism** | The world is mind-independent | Common sense; science | The hard problem |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *quantum* *measurement* *problem* and *consciousness*.** The *consciousness-causes-collapse* *view* (Wigner 1961; Stapp 2007) *makes* *consciousness* *causally* *efficacious* in the *measurement* *process*. The *view* is *one* of the *strongest* *empirical* *arguments* for *idealism*, and it is *contested* (the *decoherence* *program* *explains* the *appearance* of *collapse* without *consciousness*; Ch. 19, §5).

**2. The *hard* *problem* and *panprotopsychism*.** The *panprotopsychist* *view* (Chalmers 2015) *holds* that the *fundamental* *entities* have *proto-experiential* *properties*, and the *view* is *one* of the *most* *promising* *alternatives* to *idealism* and *physicalism*. The *debate* is *connected* to the *combination* *problem* (Ch. 19, §7.2) and to the *theory* of *mind* (Ch. 24).

**3. The *ethics* of *idealism*.** If the *world* is *mind*, the *relation* between *human* *beings* and *nature* is *different*: nature is *not* *inert* *matter*, and the *ethical* *relation* is *not* *domination* but *dialogue* (the *ecological* *view*; Ch. 51, §6). The *ethics* of *idealism* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent*.

**4. Non-Western *idealism*.** The *Yogācāra* *tradition* (§4.4) is the *most* *developed* *non-Western* *idealism*, and it is *one* of the *oldest*. The *Advaita* *Vedānta* *tradition* (Śaṅkara, 8th c. CE) developed a *radical* *monism* (the *world* is *Brahman*, and the *individual* *self* is *Brahman*), and the *view* *anticipates* the *contemporary* *cosmopsychism*. The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives*, not *curiosities* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *idealism* and *artificial* *intelligence*.** If the *world* is *mental*, the *AI* *systems* are *constructions* of *mind*, not *independent* *agents*. The *implications* for the *ethics* of *AI* (Ch. 29, §6) are *profound*, and they are *beginning* to be *explored*.

## Summary

- **Berkeley's idealism** is the *most* *radical* *form* of the *view*. The *master* *argument* is *invalid* (the *conception*/*perception* *confusion*), but the *likeness* *principle* and the *critique* of *material* *substance* are *serious*. The *view* *dissolves* the *veil* *of* *perception* by *denying* the *world* *behind* it.
- **Kant's transcendental idealism** is the *most* *sophisticated* *form*. The *view* *combines* *transcendental* *idealism* with *empirical* *realism*, and the *two-aspects* *reading* is the *most* *defensible*. The *neglected* *alternative* is a *standing* *problem*.
- **Absolute idealism** (Bradley, McTaggart) *dissolves* *relations* and *posits* the *Absolute*. The *Bradley* *regress* is *contestable*, and the *view* is *dead* as a *research* *program*.
- **The contemporary revival** — objective idealism, cosmopsychism, analytic idealism — is *driven* by the *hard* *problem*, the *structural* *realist* *conclusion*, and the *combination* *problem*. The *views* are *promising* and *contested*.
- **Neutral monism** (Mach, James, Russell) and **dual-aspect monism** (Spinoza, Nagel) *avoid* the *dualism* of *mind* and *matter*. The *views* *face* the *mystery* of the *neutral* *stuff* and the *aspects*.
- **The coherence of mind-independent reality** is the *central* *issue*. The *idealist* *argument* from the *conditions* of *representation* is *powerful*, and the *realist* *reply* (the *act*/*content* *distinction*) is *contested*.
- **The dispute is not empirically settled.** The *options* are *empirically* *equivalent*, and the *choice* is made by *theoretical* *virtues* (Ch. 4, §3). The *metaphysical* *option* is the *most* *defensible*; the *deflationary* *option* is *too* *strong*.
- **The *mind-first* *options* are *live***. They are *not* *curiosities*; they are *systematic* *alternatives* to *physicalism*, and they *deserve* the *hearing* that *Russell* *denied* them.
