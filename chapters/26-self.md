# Chapter 26 — The Self and Personal Identity

> “There is no such thing as the self. … The self is a bundle or collection of different perceptions, which succeed each other with an inconceivable rapidity, and are in a perpetual flux and movement.”
> — David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–40), I.iv.6

> “Personal identity is not what matters. … What matters is psychological connectedness and/or continuity.”
> — Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Part III, in effect

> “I am not the same person I was ten years ago” is *either* a truism *or* a metaphysical thesis, and the fact that it can be *both* is the deepest thing about the problem
> — a formulation of the problem, after Ricoeur

---

Chapter 25 examined the *content* of thought. This chapter examines the *subject* who *has* the thought.

The question is deceptively simple: **what am I?** The *answer* is not obvious, and the *difficulty* of the question is one of the oldest *data* of philosophy. We *say* that we are the *same* person over time; we *act* on the assumption that we are; we *hold others responsible* on the assumption that they are the same persons who acted; and yet, when we *look* for the *self*, we find a *flux* — perceptions, memories, thoughts, feelings — with *no* *perceivable* *substance* beneath it.

The problem was posed most sharply by Locke, and it was *transformed* by Parfit. Locke asked: what makes a *person* the *same* over time? Parfit asked a *different* question: why should we *care* about the answer? The *difference* between the two questions is the *difference* between a *metaphysical* inquiry and an *ethical* one, and the chapter's thesis is that the second question is *prior*: the metaphysics of identity is *interesting* because it bears on *what matters*, and the ethical stakes are *not settled* by the metaphysics.

The chapter has a distinctive shape, because the territory has been *contested twice*: by the *reductionists* (Hume, Parfit), who deny that identity is a *further fact*, and by the *animalists* (Olson), who deny that psychological continuity is *what we are*. The chapter's argument is that *both* critiques are *partly right*: the self is not a substance, identity is not a further fact, and psychological continuity is not the whole story — but the *pattern* of biological and psychological continuity is *real*, and the *first-person perspective* is *irreducible*.

We proceed in eight movements. §1 states the problem and maps the options. §2 examines the substantial self. §3 examines the Lockean psychological theory. §4 examines the memory objection. §5 examines animalism. §6 examines fission and Parfit's reductionism. §7 examines the narrative and illusionist alternatives. §8 examines self-knowledge. §9 examines the ethical consequences. §10 gives an honest verdict.

---

## 1. The Problem and the Options

### 1.1 Five questions

The problem of the self is *not* a *single* question. It is a *family* of *questions*, and the *questions* are *distinct* in ways that are *often* blurred.

| Question | Form | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **The *existence* question** | Is there a self at all? | Metaphysics of mind |
| **The *nature* question** | What *is* the self? (A substance, a bundle, a pattern, an illusion?) | Ontology |
| **The *persistence* question** | What makes a person *the same* over time? | Metaphysics of identity |
| **The *unity* question** | What unites simultaneous experiences into *one* subject? | Metaphysics of mind |
| **The *epistemic* question** | How do we *know* our own minds? | Epistemology |

**The *questions* are *connected*, but they are *not* the same.** The *persistence* question can be answered without an answer to the *nature* question (Locke does this, in effect). The *nature* question can be answered without an answer to the *existence* question (the illusionist *denies* the existence of a *substantial* self, while *accepting* the existence of a *constructed* one). The *epistemic* question is *relatively* independent (§8).

**The *consequence*.** The *confusion* of the *questions* is the *commonest* *error* in the *literature*, and the *disentanglement* is the *first* *task* of the *chapter*.

### 1.2 The data

**The *data***. Four *features* of *our* *ordinary* *conception* of *persons* *demand* an *explanation*.

**(i) Persistence.** We *believe* that we *persist* through *time* — that the *person* who *wrote* these *words* is the *same* *person* who is *reading* them. The *belief* is *not* *optional*; it is *constitutive* of *planning*, *promising*, and *self-concern*.

**(ii) Unity.** We *believe* that *our* *experiences* are *unified* — that the *visual* *experience*, the *tactile* *experience*, and the *thought* are *experiences* *of* a *single* *subject*. The *phenomenon* is the *“unity* *of* *consciousness”*, and it is *one* of the *deepest* *problems* in the *philosophy* of *mind*.

**(iii) First-person authority.** We *believe* that we have *privileged* *access* to our *own* *mental* *states* — that we *usually* *know* *what* we *think* and *feel* without *observation* or *inference*.

**(iv) Responsibility.** We *believe* that *persons* are *responsible* for their *actions*, and the *responsibility* *presupposes* that the *person* *who* *acted* is the *same* *person* who is *held* *responsible*.

**The *problem*.** What *accounts* for the *four* *features*?

### 1.3 The options

The *options* are a *space* of *positions*, and the *space* is the *map* of the *chapter*.

| Position | The *self* is | The *persistence* is | The *paradigm* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Substance** | An *immaterial* (or *material*) *substance* | The *continuity* of the *substance* | Descartes; Swinburne |
| **Bundle** | A *collection* of *experiences* | The *causal* *relations* among the *experiences* | Hume; Parfit |
| **Psychological** | A *pattern* of *psychological* *continuity* | The *overlapping* *chains* of *connectedness* | Locke; Parfit |
| **Animalist** | A *human* *organism* | The *biological* *continuity* of the *organism* | Olson; Snowdon |
| **Narrative** | A *self-constituting* *story* | The *coherence* of the *narrative* | Ricoeur; Schechtman |
| **Illusionist** | A *representational* *construct* (no *real* self) | The *constructed* *continuity* | Dennett; Metzinger |

**The *map*.**


**The *structure***. The *map* is a *space* of *positions*, and the *positions* differ on *two* *axes*: (i) whether the self is a *further* *entity* (substance, simple view) or a *constructed* *pattern* (bundle, psychological, narrative, illusionist); and (ii) whether the *persistence* is *grounded* in the *psychological* (Locke, Parfit), the *biological* (Olson), or the *narrative* (Ricoeur).

**The *significance*.** The *positions* are *not* *exhaustive*, and the *boundaries* are *contested*, but the *map* *locates* the *arguments*, and the *arguments* are the *subject* of the *rest* of the *chapter*.

## 2. The Substantial Self

### 2.1 The Cartesian self

**The *thesis***. The self is a *simple*, *immaterial* *substance* — a *soul* — that *persists* through *time* and *grounds* the *unity* of *experience*.

**The *statement***. Descartes, *Meditations* (1641), II and VI; the *contemporary* *defenders*: Swinburne, *The Evolution of the Soul* (1986; rev. 1997); Foster, *The Immaterial Self* (1991); Chisholm, *Person and Object* (1976).

**The *argument*** (Cartesian).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{I can doubt the existence of my body (the skeptical premise; Ch. 6, §2).} \\
2. & \text{I cannot doubt the existence of my self (the cogito).} \\
3. & \text{If A can be doubted and B cannot, A is not B.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The self is not the body (it is an immaterial substance).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument* *from* *simplicity*** (Swinburne 1986, Ch. 2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The unity of consciousness requires a subject that is not divisible into parts.} \\
2. & \text{The material substances (the brain, the body) are divisible into parts.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The subject is an immaterial, simple substance.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *explains* the *persistence* (the *substance* *persists*), the *unity* (the *substance* is *simple*), and the *first-person* *authority* (the *subject* has *direct* *awareness* of itself).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *conceivability* *objection*** (Arnauld, *Fourth* *Objections*, 1641). The *argument* *proves* only that the self and the body are *conceivable* as *distinct*, not that they *are* distinct (Ch. 23, §2.1).

**(ii) The *interaction* *problem*** (Ch. 23, §2.2). If the self is an *immaterial* *substance*, how does it *interact* with the *body*?

**(iii) The *neural* *dependence* *objection***. The self *depends* on the *brain* (*lesions*, *drugs*, *anesthesia*, *dementia*), and the *dependence* *suggests* that the self is *not* a *separate* *substance*.

**(iv) The *simplicity* *objection***. If the self is *simple*, how can it have *experiences* with *different* *contents*? The *question* is the *combination* *problem* (Ch. 19, §7.2), *applied* to the *soul*.

### 2.2 The simple view

**The *thesis*** (Chisholm 1976; Swinburne 1984; Velleman 1996). Personal *identity* is a *further* *fact*, *not* *constituted* by *any* *physical* or *psychological* *facts*. Two *possible* *worlds* can be *physically* and *psychologically* *identical* and *differ* in *who* is *identical* to *whom*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The facts of personal identity are not entailed by the physical and psychological facts (the reductionist premise, denied).} \\
2. & \text{If the facts are not entailed by the physical and psychological facts, they are further facts.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Personal identity is a further fact.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *captures* the *intuition* that *identity* is *not* a *matter* of *degree* — that you are *either* the *same* *person* or *not*, and the *question* is *settled* by a *fact* that is *independent* of the *psychological* *continuity*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *“just* *more* *facts”* *objection*** (Parfit 1984, Ch. 10; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *view* *posits* a *further* *fact* that is *not* *accessible* *to* *observation* or *inference*, and the *positing* is *unmotivated* (Ch. 22, §3).

**(ii) The *epistemic* *objection***. If *identity* is a *further* *fact*, how do we *know* it? The *answer* (the *soul* *is* *known* *by* *introspection*) is *contested* (Ch. 12, §6), and the *introspection* does *not* *reveal* a *simple* *substance* (Hume, §3.1).

**(iii) The *no* *criterion* *objection***. If *identity* is a *further* *fact*, there is *no* *criterion* by which we can *determine* it, and the *attribution* of *identity* (for *practical* *purposes*) is *unconstrained*. (The *reply*: the *fact* is *known* *by* *the* *subject* *directly*, and the *third-person* *attribution* is *inferential*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 2.3 The *cogito* and the *self*

**The *problem***. The *cogito* *establishes* only that *there* is *thinking*, not *who* is *thinking*.

**The *argument*** (Lichtenberg, *Sudelbücher*, 1793; the *contemporary* *formulation*: Williams 1978, Ch. 1; Parfit 1984, §10).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{“I think” presupposes an “I” (the grammatical subject).} \\
2. & \text{The presupposition is not given in the experience of thinking (the experience is impersonal).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The “I” is not established by the cogito.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *cogito* *establishes* the *existence* of *thought*, not of a *substantial* *self*. The *“I”* is a *grammatical* *fiction*, and the *metaphysical* *self* is *not* *given*.

**The *significance*.** The *argument* *undermines* the *Cartesian* *foundation* of the *substantial* *self*, and it *opens* the *way* to the *bundle* *theory* (§3).

## 3. The Bundle and the Psychological Theory

### 3.1 Hume's bundle

**The *thesis*** (Hume, *Treatise*, 1739–40, I.iv.6). The *self* is a *bundle* of *perceptions* — a *collection* of *experiences* *bound* *together* by *causal* *relations* — and there is *no* *impression* of a *self* apart from the *perceptions*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Every idea is derived from an impression (the empiricist principle).} \\
2. & \text{There is no impression of a persistent self (introspection reveals only particular perceptions).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{There is no idea of a self apart from the perceptions.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *substance*, and it *fits* the *empiricist* *framework*.

**The *objection***. The *bundle* *theory* *cannot* *explain* the *unity* of *experience*: if the *self* is a *collection* of *perceptions*, what *binds* the *perceptions* *together* into a *single* *subject*? The *question* is the *compresence* *problem* (Ch. 15, §3.2), *applied* to the *self*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *bundle* *theorists*; Parfit 1984, Ch. 10). The *unity* is *constituted* by the *causal* *relations* *among* the *perceptions* (the *overlapping* *chains* of *memory*, *intention*, and *character*), and the *relations* *are* *real* *without* a *further* *subject*.

### 3.2 Locke's memory criterion

**The *thesis*** (Locke, *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, 1690, II.xxvii). A *person* is a *thinking* *being* *capable* of *reason* and *reflection*, and the *identity* of the *person* *across* *time* *consists* in the *continuity* of *consciousness* — the *memory* of *past* *experiences*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\text{Person } A \text{ at } t_1 = \text{Person } B \text{ at } t_2 \quad \text{iff} \quad B \text{ remembers (or can remember) the experiences of } A
$$

**The *examples*.** The *prince* and the *cobbler*: if the *soul* of the *prince* enters the *body* of the *cobbler*, the *person* is the *prince* (the *memory* *follows* the *soul*). The *little* *finger*: if the *consciousness* of the *little* *finger* *remains* with the *finger* when it is *cut* *off*, the *finger* is the *person*.

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *substance* *question* (identity is *constituted* by *psychological* *facts*), and it *explains* the *practical* *significance* of *identity* (the *person* who is *punished* is the *person* who *remembers* the *crime*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *circularity* *objection*** (§4.1).

**(ii) The *transitivity* *objection*** (§4.2).

**(iii) The *animalist* *objection*** (§5).

### 3.3 Parfit's psychological continuity

**The *thesis*** (Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984, Part III). *Personal* *identity* *consists* in *psychological* *continuity*, and the *continuity* is a *matter* of *degree*.

**The *distinction***.

| Concept | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychological connectedness** | The *direct* *memory*, *intention*, and *character* *links* between *states* | I remember *what* I *did* *yesterday* |
| **Psychological continuity** | The *overlapping* *chains* of *connectedness* | The *chain* from *yesterday* to *today* to *tomorrow* |

**The *formal* *statement***. Let $C(x, y)$ be the *connectedness* *relation* between *person-stages* $x$ and $y$; then:

$$
\text{Continuity}(A, B) \quad \text{iff} \quad \exists x_1, \ldots, x_n\, [x_1 = A \wedge x_n = B \wedge \forall i\, C(x_i, x_{i+1})]
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Personal identity requires psychological continuity (the Lockean premise).} \\
2. & \text{The continuity is a matter of degree (the connectedness varies).} \\
3. & \text{The continuity survives the fission (the two successors are continuous with the original).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Identity is not what matters; continuity is.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *practical* *significance* of *identity* (the *continuity* *grounds* *concern*, *responsibility*, and *compensation*), and it *avoids* the *problem* of *fission* (§6).

**The *objection*.** The *theory* *makes* *identity* *a* *matter* of *degree*, and the *consequence* is *counterintuitive* (the *“*all-or-nothing*”* *intuition*; the *“*further* *fact*”* *view*; §2.2).

### 3.4 The *connectedness* *and* *continuity* *distinction*

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* is the *key* to Parfit's *revisionism*: the *connectedness* is *what* *matters* *for* the *practical* *concerns* (the *concern* *for* the *future* *self*, the *responsibility* *for* the *past* *self*), and the *continuity* is *what* *matters* *for* the *metaphysical* *unity*. The *two* *diverge* in the *cases* of *fission*, *dementia*, and *radical* *change*.

**The *application***. In the *fission* *case*, the *connectedness* is *preserved* (both *successors* *remember* the *original*'s *life*), and the *identity* *fails* (neither is *identical* to the *original* in the *ordinary* *sense*). The *consequence*: the *original* *survives* *as* *two* *people*, and the *survival* is *what* *matters*.

## 4. The Memory Objection

### 4.1 The circularity objection

**The *argument*** (Butler, *The Analogy of Religion*, 1736, Diss. I; Reid, *Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man*, 1785, III.4).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Memory is memory of my own past experiences (the definition of memory).} \\
2. & \text{If memory is memory of my own past experiences, the identity of the person is presupposed by the memory.} \\
3. & \text{The memory criterion explains identity by memory.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The criterion is circular.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *Lockean* *criterion* *cannot* *explain* *identity* *without* *presupposing* it, and the *circularity* *is* *vicious*.

**The *reply*** (Shoemaker, “Personal Identity and Memory,” 1959; *Self-Knowledge and Self-Identity*, 1963, Ch. 4). The *criterion* can be *reformulated* in *terms* of **quasi-memory**: a *quasi-memory* is a *memory-like* *state* that *carries* the *information* from a *past* *experience*, *whether* or *not* the *subject* is the *one* who *had* the *experience*. The *identity* can then be *defined* in *terms* of *quasi-memory* *chains* *without* *circularity*.

**The *definition***.

$$
\text{Quasi-memory}(S, E) \quad \text{iff} \quad S \text{ carries the information from } E \text{ and } S \text{ represents } E \text{ as its own past experience}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *reply* *breaks* the *circle*: the *quasi-memory* is *defined* *without* *reference* to *identity*, and the *identity* is *defined* *in* *terms* of the *quasi-memory*.

**The *objection*. The *reply* *assumes* that the *quasi-memory* can *be* *defined* *without* *reference* to the *subject*'s *identity*, and the *assumption* is *contested*. The *quasi-memory* *represents* the *past* *experience* as *its* *own*, and the *representation* *presupposes* the *identity* (the *“*ownership*”* *problem*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

### 4.2 The transitivity objection

**The *argument*** (Reid 1785, III.4; the *“*brave* *officer*”* *case*).

**The *scenario*.** A *boy* is *flogged* for *stealing* *apples*. The *boy* *grows* into an *officer* who *remembers* being *flogged*. The *officer* *grows* into a *general* who *remembers* being an *officer* but *not* being *flogged*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The general remembers the officer's experiences (and is therefore identical to the officer, by the memory criterion).} \\
2. & \text{The officer remembers the boy's experiences (and is therefore identical to the boy).} \\
3. & \text{The general does not remember the boy's experiences (and is therefore not identical to the boy).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The memory criterion violates transitivity (the general is identical to the officer and not identical to the boy, while the officer is identical to the boy).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *memory* *criterion* *cannot* *be* *the* *identity* *criterion*, because *identity* is *transitive* and the *memory* *relation* is *not*.

**The *reply*** (Parfit 1984, §79; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *criterion* should be *continuity*, not *connectedness*: the *general* is *continuous* with the *boy* (through the *officer*), even if not *directly* *connected*. The *continuity* is *transitive* (by *definition*), and the *connectedness* is *not*.

**The *virtue*.** The *reply* *saves* the *criterion*, and it *explains* the *intuition* that the *general* is *the* *same* *person* as the *boy* (the *continuity* is *preserved*).

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *makes* *identity* *a* *matter* of *degree* (the *continuity* *admits* of *degrees*), and the *consequence* is *counterintuitive*.

### 4.3 The *memory* *trace* *and* the *brain*

**The *problem***. The *memory* *criterion* *requires* that the *memory* *be* *grounded* in a *physical* *trace* (the *engram*, the *synaptic* *change*), and the *trace* *is* *not* the *memory* (it is the *vehicle*, not the *content*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The memory is realized by a physical trace.} \\
2. & \text{The trace can be duplicated (the “twin” case; the brain split).} \\
3. & \text{If the trace is duplicated, the memory is duplicated, and the identity is duplicated.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The memory criterion implies that identity can be duplicated, which is impossible (identity is transitive).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *memory* *criterion* *leads* to the *fission* *problem* (§6), and the *problem* *is* *the* *strongest* *argument* for *Parfit's* *reductionism*.

### 4.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *memory* *criterion* is *not* *tenable* in its *simple* *form* (the *circularity* and *transitivity* *objections*), but the *psychological* *continuity* *theory* (with the *quasi-memory* *reformulation*) is *defensible*. The *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to the *“*further* *fact*”* *intuition* (§2.2) and to the *fission* *case* (§6).

## 5. Animalism

### 5.1 The thesis

**The *view*** (Olson, *The Human Animal*, 1997; *What Are We?*, 2007; Snowdon, “Personal Identity and Brain Transplants,” 1990; van Inwagen, *Material Beings*, 1990). We are *human* *animals* — *biological* *organisms* of the *species* *Homo* *sapiens* — and *personal* *identity* *consists* in the *continuity* of the *organism*, not in *psychological* *continuity*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{You are a material object (the naturalistic premise; Ch. 19, §3).} \\
2. & \text{The material object that you are is the human animal (the biological premise).} \\
3. & \text{The human animal persists by biological continuity (the metaphysical premise).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{You persist by biological continuity.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *substance* and the *circularity* of the *memory* *criterion*, and it *fits* the *biological* *facts* (you *were* a *fetus*, you *can* *survive* *dementia*, you *can* be *unconscious*).

**The *objection*** (the *thinking* *animal* *problem*; §5.2).

### 5.2 The thinking animal problem

**The *problem*** (Olson 1997, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{There is a human animal in your chair (the biological premise).} \\
2. & \text{The animal has a brain and behaves as you do (the empirical premise).} \\
3. & \text{The animal thinks (the psychological premise).} \\
4. & \text{If you are not the animal, there are two thinkers in your chair (you and the animal).} \\
5. & \text{There is only one thinker in your chair (the common-sense premise).} \\
\hline
6. & \therefore \text{You are the animal.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *supports* *animalism*, and it *undermines* the *psychological* *theory* (which *implies* that there are *two* *thinkers*: the *person* and the *animal*).

**The *replies***.

**(i) The *constitutionalist* *reply*** (Baker, *Persons and Bodies*, 2000). You are *constituted* by the *animal*, but you are *not* *identical* to it. The *animal* and the *person* *share* the *same* *matter*, but they are *different* *kinds* of *thing* (the *person* is a *psychological* *kind*, the *animal* is a *biological* *kind*). The *reply* *avoids* the *two*-*thinkers* *problem* by *distinguishing* the *kinds*, and it *faces* the *“*too* *many* *thinkers*”* *objection* (if the *animal* *thinks*, and the *person* *thinks*, there are *two* *thinkers*).

**(ii) The *four*-*dimensionalist* *reply*** (Ch. 17, §4.3; Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism*, 2001, Ch. 5). The *animal* and the *person* are *four*-*dimensional* *wholes* that *overlap* *at* the *present* *moment*, and the *apparent* *two* *thinkers* are *two* *wholes* with a *shared* *stage*. The *reply* *avoids* the *problem* by *relativizing* *identity* to *times*, and it *faces* the *Humphrey* *objection* (Ch. 16, §4.3).

**(iii) The *eliminativist* *reply*** (the *“*no* *animal*”* *view*). There is *no* *animal* *distinct* from the *person*; the *person* *is* the *only* *thing* in the *chair*, and the *biological* *description* is a *description* of the *person*, not of a *separate* *entity*. The *reply* *faces* the *biological* *facts* (the *organism* *persists* *after* the *person* *ceases*, in *dementia*, in *PVS*).

### 5.3 The corpse and the fetus

**The *corpse* *problem*** (Olson 1997, Ch. 4). If you are the *animal*, the *corpse* is *not* you (the *corpse* is *not* *alive*), and the *question* of *when* you *cease* *to* *exist* is *the* *question* of *when* the *organism* *dies*. The *answer* is *metaphysically* *loaded* (the *definition* of *death*, the *criteria* of *brain* *death*), and the *answer* is *contested*.

**The *fetus* *problem*** (Olson 1997, Ch. 3). If you are the *animal*, you *existed* as a *fetus*, and the *psychological* *continuity* *cannot* be *essential* (the *fetus* *has* *no* *psychology*). The *problem* *supports* *animalism*, and it *undermines* the *psychological* *theory*.

**The *significance*.** The *problems* *show* that the *biological* *continuity* *matters* *independently* of the *psychological* *continuity*, and the *consequence* *supports* a *hybrid* *theory* (§10).

### 5.4 The state of animalism

**The *assessment*.** Animalism is the *most* *defensible* *alternative* to the *psychological* *theory*, and it *faces* the *thinking* *animal* *problem* and the *corpse* *problem*. The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *animalism*: the *biological* *continuity* is *necessary* for *personal* *identity*, and the *psychological* *continuity* is *what* *matters* *for* the *practical* *concerns*.

## 6. Fission, Duplication, and Parfit's Reductionism

### 6.1 The fission case

**The *scenario*** (Wiggins, *Identity and Spatio-Temporal Continuity*, 1967; Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984, §89). A *person* — *call* *her* *Alice* — *undergoes* a *procedure* that *splits* her *brain* into *two* *hemispheres*, *each* *transplanted* into a *new* *body*. The *two* *resulting* *persons* — *Alice*-*Left* and *Alice*-*Right* — *both* *remember* *Alice*'s *life*, *both* *have* her *personality*, and *both* *regard* *themselves* as *Alice*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Identity is transitive: if } a = b \text{ and } b = c, \text{ then } a = c. \\
2. & \text{If Alice = Alice-Left and Alice = Alice-Right, then Alice-Left = Alice-Right.} \\
3. & \text{But Alice-Left and Alice-Right are distinct (they are spatially separated and psychologically divergent).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Alice cannot be identical to both.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *identity* *fails*, and the *psychological* *continuity* is *preserved* (both *successors* are *continuous* with *Alice*). The *case* *shows* that *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*: the *survival* *occurs* *twice*, and the *identity* *occurs* *zero* *times*.

### 6.2 Parfit's reductionism

**The *thesis*** (Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984, Part III). Personal *identity* is *not* a *further* *fact*; it is *constituted* by the *physical* and *psychological* *facts*, and the *facts* are *describable* *without* *reference* to *identity*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The facts of personal identity are constituted by psychological continuity (the reductionist premise).} \\
2. & \text{The continuity is describable impersonally (the quasi-memory chains, the causal relations).} \\
3. & \text{The description does not require the concept of identity.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Identity is not a further fact.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *reductionism* *makes* *identity* *a* *matter* of *degree*, and it *dissolves* the *fission* *problem* (the *question* “am I *Alice*-*Left* or *Alice*-*Right*?” is *misplaced*; the *answer* is “you *survive* *as* *both*, and the *identity* *question* *has* no *determinate* *answer*”).

### 6.3 The *what* *matters* *argument*

**The *argument*** (Parfit 1984, §90).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The fission case shows that identity can fail while survival succeeds (both successors survive).} \\
2. & \text{If identity can fail while survival succeeds, identity is not what matters.} \\
3. & \text{What matters is psychological continuity (the relation that grounds survival).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Identity is not what matters; continuity is.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *revisionism* *is* *radical*: the *concern* for the *future* *self*, the *responsibility* for the *past* *self*, and the *practices* of *promising*, *punishment*, and *compensation* are *justified* by *continuity*, not by *identity*. The *consequence* is the *subject* of §9.

### 6.4 Lewis's response

**The *thesis*** (Lewis, “Survival and Identity,” 1976; the *four*-*dimensionalist* *response*). The *fission* *case* *does* not *show* that *identity* *fails*; it *shows* that the *person* *has* *two* *futures*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The pre-fission person is a four-dimensional whole (the four-dimensionalist premise; Ch. 17, §4.3).} \\
2. & \text{The whole includes both branches (the two successors).} \\
3. & \text{The two successors are stages of the whole, not entire persons.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Identity is preserved (the whole is the person).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *response* *preserves* *identity* *as* *what* *matters*, and it *avoids* the *revisionist* *consequences* of the *Parfitian* *view*.

**The *objection*.** The *response* *implies* that *Alice*-*Left* and *Alice*-*Right* are *not* *persons* (they are *parts* of a *person*), and the *implication* is *counterintuitive* (they *think*, *act*, and *regard* *themselves* as *persons*). The *response* *is* *contested*.

### 6.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *fission* *case* is the *strongest* *argument* for *Parfit's* *reductionism*, and it is *not* *decisive* (the *Lewis* *response* is *available*). The *choice* *between* *reductionism* and *the* *further*-*fact* *view* *depends* on the *weight* given to the *“*all*-or*-*nothing*”* *intuition* and to the *revisionist* *consequences*.

## 7. The Narrative and the Illusion

### 7.1 The narrative self

**The *thesis*** (Ricoeur, *Oneself as Another*, 1990; MacIntyre, *After Virtue*, 1981; Schechtman, *The Constitution of Selves*, 1996). The *self* is *constituted* by a *self*-*narrative* — a *story* that the *agent* *tells* about her *life*, and the *story* *provides* the *unity* and the *continuity* that the *metaphysical* *theories* *seek*.

**The *structure*.** The *narrative* *has* *constraints* (Schechtman 1996, Ch. 5).

| Constraint | The *demand* |
|---|---|
| **Reality** | The *narrative* must *fit* the *facts* (it *cannot* be *fantasy*) |
| **Articulation** | The *narrative* must be *coherent* and *communicable* |
| **Narrative** | The *narrative* must *make* *sense* (the *events* must *connect*) |
| **Diachronic unity** | The *narrative* must *explain* the *persistence* *across* *time* |

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *phenomenology* of *self*-*experience* (we *experience* our *lives* as *stories*), and it *explains* the *practical* *concern* for the *future* *self* (the *concern* is *constituted* by the *narrative*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *descriptive* *objection*** (Strawson, “Against Narrativity,” 2004). The *narrative* *theory* is *not* *empirically* *adequate*: *many* *people* — the *“*episodic*”* *types* — *do* not *experience* their *lives* as *narratives*, and the *theory* *excludes* them.

**(ii) The *normative* *objection*. The *theory* *imposes* a *norm* (the *“*narrative*”* *self*) on *people* who *do* not *fit* it, and the *imposition* is *politically* *contested* (Ch. 11, §4; Ch. 33, §5).

**(iii) The *circularity* *objection*. The *narrative* *presupposes* the *self* *that* *tells* it, and the *theory* *cannot* *explain* the *self* *without* *presupposing* it.

### 7.2 The illusionist self

**The *thesis*** (Dennett, “The Self as a Center of Narrative Gravity,” 1992; *Consciousness Explained*, 1991; Metzinger, *Being No One*, 2003). The *self* is *not* a *thing*; it is a *useful* *fiction* — a *“*center* *of* *narrative* *gravity*”* — that the *brain* *constructs* to *organize* its *behavior*.

**The *analogy*** (Dennett 1992). The *self* is *like* the *center* *of* *gravity*: it is a *real* *abstraction* (the *center* *of* *gravity* *is* *real*, and it *has* *causal* *effects*), but it is *not* a *thing* *over* *and* *above* the *physical* *system*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The brain constructs a narrative of its own activity (the empirical premise).} \\
2. & \text{The narrative posits a central character (the self).} \\
3. & \text{The character is not a thing over and above the narrative.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The self is a construct (a real pattern, not a substance).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *fits* the *empirical* *literature* (the *split*-*brain* *cases*, the *confabulation* *literature*, the *“*user* *illusion*”*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *substance*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *phenomenological* *objection*. The *self* *seems* to be *real* (the *first*-*person* *perspective* is *irreducible*), and the *theory* *denies* the *seeming*.

**(ii) The *moral* *objection*. If the *self* is a *fiction*, the *moral* *practices* (responsibility, punishment, rights) are *undermined*, and the *consequence* is *unacceptable* (Ch. 45, §3).

**(iii) The *self*-*refutation* *objection*. The *theory* *presupposes* a *theorist* (a *self*), and the *theory* *undermines* *itself*.

### 7.3 The Buddhist *anattā*

**The *thesis*** (Ch. 24, §7.3). The *self* is *not* a *substance*; it is a *convention*, a *stream* of *moments* (the *“*five* *aggregates*”*).

**The *argument*** (the *Anattalakkhaṇa* *Sutta*; the *Milindapañha*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If the self were a substance, it would be permanent and unchanging.} \\
2. & \text{Everything that is observed is impermanent and changing.} \\
3. & \text{The self is not observed apart from the aggregates.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The self is not a substance.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *anticipates* the *bundle* and *reductionist* *views*, and it *connects* the *philosophy* of *mind* to the *philosophy* of the *self*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *assumes* that the *self* *must* be *observed* to *exist* (the *empiricist* *principle*), and the *assumption* is *contested* (the *self* *may* be the *subject* of *experience*, not an *object*).

**The *consequence*.** The *Buddhist* *view* *is* a *systematic* *alternative* to the *Western* *substance* *theories*, and its *compatibility* with the *reductionist* *views* is *striking* (Parfit *acknowledges* the *affinity*; *Reasons and Persons*, 1984, §95).

### 7.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *narrative* and *illusionist* *views* *capture* *aspects* of the *self* that the *substance* and *psychological* *views* *miss*, and they *face* *serious* *objections*. The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *hybrid*: the *self* is a *real* *pattern* (the *illusionism* *without* the *eliminativism*), *constituted* by *narrative* and *biological* *continuity* (the *narrative* *without* the *normativity*), and *presupposed* by the *first*-*person* *perspective* (the *irreducible* *subjectivity*).

## 8. Self-Knowledge and the First Person

### 8.1 The transparency of belief

**The *thesis*** (Evans, *The Varieties of Reference*, 1982, p. 225; Ch. 12, §6.3). To *know* whether I *believe* $p$, I *look* at the *world*, not at my *mind*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & Q: \text{Do I believe } p? \\
2. & \text{I answer } Q \text{ by answering the first-order question } p. \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Self-knowledge is transparent (achieved by looking at the world).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *model* *explains* the *authority* of *self*-*knowledge* *without* *positing* a *special* *faculty*, and it *fits* the *phenomenology* of *belief*.

**The *objection*.** The *model* *works* for *belief* but *not* for *desire* or *emotion* (the *first*-*order* *question* is *about* *my* *state*, not the *world*), and the *transparency* *method* is *local*.

### 8.2 Immunity to error through misidentification

**The *thesis*** (Shoemaker, “Self-Reference and Self-Awareness,” 1968; Ch. 12, §4.4). A *judgment* “I am $F$” is *immune* to *error* *through* *misidentification* if it is *impossible* that the *predicate* $F$ applies to *someone*, but the *subject* *misidentifies* *who* that someone is.

**The *examples*.

| Judgment | Immune? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| “I feel pain” | **Yes** | The *feeling* is *mine*; the *identification* *cannot* *fail* |
| “I see a tree” | **No** | I *could* be *mistaken* about *who* is *seeing* the *tree* |
| “I am 6 feet tall” | **No** | I *could* be *mistaken* about *who* the *body* *belongs* to |

**The *significance*.** The *immunity* is the *epistemic* *mark* of *self*-*knowledge*, and it *grounds* the *first*-*person* *authority*.

**The *objection*** (the *anti*-*luminosity* *argument*; Williamson 2000, Ch. 4; Ch. 12, §6.5). The *immunity* is *not* *infallibility* (the *predicate* can *fail*), and the *luminosity* *thesis* (that *every* *mental* *state* is *knowable* *when* *one* is *in* it) is *false*. The *objection* *limits* the *scope* of the *immunity*.

### 8.3 The social dimension

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 11, §4.1). Self-knowledge is *not* *purely* *individual*: it is *shaped* by *social* *norms*, *concepts*, and *testimony*.

**The *examples*.

- **Emotional education**: I *learn* to *identify* my *emotions* through *social* *concepts* (the *“*that* is *anger*”* *case*).
- **Hermeneutical injustice**: if the *concept* for my *experience* *does* not *exist*, I *cannot* *know* what I am *feeling* (Ch. 11, §4.1).
- **Confabulation**: the *subject* *constructs* a *narrative* of her *mental* *states* that is *not* *accurate* (Nisbett & Wilson, “Telling More Than We Can Know,” 1977; the *choice*-*blindness* *literature*).

**The *consequence*.** Self-knowledge is *socially* *mediated*, and the *mediation* is *not* *always* *benign*. The *consequence* *undermines* the *individualist* *conception* of *first*-*person* *authority*.

### 8.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** Self-knowledge is *real*, *fallible*, and *socially* *mediated*. The *transparency* *method* and the *immunity* *thesis* *capture* *aspects* of it, and the *anti*-*luminosity* and *social* *objections* *limit* its *scope*. The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *transparency*: *belief* is *transparent*, *emotion* is *not*, and the *first*-*person* *authority* is *real* but *not* *absolute*.

## 9. The Metaphysics of the Person and Its Ethical Consequences

### 9.1 The general question

**The *question*.** Does the *metaphysics* of *personal* *identity* *settle* the *ethics* of *survival*, *responsibility*, and *self*-*concern*?

**The *thesis*.** The *metaphysics* *constrains* the *ethics*, but it does *not* *settle* it: the *ethical* *practices* are *justified* by *continuity* (which is *real* and *gradable*), not by *identity* (which is *contested* and *all*-or*-nothing*).

### 9.2 Advance directives and dementia

**The *problem*.** A *person* *signs* an *advance* *directive* *refusing* *treatment* *in* *the* *event* of *dementia*. When the *dementia* *arrives*, the *patient* *seems* *content* and *wants* to *live*. Does the *directive* *bind*?

**The *positions*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *then*-*self*** | The *directive* *binds* (the *person* who *signed* is *the* *same* *person*) | Dworkin, *Life’s Dominion* (1993) |
| **The *now*-*self*** | The *directive* does *not* *bind* (the *person* *now* is *different*) | Dresser & Robertson, “Quality of Life, Life’s Worth, and the Value of Continued Life” (1989) |
| **The *continuity* *view*** | The *directive* *binds* *to* the *extent* that the *continuity* is *preserved* | Parfit (the *application*) |

**The *argument*** (Dworkin). The *person* has *critical* *interests* (the *conception* of a *good* *life*) and *experiential* *interests* (the *quality* of *experience*). The *critical* *interests* *survive* the *dementia* (they are *constitutive* of the *person*), and the *directive* *protects* them.

**The *objection*** (Dresser). The *now*-*self* *has* *interests* (the *contentment*, the *experiential* *goods*), and the *interests* *should* *weigh* *equally*. The *“*then*”* *self* *cannot* *speak* for the *“*now*”* *self*.

**The *consequence*.** The *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *theory* of *identity* and the *theory* of *interests*. The *continuity* *view* *offers* a *middle* *path* (the *directive* *binds* *if* the *continuity* is *substantial*), and the *middle* *path* is *contested*.

### 9.3 Punishment and desert

**The *problem*.** The *retributive* *justification* of *punishment* *requires* that the *person* *punished* is *the* *same* *person* who *committed* the *crime*. If *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*, the *retributive* *justification* is *undermined*.

**The *argument*** (Parfit 1984, §97).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The retributive justification requires identity (the same person deserves the punishment).} \\
2. & \text{Identity is not what matters (the reductionist premise).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The retributive justification is undermined.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *deterrent* and *rehabilitative* *justifications* *survive* (they *do* not *require* *identity*), and the *retributive* *justification* *does* not. The *consequence* is *revisionary*, and it is *contested* (the *retributive* *intuition* is *powerful*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *objection*.** The *retributive* *justification* *can* be *grounded* in the *continuity* (the *person* *punished* is *continuous* with the *criminal*), and the *continuity* *is* *sufficient* for *desert*. The *reply* *saves* the *retributive* *intuition*, and it *concedes* the *reductionist* *premise*.

### 9.4 Promises, contracts, and compensation

**The *problem*.** The *practices* of *promising*, *contracting*, and *compensating* *presuppose* the *persistence* of the *person* *across* *time*. If *identity* is *a* *matter* of *degree*, the *practices* *are* *gradable*, and the *gradability* *is* *uncomfortable* for the *law*.

**The *argument*** (Parfit 1984, §98). The *practices* *can* be *reformulated* in *terms* of *continuity* (the *promise* *binds* *to* the *extent* that the *promisor* is *continuous* with the *promisee*), and the *reformulation* *preserves* the *practices* without the *identity* *assumption*.

**The *objection*.** The *law* *requires* *sharp* *boundaries* (the *contract* is *valid* or *not*), and the *gradability* *of* *continuity* *is* *incompatible* with the *legal* *determinacy*. (The *reply*: the *law* *can* *fix* *conventional* *thresholds*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

### 9.5 Self-concern and the future self

**The *problem*.** Why *should* I *care* about my *future* *self*? The *answer* (the *identity* *relation*) *grounds* the *concern* in the *sameness* of the *person*. If *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*, the *concern* must be *grounded* in *continuity*.

**The *argument*** (Parfit 1984, §99). The *concern* *should* be *proportional* to the *continuity*: I *should* care *more* about my *future* *self* *tomorrow* than about my *future* *self* in *thirty* *years*, because the *connectedness* *decreases*.

**The *objection*.** The *consequence* is *counterintuitive* (we *do* care *equally* about the *distant* *future* *self*), and the *reply* *undermines* the *prudence* *tradition* (the *“*Schelling* *point*”*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *response*.** The *concern* *can* be *grounded* in the *narrative* *identity* (the *story* *connects* the *stages*), and the *narrative* *preserves* the *concern* *without* the *substance*. The *response* *is* *promising* and *contested*.

### 9.6 The limits of the revision

**The *limits***. The *revisionism* is *powerful*, and it *faces* *three* *limits*.

**(i) The *moral* *intuition***. The *retributive* and *prudential* *intuitions* are *powerful*, and the *revision* *must* *accommodate* them (the *“*reflective* *equilibrium”* *constraint*; Ch. 3, §4).

**(ii) The *legal* *practicality***. The *law* *cannot* *operate* with *gradable* *identity*, and the *revision* *must* *provide* *conventional* *thresholds*.

**(iii) The *first*-*person* *perspective***. The *experience* of *self*-*concern* *presupposes* a *self* that *is* *concerned*, and the *revision* *cannot* *eliminate* the *first*-*person* *perspective* (Ch. 26, §8).

**The *consequence*.** The *revisionism* is *not* *a* *replacement* of the *practices*; it is a *reinterpretation* of their *grounds*. The *reinterpretation* is *defensible*, and it is *incomplete*.

## 10. The State of the Debate

### 10.1 What is settled

- **The substantial self is untenable.** The *cogito* does not establish the *substance* (the Lichtenberg objection), and the *simple* *view* *faces* the *“*just* *more* *facts”* *objection*.
- **The memory criterion is untenable in its simple form.** The *circularity* and *transitivity* *objections* are *decisive*, and the *quasi*-*memory* *reformulation* is the *most* *defensible* *repair*.
- **The psychological continuity theory is the dominant position.** Parfit's *connectedness*/*continuity* *distinction* is *influential*, and it *grounds* the *revisionist* *ethics*.
- **Animalism is the most serious alternative.** The *thinking* *animal* *problem* and the *fetus* *problem* *support* the *biological* *continuity*, and the *view* is *contested*.
- **The fission case is the strongest argument for reductionism.** The *case* *shows* that *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*, and the *Lewis* *response* is *available* but *contested*.
- **The narrative and illusionist views capture aspects of the self.** The *self* is a *real* *pattern*, *constituted* by *narrative* and *biological* *continuity*, and the *first*-*person* *perspective* is *irreducible*.
- **Self-knowledge is real, fallible, and socially mediated.** The *transparency* *method* and the *immunity* *thesis* *capture* *aspects* of it, and the *anti*-*luminosity* and *social* *objections* *limit* its *scope*.
- **The ethical consequences are not settled by the metaphysics.** The *practices* are *justified* by *continuity*, not by *identity*, and the *revision* is *defensible* and *incomplete*.

### 10.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of the self.** Is it a *substance*, a *bundle*, a *pattern*, or a *fiction*?
- **The *ground* of *persistence*.** Is it *psychological*, *biological*, or *narrative*?
- **The *status* of the *simple* *view*.** Is *identity* a *further* *fact*?
- **The *fission* *case*.** Does it *refute* the *identity* *theory*?
- **The *ethics* of *survival*.** Do *advance* *directives* *bind*?
- **The *nature* of *self*-*knowledge*.** Is it *transparent*, *constitutive*, or *socially* *mediated*?

### 10.3 The methodological lesson

The *chapter* is a *case* *study* in the *method* of *Part* I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *five* *questions*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *prince* and the *cobbler*, the *brave* *officer*, the *fission*, the *swampman* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *connectedness*/*continuity* *formula*, the *quasi*-*memory* *definition* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of the *self* is a *field* in *transition*. The *substantial* *self* is *untenable*; the *psychological* *theory* is *dominant* but *incomplete*; the *animalist* and *narrative* *alternatives* are *promising*; the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled* *answer*, but a *sharper* *understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 10.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Substance** | The self is a simple immaterial substance | Explains persistence and unity | The interaction problem |
| **Simple view** | Identity is a further fact | Preserves the all-or-nothing intuition | The “just more facts” objection |
| **Bundle** | The self is a collection of perceptions | Empiricist; parsimonious | The compresence problem |
| **Psychological** | Identity = psychological continuity | Fits the practical concerns | The circularity and transitivity objections |
| **Animalism** | Identity = biological continuity | Fits the fetus and dementia cases | The thinking animal problem |
| **Parfitian reductionism** | Identity is not what matters | Solves the fission case | The revisionist consequences |
| **Lewis's response** | Identity is preserved (four-dimensionalism) | Preserves the identity intuition | The two-persons implication |
| **Narrative** | The self is a self-constituting story | Fits the phenomenology | The descriptive and normative objections |
| **Illusionism** | The self is a useful fiction | Fits the empirical literature | The phenomenological and moral objections |
| **Buddhist *anattā*** | The self is not a substance | Systematic; ancient | The empiricist assumption |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *split*-*brain* *cases*.** The *empirical* *literature* (Sperry, Gazzaniga) *suggests* that the *commissurotomy* *patients* *have* *two* *streams* *of* *consciousness*, and the *interpretation* is *contested* (the *“*two* *minds*”* *view* vs. the *“*one* *mind,* *divided*”* *view*). The *cases* *bear* on the *unity* *question* (§1.1) and on the *bundle* *theory* (§3.1).

**2. The *empirical* *self*-*knowledge* *literature*.** The *confabulation* *studies* (Nisbett & Wilson 1977), the *choice*-*blindness* *studies* (Johansson et al. 2005), and the *“*you* *don’t* *know* *your* *own* *mind*”* *literature* (Schwitzgebel 2008) *challenge* the *first*-*person* *authority*, and they *bear* on the *epistemic* *question* (§8).

**3. The *extended* *self*.** The *“*extended* *mind*”* *thesis* (Clark & Chalmers 1998; Ch. 30, §4) *suggests* that the *self* *extends* *beyond* the *skull* (the *notebook*, the *phone*, the *archive*), and the *suggestion* *challenges* the *biological* *conception* of the *self*. The *debate* is *promising* and *underdeveloped*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of the *self*.** The *Buddhist* *anattā* (§7.3) is the *most* *developed* *non*-*Western* *account* of the *self*, and the *Advaita* *Vedānta* *developed* a *radical* *monism* that *anticipates* the *cosmopsychist* *views* (Ch. 20, §4.2). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *politics* of *identity*.** The *concept* of *identity* is *political* (the *identity* *politics*, the *recognition* *debates*, the *national* *identity* *debates*), and the *politics* *bears* on the *metaphysics* (the *social* *construction* of *identity*; Ch. 11, §4; Ch. 47, §7). The *connection* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent*.

## Summary

- **The *problem* of the *self* is a *family* of *questions** — existence, nature, persistence, unity, and self-knowledge — and the *questions* are *distinct*.
- **The *substantial* *self*** (Descartes, Swinburne) *explains* persistence and unity, but it *faces* the *interaction* problem, the *neural* *dependence*, and the *Lichtenberg* *objection* to the *cogito*.
- **The *simple* *view*** (Chisholm, Swinburne) *treats* identity as a *further* *fact*, and it *faces* the *“*just* *more* *facts”* *objection*.
- **The *bundle* *theory*** (Hume) *avoids* the *substance*, but it *faces* the *compresence* problem.
- **The *memory* *criterion*** (Locke) *faces* the *circularity* and *transitivity* *objections*, and the *quasi*-*memory* *reformulation* (Shoemaker) is the *most* *defensible* *repair*.
- **Parfit's *psychological* *continuity*** *distinguishes* *connectedness* from *continuity*, and it *grounds* the *revisionist* *claim* that *identity* is *not* *what* *matters*.
- **Animalism** (Olson) *treats* the self as a *human* *organism*, and it *faces* the *thinking* *animal* problem and the *corpse* problem.
- **The *fission* *case*** is the *strongest* *argument* for *reductionism*, and the *Lewis* *response* (four-dimensionalism) is *available* but *contested*.
- **The *narrative* and *illusionist* *views*** *capture* *aspects* of the self that the *substance* and *psychological* *views* *miss*, and they *face* *serious* *objections*.
- **Self-knowledge** is *real*, *fallible*, and *socially* *mediated*; the *transparency* *method* (Evans) and the *immunity* *thesis* (Shoemaker) *capture* *aspects* of it.
- **The *ethical* *consequences*** (advance directives, punishment, promises, self-concern) are *constrained* by the *metaphysics*, but *not* *settled* by it; the *practices* are *justified* by *continuity*, not *identity*.
