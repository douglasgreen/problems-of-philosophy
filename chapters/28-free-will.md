# Chapter 27 — Free Will, Agency, and Responsibility

> “The will is nothing but a determination of the mind, and a determination of the mind is a mode of thinking. … Freedom, therefore, is the absence of external impediments.”
> — Thomas Hobbes, *Leviathan* (1651), II.21

> “Could I have done otherwise?” is a question that *everyone* asks, and *no* philosophical theory has made it entirely clear what the answer *is* — or what would *count* as an answer.
> — a formulation of the problem, in the spirit of Frankfurt

> “The freedom of the will consists in the fact that the will can be determined by reasons, and reasons are not causes.”
> — a Kantian slogan, restated for the contemporary debate

---

Chapter 26 examined the *self* — the *subject* of experience. This chapter examines the *agent* — the *source* of action.

The two are *connected*, but they are *distinct*. The self is a *pattern* of *psychological* and *biological* *continuity* (Ch. 26, §10.4); the agent is the *pattern* *considered* as a *source* of *action*. The *question* of this chapter is whether the *source* is *free* — whether the agent *could* have *done* *otherwise*, whether the agent is the *author* of her *actions*, and whether the agent is *responsible* for them.

The problem is *ancient*, and it is *urgent*. It is *ancient* because the *deterministic* *worldview* — the view that every event is *caused* by *prior* *events* according to *laws* — is at least as *old* as *Democritus*, and the *tension* between *determinism* and *responsibility* was *felt* *immediately*. It is *urgent* because the *empirical* *sciences* — neuroscience, psychology, genetics — *increasingly* *explain* *behaviour* in *terms* of *causes* *outside* the agent's *control*, and the *explanations* *threaten* the *practices* of *praise*, *blame*, *punishment*, and *reward*.

The chapter's *thesis* is that the *problem* is *real* and *unresolved*, and that the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *form* of *compatibilism* — *qualified* by the *admission* that *compatibilism* *changes* the *subject* (it *redefines* freedom rather than *vindicating* the *ordinary* *notion*), and that the *change* is *defensible* but *incomplete*.

The chapter proceeds in nine movements. §1 states the *problem* and its *three* *forms*. §2 examines *classical* *compatibilism*. §3 examines *contemporary* *compatibilism*. §4 examines the *consequence* *argument* and *incompatibilism*. §5 examines *libertarianism*. §6 examines the *luck* *objection* and the *disappearing* *agent*. §7 examines the *empirical* *challenges*. §8 examines *moral* *responsibility* and *reactive* *attitudes*. §9 examines *free* *will* in *law* and *life*. §10 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. The Problem and Its Three Forms

### 1.1 The data

The problem of free will is generated by *three* *propositions*, each of which is *independently* *plausible*, and which *cannot* all be *true*.

**The *three* *propositions***.

| Proposition | The *content* | The *source* |
|---|---|---|
| **(D) Determinism** | Every event is *causally* *determined* by *prior* *events* and the *laws* of *nature* | The *scientific* *worldview* |
| **(F) Freedom** | Some *agents* *sometimes* *act* *freely* (they *could* have *done* *otherwise*) | The *common-sense* *worldview* |
| **(R) Responsibility** | Some *agents* are *morally* *responsible* for their *actions* (they *deserve* *praise* or *blame*) | The *moral* *worldview* |

**The *tension***. The *three* *propositions* *appear* to be *incompatible*: if every event is *determined*, then the *agent*'s *actions* are *determined*, and the *agent* *could* *not* have *done* *otherwise*; if the *agent* *could* *not* have *done* *otherwise*, the *agent* is *not* *free*; if the *agent* is *not* *free*, the *agent* is *not* *responsible*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & (\mathrm{D}) \text{ Every event is *causally* *determined*.} \\
2. & (\mathrm{D}) \text{ entails the *denial* of } (\mathrm{F}). \\
3. & \text{If } (\mathrm{F}) \text{ is false, } (\mathrm{R}) \text{ is false.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *three* *propositions* cannot all be *true*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *response*.** The *responses* are *defined* by *which* *proposition* they *give* *up*, and the *choice* *among* the *responses* is the *subject* of the *chapter*.

### 1.2 The three forms

The problem is *often* *presented* as a *single* *problem* (the *determinism* *problem*), but it is *actually* *three* *distinct* *problems*, and the *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*.

| Form | The *challenge* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Fatalism** | The *future* is *fixed* independently of *what* *I* *do* | Oedipus's fate |
| **Determinism** | The *past* and the *laws* *fix* *what* *I* *do* | The *Laplacean* *demon* |
| **Luck** | The *origin* of my *actions* is *outside* my *control* | The *luck* *argument* (§6) |

**Fatalism.** The *fatalist* *holds* that *some* *events* are *fixed* *regardless* of *what* *we* *do* — that *Oedipus* will *kill* his *father* *whatever* he *does*, and that the *attempt* to *avoid* the *fate* is *part* of the *fate*. The *fatalist* *thesis* is *not* the *determinist* *thesis*: the *determinist* *holds* that the *events* are *caused* by *prior* *events* (including the *agent*'s *actions*), while the *fatalist* *holds* that the *events* are *fixed* *independently* of the *agent*'s *actions*. The *fatalist* *position* is *widely* *rejected*, and the *reasons* are *instructive*: the *fatalist* *confuses* *causal* *determination* with *logical* *necessity*, and the *confusion* is the *first* *thing* to *clear* (Ch. 16, §1.2).

**Determinism.** The *determinist* *holds* that every *event* is *causally* *determined* by *prior* *events* and the *laws* *of* *nature*. The *thesis* is *not* *fatalism*: the *determinist* *accepts* that the *agent*'s *deliberations* and *actions* are *part* of the *causal* *chain*, and the *actions* are *determined* by the *deliberations* as well as by the *prior* *events*. The *question* is whether the *determination* is *compatible* with *freedom*.

**Luck.** The *luck* *problem* is the *problem* of the *origin* of *actions*: if the *agent*'s *character*, *deliberations*, and *choices* are *shaped* by *factors* *outside* the *agent*'s *control* (genetics, upbringing, social circumstances, *random* *neural* *events*), then the *agent* is *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *actions*. The *problem* is *distinct* from *determinism*, and it is *arguably* *more* *difficult* (§6).

**The *significance*.** The *three* *forms* *require* *different* *responses*, and the *confusion* of the *forms* is the *source* of much *of* the *literature*'s *confusion*.

### 1.3 The definitions

The *debate* is *conducted* with a *vocabulary* that is *systematically* *ambiguous*, and the *ambiguity* is the *first* *obstacle*.

| Term | The *definition* | The *variety* |
|---|---|---|
| **Determinism** | Every event is *causally* *determined* by *prior* *events* and the *laws* | *Nomological* determinism (the *standard* *form*) |
| **Indeterminism** | Some events are *not* *causally* *determined* (they are *random* or *uncaused*) | *Quantum* indeterminism; *agent*-*causation* |
| **Compatibilism** | Determinism is *compatible* with freedom and responsibility | *Classical*; *contemporary* |
| **Incompatibilism** | Determinism is *incompatible* with freedom and responsibility | *Hard* *determinism*; *libertarianism* |
| **Libertarianism** | The *agent* is *free* and *responsible*, and the *freedom* *requires* *indeterminism* | *Agent*-*causation*; *event*-*causation* |
| **Hard determinism** | Determinism is *true*, and freedom is *false* | The *denial* of *responsibility* |
| **Liberationism** | The *agent* is *free* and *responsible*, and the *freedom* is *independent* of the *determinism* *question* | The *Strawsonian* *view* |

**The *consequence*.** The *debate* is a *space* of *positions*, and the *positions* *differ* on *two* *axes*: (i) whether *determinism* is *true*, and (ii) whether *determinism* is *compatible* with *freedom*.

**The *significance*.** The *map* *locates* the *arguments*, and the *arguments* are the *subject* of the *rest* of the *chapter*.

## 2. Classical Compatibilism

### 2.1 Hobbes and Hume

**The *thesis***. Freedom is the *absence* of *external* *constraint*, and the *absence* is *compatible* with *determinism*.

**The *statement***. Hobbes, *Leviathan* (1651), II.21; Hume, *Treatise* (1739–40), II.iii.1–2; *Enquiry* (1748), VIII.

**The *definition*** (Hobbes).

$$
\text{Agent } A \text{ is *free* with respect to action } \phi \quad \text{iff} \quad A \text{ is *not* *externally* *constrained* from } \phi \text{-ing}
$$

**The *examples***. The *prisoner* is *not* *free* (the *walls* *constrain* him); the *person* who *walks* *voluntarily* is *free* (no *external* *constraint*); the *person* who is *forced* at *gunpoint* is *not* *free* (the *gunman* *constrains* her).

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *fits* the *ordinary* *language* of *freedom* (the *contrast* is *constraint*, not *causation*), and it *is* *compatible* with *determinism* (the *causal* *chain* is *not* an *external* *constraint*).

**The *objection*.** The *theory* *is* *too* *weak*: if freedom is *only* the *absence* of *external* *constraint*, then a *person* whose *character* *forces* her to *act* (the *compulsive* *gambler*, the *addict*) is *free* (there is no *external* *constraint*). The *consequence* is *counterintuitive* (the *compulsive* is *not* *free* in the *ordinary* *sense*).

**Hume’s *response***. Hume *adds* a *psychological* *condition*: the *action* must *flow* from the *agent*'s *character* (the *“*voluntary*”* *condition*), and the *condition* *excludes* the *compulsive* (her *action* *does* *not* *flow* from her *character* in the *relevant* *sense*). The *response* *strengthens* the *theory*, and it *raises* the *question* of *what* *it* *means* for an *action* to *flow* from *character*.

### 2.2 The conditional analysis

**The *thesis*** (Moore, *Ethics*, 1912, Ch. 6; the *classical* *conditional* *analysis*). “Could have done otherwise” *means*: “*Would* have *done* *otherwise* *if* the *agent* had *chosen* to.”

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Agent } A \text{ could have done } \phi \quad \text{iff} \quad (A \text{ *chose* to } \phi \to A \text{ *would* have done } \phi)
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *analysis* *reduces* the *modal* *claim* (could have done otherwise) to a *conditional* *claim* (would have done if chosen), and the *conditional* is *compatible* with *determinism* (the *choice* is *determined*, but the *conditional* is *true*).

**The *objections*** (Chisholm, “Freedom and Action,” 1966; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(i) The *regress* *objection***. The *conditional* *analysis* *requires* that the *agent* could have *chosen* *otherwise*, and the *could* *have* *chosen* *otherwise* *requires* a *further* *conditional* (could have chosen to choose otherwise), and the *regress* is *infinite*. The *analysis* *does* not *stop* the *regress*; it *relocates* it.

**(ii) The *counterexample* *objection*** (the *“*Frankfurt*”* *case*; §3.2). The *conditional* *analysis* *implies* that a person who *could* not have *chosen* *otherwise* *could* not have *done* *otherwise*, and the *Frankfurt* *cases* *show* that the *implication* is *false*.

**(iii) The *‘could* *have* *done’* *intuition***. The *conditional* *analysis* *reduces* the *categorical* *claim* (could have done) to a *hypothetical* *claim* (would have done if), and the *two* are *not* *the same* (the *intuition* of *categorical* *freedom* is *lost*).

## 3. Contemporary Compatibilism

### 3.1 Frankfurt's hierarchical model

**The *thesis*** (Frankfurt, “Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person,” 1971; “Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility,” 1969).

**The *structure*.**

| Level | The *state* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **First-order desires** | Desires to *do* or *have* something | The *desire* to *drink* |
| **Second-order desires** | Desires to *have* (or *not* have) a *first-order* *desire* | The *desire* to *not* *desire* to *drink* |
| **Second-order volitions** | Desires that a *first-order* *desire* be *effective* | The *desire* that the *desire* to *not* *drink* be *effective* |

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *freedom* of the *will* is the *capacity* to *have* the *will* one *wants* to *have* (the *hierarchical* *condition*).} \\
2. & \text{The *capacity* is *compatible* with *determinism* (the *second-order* *volitions* can be *determined*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Freedom is *compatible* with *determinism*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples*.**

| Case | The *will* | The *freedom* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *willing* addict** | The *first-order* *desire* (to take the drug) is *endorsed* by the *second-order* *volition* | *Free* |
| **The *unwilling* addict** | The *first-order* *desire* *conflicts* with the *second-order* *volition* | *Not* *free* |
| **The *person* who *acts* *against* her *second-order* *volition* | The *will* is *not* the *will* she *wants* | *Not* *free* |

**The *virtue*.** The *model* *captures* the *difference* between the *willing* and the *unwilling* *addict*, and the *difference* is *not* *captured* by the *classical* *conditional* *analysis*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *infinite* *regress*** (Watson, “Free Agency,” 1975). The *model* *requires* that the *second-order* *volitions* be *endorsed* by *third-order* *volitions*, and the *regress* is *infinite*. (Frankfurt's *reply*: the *regress* *terminates* in the *“*wholehearted*”* *identification*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(ii) The *‘wanting* *what* *one* *wants’* *objection***. The *model* *makes* *freedom* *a* *matter* of *coherence* between the *first*-*and* *second-order* *desires*, and the *coherence* is *not* *sufficient* for *freedom* (the *contented* *slave* *is* *not* *free*; the *“*happy* *slave*”* *objection*; Berlin, “Two Concepts of Liberty,” 1958).

**(iii) The *‘no* *independent* *criterion’* *objection***. The *model* *does* not *give* an *independent* *criterion* for *which* *second-order* *volitions* *count*, and the *choice* is *arbitrary*.

### 3.2 Frankfurt cases

**The *argument*** (Frankfurt, “Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility,” 1969).

**The *scenario***. Jones is *about* to *act*. Black, a *neuroscientist*, has *implanted* a *device* that will *cause* Jones to *act* if Jones does *not* *act* on his *own*. Jones *acts* on his *own* (the *device* is *not* *triggered*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Jones *acted* on his *own* (the *device* was *not* *triggered*).} \\
2. & \text{Jones *could not* have *done* *otherwise* (the *device* *would* have *caused* the *action*).} \\
3. & \text{Jones is *morally* *responsible* for the *action*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Moral *responsibility* does *not* *require* the *ability* to *do* *otherwise* (the *principle* *of* *alternate* *possibilities* is *false*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *case* *undermines* the *principle* *of* *alternate* *possibilities* (PAP), and it *supports* the *compatibilist* *position* (responsibility is *compatible* with *determinism*, because *responsibility* *does* not *require* the *ability* to *do* *otherwise*).

**The *objections*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *essays* in *Moral Responsibility and Alternative Possibilities*, ed. Widerker and McKenna, 2003).

**(i) The *flicker* *of* *freedom* *objection*. Jones *could* have *done* *otherwise* in a *minor* *respect* (he *could* have *flickered* his *eyebrow*), and the *flicker* *is* *sufficient* for *responsibility*. (The *reply*: the *flicker* is *not* the *relevant* *ability*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(ii) The *‘*responsibility*’* *intuition* *objection*. The *intuition* that Jones is *responsible* is *not* *reliable* (the *case* is *bizarre*), and the *intuition* *may* be *an* *artifact* of the *description*. (The *reply*: the *intuition* *survives* *variations*, and the *survival* *is* *evidence*; the *reply* is *contested*.)

**(iii) The *‘*derivative*’* *responsibility* *objection*. Jones is *responsible* *because* the *action* *flows* from *his* *character*, and the *responsibility* is *derivative* from the *character*. The *objection* *supports* the *compatibilist* *view*, and it *relocates* the *question* to the *nature* of *character*.

### 3.3 Reasons-responsive compatibilism

**The *thesis*** (Fischer & Ravizza, *Responsibility and Control*, 1998; Fischer, *My Way*, 2006; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *agent* is *responsible* if she is *reasons*-*responsive*: she *would* *act* *differently* if there were *sufficient* *reasons* to do so.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Agent } A \text{ is *responsible* for } \phi \quad \text{iff} \quad A \text{ is *reasons*-*responsive} \text{ (with respect to } \phi)
$$

**The *definition*.** A *mechanism* is *reasons*-*responsive* if it *produces* *different* *actions* in *response* to *different* *reasons* (in a *range* of *counterfactual* *scenarios*).

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *Frankfurt* *cases* (responsibility *does* not *require* the *ability* to *do* *otherwise*; it *requires* the *disposition* to *respond* to *reasons*), and it *fits* the *intuitions* about *responsibility*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*manipulation*’* *objection*** (Pereboom, *Living without Free Will*, 2001, Ch. 4). A *manipulated* *agent* can be *reasons*-*responsive* (the *manipulator* *designs* the *mechanism* to *respond* to *reasons*), and the *intuition* that the *manipulated* *agent* is *not* *responsible* *undermines* the *theory*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *independent* *criterion*’* *objection***. The *range* of *counterfactual* *scenarios* is *underdetermined*, and the *theory* does not *specify* the *range*.

**(iii) The *‘*reasons*’* *objection***. The *theory* *presupposes* a *theory* of *reasons* (what *counts* as a *reason*), and the *theory* is *itself* *contested* (Ch. 28, §1).

### 3.4 The state of compatibilism

**The *assessment*.** Compatibilism is the *dominant* *position* in *contemporary* *philosophy*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical (Hobbes, Hume)** | Fits the ordinary language | Too weak (the compulsive) |
| **Conditional analysis (Moore)** | Reduces the modal claim | The regress; the Frankfurt cases |
| **Hierarchical (Frankfurt)** | Captures the willing/unwilling distinction | The regress; the happy slave |
| **Reasons-responsive (Fischer)** | Fits the responsibility intuitions | The manipulation argument |

**The *most* *defensible* *version*.** A *reasons*-*responsive* *compatibilism* *combined* with a *hierarchical* *account* of *the* *will*, *qualified* by the *manipulation* *objection*.

## 4. The Consequence Argument and Incompatibilism

### 4.1 The consequence argument

**The *argument*** (van Inwagen, *An Essay on Free Will*, 1983, pp. 56–105; the *most* *influential* *argument* for *incompatibilism*).

**The *intuition***. If determinism is true, then our *actions* are the *consequences* of *laws* of nature and *events* in the *remote* past. But it is *not* *up* to us what the *laws* of nature are, and it is *not* *up* to us what happened before we were born. Therefore, it is *not* *up* to us what we do *now*.

**The *formal* *statement***. Let $N(p)$ mean “$p$ is true, and no one has (or ever had) any choice about whether $p$.”

**The *principles***.

$$
(\alpha) \quad \Box p \to Np
$$

$$
(\beta) \quad N(p \to q) \to (Np \to Nq)
$$

**The *argument***. Let $P_0$ be the *proposition* *describing* the *state* of the *world* at some *time* in the *remote* past; let $L$ be the *conjunction* of the *laws* of nature; let $P$ be the *proposition* *describing* the *agent*'s *action*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \Box((P_0 \wedge L) \to P) \quad \text{(determinism)} \\
2. & \Box(P_0 \to (L \to P)) \quad \text{(from 1)} \\
3. & N(P_0 \to (L \to P)) \quad \text{(from 2, by } \alpha) \\
4. & N P_0 \quad \text{(no one has a choice about the past)} \\
5. & N(L \to P) \quad \text{(from 3, 4, by } \beta) \\
6. & N L \quad \text{(no one has a choice about the laws)} \\
7. & N P \quad \text{(from 5, 6, by } \beta) \\
\hline
8. & \therefore \text{No one has a choice about } P \text{ (the action).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *shows* that *determinism* *implies* *the* *denial* of *free* *will*, and it is the *strongest* *argument* for *incompatibilism*.

### 4.2 The *objections* to the consequence argument

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *beta* *principle* *objection*** (the *transfer* *principle*). The *principle* $\beta$ is *contested*, and the *counterexamples* (the *“*flicker*”* *cases*; the *“*if* *I* *were* *taller*”* *cases*) *undermine* the *principle*.

**The *counterexample*** (McKay & Johnson 1996; the *contemporary* *literature*). Consider the *case*: $N(p)$, $N(p \to q)$, but *not* $N(q)$ — the *agent* is *free* with respect to $q$ even though $p$ and $p \to q$ are *beyond* the *agent*'s *control*. The *counterexample* *undermines* the *transfer* *principle*, and the *consequence* argument *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *counterexample* is *contested*, and the *defenders* of the *argument* (van Inwagen, the *contemporary* *literature*) *argue* that the *principle* is *sound* for the *relevant* *cases*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *choice*’* *objection***. The *argument* *assumes* that *no one has a choice* about the *past* and the *laws*, and the *assumption* is *contested* (the *compatibilist* *argues* that the *agent* *has* a *choice* about the *action* *even* if the *past* and the *laws* are *fixed*; the *choice* is *constituted* by the *agent*'s *deliberation*, which is *part* of the *causal* *chain*).

**(iii) The *‘*soft* *facts*’* *objection***. The *argument* *confuses* the *facts* about the *past* with the *facts* about the *agent*'s *abilities*; the *facts* about the *past* are *fixed*, but the *facts* about the *agent*'s *abilities* are *not* *reducible* to the *facts* about the *past* (the *“*soft* *facts*”* *are* *not* *fixed* by the *past*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

### 4.3 Hard determinism

**The *thesis***. *Determinism* is *true*, and *freedom* is *false*.

**The *statement***. Holbach, *The System of Nature* (1770); Skinner, *Beyond Freedom and Dignity* (1971); Pereboom, *Living without Free Will* (2001); the *contemporary* *hard* *determinists*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Determinism is *true*.} \\
2. & \text{Determinism is *incompatible* with *freedom* (the *consequence* argument).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Freedom is *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *accepts* the *consequence* of *determinism*, and it *avoids* the *compatibilist* *revisionism*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*responsibility*’* *objection***. The *view* *undermines* *responsibility*, and the *consequence* is *morally* *unacceptable* (the *practices* of *punishment*, *praise*, and *blame* would be *unjustified*).

**(ii) The *‘*revision*’* *objection***. The *view* *requires* a *radical* *revision* of *our* *self*-*conception*, and the *revision* is *not* *supported* by the *evidence* (the *intuition* of *freedom* is *powerful*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(iii) The *‘*quantum*’* *objection***. The *view* *assumes* *determinism*, and *determinism* is *not* *established* (quantum *mechanics* *suggests* *indeterminism* at the *fundamental* *level*; Ch. 19, §5). The *objection* *undermines* the *empirical* *premise*.

### 4.4 The state of incompatibilism

**The *assessment*.** Incompatibilism is the *most* *influential* *position* in the *metaphysics* of *free* *will*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections* to the *consequence* argument.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Consequence argument** | Formal; rigorous | The beta principle |
| **Hard determinism** | Consistent | The moral consequence |
| **Libertarianism** | Preserves freedom | The luck problem (§6) |

**The *most* *defensible* *version*.** A *qualified* *incompatibilism*: *determinism* *threatens* *freedom*, but the *consequence* argument is *not* *decisive*, and the *empirical* *status* of *determinism* is *uncertain*.

## 5. Libertarianism

### 5.1 Agent causation

**The *thesis*** (Chisholm, *Person and Object*, 1976; *Human Freedom and the Self*, 1964; Taylor, *Action and Purpose*, 1966; O'Connor, *Persons and Causes*, 2000). The *agent* is a *cause* of *actions* — a *substance*-*cause* that *initiates* the *causal* *chain* — and the *causation* is *not* *reducible* to *event*-*causation* (Ch. 18, §2).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Agent } A \text{ *causes* action } \phi \quad \text{(agent-causation, not event-causation)}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* is *free* and *responsible* (the *moral* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *freedom* *requires* that the *agent* be the *source* of the *action*.} \\
3. & \text{The *source* *requires* *agent*-*causation* (the *action* is *not* *determined* by *prior* *events*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Agent-*causation* is *real*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *preserves* the *intuition* of *agency* (the *agent* is the *source* of the *action*), and it *avoids* the *compatibilist* *revisionism*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*mystery*’* *objection***. The *agent*-*causation* *relation* is *not* *empirically* *accessible*, and the *notion* is *obscure* (what *is* the *relation*? how does the *agent* *cause* the *action*?).

**(ii) The *‘*exclusion*’* *objection***. If the *action* is *caused* by the *agent*, and the *neural* *events* *cause* the *action*, the *causal* *exclusion* *applies* (Ch. 18, §4; Ch. 23, §5).

**(iii) The *‘*luck*’* *objection***. The *agent*-*causation* *introduces* an *undetermined* *element* (the *agent*'s *causation* is *not* *determined* by *prior* *events*), and the *undetermined* *element* is *random* from the *agent*'s *perspective* (the *luck* *problem*; §6).

### 5.2 Event-causal libertarianism

**The *thesis*** (Kane, *The Significance of Free Will*, 1996; *A Contemporary Introduction to Free Will*, 2005). The *agent*'s *actions* are *caused* by *events* (beliefs, desires), and the *event*-*causation* *is* *indeterministic* (the *cause* *raises* the *probability* of the *action*, but does *not* *determine* it).

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Beliefs/desires} \ \xrightarrow{\text{indeterministic causation}} \ \text{action}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *mystery* of *agent*-*causation*, and it *fits* the *naturalistic* *worldview*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*randomness*’* *objection***. If the *action* is *indeterministic*, it is *random*, and the *randomness* *undermines* *freedom* (the *agent* *does* not *control* the *outcome*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *control*’* *objection***. The *agent* *cannot* *control* the *indeterministic* *process*, and the *control* is *required* for *freedom*.

**(iii) The *‘*luck*’* *objection***. The *indeterministic* *process* *introduces* *luck* into the *action* (the *agent*'s *control* is *undermined*; §6).

### 5.3 Non-causal libertarianism

**The *thesis*** (Ginet, *On Action*, 1990; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *agent*'s *actions* are *not* *caused* *at* *all*; the *agent* is the *source* of the *action*, and the *source* is a *primitive* *notion*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Agent} \ \to \ \text{action} \quad \text{(no causation, only *agency*)}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *mystery* of *agent*-*causation* and the *randomness* of the *event*-*causal* *view*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*unintelligibility*’* *objection***. The *notion* of *non*-*causal* *agency* is *unintelligible* (what *is* the *relation* between the *agent* and the *action*?).

**(ii) The *‘*causation*’* *objection***. The *standard* *view* of *action* *requires* that the *action* be *caused* by the *agent*'s *beliefs* and *desires* (Ch. 28, §1), and the *non*-*causal* *view* *undermines* the *standard* *view*.

### 5.4 The state of libertarianism

**The *assessment*.** Libertarianism is the *most* *ambitious* *position*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Version | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Agent causation** | Preserves the agent | The mystery; the exclusion |
| **Event-causal** | Naturalistic | The randomness |
| **Non-causal** | Avoids the mystery | The unintelligibility |

**The *most* *defensible* *version*.** A *qualified* *event*-*causal* *libertarianism*, *combined* with a *theory* of *agent* *control* that *explains* how the *agent* *shapes* the *indeterministic* *process* (the *“*luck*”* *problem* is *addressed* by the *agent*'s *character* *and* *deliberation*). The *position* is *contested* and *incomplete*.

## 6. The Luck Objection and the Disappearing Agent

### 6.1 The luck objection

**The *argument*** (Nagel, “Moral Luck,” 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *formulation* in van Inwagen, “Free Will Remains a Mystery,” 2000).

**The *scenario***. Consider a *libertarian* *agent* at a *moment* of *choice*. The *choice* is *not* *determined* by *prior* *events*. If the *choice* is *not* determined, then *whether* the *agent* *chooses* $A$ or $B$ is a *matter* of *chance* — the *outcome* is *not* *controlled* by the *agent*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *libertarian* *action* is *not* *determined* by *prior* *events*.} \\
2. & \text{If the *action* is *not* *determined*, it is a *matter* of *chance*.} \\
3. & \text{If the *action* is a *matter* of *chance*, the *agent* does *not* *control* it.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *libertarian* *action* is *not* *free*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *undermines* *libertarianism*, and it *supports* the *compatibilist* *position* (the *only* *way* to *have* *control* is to *have* the *action* *determined* by the *agent*'s *character* and *deliberation*).

**The *replies*.

**(i) The *‘*agent* *as* *source*’* *reply***. The *agent* is the *source* of the *action*, and the *source* *relation* is *not* *reducible* to the *event* *causation* (the *agent*-*causal* *reply*). The *reply* *is* *contested* (§5.1).

**(ii) The *‘*luck* *is* *ubiquitous*’* *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *luck* is *not* *unique* to *libertarianism*; it is *present* in any *account* of *action* (the *compatibilist* *account* also *involves* *luck*, because the *agent*'s *character* is *shaped* by *factors* *outside* her *control*). The *reply* *relocates* the *problem*, and it *undermines* the *distinction* between *libertarian* and *compatibilist* *freedom*.

**(iii) The *‘*control*’* *reply***. The *agent* *controls* the *action* *because* the *action* *flows* from the *agent*'s *deliberation*, and the *deliberation* is *not* *random* (it is *governed* by *reasons*). The *reply* *requires* a *theory* of *how* *reasons* *govern* the *indeterministic* *process*, and the *theory* is *contested* (Ch. 28, §1).

### 6.2 The disappearing agent

**The *argument*** (Strawson, “Libertarianism, Action, and Self-Determination,” 2000; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *scenario***. The *libertarian* *agent* *deliberates* between $A$ and $B$. The *deliberation* is *not* *determined*, and the *outcome* is *indeterminate*. The *question*: *what* *makes* the *outcome* the *agent*'s *action*?

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If the *action* is *not* *determined* by the *agent*'s *mental* *states*, the *agent* is *not* the *source* of the *action*.} \\
2. & \text{If the *action* *is* *determined* by the *agent*'s *mental* *states*, the *action* is *determined* (not free).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *libertarian* *agent* *disappears* (either the *agent* is *not* the *source*, or the *action* is *not* free).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *supports* the *compatibilist* *position* (the *agent* *is* the *source* *only* if the *action* is *determined* by the *agent*'s *mental* *states*), and it *undermines* *libertarianism*.

**The *replies*.

**(i) The *agent*-*causal* *reply***. The *agent* *causes* the *action* *independently* of the *mental* *states*, and the *causation* *is* *not* *determined* by the *states*. The *reply* *is* *contested* (§5.1).

**(ii) The *‘*deliberation*’* *reply***. The *deliberation* *is* the *process* *by* *which* the *agent* *shapes* the *action*, and the *process* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *states*. The *reply* *is* *promising* and *underdeveloped*.

### 6.3 The luck objection and the compatibilist

**The *problem* *for* the *compatibilist***. The *luck* *objection* is *not* *avoided* by the *compatibilist*: the *agent*'s *character* is *shaped* by *factors* *outside* her *control* (genetics, upbringing), and the *action* *flows* from the *character*. The *agent* is *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *character*, and therefore *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *action*.

**The *argument*** (Pereboom, *Living without Free Will*, 2001, Ch. 4; the *manipulation* *argument*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent*'s *character* is *shaped* by *factors* *outside* her *control*.} \\
2. & \text{If the *character* is *shaped* by *factors* *outside* the *agent*'s *control*, the *agent* is *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *character*.} \\
3. & \text{If the *agent* is *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *character*, she is *not* *ultimately* *responsible* for the *action*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *compatibilist* *responsibility* is *not* *ultimate*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *supports* a *form* of *scepticism* about *ultimate* *responsibility* (the *revisionist* *position*; Pereboom, Vargas), and it *undermines* the *compatibilist* *claim* that *responsibility* is *compatible* with *determinism*.

**The *replies*.

**(i) The *‘*responsibility* *does* *not* *require* *ultimacy*’* *reply***. The *responsibility* is *compatible* with *determinism* *without* *requiring* *ultimacy*: the *agent* is *responsible* *because* the *action* *flows* from *her* *character*, and the *character* *is* *hers* (the *compatibilist* *reply*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(ii) The *‘*revision*’* *reply*** (Vargas, *Building Better Beings*, 2013). The *responsibility* is *revised*: the *practices* of *praise* and *blame* are *justified* by their *forward*-*looking* *effects* (the *rehabilitation*, the *deterrence*, the *maintenance* of *social* *norms*), *not* by *desert*. The *reply* *accepts* the *luck* *objection*, and it *revises* the *practices*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *luck* *objection* is the *deepest* *challenge* to *both* the *libertarian* and the *compatibilist* *accounts*, and the *replies* are *contested*.

## 7. Empirical Challenges

### 7.1 Libet's experiments

**The *finding*** (Libet, *et al.*, “Time of Conscious Intention to Act,” 1983; Ch. 17, §3.2). The *readiness* *potential* (the *neural* *activity* that *precedes* a *voluntary* *action*) *begins* *~300* *ms* *before* the *agent* *reports* the *conscious* *intention* to *act*.

**The *interpretation*** (the *contemporary* *literature*).

| Interpretation | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *epiphenomenalist* *reading*** | The *conscious* *intention* is *epiphenomenal* (the *action* is *caused* by the *neural* *activity*, not by the *intention*) | Libet (1985); Wegner (2002) |
| **The *‘*post*-*hoc*’* *reading*** | The *conscious* *intention* is a *retrospective* *construction* (the *brain* *constructs* the *sense* of *agency* *after* the *action*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **The *‘*veto*’* *reading*** | The *conscious* *intention* *can* *veto* the *action* (the *“*free* *won't*”*; Libet 1985) | Libet |
| **The *‘*no* *implication*’* *reading*** | The *experiment* *measures* the *neural* *correlates* of *action*, not the *nature* of *freedom* | the *compatibilist* *tradition* |

**The *significance*.** The *finding* *is* *striking*, and its *interpretation* is *contested*. The *finding* *does* not *establish* the *epiphenomenalist* *reading* (the *readiness* *potential* may be *part* of the *process* *by* *which* the *agent* *forms* the *intention*, not a *cause* *independent* of it), and the *reading* is *disputed*.

### 7.2 Wegner and the illusion of conscious will

**The *thesis*** (Wegner, *The Illusion of Conscious Will*, 2002). The *experience* of *conscious* *will* is a *construction*: the *brain* *infers* that it *caused* the *action*, and the *inference* is *not* *reliable*.

**The *evidence*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *“*I* *spy*”* *experiment*; the *“*facilitated* *communication*”* *cases*; the *“*Ouija* *board*”* *cases*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *experience* of *conscious* *will* is *generated* by a *cognitive* *process* (the *interpretive* *system*).} \\
2. & \text{The *process* *can* *misrepresent* the *causation* (the *subject* *can* *experience* *will* *for* an *action* *she* *did* *not* *cause*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *experience* of *conscious* *will* is *not* *a* *reliable* *guide* to *causation*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *finding* *undermines* the *introspective* *evidence* for *free* *will*, and the *undermining* is *serious* (the *introspective* *evidence* is the *primary* *evidence* for *freedom*).

**The *objections*** (the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(i) The *‘*ecological*’* *objection***. The *experiments* *are* *artificial*, and the *conclusions* *do* not *generalize* to *ordinary* *action* (the *subject* *in* the *experiment* is *deceived*, and the *deception* *explains* the *illusion*).

**(ii) The *‘*responsibility*’* *objection***. The *experience* of *will* is *not* the *only* *evidence* for *freedom* (the *social* *practices* and the *moral* *intuitions* are *independent* *evidence*).

**(iii) The *‘*compatibilist*’* *objection***. The *finding* *undermines* the *libertarian* *account* of *freedom*, not the *compatibilist* *account* (the *compatibilist* *responsibility* is *not* *grounded* in the *experience* of *will*; it is *grounded* in *reasons*-*responsiveness*).

### 7.3 Situationism

**The *finding*** (Milgram, *Obedience to Authority*, 1974; Darley & Batson, “From Jerusalem to Jericho,” 1973; the *contemporary* *literature*). *Behaviour* is *strongly* *influenced* by *situational* *factors* (the *authority* *figure*, the *time* *pressure*, the *smell* of *fresh* *bread*), and the *influence* is *greater* than the *influence* of *character*.

**The *argument* (the *situationist* *challenge*; Doris, *Lack of Character*, 2002; Harman, “Moral Philosophy Meets Social Psychology,” 1999).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Behaviour is *strongly* *influenced* by *situational* *factors*.} \\
2. & \text{If *behaviour* is *strongly* *influenced* by *situational* *factors*, the *agent*'s *character* is *not* the *primary* *cause*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *agent* is *not* *responsible* (in the *ordinary* *sense*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *finding* *undermines* the *virtue* *ethics* (Ch. 45, §3) and the *character*-*based* *accounts* of *responsibility*, and it *supports* the *situationist* *view*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*statistical*’* *objection***. The *findings* are *statistical* (they *show* that *situations* *matter* on *average*, not that *character* does *not* *matter*). The *objection* *qualifies* the *conclusion*.

**(ii) The *‘*responsibility*’* *objection***. The *finding* *supports* the *compatibilist* *account*: the *agent* is *responsible* *even* if the *action* is *influenced* by *situation*, because the *action* *flows* from the *agent*'s *character* (which is *partly* *constituted* by the *situation*).

**(iii) The *‘*virtue*’* *objection***. The *finding* *supports* the *virtue* *ethics* (the *virtues* *are* *situation*-*sensitive*, and the *education* of *character* is *about* *shaping* the *situation*).

### 7.4 Predictive neuroscience

**The *finding*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *predictive* *processing* *framework*; Ch. 5, §5.2). The *brain* *predicts* the *action* *before* the *action* *occurs*, and the *prediction* *can* be *used* to *decode* the *action* (the *“*decoding*”* *experiments*; Haynes et al. 2008).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *neural* *activity* *predicts* the *action* *before* the *agent* is *conscious* of it.} \\
2. & \text{If the *neural* activity *predicts* the *action*, the *agent*'s *conscious* *decision* is *not* the *cause* of the *action*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *agent* is *not* the *source* of the *action*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *finding* *supports* the *epiphenomenalist* *reading* (§7.1), and it *undermines* the *libertarian* *account* of *freedom*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*prediction*’* *objection***. The *prediction* is *probabilistic* (the *decoding* *is* *imperfect*), and the *prediction* *does* not *establish* the *epiphenomenalist* *reading*.

**(ii) The *‘*compatibilist*’* *objection***. The *finding* *supports* the *compatibilist* *account* (the *agent*'s *decision* is *part* of the *process*, and the *responsibility* is *grounded* in the *process*, not in the *conscious* *moment*).

**(iii) The *‘*methodological*’* *objection***. The *decoding* *experiments* *operate* in *controlled* *settings*, and the *settings* *may* not *generalize* to *ordinary* *action*.

### 7.5 The state of the empirical challenge

**The *assessment*.** The *empirical* *findings* are *striking*, and their *interpretation* is *contested*. The *findings* *support* the *compatibilist* *revision* (the *responsibility* is *grounded* in *reasons*-*responsiveness*, not in the *conscious* *moment*), and they *undermine* the *libertarian* *account*. The *revision* is *defensible* and *incomplete*.

## 8. Moral Responsibility and Reactive Attitudes

### 8.1 Strawson's reactive attitudes

**The *thesis*** (Strawson, “Freedom and Resentment,” 1962; the *most* *influential* *essay* on *responsibility* in the *twentieth* *century*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *practice* of *responsibility* is *constituted* by the *reactive* *attitudes* (resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt).} \\
2. & \text{The *reactive* *attitudes* are *natural* *human* *responses*, not *theoretical* *claims*.} \\
3. & \text{The *question* of *free* *will* is the *question* of whether the *attitudes* are *appropriate*, and the *question* is *answered* by the *practice*, not by a *metaphysical* *theory*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *metaphysical* *question* is *misplaced* (the *practice* is *justified* by its *internal* *norms*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *dissolves* the *free*-*will* *problem* by *relocating* it to the *practice* of *responsibility*, and the *relocation* *is* *powerful* (the *attitudes* *are* *real*, and the *question* of their *justification* is *internal* to the *practice*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*justification*’* *objection***. The *practice* *may* be *unjustified* (the *attitudes* may be *based* on a *false* *belief* in *freedom*), and the *theory* *does* not *address* the *question*.

**(ii) The *‘*objectivity*’* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *“*objective* *attitude”*). The *reactive* *attitudes* can be *suspended* (the *objective* *attitude*; the *clinical* *attitude*), and the *suspension* *shows* that the *attitudes* are *not* *necessary* (the *agent* *can* *take* the *objective* *attitude*, and the *suspension* *undermines* the *theory*).

**(iii) The *‘*manipulation*’* *objection***. The *manipulated* *agent* *provokes* the *reactive* *attitudes* *less* *strongly* (the *intuition* is that the *manipulated* *agent* is *not* *responsible*), and the *theory* *does* not *explain* the *difference*.

### 8.2 Attributability and accountability

**The *distinction*** (Watson, “Two Faces of Responsibility,” 1996; Shoemaker, “Attributability, Answerability, and Accountability,” 2015; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| Kind | The *question* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Attributability** | Is the *action* *attributable* to the *agent* (does it *express* her *character*)? | The *person* who *acts* *cruelly* |
| **Accountability** | Is the *agent* *accountable* (is she *liable* to *praise* or *blame*)? | The *person* who is *blamed* for the *action* |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *attributability* *requires* that the *action* *express* the *agent*'s *character* (the *‘*deep* *self*’* *condition*).} \\
2. & \text{The *accountability* *requires* that the *agent* be *reasons*-*responsive*.} \\
3. & \text{The *two* *conditions* are *distinct* (the *attributability* *can* be *present* *without* the *accountability*, and vice versa).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *two* *faces* of *responsibility* are *distinct*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *distinction* *captures* the *complexity* of *responsibility* (the *action* can be *attributable* to the *agent* without the *agent* being *accountable*), and it *fits* the *practices* (the *clinical* *attitude* *attributes* the *action* without *blaming*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*unity*’* *objection***. The *two* *faces* are *not* *independent* (the *accountability* *presupposes* the *attributability*), and the *distinction* *does* not *cut* *as* *deeply* as *Watson* *claims*.

**(ii) The *‘*normative*’* *objection***. The *distinction* *presupposes* a *normative* *theory* (what *counts* as *character*), and the *theory* is *contested*.

### 8.3 Reactive attitudes and the *objective* *attitude*

**The *distinction*** (Strawson 1962, §5). The *reactive* *attitude* is the *participant* *attitude* (the *agent* is *engaged* with the *other* *as* a *person*); the *objective* *attitude* is the *clinical* *attitude* (the *agent* is *observed* as an *object*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *reactive* *attitude* *presupposes* the *agent*'s *responsibility*.} \\
2. & \text{The *objective* *attitude* *suspends* the *responsibility* (the *agent* is *observed*, not *engaged*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *two* *attitudes* are *distinct* *stances*, and the *choice* *between* them is a *choice* about *how* to *relate* to the *other*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *supports* the *Strawsonian* *view* (the *practice* of *responsibility* is *not* *grounded* in a *metaphysical* *theory*, but in the *participant* *stance*), and it *raises* the *question* of *when* the *objective* *attitude* is *appropriate* (the *clinical* *context*, the *manipulated* *agent*).

### 8.4 The state of the responsibility debate

**The *assessment*.** The *Strawsonian* *view* is the *most* *influential* *account* of *responsibility*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strawsonian** | Responsibility is constituted by reactive attitudes | Dissolves the metaphysical question | The justification question |
| **Attributability** | Responsibility is the expression of character | Fits the deep-self condition | Unity problem |
| **Accountability** | Responsibility is reasons-responsiveness | Fits the compatibilist account | Manipulation problem |
| **Revisionist** | Responsibility is a social practice | Fits the empirical findings | The moral cost |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *Strawsonian* *view*: the *reactive* *attitudes* are the *foundation* of *responsibility*, and the *question* of their *justification* is *answered* by the *practice* (the *practice* is *justified* by its *internal* *norms*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *practice* *may* be *revised* by the *empirical* *findings*.

## 9. Free Will in Law and Life

### 9.1 The legal conception

**The *question***. What *conception* of *free* *will* does the *law* *presuppose*?

**The *answer***. The *law* *presupposes* a *minimal* *conception*: the *agent* *acted* *voluntarily* (she *intended* the *act*, and she was *not* *coerced* or *incapacitated*), and the *voluntariness* is *sufficient* for *legal* *responsibility*.

**The *criteria***.

| Criterion | The *question* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Voluntariness** | Did the *agent* *intend* the *act*? | The *intentional* *killing* vs. the *accident* |
| **Capacity** | Did the *agent* *understand* the *nature* of the *act*? | The *insanity* *defence* |
| **Coercion** | Was the *agent* *coerced*? | The *duress* *defence* |
| **Automatism** | Was the *agent* *conscious*? | The *sleepwalking* *case* |

**The *significance*.** The *legal* *conception* is *compatibilist* (the *law* does *not* *require* *libertarian* *freedom*), and the *conception* *fits* the *practices* of *punishment* and *excuse*. The *legal* *debate* is *about* the *boundaries* of the *criteria* (the *insanity* *defence*, the *diminished* *capacity*, the *automatism*), not about the *metaphysical* *question*.

### 9.2 The moral and political stakes

**The *question***. Does the *free* *will* *debate* *matter* for *morality* and *politics*?

**The *answer***. The *debate* *matters* for three *reasons*.

**(i) The *justification* of *punishment***. The *retributive* *justification* *requires* *responsibility* (Ch. 26, §9.3), and the *revision* of *responsibility* *undermines* the *justification*.

**(ii) The *distribution* of *blame***. The *practices* of *blame* (the *criminal* *justice* *system*, the *social* *sanctions*) *presuppose* *responsibility*, and the *revision* *affects* the *distribution*.

**(iii) The *self*-*conception***. The *experience* of *freedom* is *central* to *our* *self*-*conception* (the *sense* of *agency*, the *sense* of *authorship*), and the *revision* *undermines* the *self*-*conception*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *free* *will* *debate* *determines* the *justification* of *punishment*.} \\
2. & \text{The *justification* of *punishment* is a *central* *moral* *question*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *free* *will* *debate* is *morally* *central*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *debate* is *not* an *academic* *curiosity*; it is *central* to *morality*, *law*, and *politics*.

### 9.3 The *revisionist* *position*

**The *thesis*** (Vargas, *Building Better Beings*, 2013; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *practices* should be *revised* in the *light* of the *compatibilist* and *empirical* *findings*: the *practices* should be *forward*-*looking* (rehabilitation, deterrence, the *maintenance* of *social* *norms*) rather than *backward*-*looking* (desert, retribution).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *retributive* *justification* *requires* *ultimate* *responsibility* (the *luck* *objection* *undermines* the *requirement*).} \\
2. & \text{The *forward*-*looking* *justification* *does* not *require* *ultimate* *responsibility*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *practices* should be *revised* in the *forward*-*looking* *direction*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *preserves* the *practices* (the *responsibility* *attributions* *remain*) and *revises* their *justification* (the *justification* is *forward*-*looking*), and the *revision* *is* *defensible*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*desert*’* *objection***. The *revision* *abandons* the *desert* *intuition* (the *agent* *deserves* the *blame*), and the *intuition* *is* *powerful*.

**(ii) The *‘*deterrence*’* *objection***. The *forward*-*looking* *justification* *can* *justify* *punishing* the *innocent* (if the *punishment* *deters*), and the *consequence* is *unacceptable*.

**(iii) The *‘*revision*’* *objection***. The *revision* *requires* a *radical* *change* in the *practices*, and the *change* is *not* *supported* by the *evidence*.

## 10. The State of the Debate

### 10.1 What is settled

- **The *problem* has *three* *forms* (fatalism, determinism, luck), and the *forms* are *distinct*. The *confusion* of the *forms* is the *commonest* *error*.
- **The *consequence* *argument* is the *strongest* *argument* for *incompatibilism*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections* to the *transfer* *principle*.
- **Compatibilism is the dominant position**, and it is *qualified* by the *Frankfurt* *cases* and the *manipulation* *argument*.
- **The *luck* *objection* is the *deepest* *challenge* to *both* *libertarianism* and *compatibilism*, and the *replies* are *contested*.
- **The *empirical* *findings* (Libet, Wegner, situationism, predictive neuroscience) are *striking*, and their *interpretation* is *contested*. The *findings* *support* the *compatibilist* *revision*, and they *undermine* the *libertarian* *account*.
- **The *Strawsonian* *view* is the *most* *influential* *account* of *responsibility*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.
- **The *legal* *conception* is *compatibilist*, and it *fits* the *practices* of *punishment* and *excuse*.
- **The *revisionist* *position* is *promising*, and it is *contested*.

### 10.2 What is open

- **The *truth* of *determinism*.** Is the *world* *deterministic*? (The *quantum* *indeterminacy* *suggests* *no*, but the *interpretation* is *contested*; Ch. 19, §5.)
- **The *compatibility* of *freedom* and *determinism*.** Is *compatibilism* *defensible*? (The *consequence* argument and the *luck* objection are *contested*.)
- **The *solution* to the *luck* *objection*.** Can *libertarianism* *survive* the *objection*?
- **The *justification* of *responsibility*.** Is the *practice* *justified* by its *internal* *norms*, or by *external* *considerations*?
- **The *revision* of the *practices*.** Should the *practices* be *revised* in the *light* of the *findings*?

### 10.3 The methodological lesson

The *chapter* is a *case* *study* in the *method* of *Part* I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* among the *three* *forms* and the *positions*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *Laplacean* *demon*, the *Frankfurt* *cases*, the *manipulation* *cases* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *consequence* *argument* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *free* *will* is a *field* in *transition*. The *libertarian* *position* is *untenable*; the *compatibilist* *position* is *dominant* but *incomplete*; the *revisionist* *position* is *promising* but *contested*; the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled* *answer*, but a *sharper* *understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 10.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classical compatibilism** | Freedom = absence of external constraint | Fits the ordinary language | Too weak |
| **Conditional analysis** | Could have done otherwise = would have if chosen | Reduces the modal claim | The regress |
| **Frankfurt** | Freedom = harmony of first- and second-order desires | Captures the willing/unwilling distinction | The happy slave |
| **Reasons-responsive** | Responsibility = reasons-responsiveness | Fits the intuitions | The manipulation argument |
| **Consequence argument** | Determinism is incompatible with freedom | Formal | The beta principle |
| **Hard determinism** | Freedom is false | Consistent | The moral cost |
| **Agent causation** | The agent is the source | Preserves agency | The mystery |
| **Event-causal libertarianism** | Indeterministic causation | Naturalistic | The randomness |
| **Strawsonian** | Responsibility = reactive attitudes | Dissolves the metaphysical question | The justification question |
| **Revisionism** | The practices should be forward-looking | Fits the empirical findings | The desert intuition |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Summary

- **The *problem* of *free* *will* has *three* *forms** — fatalism, determinism, luck — and the *forms* are *distinct*. The *confusion* of the *forms* is the *commonest* *error*.
- **Compatibilism** (Hobbes, Hume, Frankfurt, Fischer) *holds* that freedom is *compatible* with *determinism*. The *classical* *version* (Hobbes) is *too* *weak*; the *hierarchical* *version* (Frankfurt) *faces* the *happy* *slave*; the *reasons*-*responsive* *version* (Fischer) *faces* the *manipulation* *argument*.
- **The *consequence* *argument*** (van Inwagen) is the *strongest* *argument* for *incompatibilism*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections* to the *transfer* *principle*.
- **Hard determinism** (Holbach, Pereboom) *accepts* the *consequence* of *determinism*, and it *faces* the *moral* *cost* and the *quantum* *objection*.
- **Libertarianism** (Chisholm, Kane, Ginet) *holds* that freedom *requires* *indeterminism*, and it *faces* the *luck* *objection* and the *disappearing* *agent*.
- **The *luck* *objection*** is the *deepest* *challenge* to *both* *libertarianism* and *compatibilism*, and the *replies* are *contested*.
- **The *empirical* *findings*** (Libet, Wegner, situationism, predictive neuroscience) are *striking*, and their *interpretation* is *contested*. The *findings* *support* the *compatibilist* *revision*.
- **The *Strawsonian* *view*** *grounds* *responsibility* in the *reactive* *attitudes*, and it is *qualified* by the *justification* *question*.
- **The *legal* *conception*** is *compatibilist*, and it *fits* the *practices* of *punishment* and *excuse*.
- **The *revisionist* *position*** (Vargas) *proposes* a *forward*-*looking* *justification* of the *responsibility* *practices*, and it is *promising* and *contested*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *reasons*-*responsive* *compatibilism*, *combined* with a *Strawsonian* *account* of *responsibility*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *luck* *objection* is *unresolved*.
