# Chapter 47 — Justice, Freedom, and the Political Order

> “Justice is the first virtue of social institutions, as truth is of systems of thought.”
> — John Rawls, *A Theory of Justice* (1971), §1

> “Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains.”
> — Jean-Jacques Rousseau, *The Social Contract* (1762), I.1

> “Everything is what it is: liberty is liberty, not equality or fairness or justice.”
> — Isaiah Berlin, “Two Concepts of Liberty” (1958), in *Four Essays on Liberty* (1969)

> “Individuals have rights, and there are things no person or group may do to them (without violating their rights).”
> — Robert Nozick, *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974), p. ix

---

Chapter 45 examined the *content* of the moral judgment (the *good*, the *right*, the *virtuous*); Chapter 46 examined its *efficacy* (the *machinery* by which a *judgment* becomes a *motive* and an *attribution*). This chapter examines the *architecture* — the *institutions* through which the *moral* *judgments* are *enforced*, the *coercion* through which the *collective* *life* is *organized*, and the *principles* that *justify* or *condemn* the *coercion*.

The *transition* is *not* a *leap* into a *new* *subject*. The *political* *philosophy* is the *normative* *ethics* *applied* to the *institutional* *scale*, and the *problems* are the *same* (the *aggregation*, the *demandingness*, the *separateness*, the *responsibility*) *writ* *large* — plus the *problems* that *arise* *only* *collectively* (the *authority*, the *legitimacy*, the *boundary*, the *distributive* *metric*). The *distinction* between the *individual* and the *collective* *agent* is *not* the *distinction* between the *moral* and the *political*; it is the *distinction* between the *micro*-*scale* and the *macro*-*scale* of the *same* *normative* *question*.

The *chapter* proceeds in *nine* *movements*. §1 examines the *legitimacy* *problem* (the *authority*, the *consent*, the *anarchist* *challenge*). §2 examines the *theories* of *justice* (the *utilitarian*, the *liberal* *egalitarian*, the *libertarian*, the *communitarian*, the *Marxist*, the *republican*). §3 examines the *distributive* *justice* (the *Rawlsian*, the *Dworkinian*, the *luck*-*egalitarian*, the *capabilities*, the *relational*). §4 examines the *liberty* (the *negative*, the *positive*, the *republican*, the *autonomy*, the *paternalism*). §5 examines the *equality* (of *what*?, the *priority* *debate*, the *levelling* *down*, the *moral* *equality*, the *discrimination*). §6 examines the *democracy* (the *instrumental*, the *intrinsic*, the *deliberative*, the *social* *choice*, the *representation*, the *judicial* *review*, the *epistocracy*). §7 examines the *global* *justice* (the *boundaries*, the *distributive*, the *immigration*, the *climate*, the *reparations*). §8 examines the *rights* (the *Hohfeldian* *analysis*, the *foundations*, the *human* *rights*, the *limits*, the *conflicts*). §9 gives the *verdict*.

## 1. The Legitimacy Problem

### 1.1 The problem stated

**The *problem***. The *state* *claims* the *authority* to *coerce* (the *taxation*, the *punishment*, the *conscription*, the *regulation*), and the *coercion* *would* *be* *wrong* (the *violation* of the *autonomy*, the *freedom*, the *rights*) *unless* it is *justified* by something *more* than the *power* (the *“*the* *strongest* *is* *not* *strong* *enough*”*; Rousseau 1762, I.3), and the *something* *more* is the *legitimacy*.

**The *distinctions*** (the *essential* *taxonomy*).

| The *concept* | The *content* | The *question* |
|---|---|---|
| **Justice** | The *correct* *distribution*, the *correct* *rules* | Is the *coercion* *just*? |
| **Legitimacy** | The *moral* *standing* to *impose* the *rules* | May the *coercion* *be* *exercised*? |
| **Obligation** | The *duty* of the *subject* to *comply* | Must the *agent* *obey*? |
| **Authority** | The *power* to *bind* the *judgment* and the *will* | Does the *command* *pre*-*empt* the *reasons*? |
| **De facto power** | The *actual* *capacity* to *coerce* | Does the *coercion* *occur*? |

**The *logical* *relations***.

$$
\text{De facto power} \ \nrightarrow \ \text{Legitimacy} \ \nrightarrow \ \text{Obligation} \ \nrightarrow \ \text{Justice}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *distinctions* *prevent* the *commonest* *confusions* (the *identification* of the *legality* with the *legitimacy*, the *identification* of the *legitimacy* with the *justice*, the *identification* of the *obligation* with the *coercion*), and *each* is a *distinct* *philosophical* *problem*, and *each* has a *distinct* *literature*.

**The *state* *of* *nature***. The *thought* *experiment* (the *Hobbesian*, the *Lockean*, the *Rousseauvian*, the *Nozickian*) *isolates* the *problem* (the *coercion* *without* the *state*) and the *solution* (the *contract*, the *convention*, the *invisible* *hand*, the *natural* *duty*), and the *experiment* *tests* the *theories* (the *Hobbesian* *conclusion*: the *absolute* *sovereignty*; the *Lockean*: the *limited* *government*; the *Rousseauvian*: the *general* *will*; the *Nozickian*: the *minimal* *state*; the *contemporary*: the *hypothetical* *contract*, the *justification* *to* the *rational* *agent*). The *experiment* is *not* the *history* (the *anthropology* *is* *not* at *stake*); it is the *device* of *justification* (Ch. 3, §2).

### 1.2 The concept of authority

**The *thesis*** (Raz, *The Morality of Freedom*, 1986, Ch. 2–4; “The Problem of Authority,” 2006; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *authority* is the *power* to *change* the *reasons* of the *subjects* (the *second*-*order* *reason*: the *command* *excludes* the *first*-*order* *reasons*; the *pre*-*emption*), and the *authority* is *justified* by the *service* *conception* (the *authority* *serves* the *subjects* *by* *helping* them *comply* with the *reasons* *that* *apply* to them), and the *normal* *justification* *thesis* (the *NJT*) *states* the *condition*.

**The *structure*** (the *NJT*).

$$
\text{Authority of } A \text{ over } S \ \text{is justified} \ \iff \ S \ \text{is more likely to comply with the reasons that apply to him by following } A \ \text{than by deciding for himself}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *authority* is the *power* to *change* the *reasons* (the *pre*-*emption*; the *second*-*order* *role*).} \\
2. & \text{The *power* *must* be *justified* by the *service* (the *subjects*' *interests*, the *compliance* with the *reasons*).} \\
3. & \text{The *service* *requires* the *superior* *reliability* (the *NJT*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *authority* *is justified* *when* the *NJT* *holds* (the *Raz* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *authority* in the *service* (the *instrumental* *insight*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *absolute* *sovereignty* (the *authority* *is* *conditional* on the *service*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *expertise*: the *doctor*'s *authority*; the *law*'s *authority*; the *judge*'s).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *pre*-*emption*’* *objection*. The *pre*-*emption* *is* *not* *necessary* (the *agent* can *weigh* the *command* *among* the *reasons*; the *first*-*order* *account* *suffices*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Raz 1986, Ch. 2). The *authority* *would* be *pointless* *without* the *pre*-*emption* (the *agent* *consults* the *authority* *in* *order* *not* to *deliberate* *again*; the *first*-*order* *account* *collapses* into the *advice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *pre*-*emption* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *autonomy*’* *objection* (Wolff 1970; §1.5). The *authority* *conflicts* with the *autonomy* (the *agent* *must* *legislate* for *himself*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Raz 1986, Ch. 4; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *autonomy* *is* *not* the *absence* of the *authority* but the *capacity* to *choose* the *authorities* (the *autonomy* *is* *satisfied* *by* the *well*-*justified* *authority*; the *service* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *compatibility* in the *service*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *autonomy* *objection* *retains* the *force* for the *moral* *autonomy*).

### 1.3 Consent and the social contract

**The *thesis*** (Locke, *Two Treatises of Government*, 1689, II. §§95–99; Rousseau 1762, I.6; the *contemporary* *literature*; §1.4). The *political* *authority* *derives* from the *consent* of the *governed* (the *actual* *consent*, the *tacit* *consent*, the *hypothetical* *consent*), and the *consent* *grounds* the *obligation* and the *legitimacy*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Political authority} \ = \ \text{the *consent* of the *governed*} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *legitimate* *coercion*}
$$

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual consent** | The *explicit* *promise* to *obey* | The *few* *consent* (the *naturalization*; the *birth*) |
| **Tacit consent** | The *residence*, the *benefits*, the *non*-*emigration* | The *no* *choice* (the *Humean* *objection*: the *poor* *cannot* *emigrate*) |
| **Hypothetical consent** | The *rational* *agreement* (the *original* *position*, the *contract*) | The *no* *binding* (the *hypothetical* *consent* *binds* *no* *one*; the *Dworkinian* *objection*) |

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Humean* *objection*** (Hume, “Of the Original Contract,” 1748). The *tacit* *consent* *is* *not* *real* (the *peasant* *who* *cannot* *leave*; the *subject* *who* *does* not *know*; the *allegiance* *is* *habit*, *not* *consent*), and the *original* *contract* *is* *a* *fiction* (the *history* *is* *not* the *justification*), and the *consent* *theory* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *hypothetical* *consent*; Rawls 1971, §3; Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5). The *consent* is *the* *device* of *justification* (the *hypothetical* *agreement* *tests* the *principles*), *not* the *historical* *fact* (the *Humean* *objection* *misses* the *point*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *legitimacy* in the *justification*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *hypothetical* *contract* *grounds* the *justice*, *not* the *obligation*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(ii) The *no* *obligation* *objection*** (Simmons, *Moral Principles and Political Obligations*, 1979, Ch. 3–4). The *hypothetical* *consent* *does* not *ground* the *obligation* (the *obligation* *requires* the *actual* *act*; the *hypothetical* *agent* *never* *acted*), and the *contemporary* *philosophy* *abandons* the *consent* (the *philosophical* *anarchism*; §1.5), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *natural* *duty*; Rawls 1971, §18–19; Waldron, “Special Ties and Natural Duties,” 1993). The *obligation* *is* *grounded* in the *natural* *duty* of *justice* (the *duty* *to* *support* the *just* *institutions*; the *no* *consent* *required*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *obligation* in the *duty*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *no* *special* *obligation*; the *particularity* *problem*; Simmons 1979, Ch. 5).

### 1.4 The fair play and the natural duty

**The *theses***.

**(i) The *fair* *play*** (Hart, “Are There Any Natural Rights?” 1955; Rawls, “Legal Obligation and the Duty of Fair Play,” 1964; Simmons 1979, Ch. 5). The *agent* *who* *accepts* the *benefits* of a *cooperative* *scheme* *owes* a *duty* of *fair* *play* (the *reciprocity*), and the *duty* *grounds* the *obligation* to the *scheme*.

**The *objection***. The *fair* *play* *requires* the *voluntary* *acceptance* of the *benefits* (the *non*-*excludable* *public* *goods* *are* *not* *accepted*), and the *contemporary* *states* *provide* the *public* *goods* *whether* *or* *not* the *agent* *accepts* them, and the *obligation* *fails* (the *Simmons* *objection*).

**(ii) The *natural* *duty* of *justice*** (Rawls 1971, §19; Waldron 1993). The *duty* *to* *support* and *comply* with the *just* *institutions* *applies* to *all* the *agents* (the *natural* *duty*; the *no* *consent*), and the *duty* *grounds* the *obligation* to the *just* *state*.

**The *objection***. The *natural* *duty* *does* not *ground* the *particular* *obligation* (the *duty* *to* *support* the *just* *institutions* *in* *general* *does* not *pick* *out* the *own* *state*; the *particularity* *problem*; Simmons 1979, Ch. 5), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *significance*.** The *two* *theses* *show* the *difficulty* of the *political* *obligation* (the *general* *duty* *without* the *particular* *tie*; the *particular* *tie* *without* the *consent*), and the *difficulty* is the *core* of the *philosophical* *anarchism* (§1.5), and it *grounds* the *contemporary* *debate* (the *associative* *obligation*: the *membership*, the *identity*, the *gratitude*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

### 1.5 The anarchist challenge

**The *thesis*** (Wolff, *In Defense of Anarchism*, 1970, Ch. 1; the *philosophical* *anarchism*; Simmons 1979; Raz 1986, Ch. 4; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *autonomy* (the *duty* to *legislate* for *oneself*) *conflicts* with the *authority* (the *duty* to *obey*), and the *conflict* *is* *irresolvable* (the *autonomy* *requires* the *first*-*person* *judgment*, and the *authority* *requires* the *deference*), and the *political* *authority* *is* *illegitimate*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Autonomy (the *duty* of *self*-*legislation*)} \ \wedge \ \text{Authority (the *duty* of *obedience*)} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{Contradiction}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *autonomy* *requires* the *agent* to *act* *on* *her* *own* *judgment* (the *Kantian* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *authority* *requires* the *agent* to *obey* *the* *command* *regardless* of the *own* *judgment* (the *deference*).} \\
3. & \text{The *two* *cannot* be *reconciled* (the *contradiction*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *political* *authority* *is* *illegitimate* (the *philosophical* *anarchism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *argument* *is* the *strongest* *case* *against* the *legitimacy* (the *autonomy* is the *foundational* *value*), and it *divides* the *philosophical* *anarchism* (the *no* *legitimate* *state*; the *no* *general* *obligation*; the *a* *posteriori* *anarchism*) from the *political* *anarchism* (the *no* *state* *at* *all*; the *institutions* *without* the *coercion*), and it *grounds* the *debate* (the *reply* *must* *reconcile* the *autonomy* *with* the *authority*).

**The *replies***.

**(i) The *service* *reply*** (§1.2; Raz 1986, Ch. 4). The *autonomy* *is* *satisfied* *by* the *well*-*justified* *authority* (the *agent* *chooses* the *reliable* *authority*; the *service* *insight*), and the *authority* *serves* the *autonomy* (the *agent* *is* *more* *likely* to *comply* with the *reasons*), and the *reply* *reconciles* the *two*.

**The *objection***. The *service* *reply* *rescues* the *theoretical* *authority* (the *advice*) and *not* the *practical* *authority* (the *command*; the *pre*-*emption*), and the *autonomy* *requires* the *first*-*person* *judgment*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *deliberative* *reply*** (Christiano, *The Constitution of Equality*, 2008, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *autonomy* *is* *satisfied* *by* the *deliberation* (the *agent* *participates* in the *collective* *will*-*formation*), and the *authority* *is* *the* *collective* *autonomy* (the *democratic* *insight*; §6), and the *reply* *reconciles* the *two*.

**The *objection***. The *deliberative* *reply* *rescues* the *democratic* *authority* (the *collective* *autonomy*) *and* *not* the *individual* *autonomy* (the *dissenter* *is* *bound* *against* the *own* *judgment*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *a* *posteriori* *anarchism*** (Simmons 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *authority* *is* *legitimate* *when* the *actual* *conditions* *hold* (the *consent*, the *fair* *play*, the *natural* *duty*), and the *conditions* *rarely* *hold* (the *actual* *states* *fail*), and the *reply* *limits* the *anarchism* to the *actual* *states* (the *no* *general* *obligation*) and *not* the *ideal* (the *legitimate* *state* *is* *possible*).

**The *objection***. The *a* *posteriori* *anarchism* *concedes* the *anarchist* *conclusion* (the *actual* *states* *are* *illegitimate*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.6 The state of the legitimacy debate

**The *assessment***. The *legitimacy* is *defensible* in the *service* and the *deliberative* forms, and the *anarchist* *challenge* is *serious*, and the *obligation* is *contested*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Consent** | The *voluntariness* | The *few* *consent*; the *Humean* |
| **Fair play** | The *reciprocity* | The *non*-*acceptance* |
| **Natural duty** | The *universality* | The *particularity* |
| **Service** | The *reconciliation* | The *pre*-*emption* |
| **Anarchism** | The *autonomy* | The *no* *authority* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *service* *conception* *combined* with the *deliberative* *democracy*: the *authority* *is* *justified* *when* it *serves* the *compliance* with the *reasons* (the *service* *insight*), *and* the *subjects* *participate* in the *collective* *will*-*formation* (the *deliberative* *insight*), *and* the *obligation* *is* *grounded* in the *natural* *duty* *plus* the *participation* (the *natural* *duty* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *particularity* and the *anarchist* *challenge* *remain* *contested*.

## 2. Theories of Justice

### 2.1 The landscape

**The *theses***. The *theories* of *justice* *differ* on the *currency* (the *utility*, the *liberty*, the *self*-*ownership*, the *primary* *goods*, the *capabilities*, the *non*-*domination*), the *unit* (the *individual*, the *group*, the *class*, the *people*), the *method* (the *contract*, the *evolution*, the *critique*, the *practice*), and the *site* (the *state*, the *global* *order*, the *institutions*).

**The *table***.

| The *theory* | The *currency* | The *unit* | The *site* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utilitarian** | The *utility* (the *welfare*) | The *individual* (the *aggregate*) | The *state* |
| **Liberal egalitarian** | The *primary* *goods*, the *resources* | The *free* *and* *equal* *person* | The *basic* *structure* |
| **Libertarian** | The *self*-*ownership*, the *entitlements* | The *individual* | The *minimal* *state* |
| **Communitarian** | The *common* *good*, the *practice* | The *community* | The *polity* |
| **Marxist** | The *freedom*, the *non*-*exploitation* | The *class* | The *mode* of *production* |
| **Republican** | The *non*-*domination* | The *citizen* | The *constitution* |
| **Feminist** | The *care*, the *relational* *equality* | The *relational* *agent* | The *household*, the *structure* |

**The *significance*.** The *theories* *answer* the *different* *questions* (the *aggregation*, the *entitlement*, the *common* *good*, the *class*, the *freedom*), and the *choice* *depends* on the *metaethical* *position* (Ch. 44) and on the *normative* *theory* (Ch. 45), and the *most* *defensible* *theories* *combine* the *insights* (the *hybrid*; §9.4).

### 2.2 Utilitarian justice

**The *thesis*** (Bentham 1789, Ch. 1; Mill 1861; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *just* *institutions* *maximize* the *aggregate* *welfare*, and the *justice* *is* the *efficient* *distribution* (the *welfarist* *metric*), and the *distribution* *follows* the *aggregation* (the *diminishing* *marginal* *utility*: the *transfer* to the *poor* *increases* the *total*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *just* *institutions* *maximize* the *good* (the *consequentialist* *insight*; Ch. 45, §1).} \\
2. & \text{The *good* *is* the *welfare* (the *hedonism*, the *desire*; Ch. 48, §1).} \\
3. & \text{The *institutions* *affect* the *welfare* (the *incentives*, the *redistribution*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *just* *institutions* *maximize* the *aggregate* *welfare* (the *utilitarian* *justice*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *is* *simple* (the *single* *metric*), and it *grounds* the *redistribution* (the *diminishing* *marginal* *utility*), and it *fits* the *policy* (the *cost–benefit* *analysis*; Ch. 51).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *separateness* *objection*** (Rawls 1971, §5). The *utilitarianism* *does* not *take* *seriously* the *distinction* *between* the *persons* (the *aggregation* *treats* the *society* *as* a *single* *agent*; the *sacrifice* of the *few* for the *many*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *utilitarianism* *can* *accommodate* the *separateness* (the *rule*-*utilitarianism*, the *prioritarianism*; Ch. 45, §1.3–1.4), and the *reply* *grounds* the *individual* *concern* in the *aggregation* (the *equal* *weight*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *sacrifice* *remains* *possible*).

**(ii) The *utility* *monster* *objection*** (Nozick 1974, pp. 41–42). The *aggregation* *would* *require* the *sacrifice* of the *many* for the *one* (the *monster* *who* *derives* the *infinite* *pleasure*), and the *monstrous* *conclusion* *refutes* the *aggregation*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *monster* *is* *impossible* (the *diminishing* *marginal* *utility*; the *psychology*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *utilitarianism* in the *empirical* *limits* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *thought* *experiment* *tests* the *principle*, not the *possibility*).

**(iii) The *metric* *objection***. The *welfare* *is* *not* the *only* *good* (the *rights*, the *freedom*, the *relationship*), and the *welfarist* *metric* *misses* the *plural* *values*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *welfare* *is* the *inclusive* *metric* (the *satisfaction* *includes* the *rights* *if* the *agents* *value* them), and the *reply* *grounds* the *pluralism* in the *desire* (the *desire* *satisfaction*; Ch. 48, §1). The *reply* is *contested* (the *adaptive* *preferences*; the *laundering*).

### 2.3 Libertarianism

**The *thesis*** (Nozick, *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, 1974; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *left* *libertarianism*; Steiner, *An Essay on Rights*, 1994; Vallentyne & Steiner, *Left-Libertarianism and Its Critics*, 2000). The *justice* is the *entitlement* (the *historical* *principle*: the *justice* in *acquisition*, the *justice* in *transfer*, the *rectification* of *injustice*), and the *patterned* *principles* (the *equality*, the *need*) *violate* the *liberty*, and the *state* *must* be *minimal* (the *night*-*watchman*).

**The *structure*** (the *entitlement* *theory*).

$$
\text{The *entitlement* *theory*}: \ \text{the *justice* in *acquisition*} + \text{the *justice* in *transfer*} + \text{the *rectification*}
$$

**The *Wilt* *Chamberlain* *argument*** (Nozick 1974, pp. 160–164). The *patterned* *distribution* $D_1$ is *just* (the *equality*), and the *million* *people* *voluntarily* *pay* the *quarter* to *Chamberlain* ($D_2$), and the *transfer* *is* *just* (the *voluntary* *exchange*), and the *result* $D_2$ *violates* the *pattern*, and the *pattern* *cannot* be *maintained* *without* the *coercion* (the *interference*), and the *coercion* *violates* the *liberty*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *voluntary* *transfer* *is* *just* (the *liberty* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *patterned* *principle* *forbids* the *result* of the *voluntary* *transfer* (the *pattern* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *pattern* *requires* the *interference* with the *voluntary* *transfer* (the *coercion*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *patterned* *principles* *violate* the *liberty* (the *Nozick* *insight*), and the *entitlement* *theory* *is* *true* (the *historical* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *liberty* (the *self*-*ownership*), and it *fits* the *intuition* (the *voluntary* *exchange* *is* *just*), and it *avoids* the *aggregation* (the *separateness* *insight*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *self*-*ownership* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Cohen, *Self-Ownership, Freedom, and Equality*, 1995, Ch. 3). The *self*-*ownership* *is* *not* *defensible* (the *body* *is* *not* *the* *property*; the *coercion* *in* the *market*; the *slavery* *contract*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *self*-*ownership* *is* the *axiom* (the *liberty* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *intuition* in the *liberty* (the *no* *coercion*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *intuition* *does* not *settle* the *property* *status*).

**(ii) The *acquisition* *problem*** (the *Lockean* *proviso*; Nozick 1974, pp. 174–182). The *original* *acquisition* *is* *unjust* (the *conquest*, the *enclosure*, the *theft*), and the *entitlement* *theory* *requires* the *rectification*, and the *rectification* *is* *impossible* (the *historical* *injustice* *is* *deep*), and the *theory* *collapses* into the *radical* *redistribution* (the *left* *libertarianism*).

**The *reply*** (the *left* *libertarianism*; Steiner 1994, Ch. 7). The *rectification* *can* be *approximated* (the *global* *fund*, the *resource* *tax*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *rectification* in the *proviso*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *approximation* *is* *indeterminate*).

**(iii) The *no*-*policy* *objection***. The *libertarianism* *cannot* *justify* the *public* *goods* (the *defense*, the *courts*, the *infrastructure*), and the *minimal* *state* *requires* the *taxation*, and the *taxation* *violates* the *liberty*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Nozick 1974, Ch. 3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *minimal* *state* *can* be *financed* by the *voluntary* *contributions* and the *enforcement* *fees* (the *protective* *association*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *minimal* *state* in the *contract*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *free*-*rider* *problem*; the *no* *coercion*).

### 2.4 Communitarianism

**The *thesis*** (Sandel, *Liberalism and the Limits of Justice*, 1982; MacIntyre, *After Virtue*, 1981; Taylor, “Atomism,” 1985; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *justice* *cannot* be *derived* from the *abstract* *individual* (the *unencumbered* *self*; the *liberal* *atomism*), and the *values* *are* *constituted* by the *community* (the *tradition*, the *practice*, the *common* *good*), and the *justice* *is* the *excellence* of the *common* *life*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *self* *is* *constituted* by the *community* (the *constitutive* *attachment*; the *tradition*).} \\
2. & \text{The *liberal* *justice* *presupposes* the *unencumbered* *self* (the *abstract* *individual*).} \\
3. & \text{The *unencumbered* *self* *does* not *exist* (the *constitutive* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *liberal* *justice* *is* *incoherent* (the *communitarian* *insight*), and the *justice* *requires* the *common* *good* (the *common* *good* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *relational* *self* (the *identity*, the *tradition*, the *practice*), and it *challenges* the *liberal* *neutrality* (the *no* *neutral* *conception* of the *good*; §4.5), and it *grounds* the *political* *participation* (the *civic* *republicanism*; §2.6).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *coercion*’* *objection***. The *common* *good* *requires* the *enforcement* of the *conception* of the *good* (the *perfectionism*, the *paternalism*; §4.5), and the *enforcement* *violates* the *pluralism* (the *reasonable* *disagreement*; the *Rawlsian* *objection*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *common* *good* *can* be *thin* (the *public* *reason*, the *overlapping* *consensus*; Rawls 1993, Ch. 4), and the *reply* *grounds* the *common* *good* in the *reasonable* *pluralism*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *thin* *common* *good* *is* *the* *liberalism* *again*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *critique*’* *objection***. The *community* *cannot* be *criticized* (the *tradition* *is* the *standard*), and the *critique* *requires* the *transcendent* *standard* (the *justice*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (MacIntyre 1981, Ch. 14; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *critique* *is* *immanent* (the *tradition* *criticizes* *itself*: the *internal* *inconsistency*, the *practice*'s *standards*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *critique* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *immanent* *critique* *is* *the* *hermeneutic* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *radical* *critique*).

### 2.5 Marxism

**The *thesis*** (Marx, *Capital*, Vol. I, 1867, Part II; “Critique of the Gotha Program,” 1875; the *contemporary* *literature*; Cohen, *Karl Marx's Theory of History*, 1978; Roemer, *A General Theory of Exploitation and Class*, 1982). The *justice* *cannot* be *separated* from the *mode* of *production* (the *justice* is *historical*; the *ideology*), and the *capitalist* *exchange* *is* *exploitative* (the *surplus* *value*; the *labor* *theory* *of* *value*), and the *emancipation* *requires* the *abolition* of the *private* *property* (the *means* of *production*) and the *class* (the *communist* *insight*).

**The *structure*** (the *exploitation*).

$$
\text{Exploitation} \ = \ \text{the *appropriation* of the *surplus* *value* (the *unpaid* *labor*)} \ (\text{the *rate* of *surplus* *value*}: \ s/v)
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *worker* *produces* the *value* (the *labor* *theory* *of* *value*).} \\
2. & \text{The *capitalist* *pays* the *value* of the *labor* *power* (the *subsistence* *wage*), not the *value* of the *product* (the *surplus*).} \\
3. & \text{The *difference* is the *surplus* *value* (the *exploitation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *capitalist* *exchange* *is* *exploitative* (the *Marxist* *insight*), and the *justice* *requires* the *abolition* of the *exploitation* (the *emancipatory* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *justice* in the *production* (the *structural* *insight*), and it *explains* the *class* *conflict* (the *materialist* *insight*), and it *challenges* the *liberal* *neutrality* (the *ideology* *critique*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *labor* *theory* *of* *value***. The *labor* *theory* *of* *value* *is* *false* (the *marginal* *revolution*: the *value* *is* the *marginal* *utility*, not the *labor*), and the *exploitation* *argument* *collapses*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *analytical* *Marxism*; Roemer 1982, Ch. 1). The *exploitation* *can* be *defined* *without* the *labor* *theory* (the *unequal* *exchange* *of* the *labor* *for* the *goods*; the *property* *relations*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *exploitation* in the *property* *relations*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *Roemer* *account*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *justice*’* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *Marxism* *rejects* the *justice* as the *ideology* (the *justice* *is* the *bourgeois* *concept*), and the *critique* *requires* the *normative* *standard* (the *justice*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Cohen 1995, Ch. 5). The *Marxism* *has* the *normative* *standard* (the *freedom*, the *equality*, the *community*; the *communist* *vision*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *critique* in the *emancipatory* *values*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *feasibility*’* *objection***. The *communism* *is* *infeasible* (the *incentives*, the *information*, the *human* *nature*), and the *account* *is* *utopian*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *market* *socialism*, the *participatory* *economics*). The *institutions* *can* be *designed* (the *market* *socialism*, the *cooperatives*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *feasibility* in the *design*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.6 Republicanism

**The *thesis*** (Pettit, *Republicanism*, 1997; *On the People's Terms*, 2012; Skinner, *Liberty before Liberalism*, 1998; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *freedom* *is* the *non*-*domination* (the *no* *arbitrary* *power*; the *no* *master*, *even* a *benevolent* one), and the *justice* *requires* the *republican* *institutions* (the *rule* of *law*, the *contestation*, the *civic* *virtue*), and the *republic* *is* the *free* *state* (the *collective* *freedom*).

**The *structure*** (the *non*-*domination*).

$$
\text{Freedom as non-domination}: \ \neg \exists \ \text{an *agent*} \ A \ \text{with the *capacity* to *interfere* *arbitrarily* in the *affairs* of the *agent*} \ B
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *liberal* *freedom* (the *non*-*interference*) *misses* the *domination* (the *slave* of the *benevolent* *master* *is* *unfree*, *though* *not* *interfered* *with*).} \\
2. & \text{The *domination* *is* the *arbitrary* *power* (the *capacity* *to* *interfere* *without* the *accountability*).} \\
3. & \text{The *non*-*domination* *captures* the *republican* *intuition* (the *no* *master*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *republican* *freedom* *is* *distinct* and *superior* (the *republican* *insight*), and the *justice* *requires* the *non*-*domination* (the *republican* *justice*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *structural* *freedom* (the *domination* *without* the *interference*), and it *challenges* the *liberal* *dichotomy* (the *negative* vs. the *positive*; §4), and it *grounds* the *institutions* (the *contestation*, the *accountability*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *distinctness*’* *objection***. The *non*-*domination* *collapses* into the *negative* *freedom* (the *arbitrary* *power* *is* the *threat* of the *interference*, and the *negative* *account* *includes* the *threat*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Pettit 1997, Ch. 2). The *domination* *is* *not* the *threat* (the *benevolent* *master* *does* not *threaten*, and the *slave* *is* *dominated*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *capacity* (the *structural* *power*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *measurement*’* *objection***. The *non*-*domination* *cannot* be *measured* (the *arbitrary* *power* *is* *fuzzy*), and the *account* *is* *indeterminate*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Pettit 2012, Ch. 3). The *non*-*domination* *is* *measurable* (the *intensity* *of* the *interference* *×* the *probability* *×* the *range*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *measurement* in the *indices*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *institutions*’* *objection***. The *non*-*domination* *requires* the *strong* *state* (the *republican* *state* *is* *interventionist*), and the *intervention* *is* *itself* a *domination* (the *state* *as* the *master*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Pettit 1997, Ch. 6). The *state* *is* *not* the *arbitrary* *master* (the *rule* of *law*, the *contestation*, the *accountability*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *state* in the *non*-*arbitrary* *power*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *democratic* *state* *as* the *non*-*dominator*).

## 3. Distributive Justice

### 3.1 Justice as fairness

**The *thesis*** (Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, 1971, §§11–17; *Political Liberalism*, 1993; *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement*, 2001). The *principles* of *justice* are the *terms* of the *fair* *cooperation* (the *fair* *terms* among the *free* and *equal* *persons*), and the *principles* are *derived* from the *original* *position* (the *veil* of *ignorance*; the *reasonable* *choice*), and the *principles* are the *two* *principles* (the *equal* *basic* *liberties*; the *fair* *equality* of *opportunity* + the *difference* *principle*).

**The *structure*** (the *two* *principles*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *equal* *basic* *liberties* (the *liberty* *principle*).} \\
2. & \text{The *fair* *equality* of *opportunity* (the *opportunity* *principle*).} \\
3. & \text{The *difference* *principle*: the *inequalities* are *just* *iff* they are to the *greatest* *benefit* of the *least* *advantaged* *members* *of* the *society*.} \\
\hline
& \text{The *lexical* *priority*: the *first* *principle* *prior* to the *second*; the *opportunity* *prior* to the *difference*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *principles* *must* be *fair* (the *reasonable* *choice*; the *no* *arbitrary* *advantages*).} \\
2. & \text{The *veil* of *ignorance* *removes* the *arbitrary* *advantages* (the *talents*, the *class*, the *luck*; the *moral* *arbitrariness* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *reasonable* *choice* *behind* the *veil* *yields* the *two* *principles* (the *maximin* *choice*; the *strains* of *commitment*, the *self*-*respect*, the *stability*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *two* *principles* are the *just* *terms* (the *Rawlsian* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *equality* in the *fairness* (the *fair* *terms*), and it *avoids* the *aggregation* (the *separateness*; the *difference* *principle* *protects* the *least* *advantaged*), and it *provides* the *benchmark* (the *basic* *structure*, the *primary* *goods*) and the *method* (the *reflective* *equilibrium*, the *original* *position*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *maximin* *objection*** (Harsanyi, “Can the Maximin Principle Serve as a Basis for Morality?” 1975; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *maximin* *is* *irrational* (the *Bayesian* *choice* *requires* the *expected* *utility*, and the *maximin* *ignores* the *probabilities*), and the *veil* of *ignorance* *requires* the *expected* *utility* (the *Harsanyi* *insight*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Rawls 1971, §28; 2001, §29). The *maximin* *is* *reasonable* *behind* the *veil* (the *high* *stakes*, the *no* *probability* *information*, the *strains* of *commitment*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *choice* in the *reasonable* (not the *rational* *choice* *under* the *risk*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *Harsanyi* *account* *remains* *attractive*).

**(ii) The *difference* *principle* *objection*** (the *libertarian*; Nozick 1974, pp. 209–213; the *left* *critique*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *difference* *principle* *violates* the *liberty* (the *entitlement*; the *taxation*), and the *inequalities* *are* *not* *necessarily* *unjust* (the *history* *matters*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *difference* *principle* *is* *a* *fair* *condition* (the *reciprocity*; the *talents* *are* *contingent*, and the *society* *is* *a* *cooperative* *scheme*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *principle* in the *reciprocity* (the *no* *arbitrary* *advantage*). The *reply* is *contested* (the *libertarian* *account* *of* the *history* *remains*).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *motivation*’* *objection***. The *difference* *principle* *removes* the *incentive* (the *talented* *do* not *produce*, and the *least* *advantaged* *suffer*), and the *empirical* *claim* *refutes* the *principle*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Rawls 1971, §17; the *strains* of *commitment*). The *difference* *principle* *permits* the *incentives* (the *inequalities* *that* *benefit* the *least* *advantaged*; the *incentive* *argument*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *incentives* in the *difference* *principle*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *incentive* *is* *compatible*).

### 3.2 Equality of resources

**The *thesis*** (Dworkin, “What Is Equality? Part 2: Equality of Resources,” 1981; *Sovereign Virtue*, 2000, Ch. 1–2). The *equality* *is* the *equality* of the *resources* (the *external* *resources* + the *internal* *resources*: the *talents*, the *health*), and the *equality* *is* *defined* by the *envy* *test* (the *no* *one* *prefers* *another*'s *bundle*), and the *distribution* *is* *achieved* by the *auction* (the *market* *simulation*) and the *hypothetical* *insurance* (the *brute* *luck* *compensated*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Distribution} \ D \ \text{is equal} \ \iff \ \neg \exists \ i, j: \ i \ \text{prefers} \ D_j \ \text{to} \ D_i \ \ (\text{the *envy test*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *equality* *requires* the *equal* *resources* (the *external* + the *internal*).} \\
2. & \text{The *equal* *resources* *require* the *compensation* for the *brute* *luck* (the *health*, the *talents*).} \\
3. & \text{The *compensation* *is* *achieved* by the *hypothetical* *insurance* (the *behind* the *veil* *choice*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *equality* of *resources* *is* *defined* by the *envy* *test* and *achieved* by the *insurance* (the *Dworkinian* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *distinguishes* the *option* *luck* (the *choices*; the *no* *compensation*) from the *brute* *luck* (the *circumstances*; the *compensation*), and it *fits* the *intuition* (the *responsibility* *for* the *choices*), and it *grounds* the *market* (the *auction*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *endowment* *responsibility* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Anderson 1999, §2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *internal* *resources* (the *talents*, the *health*, the *ambition*) *cannot* be *separated* from the *person* (the *no* *compensation* *without* the *slavery* *of* the *talented*; the *Dworkinian* *abandonment*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Dworkin 2000, Ch. 2; the *hypothetical* *insurance*). The *compensation* is *hypothetical* (the *behind* the *veil* *choice*, *not* the *actual* *transfer* *of* the *talents*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *compensation* in the *insurance*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *insurance* *avoids* the *slavery* *of* the *talented*).

**(ii) The *no* *responsibility* *objection*** (Anderson 1999, §3; the *relational* *critique*; §3.5). The *option*/*brute* *distinction* *is* *unstable* (the *ambition*, the *taste*, the *character* *are* *shaped* by the *circumstances*), and the *responsibility* *is* *not* *the* *right* *basis* (the *relational* *insight*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *relational* *egalitarianism*). The *responsibility* *is* *one* *value* (the *option* *luck* *matters*), and the *relational* *equality* *extends* the *account* (the *no* *stigma*, the *no* *domination*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *relational* *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.3 Luck egalitarianism and its critics

**The *thesis*** (Cohen, “On the Currency of Egalitarian Justice,” 1989; Arneson, “Equality and Equal Opportunity for Welfare,” 1989; Rakowski, *Equal Justice*, 1991). The *equality* *requires* the *compensation* for the *unchosen* *disadvantages* (the *brute* *luck*, the *endowment*, the *circumstances*), and the *inequalities* *are* *just* *when* they *track* the *choices* (the *option* *luck*), and the *principle* is the *“*equality* of *opportunity* *for* *welfare*”* (the *Arneson* *form*) or the *“*equality* of *access* *to* *advantage*”* (the *Cohen* *form*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Justice} = \text{the *compensation* for the *brute* *luck*} + \text{the *responsibility* for the *option* *luck*}
$$

**The *objections*** (Anderson, “What Is the Point of Equality?” 1999, §2–3; the *relational* *critique*).

**(i) The *abandonment* *objection*** (Anderson 1999, §3). The *luck* *egalitarianism* *abandons* the *unfortunate* (the *lazy* *person* *who* *fails* *to* *insure*, the *person* *who* *takes* the *risk*, the *person* *who* *is* *unlucky* *in* the *choice*) *to* the *misery*, and the *abandonment* *is* *cruel* (the *no* *solidarity*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *basic* *needs* *floor*; the *sufficientarian* *constraint*). The *luck* *egalitarianism* *can* *include* the *floor* (the *basic* *needs*, the *dignity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *floor* in the *humanity* (the *no* *abandonment*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *floor* *is* *the* *minimum*).

**(ii) The *stigma* *objection*** (Anderson 1999, §4). The *luck* *egalitarianism* *stigmatizes* the *unfortunate* (the *means*-*tested* *benefits*, the *humiliating* *conditions*, the *“*lucky* *to* *be* *helped”*), and the *stigma* *violates* the *equal* *standing*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *universal* *provision*, the *relational* *insight*). The *benefits* *can* be *universal* (the *no* *stigma*; the *basic* *income*, the *public* *services*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *no*-*stigma* in the *universality*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *universal* *provision* *is* *the* *relational* *insight*).

**(iii) The *no* *responsibility* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *is* *the* *metaphysical* *construct* (the *no* *free* *will*; the *situationism*; Ch. 27, §5), and the *option*/*brute* *distinction* *is* *arbitrary*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *relational* *egalitarianism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *is* *the* *social* *practice* (the *accountability*, the *attributability*; Ch. 46, §4.2), and the *practice* *is* *compatible* with the *hard* *determinism* (the *compatibilism*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.4 The capabilities approach

**The *thesis*** (Sen, “Equality of What?” 1980; *Development as Freedom*, 1999; *The Idea of Justice*, 2009; Nussbaum, *Women and Human Development*, 2000; *Creating Capabilities*, 2011). The *justice* *is* the *freedom* *to* *achieve* (the *capabilities*), and the *metric* is the *set* of the *capabilities* (the *real* *opportunities*: the *health*, the *education*, the *participation*, the *attachment*), and the *justice* *requires* the *threshold* (the *central* *capabilities* *for* *all*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Justice} \ = \ \text{the *threshold* of the *central* *capabilities*} \ (\text{the *real* *freedom* to *choose* and *act*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *resources* (the *income*, the *goods*) *do* not *measure* the *freedom* (the *conversion* *varies*: the *disability*, the *climate*, the *gender*).} \\
2. & \text{The *freedom* *requires* the *capabilities* (the *real* *opportunities* to *function*).} \\
3. & \text{The *capabilities* *ground* the *justice* (the *threshold*, the *human* *dignity*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *capabilities* *are* the *metric* (the *Sen*-*Nussbaum* *insight*), and the *justice* *requires* the *threshold* (the *human* *dignity* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *difference* (the *disability*, the *gender*, the *conversion*), and it *grounds* the *any* *human* *dignity* (the *Nussbaum* *insight*), and it *fits* the *development* (the *human* *development* *index*; the *policy*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *list* *objection*** (Sen 2009, Ch. 11; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *Nussbaum* *list* *is* *paternalistic* (the *fixed* *threshold* *imposes* the *Western* *conception* of the *good*), and the *Sen* *account* *leaves* the *list* *open* (the *no* *determinacy*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Nussbaum 2011, Ch. 3; Sen 2009, Ch. 11). The *list* is *open* (the *democratic* *deliberation*; the *specification*; the *no* *imposition*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *threshold* in the *dignity* (the *thin* *universal*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *list* *is* *a* *proposal*, not a *dictate*).

**(ii) The *no* *measurement* *objection*. The *capabilities* *cannot* be *measured* (the *incommensurable*, the *qualitative*), and the *account* *is* *indeterminate*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *capabilities* *are* *measurable* (the *indices*, the *participatory* *assessment*, the *multidimensional* *poverty* *index*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *measurement* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *measurement* *is* *imperfect*).

### 3.5 Relational egalitarianism

**The *thesis*** (Anderson, “What Is the Point of Equality?” 1999; “The Fundamental Disagreement,” 2012; *The Imperative of Integration*, 2010; Scheffler, “Choice, Circumstance, and the Value of Equality,” 2005; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *equality* *is* *not* the *equal* *distribution* but the *equal* *relations* (the *equal* *standing*, the *no* *domination*, the *no* *stigma*, the *no* *exploitation*), and the *justice* *requires* the *relations* (the *democratic* *equality*, the *reciprocity*, the *no* *hierarchy*), and the *distribution* *is* *instrumental* (the *equal* *standing* *requires* the *resources*, *but* the *resources* *are* *not* the *goal*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Equality} \ = \ \text{the *equal* *relations*} \ (\text{the *no* *domination*, the *no* *stigma*, the *no* *exploitation*}) \ > \ \text{the *equal* *distribution*}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *distribution* *is* *not* the *goal* (the *equal* *distribution* *can* *coexist* *with* *domination*, *stigma*, *exploitation*).} \\
2. & \text{The *equality* *is* the *relation* (the *equal* *standing*, the *democratic* *relation*).} \\
3. & \text{The *relation* *requires* the *resources* (the *instrumental* *insight*) *and* the *institutions* (the *no* *hierarchy*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *relational* *equality* *is* the *goal* (the *Anderson* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *avoids* the *abandonment* and the *stigma* (the *luck* *egalitarianism* *critique*), and it *grounds* the *democracy* (the *equal* *standing*; §6), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *integration*, the *no* *hierarchy*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *distribution* *objection***. The *relational* *equality* *leaves* the *distribution* *indeterminate* (the *no* *metric*), and the *resources* *matter* (the *poverty* *kills*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Anderson 1999, §5; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *relation* *requires* the *sufficiency* (the *floor*, the *no* *poverty*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distribution* in the *relation* (the *sufficientarian* + the *relational*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *individual* *objection***. The *relational* *equality* *privileges* the *groups* (the *relational* *insight*) and *neglects* the *individual* *burdens* (the *choice*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *relational* *equality* *is* *individual* (the *relations* *among* the *individuals*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *individual* in the *relation*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**The *assessment***. The *Rawlsian* and the *capabilities* *approaches* are *the* *most* *developed*, and the *luck* *egalitarianism* is *seriously* *challenged* by the *relational* *critique*, and the *relational* *equality* is the *most* *promising* *advance*.

|---|---|---|

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *relational* *egalitarianism* *combined* with the *capabilities* *floor* and the *Rawlsian* *fairness*: the *equal* *relations* (the *relational* *insight*), the *central* *capabilities* (the *capabilities* *insight*), the *difference* *principle* (the *Rawlsian* *insight*), the *responsibility* *for* the *choices* (the *luck* *insight*, *qualified*), *integrated* by the *reflective* *equilibrium*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *metric* and the *responsibility* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Liberty

### 4.1 The two concepts of liberty

**The *thesis*** (Berlin, “Two Concepts of Liberty,” 1958; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *negative* *freedom*** (the *freedom* *from*): the *absence* of the *interference* (the *coercion*, the *obstruction*), the *no* *one* *interferes* with the *agent*'s *action*, and the *measure* is the *range* of the *non*-*interfered* *choices*.

**The *positive* *freedom*** (the *freedom* *to*): the *presence* of the *self*-*mastery* (the *autonomy*, the *self*-*realization*), the *agent* *is* *the* *author* of the *action*, and the *measure* is the *self*-*determination*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Negative freedom}: \ \neg \exists \ i: \ \text{Interferes}_i(\text{the agent's action}) \qquad \text{Positive freedom}: \ \text{Self-determination}(\text{the agent})
$$

**The *argument*** (Berlin 1958, §2–3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *negative* *freedom* *is* *the* *absence* of the *interference* (the *liberal* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *positive* *freedom* *is* the *self*-*mastery* (the *idealist* *insight*), and the *self*-*mastery* *can* be *imposed* (the *real* *self* *vs.* the *empirical* *self*), and the *imposition* *is* the *tyranny* (the *Berlin* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *positive* *freedom* *risks* the *totalitarianism* (the *no* *interference* *justified* *by* the *true* *self*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *negative* *freedom* *is* *safer* (the *Berlin* *insight*), and the *positive* *freedom* *is* *dangerous* (the *Berlin* *critique*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *clarifies* the *debate* (the *two* *concepts* *are* *distinct*), and it *grounds* the *liberal* *tolerance* (the *no* *interference*), and it *warns* against the *positive* *tyranny* (the *Berlin* *insight*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *MacCallum* *objection*** (MacCallum, “Negative and Positive Freedom,” 1967). The *freedom* *is* always the *single* *triadic* *relation* (the *agent* *is* *free* *from* the *constraint* *to* *do* / *be* *something*), and the *negative*/*positive* *dichotomy* *is* *a* *verbal* *dispute* (the *different* *constraints*, the *same* *structure*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *objection*.** The *dichotomy* is *a* *distinction* *of* *emphasis* (the *constraints* vs. the *self*-*mastery*), and *not* *a* *difference* *of* *logical* *form*; the *reply* *is* *contested*.

**(ii) The *positive* *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Taylor, “What's Wrong with Negative Liberty?” 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *positive* *freedom* *is* *necessary* (the *freedom* *requires* the *self*-*realization*, and the *negative* *freedom* *without* the *positive* *is* *empty*; the *Taylor* *insight*), and the *Berlin* *critique* *attacks* the *abuse*, not the *concept*, and the *reply* *rescues* the *positive* *freedom*.

**The *reply*.** The *positive* *freedom* *can* be *defended* (the *autonomy*, the *capabilities*; §3.4), and the *abuse* *is* *not* *the* *concept*, and the *reply* *is* *defensible*.

### 4.2 The third concept: non-domination

**The *thesis*** (§2.6; Pettit 1997; Skinner 1998). The *freedom* *is* the *non*-*domination* (the *no* *arbitrary* *power*), and the *concept* *is* *distinct* from both the *negative* (the *non*-*interference*: the *slave* of the *benevolent* *master* *is* *unfree*) and the *positive* (the *self*-*mastery*: the *domination* *is* *external*, *not* the *internal* *weakness*), and the *republican* *tradition* *preserves* the *concept*.

**The *structure*** (§2.6).

$$
\text{Freedom as non-domination} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *no* *mastery* (the *no* *arbitrary* *power*, *regardless* of the *interference*)}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *structural* *freedom* (the *no* *arbitrary* *power*), and it *grounds* the *institutions* (the *rule* of *law*, the *accountability*, the *contestation*), and it *connects* the *liberty* to the *justice* (§3.5).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *distinctness* *objection*** (§2.6). The *non*-*domination* *collapses* into the *negative* *freedom*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Pettit 1997, Ch. 2). The *domination* *is* the *capacity*, not the *exercise* (the *benevolent* *master*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *capacity*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *measurement* *objection*** (§2.6). The *reply* (Pettit 2012, Ch. 3). The *intensity* × the *probability* × the *range*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.3 Autonomy

**The *thesis*** (Kant 1785, 4:446; Mill 1859, Ch. 3; Frankfurt, “Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person,” 1971; Dworkin, *The Theory and Practice of Autonomy*, 1988; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *autonomy* *is* the *self*-*legislation* (the *will* *gives* *itself* the *law*), and the *autonomy* *requires* the *second*-*order* *endorsement* (the *Frankfurt* *hierarchical* *model*: the *first*-*order* *desire* *endorsed* by the *second*-*order* *desire*), and the *autonomy* *is* the *foundation* of the *dignity* (the *Kantian* *insight*).

**The *structure*** (the *hierarchical* *model*).

$$
\text{Autonomy} \ \iff \ \text{the *first*-*order* *desire} \ D_1 \ \text{is *endorsed* by the *second*-*order* *desire*} \ D_2 \ (\text{the *no* *conflict*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* *acts* *on* the *desires* (the *first*-*order* *states*).} \\
2. & \text{The *agent* *is* *autonomous* *when* the *will* *endorses* the *desires* (the *second*-*order* *state*).} \\
3. & \text{The *endorsement* *grounds* the *responsibility* (the *action* *is* the *agent*'s).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *autonomy* *is* the *hierarchical* *endorsement* (the *Frankfurt* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *distinguishes* the *autonomous* *agent* from the *wanton* (the *no* *second*-*order* *endorsement*), and it *explains* the *addiction* (the *endorsed* vs. the *unendorsed* *desire*), and it *grounds* the *responsibility* (the *attribution*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *regress* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *hierarchical* *model* *requires* the *second*-*order* *endorsement*, and the *endorsement* *requires* the *third*-*order* *endorsement*, and the *regress* *is* *vicious*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *regress* *stops* at the *cares* (the *constitutive* *commitments*, the *ground* *projects*; Frankfurt, “The Importance of What We Care About,” 1988), and the *reply* *grounds* the *autonomy* in the *care*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *content* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *autonomy* *is* the *formal* *property* (the *endorsement*), and the *content* *can* be *evil* (the *autonomous* *Nazi*), and the *autonomy* *is* *not* the *moral* *value*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *Kantian* *insight*). The *autonomy* *is* *the* *formal* *condition*, and the *moral* *content* *requires* the *universalization* (the *categorical* *imperative*; Ch. 45, §2.2), and the *reply* *separates* the *autonomy* from the *morality*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *autonomy* *is* *a* *value* *among* *others*).

### 4.4 The harm principle and paternalism

**The *thesis*** (Mill, *On Liberty*, 1859, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *harm* *principle*; Feinberg, *The Moral Limits of the Criminal Law*, 1984–88; Dworkin, “Paternalism,” 1972; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *harm* *principle***.

$$
\text{Coercion} \ \text{is justified} \ \iff \ \text{the *coercion* prevents the *harm* to the *others} \ (\text{the *only* *justification*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Mill 1859, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* *is* *the* *best* *judge* of the *own* *good* (the *epistemic* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *coercion* *for* the *agent*'s *own* *good* *is* *paternalism* (the *interference* with the *autonomy*).} \\
3. & \text{The *paternalism* *is* *not* *justified* (the *liberty* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *coercion* *is* *justified* *only* by the *harm* to the *others* (the *harm* *principle*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *varieties* *of* *paternalism***.

| The *variety* | The *content* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard** | The *interference* with the *voluntary* *choice* | The *seatbelt* *law*, the *drug* *ban* |
| **Soft** | The *interference* with the *non*-*voluntary* *choice* (the *ignorance*, the *coercion*) | The *informed* *consent*, the *child* *protection* |
| **Libertarian** | The *nudge* (the *choice*-*preserving* *intervention*) | The *default* *organ* *donation*, the *cafeteria* *layout* |

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *harm* *objection***. The *harm* *principle* *is* *indeterminate* (the *harm* *includes* the *offense*, the *moral* *harm*, the *psychological* *harm*, and the *non*-*harm* *is* *rare*), and the *principle* *cannot* *guide* (the *everything* *is* *harm*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Feinberg 1984, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *harm* is *the* *setback* of the *interests* (the *welfare* *interests*, *not* the *offense*), and the *reply* *restricts* the *harm* *concept* (the *harm* *principle* *plus* the *offense* *principle*; the *balancing*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *autonomy* *objection***. The *harm* *principle* *presupposes* the *autonomy* (the *agent* *judges* the *own* *good*), and the *autonomy* *is* *not* *defensible* (the *no* *rational* *agent*, the *bounded* *rationality*; the *behavioral* *economics*; §4.5), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *capabilities* §3.4). The *autonomy* is the *capability* (the *development*, the *education*), and the *soft* *paternalism* *protects* the *capability* (the *informed* *consent*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *autonomy* in the *capability*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *no* *neutrality* *objection***. The *harm* *principle* *presupposes* the *conception* of the *harm* (the *welfare*, the *interests*), and the *conception* *is* *contested* (the *perfectionism*, the *pluralism*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *political* *liberalism*; Rawls 1993, Ch. 4). The *harm* is *defined* by the *public* *reason* (the *overlapping* *consensus*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *harm* in the *public* *reason*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *public* *reason* *is* *the* *liberal* *neutrality*).

### 4.5 The nudge

**The *thesis*** (Thaler & Sunstein, *Nudge*, 2008, Ch. 1; the *libertarian* *paternalism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nudge* *preserves* the *choice* (the *default*, the *framing*, the *salience*, the *social* *norms*), and the *nudge* *improves* the *welfare* (the *bounded* *rationality*), and the *nudge* *is* *a* *legitimate* *intervention* (the *soft* *paternalism*, the *libertarian* *insight*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Nudge} \ = \ \text{the *choice*-*preserving* *intervention*} \ (\text{the *no* *coercion*, the *no* *significant* *cost*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agents* *are* *boundedly* *rational* (the *biases*, the *heuristics*, the *inertia*).} \\
2. & \text{The *choice* *architecture* *shapes* the *choices* (the *defaults*, the *framing*; the *no* *neutral* *architecture*).} \\
3. & \text{The *nudge* *improves* the *welfare* *without* the *coercion* (the *libertarian* *paternalism*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *nudge* *is* *a* *legitimate* *policy* (the *Thaler*-*Sunstein* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *policy* in the *psychology* (the *bounded* *rationality*; Ch. 46, §6), and it *preserves* the *liberty* (the *no* *coercion*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *defaults*, the *pensions*, the *organ* *donation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *autonomy* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nudge* *manipulates* the *agent* (the *bypassing* of the *rational* *deliberation*, the *exploitation* of the *biases*), and the *manipulation* *violates* the *autonomy* (the *no* *respect* *for* the *rational* *agency*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *no* *neutral* *architecture*). The *nudge* is *not* *manipulation* (the *transparency*, the *no* *deception*, the *disclosure*), and the *no* *neutral* *architecture* *means* the *nudge* *is* *unavoidable* (the *choice* *of* the *architecture* *is* *the* *nudge*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *nudge* in the *transparency*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *transparent* *nudge* *is* *permissible*).

**(ii) The *no* *welfare* *objection***. The *nudge* *presupposes* the *conception* of the *welfare* (the *what* *the* *agent* *wants*), and the *conception* *is* *contested* (the *no* *objective* *good*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *informed* *consent*, the *deliberation*). The *nudge* *is* *justified* *when* it *responds* to the *agent*'s *own* *values* (the *informed* *choice*, the *deliberation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *nudge* in the *values*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *nudge* *respects* the *values*).

**(iii) The *no* *efficacy* *objection***. The *nudges* *are* *weak* (the *small* *effects*; the *replication* *crisis*; Ch. 46, §6.4), and the *policy* *should* be *the* *structural* *change* (the *taxes*, the *regulation*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nudges* *can* be *effective* (the *defaults*, the *organ* *donation*; the *large* *effects* in the *some* *domains*), and the *structural* *change* *is* *complementary* (the *nudge* *plus* the *tax*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *nudge* in the *domain*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *conditional* *efficacy*).

### 4.6 The state of the liberty debate

**The *assessment***. The *liberty* *is* *three* *concepts* (the *negative*, the *positive*, the *non*-*domination*), and the *macCallum* *triadic* *structure* *unifies* them, and the *paternalism* *is* *contested*, and the *nudge* *is* *the* *contemporary* *frontier*.

| The *concept* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Negative** | The *clarity*; the *tolerance* | The *domination* |
| **Positive** | The *self*-*mastery* | The *tyranny* |
| **Non-domination** | The *structural* | The *measurement* |
| **Autonomy** | The *dignity* | The *regress* |
| **Nudge** | The *efficacy* | The *autonomy* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. ## 5. Equality

### 5.1 The equality of what?

**The *question***. The *metric* of the *equality* (the *what* is *to* be *equal*), and the *answer* *determines* the *distributive* *theory* (§3).

**The *candidates***.

| The *metric* | The *content* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Welfare** | The *happiness*, the *desire* *satisfaction* | The *adaptive* *preferences* |
| **Resources** | The *income*, the *wealth*, the *opportunity* | The *conversion* (the *disability*) |
| **Capability** | The *real* *opportunity* | The *no* *list* |
| **Opportunity** | The *fair* *chance* | The *no* *determinacy* |
| **Status** | The *relational* *standing* | The *no* *metric* |

**The *significance***. The *metric* *is* the *core* of the *distributive* *justice* (§3), and the *choice* *depends* on the *value* *theory* (Ch. 48, §1) and on the *relational* *theory* (§3.5).

### 5.2 Equality versus priority

**The *thesis*** (Parfit, “Equality and Priority,” 1997; Frankfurt, “Equality as a Moral Ideal,” 1987; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *egalitarianism***. The *equality* *matters* (the *gap* *between* the *worse* *off* and the *better* *off* is *bad* *in* *itself*).

**The *prioritarianism***. The *gains* to the *worse* *off* *matter* *more* (the *weighting*), and the *equality* *is* *not* *valuable* *in* *itself* (the *only* the *welfare* *of* the *worse* *off* *matters*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Egalitarianism}: \ \text{the *gap*} \ g \ \text{is *bad* *in* *itself} \qquad \text{Prioritarianism}: \ \text{the *welfare*} \ u \ \text{has the *diminishing* *marginal* *moral* *value}
$$

**The *argument*** (Parfit 1997, §3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *levelling* *down* (the *reducing* the *better* *off* *without* the *benefit* to the *worse* *off*) *is* *bad* (the *anti*-*levelling* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *egalitarianism* *commends* the *levelling* *down* (the *reduction* of the *gap*), and the *prioritarianism* *does* not (the *no* *benefit* to the *worse* *off*).} \\
3. & \text{The *anti*-*levelling* *insight* *favors* the *prioritarianism*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *prioritarianism* *is* *superior* (the *Parfit* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *equality* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *relational* *insight*). The *priority* *misses* the *relational* *equality* (the *equal* *standing*, the *no* *domination*, the *no* *stigma*; §3.5), and the *equality* *is* *relational* (not *distributive*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *relational* *egalitarianism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *relational* *equality* *is* *compatible* with the *prioritarian* *distribution* (the *relation* *requires* the *floor*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *relation* in the *distribution*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *measurement* *objection***. The *priority* *requires* the *interpersonal* *comparison* (the *cardinal* *welfare*), and the *comparison* *is* *not* *available*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *capabilities*). The *comparison* is *approximate* (the *indices*, the *participatory* *assessment*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *measurement* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 5.3 The levelling-down objection

**The *thesis*** (Parfit 1997, §3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *egalitarianism* *is* *committed* to the *levelling* *down* (the *reducing* the *better* *off* *to* the *level* of the *worse* *off* *is* *an* *improvement* *in* *equality*), and the *levelling* *down* *is* *absurd* (the *no* *one* is *better* *off*, and the *equality* *increases*), and the *absurdity* *refutes* the *egalitarianism* (§5.2).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Egalitarianism} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *levelling* *down} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *absurdity*}
$$

**The *significance***. The *objection* *is* the *strongest* *case* against the *distributive* *egalitarianism* (the *telic* *form*), and the *response* *is* the *relational* *equality* (the *non*-*distributional*) or the *priority* (§5.2).

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *relational* *response*** (§3.5). The *equality* *is* *the* *relation* (the *no* *domination*, the *no* *stigma*), and the *levelling* *down* *is* *irrelevant* (the *relation* *is* *not* *the* *distribution*), and the *reply* *dissolves* the *objection*.

**The *reply*** is *defensible* (the *relational* *approach* *is* *the* *most* *promising*).

**(ii) The *priority* *response*** (§5.2). The *priority* *does* *not* *commend* the *levelling* *down* (the *no* *benefit* to the *worse* *off*), and the *reply* *avoids* the *objection*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *pluralist* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *equality* *is* *one* *value* *among* the *others* (the *welfare*, the *liberty*, the *relation*), and the *pluralism* *does* *not* *commend* the *levelling* *down* (the *other* *values* *forbid* it), and the *reply* *grounds* the *equality* in the *pluralism*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

## 6. Democracy

### 6.1 The instrumental case

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *epistemic* *democracy*; Landemore, *Democratic Reason*, 2013; the *aggregate* *insight*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *collective* *judgment* *can* be *better* than the *individual* (the *Condorcet* *jury* *theorem*, the *aggregation*).} \\
2. & \text{The *democracy* *aggregates* the *information* (the *diverse* *perspectives*, the *error* *correction*).} \\
3. & \text{The *better* *judgment* *produces* the *better* *outcomes* (the *protection* of the *interests*, the *no* *tyranny*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *democracy* *is* *instrumentally* *justified* (the *epistemic* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *Condorcet* *jury* *theorem***.

$$
\text{If each voter has the probability} \ p > 1/2 \ \text{of being right,} \ \text{then} \ \Pr(\text{the majority is right}) \to 1 \ \text{as} \ n \to \infty
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *democracy* in the *outcomes* (the *epistemic* *insight*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *deliberation*, the *aggregation*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *intrinsic* *value* (the *no* *metaphysics*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *competence* *objection*** (Brennan, *Against Democracy*, 2016, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *voters* *are* *ignorant* and *irrational* (the *no* *competence*; the *p* < 1/2), and the *aggregation* *of* the *incompetent* *is* *worse* (the *no* *miracle*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Landemore 2013, Ch. 3; the *diversity* *insight*). The *diversity* *trumps* the *individual* *competence* (the *cognitive* *diversity*; the *Condorcet* *theorem* *with* the *diverse* *perspectives*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *competence* in the *diversity*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *truth* *objection***. The *instrumental* *case* *presupposes* the *truth* (the *correct* *judgment*), and the *political* *truth* *is* *contested* (the *no* *objective* *good*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *epistemic* *democracy*). The *truth* *is* the *correct* *answer* *to* the *common* *problems* (the *no* *metaphysical* *commitment*; the *procedural* *truth*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *problem*-*solving*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**The *thesis*** (Waldron, “A Right to Do Wrong,” 1981; *Law and Disagreement*, 1999; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *autonomy* *insight*; the *public* *equality*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *democracy* *is* *intrinsically* *valuable* (the *equal* *political* *standing*, the *autonomy*, the *self*-*government*), and the *democracy* *respects* the *equal* *agency* (the *no* *one*'s *judgment* *is* *privileged*), and the *democracy* *is* *the* *right* *of* *rights* (the *Waldron* *insight*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}

2. & \text{The *equal* *standing* *requires* the *equal* *participation* (the *autonomy*).} \\
3. & \text{The *participation* *is* the *democracy* (the *self*-*government*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *democracy* *is* *intrinsically* *valuable* (the *Waldron* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *democracy* in the *equality* (the *equal* *standing*), and it *avoids* the *paternalism* (the *no* *guardianship*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *majority*, the *dissent*, the *legitimacy*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intrinsic* *value* *is* *independent* of the *outcomes* (the *equal* *standing* *is* *good* *in* *itself*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *democracy* in the *equality*. 2. & \text{The *diversity* *requires* the *descriptive* *representation* (the *presence* of the *group*).} \\
3. & \text{The *presence* *improves* the *deliberation* (the *epistemic* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *descriptive* *representation* *is* *justified* (the *diversity* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *descriptive* *representation* *is* *not* *necessary* (the *substantive* *representation* *suffices*), and the *reply* *challenges* the *necessity*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *descriptive* *representation* *improves* the *substantive*).

**(ii) The *boundary* *problem*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *demos* *is* *constituted* by the *boundary* (the *who* *decides* *the* *who*), and the *boundary* *cannot* be *decided* by the *demos* (the *circularity*), and the *all*-*affected* *principle* (the *inclusion* of the *all* *affected* by the *decision*) *grounds* the *boundary*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *demos* *is* *constituted* by the *boundary* (the *no* *prior* *demos*).} \\
2. & \text{The *boundary* *requires* the *principle* (the *who* *is* *affected*).} \\
3. & \text{The *all*-*affected* *principle* *grounds* the *boundary* (the *inclusion*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *boundary* *is* *grounded* in the *affected* *principle* (the *inclusion* *insight*), and the *demos* *is* *not* *circular* (the *grounding* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection***. The *all*-*affected* *principle* *is* *global* (the *all* *affected* *by* the *decision* *includes* the *world*), and the *global* *demos* *is* *not* *feasible*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; §7). The *principle* *is* *scalar* (the *degree* of the *affected*, the *functional* *differentiation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *demos* in the *degree*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *multi*-*level* *democracy*).

### 6.6 Judicial review and epistocracy

**The *themes***.

**(i) Judicial review*** (Waldron, “The Core of the Case against Judicial Review,” 2006; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *judicial* *review* (the *courts* *striking* *down* the *laws*) *is* *not* *justified* (the *disagreement* *about* the *rights* *is* *reasonable*, and the *courts* *are* *not* *better* *at* the *rights*, and the *democracy* *is* the *legitimate* *procedure*), and the *rights* *should* be *decided* by the *legislature*.

**The *argument*** (Waldron 2006, §1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *rights* are *contested* (the *reasonable* *disagreement*).} \\
2. & \text{The *courts* *are* *not* *more* *reliable* *than* the *legislature* (the *no* *epistemic* *superiority*).} \\
3. & \text{The *legislature* *is* *the* *democratic* (the *equal* *participation*; §6.2).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *judicial* *review* *is* *not* *justified* (the *Waldron* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *constitutional* *insight*). The *courts* *protect* the *minorities* (the *no* *tyranny* of the *majority*), and the *constitution* *entrenches* the *rights* (the *pre*-*commitment*; Ch. 46, §6.4), and the *reply* *grounds* the *review* in the *protection*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *constitutional* *insight*), and *contested* (the *no* *counter*-*majoritarian* *difficulty*).

**(ii) The *epistocracy*** (Brennan, *Against Democracy*, 2016, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *democracy* *empowers* the *incompetent* (the *ignorant* *voters*, the *irrational* *masses*), and the *competence* *should* *determine* the *power* (the *epistocracy*: the *rule* of the *knowers*), and the *democracy* *is* *not* *justified*.

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *competence* *varies* (the *ignorance*, the *irrationality*).} \\
2. & \text{The *power* *should* *track* the *competence* (the *epistemic* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *democracy* *does* not *track* the *competence* (the *equal* *power* *regardless* of the *competence*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *epistocracy* *is* *justified* (the *Brennan* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (Landemore 2013, Ch. 4; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *competence* *is* *collective* (the *diversity* *insight*; §6.1), and the *epistocracy* *violates* the *equal* *standing* (the *no* *moral* *equality*; §5.4), and the *reply* *grounds* the *democracy* in the *collective* *competence* and the *equality*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

## 7. Global Justice

### 7.1 The boundaries of justice

**The *thesis*** (Rawls, *The Law of Peoples*, 1999; Beitz, *Political Theory and International Relations*, 1979; Pogge, *World Poverty and Human Rights*, 2002; Nagel, “The Problem of Global Justice,” 2005; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *statist* *view*** (Rawls 1999; Nagel 2005). The *justice* *applies* *within* the *state* (the *coercive* *institutions*, the *common* *membership*), and the *global* *order* *is* *not* *coercive* (the *no* *state*), and the *global* *duties* *are* *humanitarian* (the *assistance*, the *no* *justice*).

**The *cosmopolitan* *view*** (Beitz 1979; Pogge 2002). The *justice* *applies* *globally* (the *equal* *moral* *worth*, the *global* *institutions*), and the *global* *order* *is* *coercive* (the *the* *international* *institutions*, the *trade*, the *finance*), and the *duties* *are* the *justice* (the *global* *distributive* *justice*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Statism}: \ \text{Justice}_{\text{domestic}} \ \wedge \ \text{Humanity}_{\text{global}} \qquad \text{Cosmopolitanism}: \ \text{Justice}_{\text{global}} \ \wedge \ \text{the *no* *boundary*}
$$

**The *argument* (the *cosmopolitan***; Beitz 1979, Pt. III).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *global* *institutions* *are* *coercive* (the *trade*, the *finance*, the *intellectual* *property*; Pogge 2002, Ch. 1).} \\
2. & \text{The *coercion* *requires* the *justification* (the *justice*).} \\
3. & \text{The *justification* *is* *the* *same* *globally* (the *equal* *moral* *worth*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *global* *justice* *is* *required* (the *cosmopolitan* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *institutions* *objection*** (Nagel 2005, §2). The *global* *order* *is* *not* *the* *state* (the *no* *coercive* *sovereignty*), and the *justice* *requires* the *state* (the *coercion* *grounds* the *duty*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Pogge 2002, Ch. 1). The *global* *institutions* *are* *coercive* (the *resource* *privilege*, the *trade* *regime*, the *finance*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *justice* in the *institutional* *coercion*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *difference* *principle*’* *objection***. The *Rawlsian* *difference* *principle* *applies* *within* the *peoples* (the *Law* of *Peoples* *rejects* the *global* *difference* *principle*), and the *global* *distribution* *is* *not* *required*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *cosmopolitan* *Rawlsianism*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *difference* *principle* *applies* *globally* (the *original* *position* *behind* the *global* *veil*; the *no* *arbitrary* *boundary*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *global* *principle* in the *fairness*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *Rawlsian* *peoples* *remain*).

### 7.2 Global distributive justice

**The *theses***.

**(i) The *global* *difference* *principle***. The *inequalities* *are* *just* *iff* they are *to* the *greatest* *benefit* of the *global* *worst* *off*, and the *global* *tax* (the *Tobin* *tax*, the *global* *resources* *dividend*; Pogge 2002, Ch. 6) *implements* the *principle*.

**(ii) The *capabilities* *approach*** (§3.4). The *global* *justice* *requires* the *threshold* (the *central* *capabilities* *for* *all*), and the *development* *is* the *freedom* (Sen 1999), and the *policy* is *the* *human* *development*.

**(iii) The *sufficientarian* *view***. The *duty* *is* the *floor* (the *basic* *needs*, the *no* *poverty*), and *not* the *equality* (the *no* *global* *difference* *principle*), and the *duty* *is* *grounded* in the *humanity* (the *no* *one* *should* *be* *below* the *floor*).

**The *significance***. The *theories* *differ* on the *demandingness* (the *difference* *principle* *is* *radical*; the *sufficiency* *is* *moderate*), and the *demandingness* *connects* the *global* *justice* to the *effective* *altruism* (Ch. 45, §1.3) and to the *applied* *ethics* (Ch. 51, §6).

### 7.3 Immigration

**The *thesis*** (Carens, “Aliens and Citizens,” 1987; *The Ethics of Immigration*, 2013; the *open* *borders* *view*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *open* *borders* *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *freedom* *of* *movement* *is* *a* *basic* *liberty* (the *autonomy*, the *no* *coercion*).} \\
2. & \text{The *border* *coercion* *restricts* the *movement* (the *coercion*).} \\
3. & \text{The *coercion* *requires* the *justification* (the *no* *arbitrary* *restriction*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *open* *borders* *are* *justified* (the *Carens* *insight*), and the *closed* *borders* *are* *unjust* (the *no* *justification*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *migration* in the *liberty* (the *autonomy*), and it *fits* the *equal* *moral* *worth* (the *no* *arbitrary* *birthright*), and it *challenges* the *statism* (§7.1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *state* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *communitarian* *insight*). The *state* *requires* the *membership* (the *common* *good*, the *solidarity*, the *culture*), and the *open* *borders* *undermine* the *state*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *membership* *can* *be* *compatible* with the *migration* (the *integration*, the *civic* *membership*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *migration* in the *integration*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *welfare* *objection***. The *open* *borders* *undermine* the *welfare* *state* (the *fiscal* *burden*, the *no* *support*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *welfare* *is* *global* (the *global* *redistribution*; §7.2), and the *reply* *grounds* the *welfare* in the *global* *justice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *no* *sovereignty* *objection***. The *open* *borders* *undermine* the *sovereignty* (the *self*-*determination*, the *democratic* *control*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *sovereignty* *is* *not* *absolute* (the *human* *rights* *constrain*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *sovereignty* in the *rights*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *qualified* *sovereignty*).

### 7.4 Climate justice

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Gardiner, *A Perfect Moral Storm*, 2011; Shue, “Global Environment and International Inequality,” 1999; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *climate* *change* *raises* the *distinctive* *problems* (the *global* *diffusion* of the *causes*, the *fragmentation* of the *agency*, the *spatial* and the *temporal* *dispersion* of the *effects*, the *uncertainty*), and the *justice* *requires* the *differentiated* *responsibilities* (the *historical* *emissions*, the *capacity*, the *beneficiary* *pays*), and the *future* *generations* (the *no* *reciprocity*, the *no* *consent*) *have* the *claims*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Responsibility} \ = \ f(\text{the *historical* *emissions*}, \ \text{the *capacity*}, \ \text{the *benefit*}) \ \ (\text{the *differentiated* *responsibility*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Shue 1999, §2–3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *emissions* *cause* the *harm* (the *causal* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *harm* *falls* *disproportionately* on the *poor* (the *vulnerability* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *beneficiaries* *have* the *duty* (the *no* *free*-*riding*, the *capacity*, the *historical* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *differentiated* *responsibility* *is* *required* (the *climate* *justice* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *problems***.

**(i) The *no* *causal* *connection* *problem*** (the *non*-*identity* *problem*; Parfit 1984, Ch. 16; Ch. 48, §6). The *future* *persons* *would* *not* *exist* *but* *for* the *climate* *policies* (the *different* *people* *are* *born*), and the *harm* *to* the *particular* *persons* *is* *not* *identifiable* (the *no* *causal* *connection*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *harm* *is* *to* the *humanity* (the *collective* *insight*), and the *non*-*identity* *problem* *does* not *excuse* (the *no* *excuse* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *duty* in the *humanity*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *responsibility* *problem*** (the *tragedy* of the *commons*; Gardiner 2011, Ch. 2). The *climate* is the *collective* *action* *problem* (the *no* *individual* *responsibility*; the *no* *incentive*), and the *justice* *requires* the *collective* *action*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *collective* *responsibility* *exists* (the *shared* *agency*; Ch. 46, §4.4), and the *institutions* *can* *solve* the *problem* (the *global* *governance*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *collective* *agency*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 7.5 Historical injustice and reparations

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Boxill, “The Morality of Reparation,” 1972; Waldron, “Superseding Historic Injustice,” 1992; Thompson, “Historical Injustice and Reparation,” 2001; Kutz, “Justice in Reparations,” 2004; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *reparations* *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *historical* *injustice* *occurred* (the *slavery*, the *colonialism*, the *dispossession*).} \\
2. & \text{The *injustice* *leaves* the *ongoing* *harm* (the *structural* *inequality*, the *wealth* *gap*, the *stigma*; §3.5).} \\
3. & \text{The *harm* *requires* the *remedy* (the *compensation*, the *rectification*, the *structural* *change*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *reparations* *are* *required* (the *remedial* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *identity* *objection*** (Waldron 1992, §2). The *past* *victims* are *dead* and the *past* *perpetrators* are *dead* (the *no* *identity* of the *parties*), and the *rights* *do* not *survive* (the *no* *inheritance*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Thompson 2001, §3; the *structural* *insight*). The *groups* *persist* (the *corporate* *identity*, the *institutional* *continuity*; the *no* *individual* *agency* *required*), and the *harm* *persists* (the *structural* *insight*; §3.5), and the *reply* *grounds* the *reparations* in the *continuing* *harm*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *counterfactual* *objection***. The *reparations* *require* the *counterfactual* (the *what* would *have* *been*), and the *counterfactual* *is* *unknowable* (the *no* *measurement*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *structural* *remedy*). The *reparations* are *forward*-*looking* (the *structural* *change*, the *capabilities*, the *relational* *equality*), and the *no* *precise* *counterfactual* is *required*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *reparations* in the *structural* *change*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *no* *duty* *objection***. The *current* *generations* are *not* *responsible* for the *past* (the *no* *inherited* *guilt*; the *individual* *insight*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *beneficiary* *insight*). The *current* *generations* *benefit* from the *past* (the *inheritance* of the *wealth*, the *structural* *advantage*), and the *benefit* *grounds* the *duty* (the *no* *unjust* *enrichment*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *duty* in the *benefit*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

## 8. Rights

### 8.1 The Hohfeldian analysis

**The *thesis*** (Hohfeld, *Fundamental Legal Conceptions*, 1919; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *rights* are *not* a *single* *concept* but the *four* *incidents* (the *claim*, the *liberty*, the *power*, the *immunity*), and *each* *incident* *has* the *correlative* (the *duty*, the *no*-*claim*, the *liability*, the *disability*).

**The *structure***.

| The *incident* | The *content* | The *correlative* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim** | The *right* *that* *another* *do* / *not* *do* | The *duty* | The *right* to the *promise* |
| **Liberty** | The *no* *duty* *to* *do* / *not* *do* | The *no*-*claim* | The *right* to the *speech* |
| **Power** | The *capacity* to *change* the *rights* | The *liability* | The *right* to *contract* |
| **Immunity** | The *no* *liability* to the *change* | The *disability* | The *constitutional* *right* |

**The *significance*.** The *analysis* *clarifies* the *rights* *talk* (the *confusion* of the *claim* with the *liberty*; the *“*right*”* to the *healthcare* *as* the *claim* vs. the *liberty*), and it *grounds* the *legal* and the *moral* *analysis* (the *correlativity*), and it *explains* the *conflicts* (the *claim* vs. the *liberty*; §8.4).

### 8.2 The foundations

**The *theories***.

| The *theory* | The *content* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest** | The *rights* *protect* the *interests* | Raz, “On the Nature of Rights,” 1984 |
| **Will** | The *rights* *protect* the *choice* (the *waiver*) | Hart, “Are There Any Natural Rights?” 1955 |
| **Status** | The *rights* *protect* the *status* (the *dignity*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *argument* (the *interest* *theory***; Raz 1984, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *rights* *exist* *for* the *sake* of the *right*-*holders* (the *interests*).} \\
2. & \text{The *interest* *grounds* the *duty* of the *others* (the *sufficient* *interest*).} \\
3. & \text{The *duty* *is* *the* *content* of the *right* (the *correlativity*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *rights* *protect* the *interests* (the *interest* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *rights* in the *interests* (the *welfare*), and it *extends* the *rights* to the *non*-*choosers* (the *animals*, the *infants*, the *dementia* *patients*; Ch. 51, §5), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *legal* *rights* *protect* the *interests*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no* *waiver* *objection*** (the *will* *theory*; Hart 1955). The *interest* *theory* *cannot* *explain* the *waiver* (the *right* *to* *the* *lawyer* *waived*; the *promise* *released*), and the *choice* *is* the *essence* of the *right*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Raz 1984, §3). The *waiver* *is* the *power* (the *Hohfeldian* *incident*; §8.1), and the *interest* *theory* *includes* the *power* (the *interest* in the *control*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *waiver* in the *power*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *no* *animal* *objection*** (the *will* *theory*). The *will* *theory* *excludes* the *animals* (the *no* *choice*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *interest* *theory* *includes* the *animals* (the *interests* in the *no* *suffering*), and the *interest* *theory* *is* *superior* (the *extension* *insight*). The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 8.3 The human rights

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Griffin, *On Human Rights*, 2008; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *Universal* *Declaration* *of* *Human* *Rights*, 1948). The *human* *rights* *are* the *universal* *moral* *rights* (the *all* *humans* *hold* them), and the *grounds* *are* the *agency* (the *personhood*; the *Griffin* *insight*) or the *interests* (the *Raz* *insight*), and the *content* *is* the *minimal* *standard* (the *no* *slavery*, the *no* *torture*, the *no* *discrimination*, the *due* *process*, the *subsistence*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Human right} \ = \ \text{the *universal* *moral* *claim*} \ (\text{the *no* *exception*} \ \text{on the *grounds* of the *nationality*, the *status*, the *culture*})
$$

**The *argument*** (Griffin 2008, Ch. 2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
