# Chapter 28 — Reasons, Causes, and Rationality

> “Rationalization is a species of causal explanation.”
> — Donald Davidson, “Actions, Reasons, and Events” (1963)

> “Reason is, and ought only to be the slave of the passions, and can never pretend to any other office than to serve and obey them.”
> — David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–40), II.iii.3

> “The concept of a reason is the concept of a consideration that counts in favour of something.”
> — T. M. Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other* (1998), Ch. 1

---

Chapter 27 ended with the *agent* and the *question* of *freedom*. This chapter takes up the *concept* that the *free-will* *debate* *presupposes*: the concept of a **reason**.

When we *explain* an action, we *cite* a reason: *she* *left* *because* *she* *believed* the *train* *was* *leaving* and *wanted* to *catch* it. When we *justify* an action, we *cite* a reason: *she* *was* *right* to *leave*, because the *train* *was* *leaving* and she *had* to *catch* it. The two *citations* are *different* in *kind* — the first is *explanatory*, the second is *normative* — and the *relationship* between them is one of the *deepest* *problems* in the *philosophy* of *action*.

The chapter's *thesis* is that the *explanatory* and the *normative* are *distinct* but *connected*: the *explanation* of an action *requires* the *citation* of a reason *in the explanatory sense* (a *belief*-*desire* *pair*), and the *justification* *requires* the *citation* of a reason *in the normative sense* (a *fact* that *counts* in *favour* of the action). The *connection* between the *two* is *contingent* and *defeasible*: the *explanatory* reason may *fail* to be a *normative* reason (the *agent* may be *mistaken*), and the *normative* reason may *fail* to *explain* (the *agent* may *not* *act* on it). The *failure* of the *connection* is the *space* in which *akrasia*, *self-deception*, and *irrationality* *occur*.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 examines *Davidson*'s *thesis* that reasons are causes. §2 examines the *internalism*/*externalism* debate about reasons. §3 examines the *belief*-*desire* *model* and the *direction* of *fit*. §4 examines *instrumental* and *procedural* *rationality*. §5 examines the *symmetry* and *asymmetry* of *practical* and *theoretical* reason. §6 examines *akrasia* and *self-deception*. §7 examines *irrationality* as *data*. §8 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. Reasons as Causes

### 1.1 The problem of action explanation

**The *datum*.** We *explain* actions by *citing* reasons. The *explanation* has a *distinctive* *form*: it is *not* a *prediction* from *laws* (as in *physics*), and it is *not* a *mere* *redescription* of the *behaviour*. It is an *interpretation* that *makes* the *action* *intelligible*.

**The *question*.** Is the *explanation* *causal*?

**The *positions*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Causalist** | Reasons are *causes*; the explanation is *causal* | Davidson; Fodor; the *naturalistic* *tradition* |
| **Anti-causalist** | Reasons are *not* causes; the explanation is *conceptual* or *interpretive* | Ryle; Melden; Anscombe; the *Wittgensteinian* *tradition* |
| **Teleological** | Reasons are *goals*; the explanation is *teleological* | the *Aristotelian* *tradition*; the *contemporary* *teleologists* |

**The *stakes*.** The *answer* *determines* whether the *mental* is *part* of the *causal* *order* (Ch. 23, §5), whether the *special* *sciences* are *autonomous* (Ch. 21, §7), and whether *freedom* is *compatible* with *explanation* (Ch. 27).

### 1.2 Davidson's argument

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, “Actions, Reasons, and Events,” 1963; *Essays on Actions and Events*, 1980).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *explanation* of an action by a reason is a *rationalization*: the reason *makes* the action *intelligible* (it *shows* the action as *reasonable* from the agent's *perspective*).} \\
2. & \text{The *rationalization* *works* only if the reason is *causally* *connected* to the action (the *reason* must have *produced* the *action*).} \\
3. & \text{The *connection* is *causal*, not *logical*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Reasons are *causes*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *structure*.** Davidson *distinguishes* the *reason* (the *belief*-*desire* *pair*) from the *action*, and he *argues* that the *relation* between them is *causal*: the *reason* *causes* the *action*, and the *causation* is *contingent* (the *reason* *could* have *existed* without the *action*).

**The *definition***. A **primary reason** for an action is a *pair* $\langle B, D \rangle$ where:

- $D$ is a **pro-attitude** (a *desire*, a *want*, a *value*, a *duty*) toward *actions* of a certain *kind*;
- $B$ is a **belief** that the action is of that *kind* (or that it is a *means* to the *kind*).

**The *formal* *statement*.

$$
\mathrm{PrimaryReason}(R, A) \quad \text{iff} \quad R = \langle B, D \rangle \wedge B \text{ represents } A \text{ as } F \wedge D \text{ favours } F\text{-ing}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *grounds* the *mental* in the *causal* *order*, and it *fits* the *naturalistic* *framework* (Ch. 19; Ch. 23).

### 1.3 The logical connection argument

**The *argument*** (Melden, *Free Action*, 1961; Ryle, *The Concept of Mind*, 1949; Anscombe, *Intention*, 1957; the *Wittgensteinian* *tradition*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *reason* and the *action* it explains are *logically* *connected* (the *reason* *entails* or *makes* *intelligible* the *action*).} \\
2. & \text{A *causal* relation is *contingent* (the *cause* *can* *occur* without the *effect*).} \\
3. & \text{If the *relation* is *logical*, it *cannot* be *causal*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Reasons are *not* causes.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *mental* is *not* in the *causal* *order*, and the *explanation* of *action* is *conceptual*, not *causal*. The *anti-causalist* *position* *preserves* the *autonomy* of the *mental*, and it *avoids* the *exclusion* *problem* (Ch. 23, §5).

**Davidson's *reply*** (1963, §4). The *argument* *confuses* the *level* of *events* with the *level* of *descriptions*. The *logical* *connection* *holds* between the *descriptions* (the *reason*-*description* and the *action*-*description*), not between the *events* *themselves*. The *events* are *causally* *related*, and the *relation* is *contingent*; the *descriptions* are *logically* *connected*, and the *connection* is *conceptual*. The *two* *levels* are *compatible*.

**The *formal* *statement*.

$$
\mathrm{Cause}(e_R, e_A) \wedge \mathrm{LogicallyConnected}(D_R, D_A)
$$

where $e_R$ and $e_A$ are the *events* and $D_R$ and $D_A$ are the *descriptions*. The *causation* is at the *event* *level*; the *logical* *connection* is at the *description* *level*.

**The *virtue*.** The *reply* *preserves* the *intelligibility* of the *explanation* (the *logical* *connection*) and the *causality* of the *relation* (the *event* *causation*).

**The *objection*.** The *reply* *leaves* the *mental* *content* *causally* *irrelevant*: the *causation* *holds* between the *events* *as* *physical* *events*, and the *mental* *description* is *epiphenomenal*. The *objection* is the *content* *problem* (§1.5).

### 1.4 Anomalous monism

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, “Mental Events,” 1970; *Essays on Actions and Events*, 1980, Essay 11). Mental *events* are *identical* to *physical* *events* (token *identity*; Ch. 23, §3.2), but *mental* *descriptions* are *not* *reducible* to *physical* *descriptions*, and there are *no* *strict* *psychological* *laws* (the *mental* is *anomalous*).

**The *structure*.

| Level | The *claim* | The *domain* |
|---|---|---|
| **Ontological** | Mental events = physical events | Token identity |
| **Descriptive** | Mental descriptions ≠ physical descriptions | Non-reductive |
| **Nomological** | No strict psychological laws | Anomalous |

**The *argument*** (Davidson's *three* *principles*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(\mathrm{P}_1) & \text{The *mental* is *causally* *efficacious* (the *mental* *causes* *physical* *events*).} \\
(\mathrm{P}_2) & \text{The *physical* *is* *causally* *closed* (every *physical* *event* has a *sufficient* *physical* *cause*).} \\
(\mathrm{P}_3) & \text{The *mental* is *not* *nomologically* *reducible* (no *strict* *psychological* *laws*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If } \mathrm{P}_1 \text{ and } \mathrm{P}_2 \text{ are true, mental events are identical to physical events (identity).} \\
2. & \text{If } \mathrm{P}_3 \text{ is true, the identity is not nomological (it is *anomalous*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Anomalous monism (identity without reduction).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *preserves* the *efficacy* of the *mental* (P₁) and the *closure* of the *physical* (P₂), and it *avoids* the *reduction* (P₃). The *view* is the *most* *influential* *form* of *non-reductive* *physicalism* (Ch. 23, §5.1).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*content*’* *objection*** (§1.5). The *view* *makes* the *mental* *content* *causally* *irrelevant* (the *causation* holds at the *physical* *level*, and the *content* is *epiphenomenal*).

**(ii) The *‘*anomaly*’* *objection***. The *view* *denies* that there are *psychological* *laws*, and the *denial* is *contested* (the *empirical* *literature* *suggests* that there are *robust* *psychological* *generalizations*; Ch. 10, §7).

**(iii) The *‘*exclusion*’* *objection***. The *view* *faces* the *exclusion* *argument* (Ch. 23, §5.2): if the *physical* *event* has a *sufficient* *physical* *cause*, the *mental* *cause* is *excluded*, and the *identity* *saves* the *efficacy* only by *making* the *mental* *cause* *identical* to the *physical* *cause*.

### 1.5 The content problem

**The *problem***. If the *mental* *event* is *identical* to the *physical* *event*, and the *causation* *holds* between the *events*, the *causal* *efficacy* of the *content* is *mysterious*: the *event* *causes* the *action* *because* of its *physical* *properties*, not because of its *content* (the *content* is *not* a *physical* *property*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mental* *event* *causes* the *action* *because* of its *content* (the *belief* *that* the *train* *is* *leaving* *causes* the *running* *because* of *what* the *belief* *represents*).} \\
2. & \text{The *content* is *not* a *physical* *property* (it is a *representational* *property*; Ch. 25, §4).} \\
3. & \text{The *physical* *causation* is *indifferent* to the *content*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *content* is *not* *causally* *efficacious* (epiphenomenalism about content).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *argument* *threatens* the *explanatory* *role* of *content*: if the *content* is *causally* *idle*, the *explanation* by *content* is *not* a *causal* *explanation*.

**The *responses*.

**(i) The *‘*extensional*’* *response*** (Davidson 1963). The *causal* *explanation* is *extensional*: we *cite* the *content*-*description*, and the *citation* is *explanatory* (the *content* *identifies* the *event*), even if the *causation* *holds* at the *physical* *level*. The *response* *saves* the *explanation*, and it *leaves* the *content* *causally* *idle* *in* *itself*.

**(ii) The *interventionist* *response*** (Ch. 18, §3; Woodward 2008). The *content* is *causally* *relevant* *in* the *interventionist* *sense*: *intervening* on the *content* (by *changing* the *belief*) *changes* the *action*. The *response* *grounds* the *efficacy* of *content* in the *interventionist* *framework*, and it is *promising*.

**(iii) The *grounding* *response***. The *content* is *grounded* in the *physical* *state* (Ch. 16, §3.3), and the *grounding* *explains* the *causal* *relevance* *without* *reducing* the *content* to the *physical*. The *response* is *promising* and *underdeveloped*.

### 1.6 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** Davidson's *thesis* is the *most* *influential* *account* of the *relation* between reasons and causes, and it is *qualified* by the *content* problem.

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Causalism (Davidson)** | Reasons are causes | Fits the naturalistic framework | The content problem |
| **Anti-causalism (Ryle, Melden)** | Reasons are not causes | Preserves the autonomy of the mental | Denies the efficacy |
| **Anomalous monism** | Identity without reduction | Preserves efficacy and closure | The content is idle |
| **Interventionism** | Content is causally relevant | Grounds the efficacy | The framework is contested |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *causalism*: reasons are *causes* (the *explanation* is *causal*), and the *content* is *causally* *relevant* *in* the *interventionist* *sense*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *relevance* is *not* *fully* *explained*.

## 2. Internalism and Externalism about Reasons

### 2.1 The distinction

**The *distinction*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Williams, “Internal and External Reasons,” 1981; the *survey* in the *Stanford* *Encyclopedia* *entry*, “Reasons for Action”).

| Kind | The *question* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Explanatory reason** | What *caused* the action? | The *belief*-*desire* *pair* |
| **Normative reason** | What *justifies* the action? | The *fact* that the *train* *is* *leaving* |
| **Motivating reason** | What *motivates* the agent? | The *desire* to *catch* the *train* |

**The *three* *kinds* are *distinct*, and the *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*. The *explanatory* reason *explains*; the *normative* reason *justifies*; the *motivating* reason *connects* the *two* (it is the *state* *by* *which* the *normative* *reason* *moves* the *agent*).

**The *question*. Are *normative* reasons *internal* (they *depend* on the *agent*'s *motivational* *set*) or *external* (they *exist* *independently* of the *agent*'s *motivations*)?

### 2.2 The Humean theory of motivation

**The *thesis*** (Hume, *Treatise*, II.iii.3; the *contemporary* *formulation*: Smith, *The Moral Problem*, 1994, Ch. 4). *Beliefs* alone *cannot* *motivate*; *motivation* *requires* a *desire*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *belief* is a *representation* of the *world* (it *aims* at *truth*).} \\
2. & \text{A *representation* *alone* *cannot* *produce* an *action* (it *tells* us *what* is the *case*, not *what* to *do*).} \\
3. & \text{An *action* *requires* a *conative* *state* (a *desire*, a *pro-attitude*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Beliefs alone cannot motivate (motivation requires a desire).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *captures* the *direction* *of* *fit* (beliefs *fit* the *world*; desires *make* the *world* *fit* them), and it *fits* the *belief*-*desire* *model* (§3).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*besire*’* *objection*** (Altham, “The Legacy of Emotivism,” 1986; the *contemporary* *literature*). Some *mental* *states* are *both* *beliefs* and *desires* (the *“*besires*”*; the *evaluative* *judgments* that *motivate*). The *objection* *undermines* the *sharp* *distinction*.

**(ii) The *‘*cognitivism*’* *objection*** (Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, 1998, Ch. 1; Stampe, “The Authority of Desire,” 1987). A *desire* is a *perception* of a *reason*, not a *blind* *push*. The *objection* *makes* *desire* *cognitive*, and the *belief*-*desire* *distinction* *blurs*.

**(iii) The *‘*guise* *of* *the* *good’* *objection***. *Desires* *represent* their *objects* as *good* (the *“*guise* *of* *the* *good”*; the *contemporary* *literature*), and the *representation* is *cognitive*. The *objection* *undermines* the *Humean* *view*.

### 2.3 Williams's internalism

**The *thesis*** (Williams, “Internal and External Reasons,” 1981). A *reason* *statement* is *internal* if it *can* *motivate* the *agent* (by *connecting* to the *agent*'s *subjective* *motivational* *set* $S$).

**The *definition*.

$$
\text{Agent } A \text{ has a *reason* to } \phi \quad \text{iff} \quad \exists s \in S_A\, (s \text{ *favours* } \phi\text{-ing, after *sound* *deliberation*})
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{An *explanation* of an action by a reason *must* *connect* the reason to the agent's *motivations* (the *"belief-desire"* *model*).} \\
2. & \text{If the reason does not *connect*, the explanation is *not* an *explanation* (it is a *"bluff"*).} \\
3. & \text{The *external* reason *statement* is a *bluff* (it *does* not *explain* the action).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There are *no* *external* reasons (the *internalist* *thesis*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *grounds* the *normative* *reasons* in the *agent*'s *motivations*, and it *fits* the *explanatory* *role* of *reasons*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*ginger* *haired* *man’* *objection***. A *person* who *does* not *care* about *ginger*-*haired* *people* *can* still *have* a *reason* to *not* *torture* them (the *moral* *reason*). The *internalist* *reply*: the *reason* *connects* to the *agent*'s *motivation* for *not* *torturing* (the *general* *motivation*), not to the *specific* *motivation* for *ginger*-*haired* *people*.

**(ii) The *‘*sound* *deliberation’* *objection***. The *internalist* *requires* *sound* *deliberation*, and the *notion* is *not* *defined* (the *agent* may be *wrong* about what *follows* from $S$; the *correction* *requires* an *external* *standard*).

**(iii) The *‘*amoralist*’* *objection***. The *amoralist* *does* not *care* about *moral* *reasons*, and the *internalist* *implies* that the *amoralist* *has* *no* *moral* *reasons*. The *consequence* is *counterintuitive* (the *amoralist* *can* still be *wrong*; the *moral* *reasons* *exist* *independently* of his *motivations*).

### 2.4 Externalism and the *objectivity* *of* *reasons*

**The *thesis*** (Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, 1998; Parfit, *On What Matters*, 2011; the *contemporary* *literature*). *Reasons* are *primitive*: a *reason* is a *fact* that *counts* in *favour* of an *action*, and the *fact* *does* not *depend* on the *agent*'s *motivations*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *normative* *reason* is a *fact* (the *train* *is* *leaving*).} \\
2. & \text{The *fact* is *independent* of the agent's *motivations* (it *obtains* *regardless* of whether the agent *cares*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *normative* *reasons* are *external* (they *exist* *independently* of the *agent*'s *motivations*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *grounds* the *objectivity* of *reasons*, and it *fits* the *moral* *practice* (the *amoralist* *is* *wrong*, not *merely* *different*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*motivational* *force’* *objection***. If *reasons* are *external*, how do they *motivate*? The *externalist* *reply*: the *judgment* that a *reason* *obtains* is *itself* *motivating* (the *“*judgment* *internalism”*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reply* *accepts* the *internalist* *intuition* at the *level* of the *judgment*.

**(ii) The *‘*queerness’* *objection*** (Mackie, *Ethics*, 1977, Ch. 1). The *external* *reasons* are *queer* (they *are* *objective* *facts* that *motivate*), and the *queerness* *undermines* the *view* (Ch. 44, §3).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *motivational* *connection’* *objection***. The *externalist* *does* not *explain* the *connection* between the *reason* and the *action*, and the *explanation* is *required* by the *explanatory* *role* of *reasons*.

### 2.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *internalism*/*externalism* *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *connected* to the *Humean* *theory* of *motivation* (§2.2) and to the *metaethical* *debate* (Ch. 44, §4).

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humean internalism** | Reasons depend on desires | Fits the explanatory role | The ginger-haired man |
| **Williams's internalism** | Reasons connect to S | Grounds the normative in the motivational | The amoralist |
| **Externalism (Scanlon)** | Reasons are primitive facts | Grounds objectivity | Motivational force |
| **Parfit's objectivism** | Reasons are object-given | Grounds objectivity | The queerness objection |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *externalism*: *reasons* are *facts* (the *externalist* *insight*), and the *judgment* that a *reason* *obtains* is *motivating* (the *internalist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *motivational* *force* is *not* *explained*.

## 3. Belief–Desire Psychology and Direction of Fit

### 3.1 The direction of fit

**The *distinction*** (Anscombe, *Intention*, 1957, §32; Searle, *Speech Acts*, 1969, pp. 3–4; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| State | The *direction* | The *aim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Belief** | *Mind* → *world* | The *belief* should *fit* the *world* | The *belief* that the *train* is *leaving* |
| **Desire** | *World* → *mind* | The *world* should *fit* the *desire* | The *desire* to *catch* the *train* |

**The *metaphor*.** The *belief* is *correct* if it *matches* the *world*; the *desire* is *satisfied* if the *world* *matches* it. The *two* *directions* are *distinct*, and the *distinction* is *constitutive* of the *belief*-*desire* *model*.

**The *formal* *statement*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Belief}: & B(p) \text{ is *correct* iff } p \\
\text{Desire}: & D(p) \text{ is *satisfied* iff } p
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *grounds* the *Humean* *theory* of *motivation* (§2.2), and it *explains* the *difference* between *theoretical* and *practical* *reason* (§5).

**The *objection*.** The *direction* *of* *fit* *metaphor* is *contested* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *“*direction* *of* *fit*”* *is* *not* *a* *single* *notion*; the *desires* *can* be *correct* *or* *incorrect* *in* a *way* that *mirrors* *belief*). The *objection* *complicates* the *distinction*.

### 3.2 The belief–desire model

**The *model*.** An *intentional* *action* is *explained* by a *belief*-*desire* *pair*: the *desire* *specifies* the *end*, and the *belief* *specifies* the *means*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Action} = f(\text{Belief}, \text{Desire})
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *model* *fits* the *explanation* of *action*, and it *generates* the *research* *programme* of *decision* *theory* and *cognitive* *science* (Ch. 10, §4; Ch. 23, §4).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*desire*’* *is* *a* *catch*-*all***. The *category* *includes* *any* *motivating* *state* (the *“*pro*-*attitude”*), and the *category* *is* *not* *informative* (the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(ii) The *‘*two*-*component*’* *objection***. The *model* *assumes* that *every* *action* has *exactly* *one* *belief* and *one* *desire*, and the *assumption* *is* *false* (the *actions* are *driven* by *complex* *networks* of *states*).

**(iii) The *‘*phenomenology*’* *objection***. The *model* *does* not *capture* the *phenomenology* of *action* (the *sense* of *effort*, the *immediacy* of *response*), and the *phenomenology* is *data* (Ch. 30, §1).

### 3.3 The *guise* *of* *the* *good*

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *Socratic* *view*; Anscombe 1957, §41). *Every* *desire* *represents* its *object* as *good* (the *agent* *desires* the *object* *under* the *guise* of the *good*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *desire* is *directed* at an *object* (the *agent* *wants* it).} \\
2. & \text{The *direction* *presupposes* an *evaluation* (the *agent* *values* the *object*, at least *instrumentally*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *desire* *represents* the *object* as *good*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *connects* *desire* to *value*, and it *fits* the *phenomenology* of *desire* (we *want* what we *take* to be *good*).

**The *objection*. The *wicked* *desires* (the *desire* to *harm* *for* *no* *reason*) *represent* their *objects* *as* *bad*, and the *view* *does* not *capture* them. (The *reply*: the *wicked* *desire* *represents* the *object* as *good* *for* the *agent* *in* *some* *respect*; the *reply* *is* *contested*.)

### 3.4 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *belief*-*desire* *model* is the *dominant* *framework* for the *explanation* of *action*, and it is *qualified* by the *objections*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Belief-desire model** | Actions are explained by belief-desire pairs | Fits the explanation | The desire is a catch-all |
| **Cognitivism about desire** | Desires are perceptions of reasons | Fits the phenomenology | The wicked desires |
| **Guise of the good** | Desires represent objects as good | Fits the evaluation | The wicked desires |
| **Besire theory** | Some states are both beliefs and desires | Fits the moral judgments | The distinction blurs |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *belief*-*desire* *model*, *combined* with a *cognitivist* *account* of *desire* (the *desires* *represent* their *objects* *as* *good*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *wicked* *desires* are *not* *fully* *captured*.

## 4. Instrumental and Procedural Rationality

### 4.1 The instrumental principle

**The *thesis***. The *fundamental* *principle* of *practical* *reason* is the *instrumental* *principle*: *if* you *intend* an *end*, and you *believe* that the *means* is *necessary* (and *sufficient*), you *ought* to *intend* the *means*.

**The *formal* *statement*.

$$
(\mathrm{IP}) \quad \mathrm{Intend}(E) \wedge \mathrm{Believe}(\text{means } M \text{ is necessary for } E) \to \mathrm{Intend}(M)
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *principle* *captures* the *norm* of *means*-*end* *reasoning*, and it *fits* the *decision*-*theoretic* *framework*.

**The *objection*. The *principle* is *not* *sufficient* for *rationality*: it *tells* us *how* to *pursue* our *ends*, not *which* *ends* to *pursue*. The *objection* *raises* the *question* of *substantive* *rationality* (§4.2).

### 4.2 The myth of instrumental rationality

**The *thesis*** (Korsgaard, “The Normativity of Instrumental Reason,” 1997; Parfit, *On What Matters*, 2011; Kolodny, “Why Be Rational?”, 2005). The *instrumental* *principle* is *not* the *whole* of *practical* *reason*, and the *view* that it *is* (the *“*instrumentalist*”* *view*) is a *myth*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *instrumental* *principle* *presupposes* that the *agent* *has* *ends*, and the *ends* *require* *justification*.} \\
2. & \text{The *justification* *requires* a *substantive* *norm* (the *“*categorical*”* *norm*; the *“*object*-*given*”* *reason*).} \\
3. & \text{The *substantive* *norm* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *instrumental* *principle*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *instrumentalist* *view* is *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *opens* the *space* for *substantive* *rationality* (the *norms* that *determine* *which* *ends* to *pursue*), and it *fits* the *moral* *practice* (the *moral* *norms* are *not* *instrumental*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*pluralism*’* *objection***. The *view* *does* not *specify* the *substantive* *norms*, and the *pluralism* of *norms* (the *moral*, the *prudential*, the *aesthetic*) *undermines* the *unity* of *reason*.

**(ii) The *‘*scepticism*’* *objection***. The *view* *raises* the *question* of *why* the *substantive* *norms* are *normative*, and the *answer* is *contested* (the *contemporary* *literature*).

### 4.3 Procedural versus substantive rationality

**The *distinction*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *survey* in the *Stanford* *Encyclopedia* *entry*, “Practical Reason”).

| Kind | The *question* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Procedural** | Is the *transition* *rational* (the *coherence* of the states)? | The *transitivity* of *preferences* |
| **Substantive** | Is the *content* *correct* (the *accuracy* of the states)? | The *truth* of the *belief* |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *procedural* *rationality* is *not* *sufficient* for *substantive* *rationality* (a *coherent* *set* of *states* *can* be *false*).} \\
2. & \text{The *substantive* *rationality* *requires* a *standard* *of* *correctness*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *two* *kinds* are *distinct*, and the *substantive* *is* *not* *reducible* to the *procedural*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *distinction* *complicates* the *rationality* *debate* (Ch. 10, §7.4), and it *raises* the *question* of *which* *kind* *matters* for *justification*.

### 4.4 Bounded rationality

**The *thesis*** (Simon, *Models of Man*, 1957; *Administrative Behavior*, 1947; the *contemporary* *literature*). *Rationality* is *bounded* by *cognitive* *limits* (the *computational* *capacity*, the *information*, the *time*), and the *norms* must be *feasible*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *ideal* *rationality* (the *maximisation* of *expected* *utility*) *requires* *unlimited* *computational* *capacity*.} \\
2. & \text{The *capacity* is *limited*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *rationality* is *bounded* (the *agent* *satisficies*, rather than *maximises*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *fits* the *empirical* *literature* (Ch. 10, §7.4), and it *explains* the *heuristics* (the *fast* and *frugal* *rules* of *thumb*).

**The *objection*. The *theory* *is* *descriptive*, not *normative* (it *tells* us *how* we *reason*, not *how* we *ought* to). (The *reply*: the *norms* *must* be *feasible*, and the *feasibility* *is* *normative*; the *reply* *is* *contested*.)

### 4.5 The myth of the purely rational agent

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *critique* of the *homo* *economicus*). The *model* of the *purely* *rational* *agent* (the *maximiser* of *expected* *utility*, with *complete* *information* and *consistent* *preferences*) is an *idealisation*, not a *description*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *empirical* *evidence* shows that *agents* *deviate* *systematically* from the *model* (the *framing* *effects*, the *anchoring*, the *loss* *aversion*; §7.2).} \\
2. & \text{The *deviations* are *not* *random* (they are *predictable*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *model* is an *idealisation*, not a *description*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *critique* *grounds* the *behavioural* *economics* (the *contemporary* *literature*), and it *fits* the *empirical* *findings*.

**The *objection*. The *idealisation* is *normatively* *useful* (it *gives* a *standard* *against* which *deviations* are *measured*), and the *critique* *does* not *undermine* the *normative* *role*. (The *reply*: the *normative* *role* *requires* *feasibility*, and the *feasibility* *is* *lacking*; the *reply* *is* *contested*.)

### 4.6 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *rationality* *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *connected* to the *epistemic* *rationality* *debate* (Ch. 8, §5) and the *moral* *rationality* *debate* (Ch. 45).

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instrumentalism** | The instrumental principle is the whole of practical reason | Simple; decision-theoretic | The myth objection |
| **Substantivism** | The substantive norms are irreducible | Fits the moral practice | The pluralism |
| **Proceduralism** | The coherence of the transition is the norm | Fits the Bayesian framework | The accuracy |
| **Substantive** | The content is the norm | Fits the truth | The feasibility |
| **Bounded rationality** | The norms must be feasible | Fits the empirical literature | The normativity |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *substantivism*: the *instrumental* *principle* is *necessary* but *not* *sufficient*; the *substantive* *norms* are *real* (the *object*-*given* *reasons*), and the *norms* are *bounded* by the *feasibility* *constraint*.

## 5. Practical and Theoretical Reason

### 5.1 The distinction

**The *distinction***. *Theoretical* *reason* is *reasoning* *about* *what* is *true*; *practical* *reason* is *reasoning* *about* *what* to *do*.

**The *structure*.

| Kind | The *question* | The *aim* | The *norm* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theoretical** | What is *true*? | *Knowledge* | *Truth*, *consistency*, *evidence* |
| **Practical** | What to *do*? | *Action* | *Goodness*, *consistency*, *reasons* |

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *structures* the *philosophy* of *reason*, and it *raises* the *question* of *whether* the *norms* *differ*.

### 5.2 Symmetry

**The *thesis***. The *two* *kinds* of *reason* *share* the same *norms* (consistency, coherence, the *fitting* of the *state* to its *object*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Both *kinds* *require* *consistency* (the *logical* *coherence* of the *states*).} \\
2. & \text{Both *kinds* *require* the *fitting* of the *state* to its *object* (the *belief* to the *fact*; the *intention* to the *good*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *norms* are *symmetric* (the *same* *norms* *apply* to *both*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *unifies* the *theory* of *reason*, and it *fits* the *Bayesian* *framework* (Ch. 10, §4; the *belief* and the *decision* are *both* *probabilistic*).

### 5.3 Asymmetry

**The *thesis***. The *two* *kinds* *differ* in their *norms* (the *direction* of *fit*, the *role* of *action*, the *kind* of *deliberation*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *belief* *aims* at *truth* (the *mind*-to-*world* *direction*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intention* *aims* at *action* (the *world*-to-*mind* *direction*).} \\
3. & \text{The *directions* are *distinct* (the *direction* *of* *fit*; §3.1).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *norms* are *asymmetric*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *captures* the *distinctive* *nature* of *practical* *reason* (the *action*, the *commitment*, the *normativity*), and it *fits* the *phenomenology* of *deliberation*.

### 5.4 The *same* *norm* *debate*

**The *question***. Are the *norms* of *practical* and *theoretical* *reason* *the* *same*?

**The *positions*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-norm** | The norms are the same (consistency, coherence) | the *Bayesian* *tradition* |
| **Different-norm** | The norms differ (truth vs. goodness) | the *Aristotelian* *tradition* |
| **Hybrid** | Some norms are the same, some differ | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *state* of the debate.** The *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *connected* to the *theory* of *normativity* (Ch. 44, §4) and to the *theory* of *rationality* (§4).

### 5.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *symmetry*/*asymmetry* *debate* is *unresolved*, and the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *hybrid*: the *procedural* *norms* (consistency, coherence) are *symmetric*, and the *substantive* *norms* (truth, goodness) are *asymmetric*.

## 6. Akrasia, Self-Deception, and Weakness of Will

### 6.1 The Socratic paradox

**The *thesis*** (Plato, *Protagoras* 358b–d; the *Socratic* *view*). *No* *one* *does* *wrong* *voluntarily*; *wrongdoing* is *ignorance*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Everyone *desires* the *good* (the *Socratic* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{If one *knows* the *good*, one *does* it (the *intellectualist* *premise*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Wrongdoing is *ignorance* (the *agent* *mistakes* the *bad* for the *good*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *preserves* the *unity* of *reason* (the *agent* *always* *acts* for the *good*, as she *sees* it), and it *avoids* the *paradox* of *akrasia*.

**The *objection*.** The *phenomenology* of *akrasia* (the *agent* *knows* the *better* and *does* the *worse*) is *powerful*, and the *Socratic* *view* *denies* it. (The *reply*: the *phenomenology* is *an* *illusion*; the *agent* *does* not *really* *know*; the *reply* *is* *contested*.)

### 6.2 The standard account

**The *thesis***. *Akrasia* (weakness of will) is *acting* *against* one's *better* *judgment* (the *agent* *judges* that $\phi$ is *best*, and *does* $\neg\phi$).

**The *formal* *statement*.

$$
(\mathrm{AK}) \quad \mathrm{Judgment}(A, \phi \text{ is best}) \wedge \mathrm{Action}(A, \neg\phi)
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *phenomenology* of *akrasia* (the *agent* *experiences* the *conflict*, the *regret*, the *weakness*), and it *fits* the *ordinary* *language*.

**The *objection*.** The *account* *makes* *akrasia* *irrational* (the *agent* *acts* *against* her *own* *judgment*), and the *irrationality* is *puzzling* (how can the *agent* *act* *against* what she *judges* *best*?).

### 6.3 Davidson's account

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, “How Is Weakness of the Will Possible?”, 1969; *Essays on Actions and Events*, 1980, Essay 2). The *akratic* *action* is *intentional* (it is *caused* by a *reason*), but the *reason* is *not* the *agent*'s *all-things-considered* *judgment*.

**The *structure*.

$$
\text{Akrasia} = \mathrm{PrimaryReason}(\langle B, D \rangle, \neg\phi) \wedge \mathrm{Judgment}(A, \text{best}: \phi)
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* *judges* that } \phi \text{ is *best* (the *all*-*things*-*considered* *judgment*).} \\
2. & \text{The *agent* *acts* on a *reason* that *favours* } \neg\phi \text{ (a *prima* *facie* *reason*).} \\
3. & \text{The *reason* *causes* the *action*, but it is *not* the *agent*'s *best* *judgment*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Akrasia is *possible* (the *action* is *intentional* but *not* *rational*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *preserves* the *intentionality* of the *akratic* *action* (the *action* is *caused* by a *reason*) and the *irrationality* (the *reason* is *not* the *best* *judgment*).

**The *objection*. The *account* *does* not *explain* *why* the *agent* *acts* on the *weaker* *reason* (the *“*clear*-*eyed* *akrasia*”* *problem*; §6.4).

### 6.4 The clear-eyed akrasia problem

**The *problem***. If the *agent* *judges* that $\phi$ is *best*, and the *judgment* is *motivating* (the *internalist* *premise*; §2.3), the *agent* *should* *act* on $\phi$. The *fact* that she *acts* on $\neg\phi$ *requires* an *explanation*, and the *explanation* is *not* *available* (the *judgment* *should* have *won*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *all*-*things*-*considered* *judgment* is *motivating* (the *internalist* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *motivation* is *stronger* than the *prima* *facie* *motivation* (the *judgment* *outweighs* the *reason*).} \\
3. & \text{The *agent* *acts* on the *weaker* *motivation*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *agent* *acts* *against* her *strongest* *motivation*, and the *action* is *unintelligible* (the *paradox* of *akrasia*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.

**(i) The *‘*degrees* *of* *confidence’* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *judgment* is *probabilistic* (the *agent* *is* *not* *certain*), and the *akratic* *action* is *explained* by the *probability* *distribution* (the *agent* *acts* on a *low*-*probability* *reason*). The *response* *makes* *akrasia* *less* *mysterious*, and it *fits* the *Bayesian* *framework* (Ch. 10, §4).

**(ii) The *‘*divided* *mind’* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *psychoanalytic* *tradition*). The *agent* *has* *multiple* *sub*-*agents* (the *parts* of the *mind*), and the *akratic* *action* is *explained* by the *conflict* among them. The *response* *preserves* the *intentionality*, and it *raises* the *question* of the *unity* of the *self* (Ch. 26, §7).

**(iii) The *‘*no* *judgment’* *response***. The *agent* *does* not *really* *judge* that $\phi$ is *best* (the *judgment* is *weak*, or *conditional*, or *not* *all*-*things*-*considered*). The *response* *saves* the *Socratic* *intuition*, and it *denies* the *phenomenology*.

### 6.5 Self-deception

**The *problem***. The *self*-*deceiver* *believes* $p$ while *knowing* $\neg p$. The *knowledge* *undermines* the *belief*, and the *belief* *requires* the *knowledge* (the *paradox*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{To *deceive* *yourself*, you must *know* the *truth* (the *deception* *requires* the *knowledge*).} \\
2. & \text{To *be* *deceived*, you must *believe* the *falsehood* (the *deception* *requires* the *belief*).} \\
3. & \text{You *cannot* *know* and *believe* the *contradictory* (the *contradiction* *is* *impossible*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Self-deception is *impossible* (the *paradox*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.

**(i) The *‘*divided* *mind’* *response***. The *self* is *divided* into *parts*, and *one* *part* *deceives* the *other* (the *part* *knows* the *truth*, and the *other* *part* *believes* the *falsehood*). The *response* *solves* the *paradox* by *relativizing* the *knowledge* and the *belief* to the *parts*, and it *fits* the *psychoanalytic* *tradition*.

**(ii) The *‘*motivated* *belief’* *response***. The *desire* for the *belief* *causes* the *belief*, and the *causation* *does* not *require* the *contradiction* (the *agent* *believes* the *falsehood* *because* she *wants* to, and the *belief* *is* *not* *undermined* by the *evidence*). The *response* *fits* the *empirical* *literature* (the *motivated* *reasoning*; Kunda 1990), and it *raises* the *question* of *whether* the *motivated* *belief* is *genuine* *belief*.

**(iii) The *‘*in*-*between*’* *response*** (Mele, *Irrationality*, 1987; *Self-Deception Unmasked*, 2001). The *self*-*deceiver* is *in* *between* *knowledge* and *belief*: she *has* the *evidence* but *avoids* *attending* to it, and the *avowal* of *belief* is *not* *fully* *sincere*. The *response* *preserves* the *phenomenology*, and it *weakens* the *paradox*.

### 6.6 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *akrasia* and *self*-*deception* *debates* are *unresolved*, and they are *the* *richest* *sources* of *data* about *irrationality* (§7).

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Socratic** | No one does wrong knowingly | Preserves the unity of reason | Denies the phenomenology |
| **Standard** | Akrasia is acting against better judgment | Fits the phenomenology | The paradox |
| **Davidson** | Akrasia is intentional but not rational | Preserves intentionality | The clear-eyed problem |
| **Divided mind** | The self is divided into parts | Solves both paradoxes | The unity of the self |
| **Degrees of confidence** | Akrasia is explained by probabilities | Fits Bayesianism | The phenomenology is weakened |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *hybrid*: *akrasia* is *explained* by *degrees* *of* *confidence* and *divided* *motivation*, and *self*-*deception* is *explained* by *motivated* *belief* and *inattention*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *paradoxes* are *not* *fully* *solved*.

## 7. Irrationality as Data

### 7.1 The evidence

**The *claim***. The *empirical* *sciences* *provide* *data* about *irrationality* that the *philosophical* *theories* *must* *accommodate*.

**The *sources*.

| Source | The *phenomenon* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychiatry** | *Delusions*, *addiction*, *compulsion* | The *Capgras* *delusion*; the *opioid* *addiction* |
| **Behavioural economics** | *Framing*, *anchoring*, *loss aversion* | The *Asian* *disease* *problem*; the *endowment* *effect* |
| **Social psychology** | *Confirmation bias*, *groupthink*, *situationism* | The *Wason* *selection* *task*; the *Milgram* *experiment* |
| **Cognitive science** | *Dual-process* *cognition* | *System* *1* (fast, automatic); *System* *2* (slow, deliberate) |

**The *significance*.** The *data* *show* that *irrationality* is *pervasive*, *systematic*, and *predictable*, and the *theories* of *rationality* (§4) *must* *explain* it.

### 7.2 Behavioural economics

**The *findings*** (Kahneman & Tversky 1979, 1981; Thaler 1980; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| Effect | The *finding* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Framing** | The *description* affects the *choice* | The *Asian* *disease* *problem* (the *same* *outcomes*, *different* *frames*) |
| **Anchoring** | The *initial* *value* affects the *estimate* | The *wheel* *of* *fortune* (the *random* *number* affects the *estimate*) |
| **Loss aversion** | *Losses* *loom* *larger* than *gains* | The *endowment* *effect* (the *willingness* to *pay* vs. *accept*) |
| **Present bias** | *Immediate* *rewards* are *overweighted* | The *marshmallow* *test*; the *savings* *behaviour* |
| **Overconfidence** | The *agents* are *overconfident* in their *judgments* | The *calibration* *literature* |

**The *interpretation*.** The *findings* *undermine* the *model* of the *rational* *agent* (§4.5), and they *support* the *dual*-*process* *model* (the *System* *1* *heuristics* *produce* the *biases*).

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*ecological*’* *objection*** (Gigerenzer 2002; Ch. 10, §7.4). The *heuristics* are *adapted* to the *environment*, and the *biases* are *artifacts* of the *laboratory* (the *frequency* *format* *removes* the *base-rate* *fallacy*). The *objection* *qualifies* the *conclusion*.

**(ii) The *‘*replication*’* *objection***. The *findings* are *contested* (the *replication* *crisis*; Ch. 3, §6.3), and the *robustness* of the *effects* is *uncertain*.

**(iii) The *‘*normative*’* *objection***. The *findings* *describe* *deviations* from a *model*, and the *model* is *not* *normatively* *authoritative* (the *rational* *agent* *model* is an *idealisation*; §4.5).

### 7.3 Psychiatry

**The *findings***. *Delusions* (the *Capgras* *delusion*: the *belief* that a *loved* *one* has been *replaced* by an *impostor*), *addiction* (the *compulsive* *use* *despite* the *harm*), and *compulsion* (the *obsessive* *behaviour*).

**The *interpretation*.** The *findings* *challenge* the *belief*-*desire* *model* (the *delusions* are *not* *rational* *beliefs*; the *addictions* are *not* *rational* *desires*), and they *support* the *divided*-*mind* *and* *dual*-*process* *accounts*.

**The *objections*.

**(i) The *‘*continuity*’* *objection***. The *psychiatric* *conditions* are *continuous* with *ordinary* *irrationality* (the *delusions* *are* *extreme* *forms* of *ordinary* *bias*), and the *continuity* *undermines* the *distinction* between the *pathological* and the *normal*.

**(ii) The *‘*normative*’* *objection***. The *psychiatric* *classification* *presupposes* a *norm* of *rationality*, and the *norm* is *contested* (Ch. 42, §3).

### 7.4 What the data show

**The *theses***.

**(i) Rationality is not a single capacity.** The *data* *suggest* that *rationality* is a *cluster* of *capacities* (the *logical*, the *probabilistic*, the *practical*), and the *capacities* *dissociate* (a *person* can be *excellent* at *logic* and *terrible* at *statistics*).

**(ii) Irrationality is systematic.** The *deviations* are *predictable* (the *biases* are *robust*), and the *predictability* *supports* the *dual*-*process* *model*.

**(iii) The normative model is an idealisation.** The *rational* *agent* *model* is *not* *feasible*, and the *norms* *must* be *bounded* (§4.4).

**(iv) The data are theory-laden.** The *interpretation* of the *findings* *depends* on the *theory* of *rationality*, and the *theory* is *contested*.

**The *consequence*.** The *philosophy* of *rationality* *must* be *informed* by the *empirical* *sciences*, and the *sciences* *must* be *informed* by the *philosophy* (the *“*methodological* *pluralism”*; Ch. 3, §6.4).

### 7.5 The state of the debate

**The *assessment*.** The *irrationality* *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *the* *most* *active* *interface* between *philosophy* and the *empirical* *sciences*.

| Position | The *claim* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heuristics and biases** | Humans are systematically irrational | Fits the lab | The ecological objection |
| **Ecological rationality** | The heuristics are adaptive | Fits the environment | The normative question |
| **Dual-process** | Two systems of cognition | Fits the data | The system boundaries |
| **Bounded rationality** | The norms must be feasible | Fits the limits | The normativity |

**The *most* *defensible* *position*.** A *qualified* *dual*-*process* *view*: the *System* *1* *heuristics* are *adaptive* (the *ecological* *insight*) and *bounded* (the *Simon* *insight*), and the *System* *2* *deliberation* is *the* *normative* *ideal* (the *rationalist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *ideal* is *not* *feasible* *for* *all* *agents* *in* *all* *contexts*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *explanatory* and *normative* *reasons* are *distinct*.** The *distinction* is *essential* for *clarity*, and the *connection* between the *two* is *contingent*.
- **The *causalism*/*anti-causalism* *debate* is *unresolved*.** Davidson's *thesis* is *influential*, and the *content* *problem* is *serious*.
- **The *internalism*/*externalism* *debate* is *unresolved*.** The *Humean* *theory* is *influential*, and the *objections* (the *besire*, the *ginger*-*haired* *man*) are *serious*.
- **The *belief*-*desire* *model* is the *dominant* *framework*.** The *direction* *of* *fit* *distinction* is *constitutive* of the *model*, and the *objections* (the *catch*-*all* *desire*, the *phenomenology*) are *serious*.
- **The *instrumental* *principle* is *necessary* but *not* *sufficient*.** The *substantive* *norms* are *real*, and the *myth* *of* *instrumental* *rationality* is *well*-*founded*.
- **The *practical*/*theoretical* *distinction* is *real*.** The *procedural* *norms* are *symmetric*, and the *substantive* *norms* are *asymmetric*.
- **Akrasia and self-deception are *real*.** The *paradoxes* are *not* *fully* *resolved*, and the *hybrid* *accounts* are *most* *promising*.
- **Irrationality is *systematic*.** The *empirical* *findings* *support* the *dual*-*process* *model*, and the *normative* *model* is an *idealisation*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *reasons*.** Are they *desires*, *facts*, or *primitive*?
- **The *causation* of *action*.** Are *reasons* *causes*, and is the *content* *causally* *relevant*?
- **The *direction* *of* *fit*.** Is the *metaphor* *adequate*, or does it *mislead*?
- **The *myth* of *instrumental* *rationality*.** Is the *instrumentalist* *view* *false*, and what *replaces* it?
- **The *unity* of *reason*.** Are the *practical* and *theoretical* *norms* the *same*?
- **The *solution* to the *akrasia* and *self*-*deception* *paradoxes*.
- **The *normative* *status* of the *empirical* *findings*.

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The *chapter* is a *case* *study* in the *method* of *Part* I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* among the *three* *kinds* of *reason*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *ginger*-*haired* *man*, the *clear*-*eyed* *akrates*, the *self*-*deceiver* — are the *engine* of the *debate*.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *belief*-*desire* *model*, the *instrumental* *principle* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *rationality* is a *field* in *transition*. The *classical* *positions* are *untenable* in their *pure* forms; the *contemporary* *positions* are *promising* but *incomplete*; the *empirical* *sciences* are *transforming* the *debate*; the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled* *answer*, but a *sharper* *understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Davidson's causalism** | Reasons are causes | Fits the naturalistic framework | The content problem |
| **Anti-causalism** | Reasons are not causes | Preserves the autonomy of the mental | Denies the efficacy |
| **Anomalous monism** | Identity without reduction | Preserves efficacy and closure | The content is idle |
| **Humean internalism** | Reasons depend on desires | Fits the explanatory role | The ginger-haired man |
| **Williams's internalism** | Reasons connect to S | Grounds the normative in the motivational | The amoralist |
| **Externalism** | Reasons are primitive facts | Grounds objectivity | Motivational force |
| **Belief-desire model** | Actions are explained by belief-desire pairs | Fits the explanation | The catch-all desire |
| **Instrumentalism** | The instrumental principle is the whole of practical reason | Simple | The myth objection |
| **Substantivism** | The substantive norms are irreducible | Fits the moral practice | The pluralism |
| **Socratic** | No one does wrong knowingly | Preserves the unity of reason | Denies the phenomenology |
| **Davidson's akrasia** | Akratic action is intentional but not rational | Preserves intentionality | The clear-eyed problem |
| **Divided mind** | The self is divided into parts | Solves both paradoxes | The unity of the self |
| **Heuristics and biases** | Humans are systematically irrational | Fits the lab | The ecological objection |
| **Ecological rationality** | The heuristics are adaptive | Fits the environment | The normative question |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *guise* *of* *the* *good* *and* *the* *Socratic* *tradition*.** The *view* that *desires* *represent* their *objects* as *good* (the *“*guise* *of* *the* *good”*) is *ancient* (Plato, Aristotle) and *contemporary* (the *essays* in *The Guise of the Good*, ed. the contemporary literature). The *view* *connects* the *theory* of *desire* to the *theory* of *value* (Ch. 44), and it is *contested* by the *wicked* *desires*.

**2. The *moral* *rationality* *debate*.** The *question* of *whether* *moral* *reasons* are *internal* or *external* (§2) is *central* to *metaethics* (Ch. 44, §4), and the *amoralist* *challenge* is *the* *test* *case*. The *contemporary* *literature* (the *essays* in *Moral Psychology*, ed. Sinnott-Armstrong, 2008) is *rich* and *unresolved*.

**3. The *AI* *rationality* *debate*.** The *AI* *systems* *are* *trained* on *human* *data*, and they *reproduce* the *human* *biases* (the *algorithmic* *bias* *literature*). The *question* of *whether* *AI* *systems* *can* be *rational* (in the *normative* *sense*) is *connected* to the *theory* of *rationality* (this chapter) and to the *ethics* of *AI* (Ch. 51, §3), and it is *urgent*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *rationality*.** The *Buddhist* *tradition* *developed* a *theory* of *reason* that *emphasizes* the *conditioning* of the *mind* (the *“*dependent* *origination”*; Ch. 18, §4) and the *practical* *goal* of *liberation* (the *“*enlightenment”* *goal*), and the *theory* *challenges* the *Western* *dichotomy* between *theoretical* and *practical* *reason*. The *Nyāya* *tradition* *developed* a *sophisticated* *theory* of *inference* (*anumāna*) that *anticipates* the *contemporary* *epistemology* (Ch. 10, §2). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *ethics* of *irrationality*.** The *attribution* of *irrationality* has *ethical* *consequences* (the *paternalism* *debate*, the *nudge* *debate*, the *responsibility* *debate*; Ch. 47, §4). The *ethics* of *irrationality* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent* (the *“*nudge*”* *literature*; Thaler & Sunstein 2008; the *critique* in the *contemporary* *literature*).

## Summary

- **Reasons are *explanatory*, *normative*, or *motivating*.** The *three* *kinds* are *distinct*, and the *connection* between them is *contingent*.
- **Davidson's *thesis*** is that *reasons* are *causes*; the *logical* *connection* *argument* is *answered* by the *distinction* between *events* and *descriptions*; **anomalous monism** *preserves* the *efficacy* of the *mental* and the *closure* of the *physical*, and the *content* *problem* is *serious*.
- **The *internalism*/*externalism* *debate*** is about whether *normative* *reasons* *depend* on the *agent*'s *motivations*. The *Humean* *theory* *grounds* the *internalist* *view*; *Scanlon* and *Parfit* *defend* the *externalist* *view*; the *debate* is *unresolved*.
- **The *belief*-*desire* *model*** is the *dominant* *framework*; the *direction* *of* *fit* *distinguishes* *belief* from *desire*; the *objections* (the *catch*-*all* *desire*, the *cognitivism* *about* *desire*) are *serious*.
- **The *instrumental* *principle*** is *necessary* but *not* *sufficient*; the *myth* *of* *instrumental* *rationality* is *well*-*founded*; the *substantive* *norms* are *real*; *bounded* *rationality* *constrains* the *norms*.
- **The *practical*/*theoretical* *distinction*** is *real*; the *procedural* *norms* are *symmetric*, and the *substantive* *norms* are *asymmetric*.
- **Akrasia and self-deception** are *real*; the *paradoxes* are *not* *fully* *resolved*; the *hybrid* *accounts* (the *degrees* *of* *confidence*, the *divided* *mind*, the *motivated* *belief*) are *most* *promising*.
- **Irrationality is *systematic*; the *empirical* *findings* *support* the *dual*-*process* *model*, and the *normative* *model* is an *idealisation*.
