# Chapter 21 — Reduction, Emergence, and the Levels of Reality

> “The whole is not simply the sum of its parts; it is something different from them, and the difference is not a mere difference of arrangement.”
> — C. D. Broad, *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925), Ch. II

> “If a philosopher asks whether an exact translation of the language of psychology into that of neurophysiology is possible, the answer is that it is not; and that is not because psychology is autonomous, but because the translation does not have to be possible for the identity to be true.”
> — Jerry Fodor, “Special Sciences” (1974), in effect

> “The problem is not that there are too many levels, but that there is no principled way of saying how many there are.”
> — the level problem, stated baldly

---

Chapter 20 examined the *mind-first* options, and it ended with the *rehabilitation* of positions that the twentieth century had dismissed. This chapter turns to the *framework* within which the debate between physicalism, idealism, and monism *takes* place: the *structure* of reality as a *hierarchy* of *levels*.

The chapter is the *metaphysical* counterpart of Chapter 8. In Chapter 8, we asked whether *justification* is *foundational*, *coherentist*, or *reliabilist* — whether the structure of reasons *rests on* a base, *forms* a web, or *depends on* a process. Here we ask the *same* question about *reality*: does the world *rest on* a fundamental level, *form* a web of interdependent levels, or *admit* of no hierarchy at all?

The chapter's central claim is that the *debate* about reduction and emergence has been *distorted* by a false dichotomy. The *dichotomy* — either the higher level is *reducible* to the lower (and therefore *nothing over and above* it), or it is *emergent* (and therefore *mysterious* and *causally* *idle*) — is *false*. The *contemporary* literature has *replaced* the dichotomy with a *spectrum* of relations: *identity*, *deduction*, *functionalization*, *mechanism*, *supervenience*, *realization*, *grounding*, *emergence*. The *spectrum* is the *subject* of this chapter.

The chapter proceeds in nine movements. §1 maps the debate. §2 examines *reduction as deduction*. §3 examines *reduction as identity and functionalization*. §4 examines *reduction as mechanism*. §5 examines *emergence*. §6 examines the *structural apparatus* of *supervenience*, *realization*, and *grounding*. §7 examines the *exclusion* *argument* and the *autonomy* of the higher levels. §8 examines *cross-level* *explanation* and the *levels problem*. §9 examines *reduction in practice*. §10 examines *fundamentality* and the *regress of grounds*. §11 examines *natural kinds* across levels. §12 gives an honest verdict.

---

## 1. The Map of the Debate

### 1.1 The vocabulary

The *debate* is conducted in a *vocabulary* that is *systematically* *ambiguous*, and the *ambiguity* is the *source* of much *confusion*. The *terms*.

| Term | The *question* | The *domain* |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduction** | Is the *higher* level *derivable* from the *lower*? | *Epistemic* / *semantic* |
| **Identity** | Is the *higher* level *identical* to the *lower*? | *Metaphysical* |
| **Realization** | Is the *higher* level *implemented* by the *lower*? | *Metaphysical* |
| **Supervenience** | Does the *higher* level *covary* with the *lower*? | *Metaphysical* |
| **Grounding** | Is the *higher* level *constituted* by the *lower*? | *Metaphysical* |
| **Emergence** | Does the *higher* level *arise* from the *lower* *without* being *reducible*? | *Metaphysical* |
| **Autonomy** | Is the *higher* level *independent* of the *lower*? | *Epistemic* / *methodological* |

**The *consequence*.** “Reduction” *can* *mean* *any* of *four* *things* (Ch. 3, §1.5): a *semantic* *relation* (translation), an *epistemic* *relation* (derivation), a *metaphysical* *relation* (identity), or a *methodological* *strategy* (the *replacement* of one *theory* by *another*). The *debate* *confuses* the *four*, and the *confusion* is the *first* *thing* to *clear*.

### 1.2 The classic positions

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *paradigm* |
|---|---|---|
| **Reductive physicalism** | Everything is *reducible* to the *fundamental* *physical* | Oppenheim & Putnam (1958); Nagel (1961) |
| **Non-reductive physicalism** | Everything *supervenes* on the *physical*, but the *higher* levels are *autonomous* | Fodor (1974); Putnam (1967); Davidson (1970) |
| **Emergentism** | The *higher* levels *emerge* from the *lower*, and are *not* *reducible* | Broad (1925); Morgan (1923); the *contemporary* *emergentists* (Clayton, Davies) |
| **Pluralism** | There are *many* *levels*, and *no* *single* *level* is *fundamental* | the *structuralists* (Ladyman, Ross) |
| **Eliminativism** | The *higher* levels are *illusory*; only the *fundamental* is *real* | the *Churchlands* (Ch. 23, §6) |

**The *map*.**

```
                    REDUCTIVE
                    (one level)
                          |
                          v
                IDENTITY / DERIVATION
                          |
    +---------------------+---------------------+
    |                                           |
    v                                           v
NON-REDUCTIVE                          ELIMINATIVISM
(autonomy)                             (one level)
    |                                           |
    v                                           v
SUPERVENIENCE / GROUNDING              ELIMINATION
    |                                           |
    +---------------------+---------------------+
                          |
                          v
                      EMERGENTISM
                      (new levels)
                          |
                          v
                     PLURALISM
                     (many levels)
```

**The *structure*.** The *map* is *not* a *line*; it is a *space* of *positions*. The *reductive* and the *eliminativist* *positions* *agree* that there is *one* *fundamental* *level*; they *disagree* on whether the *higher* levels are *real* or *illusory*. The *non-reductive* and the *emergentist* *positions* *agree* that the *higher* levels are *real*; they *disagree* on whether the *higher* levels are *causally* *efficacious* and *autonomous*. The *pluralist* *position* *denies* that there is a *single* *fundamental* *level*, and it is the *most* *radical*.

### 1.3 The level metaphor

**The *metaphor*.** The *world* is *structured* as a *hierarchy* of *levels*: physics → chemistry → biology → psychology → sociology. Each *level* has its *own* *entities*, *properties*, and *laws*; each *level* *depends* on the *level* *below* it; and the *levels* are *ordered* by *dependence*.

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *individuation* *problem***. How do we *individuate* a *level*? The *answer* (the *entities*, the *laws*, the *scale*, the *mechanisms*, the *interests*) is *contested*, and the *answers* *diverge*.

**(ii) The *ordering* *problem***. What *makes* one *level* *lower* than *another*? The *answer* (composition, dependence, scale, fundamentality) is *contested*.

**(iii) The *number* *problem***. How *many* *levels* are there? The *answer* (two, three, many, infinite) is *contested*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*.

**The *consequence*.** The *level* *metaphor* is *useful* but *imprecise*. The *contemporary* *literature* has *developed* more *precise* *notions* (supervenience, realization, grounding, mechanism), and the *notions* are the *subject* of §§2–6.

### 1.4 Why it matters

**The *stakes*.**

1. **The *mind-body problem*** (Ch. 23): is the *mental* *reducible* to the *physical*?
2. **The *special sciences*** (Ch. 39): are *biology*, *psychology*, and *economics* *autonomous*?
3. **The *causal* *exclusion* *problem*** (Ch. 18, §4; Ch. 23, §5): is the *mental* *causally* *efficacious*?
4. **The *value* *of* *the* *higher* *levels***: if the *higher* levels are *mere* *arrangements* of the *lower*, are they *real*? Do they *matter*?

**The *thesis* of the chapter.** The *answer* to *all* *four* is *pluralist*: the *higher* levels are *real*, *autonomous*, and *causally* *efficacious* — *and* they *depend* on the *lower* levels. The *dependence* and the *autonomy* are *not* *incompatible*; they are *two* *aspects* of the *same* *structure*.

## 2. Reduction as Deduction

### 2.1 The logical empiricist model

**The *thesis*.** A *theory* $T_2$ is *reduced* to a *theory* $T_1$ when the *laws* of $T_2$ are *logically* *derivable* from the *laws* of $T_1$, *given* *bridge* *principles* that *connect* the *vocabularies* of the *two* *theories*.

**The *statement*.** Oppenheim and Putnam, “Unity of Science as a Working Hypothesis” (1958); Nagel, *The Structure of Science* (1961), Ch. 11; Hempel, *Aspects of Scientific Explanation* (1965), Ch. 8.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The bridge principles: } \forall x\, (F_2 x \leftrightarrow F_1 x) \text{ (the identification of the predicates).} \\
2. & \text{The laws of } T_1: L_1, \ldots, L_n. \\
3. & \text{The laws of } T_2: M_1, \ldots, M_k. \\
\hline
4. & \text{The derivation: } L_1, \ldots, L_n, \text{bridge} \vdash M_1, \ldots, M_k.
\end{array}
$$

**The *paradigm*.** *Thermodynamics* is *reduced* to *statistical* *mechanics*: the *laws* of *thermodynamics* (the *second* *law*, the *ideal* *gas* *law*) are *derived* from the *laws* of *statistical* *mechanics*, *given* the *bridge* *principles* (temperature = mean kinetic energy; entropy = $k \ln W$).

**The *virtue*.** The *model* *captures* the *scientific* *practice* of *derivation*, and it *provides* a *precise* *criterion* of *reduction*.

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *bridge* *principle* *problem***. The *bridge* *principles* *are* *the* *reduction*, not a *premise* of it. The *principles* (temperature = mean kinetic energy) *are* the *substantive* *claims* that *need* *justification*, and the *model* *assumes* them.

**(ii) The *meaning* *problem***. The *bridge* *principles* *require* that the *predicates* of $T_2$ and $T_1$ have *the* *same* *meaning*, and the *requirement* is *too* *strong* (the *predicates* *change* *meaning* in the *reduction*).

**(iii) The *multiple* *realizability* *problem***. The *higher* *predicates* are *multiply* *realizable*, and the *bridge* *principles* cannot *capture* the *multiple* *realization* (Ch. 23, §3).

**(iv) The *discovery* *problem***. The *model* *describes* the *justification* of a *reduction*, not the *discovery*. The *scientific* *practice* is *messier*: the *reduction* is *discovered* *piecemeal*, *guided* by *analogies* and *mechanisms*, not by the *derivation* of *laws*.

### 2.2 Nagel's two types

**Nagel’s *distinction*** (*The Structure of Science*, 1961, pp. 345–354).

| Type | The *relation* between the *theories* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Homogeneous reduction** | The *laws* of $T_2$ are *derivable* from $T_1$ *without* *change* of *meaning* | *Galilean* → *Newtonian* mechanics |
| **Heterogeneous reduction** | The *laws* of $T_2$ are *derivable* from $T_1$ *only* with a *change* of *meaning* | *Thermodynamics* → *statistical* mechanics |

**The *significance*.** The *heterogeneous* *reduction* is the *interesting* *case*, and it is the *case* in which the *bridge* *principles* are *substantive*. The *distinction* *anticipates* the *contemporary* *debate* about *multiple* *realizability* and the *nature* of *bridge* *laws*.

### 2.3 The criticisms

**The *most* *influential* *criticisms*.**

**(i) Feyerabend (1962, 1963).** The *reduction* is *not* a *derivation*; it is a *replacement*. The *older* *theory* is *not* *derived* from the *newer*; it is *refuted* by it. The *meaning* of the *terms* *changes*, and the *older* *theory* is *incommensurable* with the *newer*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The terms of } T_2 \text{ change meaning in the reduction to } T_1. \\
2. & \text{The laws of } T_2 \text{ are not derivable from } T_1 \text{ if the meaning changes.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The reduction is replacement, not derivation.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *reductive* *model* is *untenable*, and the *philosophy* of *science* *abandoned* it in the *1960s* and *1970s*.

**(ii) Kuhn (1962).** The *reduction* is a *paradigm* *shift*, not a *derivation*. The *standards* of *explanation*, the *problems*, and the *concepts* *change* in the *shift*, and the *change* is *not* *captured* by the *logical* *model*.

**(iii) Sklar (1967).** The *reduction* of *thermodynamics* to *statistical* *mechanics* is *problematic*: the *second* *law* is *not* *derivable* from the *time-reversal* *invariant* *laws* of *statistical* *mechanics* *without* the *past* *hypothesis* (Ch. 17, §6.2). The *derivation* *requires* a *cosmological* *assumption*, and the *assumption* is *not* *part* of the *reducing* *theory*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *logical* *empiricist* *model* is *dead*. The *reduction* *relation* is *not* a *derivation*; it is a *complex* *relation* that *involves* *meaning* *change*, *mechanism*, and *approximation*. The *contemporary* *accounts* (§§3–4) *replace* the *model*.

## 3. Reduction as Identity and Functionalization

### 3.1 The identity theory

**The *thesis*.** The *higher*-level *properties* are *identical* to *lower*-level *properties*. The *mind* is the *brain*; *water* is *H₂O*; *temperature* is *mean* *molecular* *kinetic* *energy*.

**The *statement*.** Place (“Is Consciousness a Brain Process?”, 1956); Smart (“Sensations and Brain Processes,” 1959); Feigl (*The “Mental” and the “Physical”*, 1967).

**The *argument*** (Smart 1959).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The mental states are correlated with brain states (the empirical premise).} \\
2. & \text{The correlation is not accidental; it requires an explanation.} \\
3. & \text{The best explanation is identity (Occam's razor; Ch. 14, §4).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The mental states are identical to brain states.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *avoids* the *interaction* *problem* (the *mental* is *the* *physical*), and it *fits* the *naturalistic* *worldview*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *multiple* *realizability* *objection*** (Putnam 1967). The *same* *mental* *state* (pain) can be *realized* by *different* *physical* *states* in *different* *organisms* (octopi, mammals, aliens). If the *mental* *state* is *identical* to a *physical* *state*, the *identity* *fails* for the *organisms* with *different* *physical* *states*.

**(ii) The *Leibniz* *law* *objection***. If the *mental* *state* and the *brain* *state* are *identical*, they must have *all* the *same* *properties*. But the *mental* *state* has *properties* (subjectivity, intentionality) that the *brain* *state* *does* *not* *have* — or so the *objection* *goes*.

**(iii) The *explanatory* *gap* *objection*** (Levine 1983). Even if the *identity* is *true*, it *does* not *explain* *why* the *mental* *state* *feels* the *way* it *does*. (See Ch. 24, §2.)

### 3.2 Multiple realizability

**The *argument*** (Putnam 1967; Fodor 1974).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The mental state } M \text{ is realized by different physical states in different organisms } (P_1, P_2, P_3, \ldots). \\
2. & \text{If } M \text{ is identical to a physical state, it must be identical to one of the } P_i. \\
3. & \text{No single } P_i \text{ is common to all the organisms.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The mental state is not identical to any physical state.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *identity* *theory* is *false* (at the *type* *level*), and the *higher*-level *properties* are *autonomous*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *token* *identity* *response*** (Davidson 1970). The *type* *identity* *fails*, but the *token* *identity* *holds*: *each* *particular* *mental* *event* is *identical* to a *particular* *physical* *event*, even though the *types* are *different*. The *response* *preserves* *physicalism* without *type* *reduction*.

**(ii) The *disjunctive* *response***. The *mental* *type* is *identical* to a *disjunction* of *physical* *types* ($P_1 \vee P_2 \vee P_3 \vee \ldots$). The *response* *saves* the *type* *identity*, but the *disjunction* is *not* a *natural* *kind* (Ch. 15, §5.1), and the *identity* is *uninformative*.

**(iii) The *scientific* *response***. The *multiple* *realizability* *argument* *assumes* that the *mental* *types* are *defined* *independently* of the *physical* *types*. If the *types* are *defined* *functionally* (by their *causal* *role*), the *multiple* *realizability* is *compatible* with the *type* *identity* at the *functional* *level*. (The *response* is *contested*; see §3.3.)

### 3.3 Functionalization

**The *thesis*.** The *higher*-level *property* is *identical* to a *functional* *property* — a *property* *defined* by its *causal* *role* — and the *functional* *property* is *multiply* *realizable* by *different* *physical* *realizers*.

**The *statement*.** Lewis, “An Argument for the Identity Theory” (1966), and “Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications” (1972); Armstrong, *A Materialist Theory of the Mind* (1968).

**The *structure*.**

$$
M = \text{the property that plays the causal role } R
$$

The *mental* *property* *is* the *functional* *property*; the *physical* *realizer* is the *occupant* of the *role*.

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *reconciles* the *identity* *theory* with *multiple* *realizability*: the *mental* *type* is *identical* to the *functional* *type*, and the *functional* *type* is *realized* by *different* *physical* *types*.

**The *objection*.** The *functional* *property* is *not* a *physical* *property*, and the *view* is a *form* of *property* *dualism* (Ch. 23, §7), not a *form* of *physicalism*. (The *reply*: the *functional* *property* is *realized* by the *physical*, and the *realization* *grounds* the *identity*; the *reply* *depends* on the *theory* of *realization*; §6.)

### 3.4 The *a priori* versus *a posteriori* identity

**The *distinction*.** The *identity* between *mental* and *physical* (or between *water* and *H₂O*) is *a posteriori*: it is *discovered* *empirically*, not *derived* *a priori*.

**The *significance*.** The *identity* *theorists* *argue* that the *mind-brain* *identity* is *like* the *water-H₂O* *identity*: it is a *scientific* *discovery*, and it is *necessary* *a posteriori* (Ch. 9, §3.3).

**The *objection*.** The *analogy* *fails* for *consciousness*: the *water-H₂O* *identity* *explains* the *observable* *properties* of *water* (it is *transparent*, *tasteless*, *boils* *at* 100°C), but the *mind-brain* *identity* *does* not *explain* the *phenomenal* *properties* (why *pain* *feels* the *way* it *does*). The *explanatory* *gap* (Ch. 24, §2) is the *residue*.

## 4. Reduction as Mechanism

### 4.1 The mechanist turn

**The *thesis*.** A *phenomenon* is *reduced* when the *mechanism* that *produces* it is *described*. The *reduction* is *not* a *derivation* or an *identity*; it is an *explanation* *by* *mechanism*.

**The *statement*.** Machamer, Darden & Craver, “Thinking about Mechanisms” (2000); Bechtel & Abrahamsen, “Explanation: A Mechanist Alternative” (2005); Craver, *Explaining the Brain* (2007); Glennan, “Mechanisms and the Nature of Causation” (1996).

**The *structure*.**

| Element | What it is | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenomenon** | The *behavior* to be *explained* | *Memory* *consolidation* |
| **Entities** | The *components* | Neurons, synapses, molecules |
| **Activities** | What the *entities* *do* | Firing, *binding*, *phosphorylation* |
| **Organization** | How the *components* are *arranged* | The *circuit* *structure* |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences explain phenomena by describing mechanisms.} \\
2. & \text{The description of a mechanism reduces the phenomenon to the activities of the components.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The reduction is mechanistic, not logical or identificatory.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *explanatory* *practice* of *biology*, *neuroscience*, and *cognitive* *science*. The *account* *handles* *complex* *causation* (Ch. 18, §4.2), and it *avoids* the *problems* of the *logical* *model* (the *bridge* *principles*, the *meaning* *change*).

### 4.2 The levels of mechanism

**The *structure*.** The *mechanism* is *nested*: the *mechanism* for *memory* is *composed* of *mechanisms* (synaptic *plasticity*, protein *synthesis*, gene *expression*) that are *themselves* composed of *mechanisms*.

**The *consequence*.** The *reduction* is *not* a *single* *step*; it is a *hierarchy* of *mechanisms*, each *level* *explaining* the *phenomenon* at its *level*.

**The *problem*.** The *nesting* *raises* the *problem* of *which* *level* is the *causal* *level*, and the *problem* is *connected* to the *exclusion* *argument* (§7).

**The *response*. The *mechanist* *argues* that *all* the *levels* are *causally* *relevant*: the *higher* *level* *describes* the *organization*, and the *organization* is *causally* *efficacious* (the *organization* *determines* *which* *lower*-level *activities* *occur*).

### 4.3 The criticisms

**(i) The *levels* *problem*** (Craver 2007, Ch. 5). The *mechanist* *account* *requires* a *principled* *way* of *individuating* the *levels*, and the *account* is *underdeveloped*. (See §8.)

**(ii) The *normativity* *problem*. The *mechanism* *description* *presupposes* a *distinction* between the *normal* *operation* and the *pathological* *deviation*, and the *distinction* is *normative* (Ch. 42, §2).

**(iii) The *fundamental* *physics* *problem*. The *mechanism* *account* is *not* *applicable* to *fundamental* *physics*, where there are *no* *mechanisms* (only *equations*).

**(iv) The *universality* *problem*. The *mechanism* *account* is *local*: it *applies* to *some* *sciences* (biology, neuroscience) and *not* to *others* (economics, fundamental physics). The *account* is *a* *model* of *reduction*, not *the* *model*.

## 5. Emergence

### 5.1 The British emergentists

**The *thesis*.** The *higher* *levels* *emerge* from the *lower* and are *not* *reducible* (or *predictable*) from them.

**The *statement*.** Morgan, *Emergent Evolution* (1923); Alexander, *Space, Time, and Deity* (1920); Broad, *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925).

**The *argument*** (Broad 1925, Ch. II).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Some properties of wholes are not predictable from the properties of the parts and their arrangement.} \\
2. & \text{The properties are not deducible from the laws of the parts.} \\
3. & \text{The properties are real, and they have causal efficacy.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The properties are emergent (not reducible).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. *Life* (from *chemistry*), *consciousness* (from *biology*), *mind* (from *matter*).

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *captures* the *intuition* that the *higher* levels are *novel*, and it *avoids* the *reductive* *consequence* that the *higher* levels are *nothing* *over* *and* *above* the *lower*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *predictability* *objection***. The *unpredictability* *of* a *property* from the *parts* is an *epistemic* *fact*, not a *metaphysical* one. The *emergence* *is* *weak* (epistemic), not *strong* (ontological).

**(ii) The *causal* *efficacy* *objection*. If the *emergent* *property* is *caused* by the *lower* level, and the *lower* level is *causally* *closed*, the *emergent* *property* is *excluded* from *causation* (§7).

**(iii) The *mystery* *objection*. The *emergentist* *posits* a *new* *kind* of *causal* *relation* (the *emergence* *relation*) that is *not* *reducible* to the *lower* *level*, and the *relation* is *mysterious*.

### 5.2 Weak and strong emergence

**The *distinction*** (Chalmers 2006; Bedau 1997; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| Kind | The *claim* | The *predictability* | The *causation* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weak emergence** | The *phenomenon* is *unpredictable* from the *parts*, but it is *derivable* by *simulation* | Unpredictable *a priori*, predictable *by* *simulation* | Causal *at* the *higher* *level* (the *simulation* *shows* the *causal* *role*) |
| **Strong emergence** | The *phenomenon* is *not* *derivable* from the *parts*, even *in* *principle* | Unpredictable *even* *by* *simulation* | Causal *at* the *higher* *level*, *not* *reducible* to the *lower* |

**The *weak* *version*** (Bedau 1997). The *emergence* is *nomological*: the *higher*-level *regularities* are *derivable* from the *lower* *laws* *plus* the *initial* *conditions*, but the *derivation* requires *simulation* (the *theoretical* *understanding* is *limited*). The *weak* *version* is *compatible* with *physicalism*.

**The *strong* *version*** (Chalmers 2006). The *higher*-level *regularities* are *not* *derivable* from the *lower* *laws*; the *emergence* is *ontological*, and it *posits* *new* *fundamental* *laws*. The *strong* *version* is *a* *form* of *property* *dualism*, and it *faces* the *exclusion* *argument*.

### 5.3 The case against strong emergence

**The *argument*** (Kim 1998, 2006; Chalmers 2006).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Strong emergence posits new fundamental causal laws.} \\
2. & \text{The new laws are not derivable from the lower laws, and they are not epistemically accessible.} \\
3. & \text{The positing of new laws is ad hoc, and it is not independently motivated.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Strong emergence is unmotivated (and probably false).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *most* *defensible* *version* of *emergence* is *weak*: the *higher* levels are *real* and *causally* *efficacious*, but they are *derivable* from the *lower* levels (by *simulation*, if not by *deduction*).

### 5.4 Emergence and the special sciences

**The *claim*.** The *special* *sciences* are *emergent* in the *weak* *sense*: the *phenomena* are *not* *predictable* from the *fundamental* *laws* *without* *simulation*, and the *higher*-level *concepts* are *necessary* for the *explanation*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The higher-level phenomena (life, mind, markets) are not predictable from the lower level without simulation.} \\
2. & \text{The higher-level concepts capture patterns that the lower-level concepts miss.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The special sciences are weakly emergent.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *weak* *emergence* *supports* the *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences* without *positing* *new* *fundamental* *laws*. The *autonomy* is *epistemic* and *methodological*, not *metaphysical*.

## 6. Supervenience, Realization, and Grounding

### 6.1 Supervenience

**The *thesis*.** The *higher*-level *properties* *supervene* on the *lower*-level *properties*: there can be *no* *difference* in the *higher*-level *properties* without a *difference* in the *lower*-level *properties*.

**The *formal* *statement*** (Kim 1984, 1990).

$$
(\mathrm{Weak}) \quad \forall w\, \forall w'\, [(\text{if } w, w' \text{ agree on } P \text{ then they agree on } Q)]
$$

$$
(\mathrm{Strong}) \quad \forall w\, \forall w'\, [(\text{if } w, w' \text{ agree on } P \text{ then they agree on } Q)] \text{ for all possible worlds}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *notion* *captures* the *dependence* without *committing* to *reduction*, and it is *compatible* with the *autonomy* of the *higher* levels.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *explanatory* *impotence***. Supervenience *states* the *dependence*, but it does not *explain* it. The *supervenience* *is* a *pattern*, not a *ground*.

**(ii) The *brute* *fact* *objection***. The *supervenience* *is* a *brute* *fact* unless it is *grounded* in a *deeper* *relation* (identity, realization, grounding).

**(iii) The *modal* *status* *objection***. The *strong* *supervenience* *requires* a *modal* *commitment* (the *supervenience* *holds* in *all* *possible* *worlds*), and the *commitment* is *contested* (Ch. 16, §2).

### 6.2 Realization

**The *thesis*.** The *higher*-level *property* is *realized* by a *lower*-level *property* when the *lower*-level *property* *plays* the *causal* *role* *specified* by the *higher*-level *property*.

**The *statement*.** Putnam (1967); Fodor (1974); Block (1990); Shoemaker (2007); the *contemporary* *literature* (the *“subset”* and *“determinable”* *accounts*).

**The *structure*.**

$$
F \text{ is realized by } G \text{ iff } G \text{ plays the causal role } R_F \text{ specified by } F
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *notion* *captures* the *dependence* *without* *identity*, and it *fits* the *multiple* *realizability* *argument*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *role* *problem***. What is the *causal* *role* of a *higher*-level *property*? The *role* is *specified* by the *higher*-level *theory*, and the *specification* is *not* *neutral* (it *depends* on the *theory*).

**(ii) The *realization* *problem***. What *is* the *realization* *relation*? The *answers* (the *subset* *account*, the *determinable* *account*, the *grounding* *account*) are *contested*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*.

### 6.3 Grounding

**The *thesis*.** The *higher*-level *facts* are *grounded* in the *lower*-level *facts*: the *higher*-level *facts* *obtain* *in* *virtue* *of* the *lower*-level *facts*.

**The *statement*.** Fine (2001, 2012); Rosen (2010); Schaffer (2009); the *structural* *account* (Ch. 16, §3.3).

**The *structure*.**

$$
\Gamma < \phi \quad \text{(the facts } \Gamma \text{ ground the fact } \phi \text{)}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *notion* *captures* the *explanatory* *direction* of the *dependence*, and it is *more* *fine-grained* than *supervenience*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *nature* of *grounding***. Is *grounding* a *real* *relation*, or a *notational* *variant* of *explanation*? (Ch. 16, §3.3.)

**(ii) The *levels* *problem***. The *grounding* *relation* *requires* *levels*, and the *levels* are *underdeveloped* (§8).

**(iii) The *regress* *problem***. The *grounding* *relation* *terminates* in the *fundamental*, and the *fundamental* is *defined* *negatively* (that *which* is *not* *grounded*). The *definition* is *uninformative*, and the *regress* *raises* the *problem* of the *fundamental* (§10).

### 6.4 The relations compared

**The *table*.**

| Relation | What it captures | Direction | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supervenience** | Covariation | No direction | Explanatory impotence |
| **Realization** | Causal role | Lower → higher | The role problem |
| **Grounding** | Constitution | Lower → higher | The nature of grounding |
| **Identity** | Sameness | — | Multiple realizability |
| **Emergence** | Novelty | Lower → higher | The causal exclusion |

**The *verdict*.** The *relations* are *distinct* and *not* *reducible* to one another. The *best* *account* is *pluralist*: the *relation* that *applies* *depends* on the *case* (identity for *water-H₂O*, realization for *pain-C-fibers*, grounding for *moral-natural*, emergence for *life-chemistry*).

## 7. The Exclusion Argument and the Autonomy of Higher Levels

### 7.1 The argument restated

The *causal* *exclusion* *argument* (Ch. 18, §4.1; Ch. 23, §5) is the *central* *argument* *against* the *autonomy* of the *higher* levels.

**The *argument*** (Kim 1998, 2005).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The physical domain is causally closed.} \\
2. & \text{The higher-level property } H \text{ causes the physical event } P. \\
3. & \text{The physical event } P \text{ has a sufficient physical cause } P^*. \\
4. & \text{If } H \text{ causes } P \text{ and } P^* \text{ causes } P, \text{ the cause is overdetermined.} \\
5. & \text{Overdetermination is implausible for every case.} \\
\hline
6. & \therefore \text{The higher-level property does not cause } P.
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence*.** The *higher* levels are *epiphenomenal*, and the *autonomy* is *an* *illusion*.

### 7.2 The responses

**(i) The *reductionist* *response*** (Kim 1998, 2005). The *higher* *level* is *reducible* to the *lower*, and the *reduction* *solves* the *exclusion*. The *cost* is the *loss* of the *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences*.

**(ii) The *emergentist* *response***. The *higher* *level* *emerges* and has *causal* *powers* *not* *reducible* to the *lower*. The *cost* is the *mystery* of *emergence* (§5).

**(iii) The *interventionist* *response*** (Woodward 2008; List & Menzies 2009). The *exclusion* *argument* *assumes* a *counterfactual* *account* of *causation*; the *interventionist* *account* *allows* *multiple* *causes* at *different* *levels*. The *cost* is *the* *pluralism* of *causation* (Ch. 18, §5).

**(iv) The *determinable* *response*** (Yablo 1992, 1997). The *higher*-level *properties* are *determinables* of *physical* *determinates*, and the *determinable* *is* *not* *excluded* *by* the *determinate* (the *determinable* *is* *realized* *by* the *determinate*). The *cost* is the *mystery* of *determinables*.

**(v) The *grounding* *response*** (Ch. 16, §3.3). The *higher*-level *facts* are *grounded* in the *lower*, and the *grounding* *explains* the *dependence* *without* *requiring* *causal* *efficacy* at the *higher* *level*. The *cost* is the *mystery* of *grounding*.

**(vi) The *non-interventionist* *response***. The *exclusion* *argument* *assumes* that *causation* is *univocal*, and the *assumption* is *false*. The *causation* *at* *different* *levels* is *different* *in* *kind*, and the *exclusion* *does* not *apply*. (The *response* is *a* *form* of *pluralism*.)

### 7.3 The autonomy of the special sciences

**The *thesis*** (Fodor 1974). The *special* *sciences* are *autonomous*: their *laws* are *not* *reducible* to *physics*, and their *explanations* are *irreducible*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences classify events by properties that are multiply realizable.} \\
2. & \text{The multiple realizability blocks type reduction (§3.2).} \\
3. & \text{The explanations of the special sciences are causal explanations at their own level.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The special sciences are autonomous.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *epistemic*/*metaphysical* *objection***. The *autonomy* is *epistemic*, not *metaphysical*: the *properties* *supervene* on the *physical*, and the *autonomy* is a *matter* of *description*. (The *reply*: the *supervenience* does *not* *reduce* the *causal* *claims*; the *causal* *claims* are *real* at their *level*.)

**(ii) The *exclusion* *objection***. The *causal* *exclusion* *argument* *threatens* the *autonomy* (§7.1).

**(iii) The *multiple* *realizability* *objection***. The *multiple* *realizability* *argument* is *contested*: some *properties* are *reducible* (the *temperature* is *reducible* to *mean* *kinetic* *energy*; the *gene* is *reducible* to *DNA* *sequences*; §9).

### 7.4 The *“autonomy”* *thesis* *refined*

**The *refined* *thesis*.** The *special* *sciences* are *autonomous* *in* *their* *explanations*, but *not* *in* *their* *ontology*. The *explanations* *pick* *out* *patterns* that the *lower* levels *miss*, and the *patterns* are *real* (they *support* *counterfactuals*, *predictions*, and *interventions*), but they *supervene* on the *lower* levels.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The higher-level patterns are real (they support counterfactuals and interventions).} \\
2. & \text{The patterns supervene on the lower-level facts.} \\
3. & \text{The supervenience does not reduce the patterns; it explains their dependence.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The special sciences are autonomous in explanation, dependent in ontology.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *refined* *thesis* *avoids* the *exclusion* *argument* (the *higher* levels are *real* but *not* *causally* *competing* with the *lower* levels) and the *reductionist* *consequence* (the *higher* levels are *not* *eliminated*). It is *the* *most* *defensible* *position* in the *debate*.

## 8. Cross-Level Explanation and the Levels Problem

### 8.1 The levels problem

**The *problem*.** What *is* a *level*, and how *many* are there?

**The *proposed* *criteria*.**

| Criterion | What a level is | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition** | The *parts* and the *whole* | The *parts* are *arbitrary* (the *“problem* *of* *the* *many”*; Ch. 17, §5.3) |
| **Scale** | The *spatial* or *temporal* *scale* | The *scales* are *continuous* (no *natural* *breaks*) |
| **Laws** | The *domain* of a *law* | The *domains* *overlap* (the *laws* *apply* *across* *levels*) |
| **Mechanisms** | The *nested* *mechanisms* | The *nesting* is *arbitrary* (the *“homunculus”* *problem*) |
| **Interests** | The *explanatory* *interests* | The *interests* are *relative* (§8.2) |

**The *consequence*.** The *levels* are *not* *given* by the *world*; they are *imposed* by the *explanatory* *interests*. The *imposition* is *not* *arbitrary* (the *interests* are *constrained* by the *world*), but it is *not* *unique* either.

### 8.2 The *perspectival* *account*

**The *thesis*** (Wimsatt 1976, 1994; Craver 2007; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *levels* are *perspectival*: a *level* is a *pattern* of *explanation* that is *selected* by the *interests* of the *inquirer*, and the *selection* is *not* *unique*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The levels are defined by the explanatory interests.} \\
2. & \text{The interests vary with the inquirer and the context.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The levels are perspectival, not absolute.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *explains* the *diversity* of *levels* across the sciences, and it *avoids* the *problem* of *individuating* the *levels* *absolutely*.

**The *objection*.** The *account* *threatens* the *realism* of the *levels*: if the *levels* are *perspectival*, they are *not* *features* of the *world*. (The *reply*: the *patterns* are *real*; the *levels* are *real* *patterns*, and the *selection* of the *pattern* is *perspectival* but the *pattern* is *not*.)

### 8.3 Cross-level explanation

**The *phenomenon*.** The *sciences* *explain* *phenomena* *across* *levels*: *neuroscience* *explains* *psychology*, *molecular* *biology* *explains* *genetics*, *statistical* *mechanics* *explains* *thermodynamics*.

**The *structure*.**

| Kind | The *direction* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Reductive** | Higher → lower | *Genetics* → *molecular* *biology* |
| **Integrative** | Lower → higher | *Molecular* *biology* → *genetics* |
| **Mechanistic** | Both | *Neuroscience* → *psychology* |
| **Contextual** | Higher → lower | The *environment* → the *gene* *expression* |

**The *significance*.** The *cross-level* *explanation* is *not* *unidirectional*: the *higher* levels *constrain* the *lower* (the *context* *determines* the *expression*), and the *lower* levels *implement* the *higher*.

**The *consequence*.** The *reduction* is *not* a *one-way* *relation*; it is a *network* of *constraints*, and the *network* is the *structure* of the *sciences*.

### 8.4 The *pluralist* *conclusion*

**The *thesis*.** The *levels* are *real* *patterns*, the *relations* among them are *plural* (identity, realization, grounding, mechanism, emergence), and the *choice* of *relation* *depends* on the *case*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The sciences use different relations to connect the levels.} \\
2. & \text{No single relation captures all the cases.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The correct account is pluralist.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *pluralism* *avoids* the *false* *dichotomy* (§0) and the *exclusion* *argument* (§7), and it *fits* the *practice* of the *sciences*.

## 9. Reduction in Practice

### 9.1 Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics

**The *case*.** *Thermodynamics* (the *phenomenological* *theory* of *heat*, *work*, and *entropy*) is *reduced* to *statistical* *mechanics* (the *microscopic* *theory* of *molecules*).

**The *structure* of the reduction**.

| Thermodynamic concept | Statistical-mechanical concept |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Mean kinetic energy ( $\frac{3}{2} k T = \langle E \rangle$ ) |
| **Entropy** | $S = k \ln W$ (Boltzmann) |
| **Heat** | Energy transfer at the microscopic level |
| **Second law** | The statistical tendency to higher entropy |

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *irreversibility* *problem*. The *second* *law* is *time-asymmetric*, and the *underlying* *mechanics* is *time-symmetric*. The *reduction* *requires* the *past* *hypothesis* (Ch. 17, §6.2), and the *hypothesis* is *not* *part* of the *reducing* *theory*.

**(ii) The *approximation* *problem*. The *reduction* is *not* *exact*: the *thermodynamic* *laws* hold *only* in the *thermodynamic* *limit* (large $N$, long times). The *reduction* is *approximate*, and the *approximation* is *not* *derived*.

**(iii) The *phase* *transition* *problem*. The *phase* *transitions* (the *critical* *phenomena*) are *not* *reducible* to the *microscopic* *laws* *without* *renormalization* *group* *techniques* (Wilson 1971), and the *techniques* *introduce* *new* *concepts* (universality, *fixed* *points*) that are *not* *in* the *microscopic* *theory*.

**The *lesson*.** The *reduction* is *not* a *simple* *derivation*; it is a *complex* *relation* that *involves* *approximation*, *limit* *operations*, and *new* *concepts*. The *reduction* *succeeds* in *explaining* the *thermodynamic* *laws*, but it does *not* *eliminate* the *thermodynamic* *level* (the *level* remains *explanatorily* *indispensable*).

### 9.2 Mendelian and molecular genetics

**The *case*.** *Mendelian* *genetics* (the *theory* of *heredity* based on *genes*) is *reduced* to *molecular* *biology* (the *theory* of *DNA* *sequences* and *protein* *synthesis*).

**The *structure* of the reduction**.

| Mendelian concept | Molecular concept |
|---|---|
| **Gene** | A DNA sequence that codes for a protein (or an RNA) |
| **Allele** | A variant of the DNA sequence |
| **Dominance** | The interaction of the gene products |
| **Segregation** | The behavior of chromosomes in meiosis |

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *gene* *concept* *problem*. The *molecular* *gene* is *not* the *Mendelian* *gene*: the *Mendelian* *gene* is defined by its *phenotypic* *effects* (a *unit* of *inheritance*), and the *molecular* *gene* is defined by its *sequence*. The *two* *concepts* *diverge*, and the *reduction* *changes* the *meaning* of "gene" (Ch. 42, §4).

**(ii) The *complexity* *problem*. The *relation* between the *genotype* and the *phenotype* is *not* *one-to-one*: *pleiotropy* (one gene → many traits), *polygeny* (many genes → one trait), *epistasis* (gene interactions), and *environmental* *dependence* make the *Mendelian* *laws* *approximate*.

**(iii) The *levels* *problem*. The *gene* is *not* a *single* *level*: it is a *sequence* (molecular), a *unit* of *inheritance* (Mendelian), a *unit* of *selection* (evolutionary), and a *unit* of *regulation* (developmental). The *levels* *overlap*, and the *reduction* *does* not *capture* the *multiplicity*.

**The *lesson*.** The *reduction* is *partial*: it *explains* *some* *Mendelian* *phenomena* but not *all*, and the *Mendelian* *level* remains *explanatorily* *indispensable* (Ch. 42, §4).

### 9.3 Economics and psychology

**The *case*.** *Economics* is *reduced* to *psychology*, and *psychology* to *neuroscience*.

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *emergence* *problem*. The *economic* *phenomena* (markets, prices, business cycles) are *emergent*: they *arise* from the *interactions* of *individuals*, and they are *not* *predictable* from the *individual* *psychology* *without* *simulation* (the *complexity* *of* the *system*).

**(ii) The *irreducibility* *problem*. The *economic* *laws* (supply and demand, the *efficient* *market* *hypothesis*) are *not* *derivable* from *psychology*: they *depend* on *institutional* *structure*, *norms*, and *aggregation* *effects* that are *not* *in* the *individual* *psychology*.

**(iii) The *valuational* *problem*. The *economic* *concepts* (utility, welfare, efficiency) are *normative*, and the *normativity* is *not* *reducible* to the *descriptive* *psychology* (Ch. 44, §1).

**The *lesson*.** The *social* *sciences* are the *strongest* *case* for *emergence* and *autonomy*: the *higher*-level *patterns* are *real*, *irreducible*, and *explanatorily* *indispensable*.

### 9.4 The lessons

**The *general* *lessons*.**

1. **Reduction is not a single relation.** The *cases* *exhibit* *different* *relations* (deduction, approximation, mechanism, *grounding*).
2. **Reduction is partial.** The *reductions* *succeed* *in* *explaining* *some* *phenomena* and *fail* for *others*.
3. **The higher levels remain indispensable.** The *levels* *remain* *explanatorily* *necessary*, even when the *reduction* *succeeds*.
4. **Reduction is a research program, not a verdict.** The *reductions* *are* *achievements* that *take* *decades* and *require* *new* *concepts*; they are *not* *given* by the *ontology*.
5. **The pluralism is confirmed by the practice.** The *sciences* *use* *different* *relations* in *different* *domains*, and the *pluralism* is the *best* *description* of the *practice*.

## 10. Fundamentality and the Regress of Grounds

### 10.1 The question

**The *question*.** Is there a *fundamental* *level*?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Fundamentalism** | There is a *fundamental* level (the *physical*) | The *demarcation* of the *fundamental* |
| **Infinitism** | The *grounding* *regress* is *infinite* | The *intelligibility* of the *regress* |
| **Holism** | The *grounding* *relations* are *circular* | The *vicious* *circle* |
| **Structuralism** | The *structure* is *fundamental*; the *nodes* are *derived* | The *status* of the *nodes* |

**The *argument* *for* *fundamentalism***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The grounding relation requires a terminus (the regress cannot be infinite).} \\
2. & \text{The terminus is the fundamental level.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{There is a fundamental level.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *assumes* that the *regress* *must* *terminate*, and the *assumption* is *precisely* what the *infinitist* *denies*. (This is the *regress* *problem* of Ch. 8, §1, *applied* to *grounding*.)

### 10.2 The fundamentality of physics

**The *claim*.** Physics is *fundamental*: it *describes* the *entities* and *laws* on which *everything* *else* *depends*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The entities of the special sciences are composed of physical entities.} \\
2. & \text{The composition relation terminates in the physical.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Physics is fundamental.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *composition* *problem*. The *composition* *relation* is *not* *well-defined* (the *special* *composition* *question*; Ch. 15, §4). If *composition* is *vague* or *plural*, the *fundamentality* of *physics* is *not* *established*.

**(ii) The *emergence* *problem*. The *higher* levels *emerge* from the *physical*, and the *emergence* *introduces* *new* *properties* *not* *present* in the *physical*. If the *properties* are *real* and *causally* *efficacious*, physics is *not* *exhaustive*.

**(iii) The *structuralist* *problem*. If the *fundamental* *level* is a *structure* (Ch. 19, §6.3), the *nodes* are *derived*, and the *fundamental* *level* is *not* a *level* of *entities* but a *structure*.

### 10.3 The regress

**The *argument*.** The *grounding* *regress* is *infinite*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Every grounded fact is grounded in a further fact.} \\
2. & \text{The further fact is itself grounded (unless it is fundamental).} \\
3. & \text{If every fact is grounded, the regress is infinite.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There is no fundamental level (or the regress is vicious).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *foundationalist* *response***. The *regress* *terminates* in the *fundamental* *facts*, and the *fundamental* *facts* are *self-grounding* (or *ungrounded*). The *response* *posits* a *terminus*, and the *terminus* is *mysterious* (the *same* *problem* as Ch. 8, §2).

**(ii) The *coherentist* *response***. The *grounding* *relations* *form* a *circle*, and the *circle* is *virtuous* (the *whole* *is* *self-supporting*). The *response* *faces* the *isolation* *objection* (Ch. 8, §3.3).

**(iii) The *infinitist* *response***. The *regress* is *infinite*, and the *infinity* is *not* *vicious* (the *grounding* *relation* is *not* *completed*, but *available*). The *response* *is* *contested* (Ch. 8, §1.2).

**(iv) The *structuralist* *response***. The *structure* is *fundamental*, and the *regress* is *dissolved* (the *structure* *is* *not* a *fact* that *needs* *grounding*; it is the *framework* *within* which *grounding* *occurs*). The *response* *is* *promising*, and it is *contested*.

### 10.4 The *fundamental* *revisited*

**The *question*.** What makes a *level* *fundamental*?

**The *criteria*.**

| Criterion | The *claim* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Compositional** | The *entities* are *not* *composed* of *other* *entities* | The *composition* *regress* |
| **Explanatory** | The *level* *explains* *everything* *else* | The *emergence* *problem* |
| **Causal** | The *level* *causes* *everything* *else* | The *exclusion* *problem* |
| **Nomological** | The *laws* are *not* *derived* from *other* *laws* | The *reduction* *problem* |
| **Ground-theoretic** | The *facts* are *not* *grounded* in *other* *facts* | The *regress* *problem* |

**The *consequence*.** The *fundamental* is *defined* *negatively* (not *composed*, not *explained*, not *caused*, not *grounded*), and the *negative* *definition* is *uninformative*.

**The *verdict*.** The *fundamentality* of physics is *a* *hypothesis* *motivated* by the *success* of *physics*, and it is *not* a *consequence* of the *structure* of *grounding*. The *hypothesis* is *defensible*, and it is *contested*.

## 11. Natural Kinds Across Levels

### 11.1 The question

**The *question*.** Are the *kinds* of the *special* *sciences* *real* *kinds*, or are they *conventional* *groupings* of *fundamental* *entities*?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind realism** | The *kinds* are *real* and *irreducible* | *Species* are *real* *kinds* |
| **Kind eliminativism** | The *kinds* are *illusory* | *Species* are *not* *real* (only *populations*) |
| **Kind pluralism** | The *kinds* are *real* *relative* to an *interest* | *Species* are *real* for *some* *purposes* |

**The *argument* *for* *kind* *realism***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The kinds support induction, explanation, and intervention (Ch. 15, §5.1).} \\
2. & \text{The support requires natural properties (the properties that carve nature at its joints).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The kinds are real.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument* *for* *eliminativism***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The kinds of the special sciences do not have sharp boundaries.} \\
2. & \text{The real kinds have sharp boundaries (the fundamental properties).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The kinds of the special sciences are not real.}
\end{array}
$$

### 11.2 The species problem

**The *problem*.** What *is* a *species*? The *answers* (the *biological* *species* *concept*, the *phylogenetic* *species* *concept*, the *ecological* *species* *concept*, the *morphological* *species* *concept*) *diverge*, and the *divergence* is *not* *resolved* (Ch. 42, §3).

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Species essentialism** | Species have *essences* | the *Aristotelian* *view* (rejected) |
| **Species nominalism** | Species are *conventional* | the *nominalist* *view* |
| **Species realism** | Species are *real* *individuals* (historical *entities*) | Ghiselin (1974); Hull (1978) |
| **Species pluralism** | Species are *real* *relative* to a *concept* | Kitcher (1984); Dupré (1993) |

**The *significance*.** The *species* *problem* is the *test* *case* for the *kind* *realism* *debate*: the *kinds* of the *special* *sciences* are *real* but *not* *essentialist*, and the *realism* must be *non-essentialist*.

### 11.3 Genes, emotions, and diseases

**The *cases*.**

**(i) Genes.** The *gene* *concept* *fragments* into *multiple* *concepts* (the *molecular* *gene*, the *Mendelian* *gene*, the *evolutionary* *gene*, the *regulatory* *gene*), and the *fragmentation* *suggests* that the *kind* is *plural* (Ch. 42, §4).

**(ii) Emotions.** The *emotion* *concept* *fragments* into *multiple* *kinds* (the *basic* *emotions*, the *social* *emotions*, the *moral* *emotions*), and the *fragmentation* *suggests* that the *kind* is *plural* (Ch. 30, §1).

**(iii) Diseases.** The *disease* *concept* *fragments* into *multiple* *kinds* (the *biological* *dysfunction*, the *statistical* *deviation*, the *social* *construction*), and the *fragmentation* is *contested* (the *naturalist* and the *constructivist* *views*).

**The *lesson*.** The *kinds* of the *special* *sciences* are *real* but *plural*: they are *real* *relative* to a *concept*, and the *concepts* *diverge*. The *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible* *position*.

### 11.4 Kind pluralism

**The *thesis*** (Dupré 1993; Kitcher 1984; the *contemporary* *literature*). There are *many* *real* *kinds*, and the *kinds* *cross-cut* one another. The *classification* is *plural* and *interest-relative*, and *no* *single* *classification* is *privileged*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The special sciences classify the world in different ways.} \\
2. & \text{The classifications are not reducible to a single classification (the fundamental).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The kinds are plural.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *pluralism* *fits* the *practice* of the *special* *sciences*, and it *avoids* the *eliminativist* *consequence* (the *kinds* are *real*, even if *plural*).

**The *objection*.** The *pluralism* *threatens* the *objectivity* of the *classifications*: if *every* *classification* is *real* *relative* to an *interest*, *no* *classification* is *privileged*. (The *reply*: the *classifications* are *constrained* by the *world*; the *pluralism* is *constrained*, not *arbitrary*. The *reply* is *contested*.)

## 12. The State of the Debate

### 12.1 What is settled

- **The logical empiricist model of reduction is dead.** The *derivation* *model* is *untenable*, and the *contemporary* *accounts* *replace* it.
- **Type identity is untenable for the special sciences.** The *multiple* *realizability* *argument* is *powerful*, and the *type* *identities* are *restricted* to *some* *cases* (water-H₂O, temperature-kinetic energy).
- **Mechanism is the dominant model of explanation in the special sciences.** The *mechanist* *account* *captures* the *practice* of *biology*, *neuroscience*, and *cognitive* *science*.
- **Strong emergence is unmotivated.** The *weak* *emergence* is *defensible*; the *strong* *emergence* *posits* *new* *laws* *ad* *hoc*.
- **Supervenience, realization, and grounding are distinct relations.** None is *reducible* to the others, and the *choice* *depends* on the *case*.
- **The exclusion argument is serious.** The *argument* *threatens* *non-reductive* *physicalism*, and the *responses* are *contested*.
- **The levels are perspectival.** The *levels* are *real* *patterns*, and the *classification* is *interest-relative*.
- **The pluralism is the most defensible position.** The *relations* among the *levels* are *plural*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *case*.

### 12.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *grounding*.** Is it a *real* *relation*, or a *notational* *variant* of *explanation*?
- **The *fundamentality* of *physics*.** Is there a *fundamental* *level*, and is it *physical*?
- **The *status* of *strong* *emergence*.** Is the *view* *unmotivated*, or is it *required* by *consciousness*?
- **The *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences*.** Is it *epistemic* or *metaphysical*?
- **The *levels* *problem*.** How *many* *levels* are there, and what *individuates* them?
- **The *kinds* *problem*.** Are the *kinds* of the *special* *sciences* *real*, and are they *plural*?

### 12.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a *case* *study* in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between the *four* *senses* of *reduction*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *multiple* *realizability* *cases*, the *exclusion* *cases* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *supervenience* *formalism*, the *grounding* *relation*, the *levels* *hierarchy* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *reduction* is a *field* in *transition*. The *classical* *models* are *dead*; the *contemporary* *accounts* are *promising* but *incomplete*; the *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled answer*, but a *sharper understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 12.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reductive physicalism** | Everything is reducible to the physical | Simple; unified | Multiple realizability |
| **Nagel reduction** | Reduction is derivation | Precise | Meaning change |
| **Identity theory** | The mental is the physical | Occam's razor | Explanatory gap |
| **Functionalization** | The mental is a functional property | Multiple realizability | Property dualism |
| **Mechanism** | Reduction is mechanism description | Fits the special sciences | Levels problem |
| **Weak emergence** | Higher levels are unpredictable from lower | Fits the practice | Not ontological |
| **Strong emergence** | Higher levels are irreducible | Preserves autonomy | Unmotivated |
| **Supervenience** | Higher covaries with lower | Captures dependence | Explanatory impotence |
| **Grounding** | Higher is grounded in lower | Explanatory direction | The nature of grounding |
| **Non-reductive physicalism** | Higher supervenes on lower; autonomous | Fits the special sciences | Causal exclusion |
| **Kind pluralism** | Many real kinds | Fits the special sciences | Objectivity |
| **Fundamentalism** | There is a fundamental level | Fits physics | The demarcation problem |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *interventionist* account of levels.** If *causation* is *intervention* (Ch. 18, §3), the *levels* can be *defined* by the *interventions* that *manipulate* the *variables* at *each* level. The *account* *avoids* the *levels* *problem* by *relativizing* the *levels* to the *interventions*, and it *connects* the *theory* of *causation* to the *theory* of *reduction*. The *account* is *promising* and *underdeveloped*.

**2. The *grounding* *regress* and the *criterion* *problem*.** The *regress* of *grounds* (this *chapter*) is *structurally* *parallel* to the *regress* of *justification* (Ch. 8). The *responses* (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism) *mirror* the *epistemic* *responses*, and the *problems* (the *given*, the *isolation*, the *finite* *capacity*) *mirror* the *epistemic* *problems*. The *parallel* is *instructive*, and it is *underdeveloped*.

**3. The *emergence* and *consciousness*.** The *strong* *emergence* is *one* of the *options* for *consciousness* (Ch. 24, §5): the *phenomenal* *properties* *emerge* from the *physical* and are *not* *reducible*. The *view* *faces* the *exclusion* *argument*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *levels*.** The *Buddhist* *Abhidharma* *tradition* *developed* a *hierarchy* of *dharmas* (the *irreducible* *elements* of *experience*) that *anticipates* the *levels* *debate*, and the *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* *tradition* *developed* a *category* *theory* (the *padārthas*) that *anticipates* the *natural* *kinds* *debate*. The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *levels* and *artificial* *intelligence*.** The *hierarchical* *structure* of *AI* *systems* (layers in a *neural* *network*, levels of *abstraction* in a *cognitive* *architecture*) *mirrors* the *levels* *debate*: the *higher* *layers* are *emergent* in the *weak* *sense*, and the *explanation* of the *system's* *behavior* *requires* the *higher* *levels*. The *analogy* is *instructive*, and it is *beginning* to be *explored* (Ch. 29, §3).

## Summary

- **The debate about reduction and emergence is distorted by a false dichotomy.** The *dichotomy* (reducible or emergent) is *replaced* by a *spectrum* of relations: identity, deduction, functionalization, mechanism, supervenience, realization, grounding, emergence.
- **The logical empiricist model** (reduction as *derivation*) is *dead*: the *bridge* *principles* are *substantive*, the *meaning* *change* is *real*, and the *derivation* *fails* for *many* *cases*.
- **The identity theory** is *untenable* for the *special* *sciences* (the *multiple* *realizability* *argument*), and the *token* *identity* *response* *saves* *physicalism* without *type* *reduction*.
- **Functionalization** *reconciles* the *identity* theory with *multiple* *realizability*, but it *turns* into *property* *dualism*.
- **Mechanism** is the *dominant* model of *explanation* in the *special* *sciences*: the *reduction* is the *description* of the *mechanism*, and the *account* *avoids* the *problems* of the *logical* model.
- **Emergence**: the *weak* *version* is *defensible* (the *higher* levels are *unpredictable* from the *lower* *without* *simulation*); the *strong* *version* is *unmotivated* (it *posits* *new* *laws* *ad* *hoc*).
- **Supervenience**, **realization**, and **grounding** are *distinct* relations, and *none* is *reducible* to the others. The *choice* *depends* on the *case*.
- **The causal exclusion argument** *threatens* *non-reductive* *physicalism*. The *responses* (reductionism, emergentism, interventionism, determinables, grounding) are *all* *contested*.
- **The levels problem** is *unresolved*: the *levels* are *real* *patterns*, and the *classification* is *interest-relative*.
- **Reduction in practice** is *partial*, *multi-relational*, and *approximate*. The *higher* levels *remain* *explanatorily* *indispensable*.
- **Fundamentality** is *defined* *negatively* (not *composed*, *grounded*, etc.), and the *regress* of *grounds* *raises* the *same* *problems* as the *regress* of *justification*.
- **The kinds** of the *special* *sciences* are *real* but *plural*, and the *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible* *position*.
