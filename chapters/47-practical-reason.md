---

Chapter 44 examined the *nature* of the moral judgment; Chapter 45 examined its *content*. This chapter examines its *efficacy* — the *machinery* by which a *judgment* becomes a *motive*, a *character*, an *action*, and an *attribution of responsibility*.

The *distinction* between the *content* and the *efficacy* is *real*, and the *chapter* is *organized* around it. The *normative* *theories* of Ch. 45 *presuppose* a *psychological* *picture*: the *deontology* *presupposes* the *agent* *capable* of *acting* *from* *duty* (against *inclination*); the *consequentialism* *presupposes* the *agent* *capable* of *impersonal* *calculation* (against the *partiality* of the *emotion*); the *virtue* *ethics* *presupposes* the *agent* *capable* of *habituation* (the *stable* *disposition*). Each *presupposition* is *empirical*, and each is *contested* — and the *contests* are the *subject* of this chapter.

The *chapter* proceeds in *nine* *movements*. §1 examines the *Humean* *theory* of *motivation* and the *belief–desire* *psychology*. §2 examines *internalism*, *externalism*, and the *amoralist*. §3 examines the *reasons* for *action*. §4 examines the *moral* *luck* and the *architecture* of *responsibility*. §5 examines the *moral* *emotions*, *empathy*, and *development*. §6 examines the *willpower*, *self-control*, and *akrasia*. §7 examines the *implicit* *bias* and the *moral* *ignorance*. §8 examines the *moral* *testimony* and the *moral* *understanding*. §9 gives the *verdict*.

## 1. The Humean Theory of Motivation

### 1.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Hume, *Treatise*, II.iii.3; Smith, “The Humean Theory of Motivation,” 1987; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *beliefs* and the *desires* are *distinct* *existences* (the *no*-*reduction* *thesis*), and the *beliefs* *alone* *cannot* *motivate* the *action* (the *inertness* *thesis*), and the *motivation* *requires* a *desire* (the *conative* *thesis*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Motivating reason} \ = \ \langle \text{belief (the *means*)}, \ \text{desire (the *end*)} \rangle
$$

**The *components***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *contested* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *distinctness*** | The *belief* ≠ the *desire* | The *besire* (§1.3) |
| **The *inertness*** | The *belief* *alone* *cannot* *motivate* | The *motivational* *cognitivism* (§1.3) |
| **The *conative* *necessity*** | The *motivation* *requires* the *desire* | The *pure* *cognitivism* (§1.4) |

**The *significance*.** The *thesis* is the *most* *influential* *account* of the *practical* *reason* (the *Humean* *tradition*: the *instrumental* *conception*; §3.4), and it *constrains* the *metaethics* (the *expressivism* *is* *built* on it; Ch. 44, §3.2), and it *constrains* the *normative* *ethics* (the *deontology* *must* *explain* how the *duty* can *motivate* *against* the *desire*; §2).

### 1.2 Direction of fit

**The *distinction*** (Anscombe, *Intention*, 1957, §32 — the *shopping* *list* and the *detective*; Searle, *Intentionality*, 1983, Ch. 1; Platts, “Moral Reality,” 1979, §1).

| The *state* | The *direction* | The *condition* of *success* |
|---|---|---|
| **Belief** | The *mind*-to-*world* | The *mind* *fits* the *world* (the *truth*) |
| **Desire** | The *world*-to-*mind* | The *world* *fits* the *mind* (the *satisfaction*) |

**The *illustration*** (Anscombe 1957, §32). The *shopper* *writes* the *list* (the *shopping* *list*: the *world* *must* *fit* the *list*) and the *detective* *writes* the *list* (the *record*: the *list* *must* *fit* the *world*). The *error* *locates* *differently*: the *shopper*'s *error* is the *world*'s (the *wrong* *items* *bought*); the *detective*'s *error* is the *list*'s (the *wrong* *items* *recorded*).

**The *argument*** (Smith 1987, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Having a *motivating* *reason* is, inter alia, *having* a *goal* (the *purposive* *state*).} \\
2. & \text{Having a *goal* is being in a *state* with which the *world* *must* *fit* (the *world*-to-*mind* *direction*).} \\
3. & \text{Being in such a *state* is *desiring* that } p \ (\text{the *conative* *state}). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *motivating* *reason* *involves* the *desire* (the *Humean* *thesis*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *grounds* the *inertness* *thesis* in the *direction* of *fit* (the *belief* *represents*, and the *representation* *alone* *does* not *direct* the *action*), and it *explains* the *asymmetry* of the *error* (the *belief* *errs* by *misfitting* the *world*; the *desire* *errs* by *the* *world's* *not* *fitting* the *mind*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *deliberation* *moves* from the *end* to the *means*).

**The *problem***. The *direction* of *fit* *dichotomy* *is* *too* *coarse* (the *mixed* *states*: the *hope*, the *fear*, the *intention*, the *trust*, the *shame*; the *linguistic* *illocutionary* *acts*: the *promise* *creates* the *obligation*; the *permission*; the *exercise* of *authority*), and the *dichotomy* *does* not *settle* the *question* (the *belief* can be *world*-to-*mind* *and* *motivate*; the *desire* can be *mind*-to-*world* *and* *represent*).

**The *significance***. The *argument* is the *engine* of the *Humean* *theory*, and the *critique* (the *mixed* *states*, the *direction* of *fit* *pluralism*) is the *contemporary* *frontier*.

### 1.3 The besire

**The *thesis*** (Altham, “The Legacy of Emotivism,” 1986, §3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *besires* are the *states* that are *both* *beliefs* and *desires* (the *moral* *judgments* are the *paradigm*: the *judgment* that *cruelty* *is* *wrong* *represents* the *fact* *and* *disposes* the *agent* to *avoid* the *cruelty*), and the *existence* *refutes* the *Humean* *distinctness* *thesis*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral judgment} \ = \ \text{a state with both the *belief* content and the *desire* direction of fit}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *judgment* *represents* the *fact* (the *belief*-*like* *content*).} \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *judgment* *disposes* the *agent* to the *action* (the *desire*-*like* *direction*).} \\
3. & \text{The *judgment* is *indivisible* (the *representation* *and* the *disposition* *are* *one* *state*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *besire* *exists* (the *refutation* of the *Humean* *distinctness*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (Smith 1987, §4; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *besire* is *incoherent* (a *state* *cannot* have *both* *directions* of *fit*: the *conflict* *between* the *fact*-*tracking* and the *satisfaction*-*tracking* *is* the *mark* of the *distinct* *states*), and the *apparent* *unity* *is* the *co-occurrence* of the *belief* and the *desire* (the *causal* *connection*, not the *identity*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinctness* in the *conflict*.

**The *reply* *is* *contested*** (the *contemporary* *literature*: the *besire* *defenders* *argue* that the *direction* of *fit* *is* *not* *exclusive*, and the *propositional* *attitudes* *can* be *mixed*; the *debate* *is* *active*).

### 1.4 The pure cognitivism

**The *thesis*** (Nagel, *The Possibility of Altruism*, 1970, Ch. 5–6; Dancy, *Practical Reality*, 2000, Ch. 1–3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *motivating* *reasons* are the *states* of *affairs* (not the *mental* *states*: the *reason* for the *action* is the *fact* that *the* *child* *is* *drowning*, not the *belief* *about* it), and the *belief* *in* the *normative* *fact* *can* *motivate* *without* the *desire* (the *pure* *cognitivism*), and the *Humean* *theory* *misdescribes* the *deliberation* (the *agent* *acts* *for* the *reason*, not *for* the *desire*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Motivating reason} \ = \ \text{the *fact*} \ (\text{the state of affairs}) \ \text{that} \ \text{counts in favor of the action}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* *acts* *for* a *reason* (the *reason* *explains* the *action*).} \\
2. & \text{The *reason* *is* the *fact* (the *child* *is* *drowning*), not the *belief* or the *desire* (the *externalism* of the *reasons*).} \\
3. & \text{The *belief* *in* the *normative* *fact* *can* *motivate* (the *fact* *presents* the *action* *as* *called* *for*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *belief* *can* *motivate* (the *pure* *cognitivism*), and the *Humean* *theory* *fails* (the *no*-*desire* *thesis*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *phenomenology* of *the deliberation* (the *agent* *responds* to the *facts*, not to the *desires*), and it *fits* the *moral* *experience* (the *duty* *presents* the *action* *as* *required*, not as *desired*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *motivation* in the *value* (not the *inclination*).

**The *objections*** (the *Humean* *reply*; Smith 1987, §5; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**(i) The *‘*no* *goal*’* *objection***. The *fact* *presents* the *action* *as* *called* *for* *only* *relative* to a *desire* (the *agent* *who* *cares* *about* the *child* *responds* to the *fact*; the *agent* *who* *does* not *does* not), and the *motivation* *requires* the *conative* *background*, and the *reply* *reinstates* the *Humean* *theory*.

**The *reply*** (Dancy 2000, Ch. 3; Nagel 1970, Ch. 6). The *normative* *fact* *presents* the *action* *as* *called* *for* *to* the *rational* *agent* (the *rational* *agent* *responds* to the *reasons* *as* *such*, not *to* the *desires*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *motivation* in the *rationality* *itself*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *rational* *agent* *is* *an* *idealization*; the *ordinary* *agent* *may* *require* the *desire*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection* (the *causal* *theory* of *action*; Davidson, “Actions, Reasons, and Causes,” 1963; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reason* *explains* the *action* *only* *if* it *is* the *cause*, and the *cause* *is* the *mental* *state* (the *belief*-*desire* *pair*), and the *fact* *alone* *cannot* *cause* (the *facts* *are* *not* *in* the *causal* *order*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *Humean* *theory* in the *causation*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *disjunctivist* *theory* of *reasons*; Dancy 2000, Ch. 6). The *fact* *is* *the* *reason*, and the *belief* *in* the *fact* *is* the *cause* (the *reason* *and* the *cause* *are* *distinct*; the *reason* *explains* the *action* *by* *normative* *relation*, not *by* *causation*), and the *reply* *separates* the *motivating* *reason* from the *causal* *explanation*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *normative* *explanation* *without* the *causation* *is* *obscure*).

### 1.5 The empirical evidence

**The *themes***.

**(i) The *wanting*/*liking* *distinction*** (Berridge & Robinson, “Parsing Reward,” 2003; the *neuroscience*). The *wanting* (the *incentive* *salience*; the *dopaminergic* *system*) and the *liking* (the *hedonic* *pleasure*; the *opioid* *system*) are *dissociable*, and the *dissociation* *shows* that the *desire* *is* *not* a *single* *natural* *kind* (the *conative* *state* *is* *plural*).

**(ii) The *motivated* *reasoning*** (Kunda, “The Case for Motivated Reasoning,” 1990; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reasoning* *is* *shaped* by the *desire* (the *conclusion* *the* *agent* *wants*), and the *shaping* *supports* the *Humean* *insight* (the *desire* *influences* the *belief*), and it *complicates* the *pure* *cognitivism*.

**(iii) The *confabulation*** (Nisbett & Wilson, “Telling More Than We Can Know,” 1977; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *agents* *report* the *reasons* that *do* not *cause* the *action* (the *left*-*hemisphere* *interpreter*; Gazzaniga), and the *reports* *undermine* the *transparency* of the *deliberation*, and they *support* the *two*-*track* *picture* (§2.4).

**The *significance*.** The *evidence* *does* not *settle* the *philosophical* *debate* (the *wanting*/*liking* *distinction* *is* *compatible* with the *Humean* *theory* *and* with the *cognitivism*), and it *shows* that the *desire* and the *belief* are *natural* *kinds* *with* *fuzzy* *boundaries*, and the *empirical* *turn* *is* *methodologically* *central* (Ch. 3, §6).

### 1.6 The state of the Humean theory

**The *assessment***. The *Humean* *theory* is *defensible* in the *direction*-of-*fit* *form*, and the *besire* and the *pure* *cognitivism* are *serious* *challenges*, and the *debate* is *unresolved*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Humean** | The *direction* of *fit*; the *explanation* | The *besire*; the *deliberation* |
| **Besire** | The *unity* of the *judgment* | The *coherence* |
| **Pure cognitivism** | The *facts* as *reasons* | The *no* *goal*; the *causation* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Humean* *theory*: the *beliefs* and the *desires* are *distinct* (the *Humean* *insight*), the *motivation* *requires* the *conative* *background* (the *direction* of *fit* *insight*), and the *besires* are *not* *distinct* *states* but the *co*-*occurrence* of the *belief* and the *desire* (the *reply*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *pure* *cognitivism* *remains* *defensible* and the *empirical* *evidence* *is* *compatible* with both.

## 2. Internalism, Externalism, and the Amoralist

### 2.1 The thesis

**The *debate***. The *motivational* *judgment* *internalism* (MJI): *necessarily*, if an *agent* *judges* that *she* *ought* to $\phi$, then *she* is *at least* *somewhat* *motivated* to $\phi$ (the *necessary* *connection*). The *externalism*: the *connection* is *contingent* (the *empirical* *regularity*, not the *conceptual* *necessity*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{MJI}: \ \Box \big( J_{\text{ought}}(\phi) \ \rightarrow \ M(\phi) \big) \qquad \text{Externalism}: \ \Diamond \big( J_{\text{ought}}(\phi) \ \wedge \ \neg M(\phi) \big)
$$

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong internalism** | The *judgment* *necessarily* *motivates* *all* *agents* | the *Kantian* *tradition* |
| **Weak internalism** | The *judgment* *motivates* the *rational* *agents* (the *restricted* form) | Smith 1994 |
| **Conditional internalism** | The *judgment* *motivates* *unless* the *agent* is *practically* *irrational* | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Externalism** | The *connection* is *empirical* (the *reliable* *correlation*) | Brink 1986 |

**The *significance***. The *debate* *connects* the *metaethics* (the *cognitivism*, the *expressivism*; Ch. 44, §3.2) to the *normative* *ethics* (the *deontology* *requires* the *duty* to *motivate*; Ch. 45, §2) and to the *moral* *psychology* (the *amoralist*, the *psychopath*; §2.3).

### 2.2 The argument for internalism

**The *argument*** (Smith, *The Moral Problem*, 1994, Ch. 3; the *practicality* *requirement*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *judgments* are *practical* (they *tell* the *agent* what to *do*; the *action*-*guiding* *nature*).} \\
2. & \text{The *practical* *judgments* *would* be *intolerably* *queer* if they *did* not *motivate* (the *sincerity* *condition*: the *judgment* *without* the *motivation* *is* *insincere*).} \\
3. & \text{The *sincerity* *condition* *requires* the *internal* *connection* (the *judgment* *motivates* *by* *its* *nature*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *MJI* *is* *true* (the *internalism* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *evidence***. The *first*-*person* *experience* (the *judgment* *that* the *cruelty* *is* *wrong* *is* *felt* as a *demand*, not as a *mere* *description*), the *linguistic* *practice* (the *“*you* *say* it is *wrong* but *you* *do* it”* *is a *rebuke*, not a *description*), the *moral* *education* (the *teaching* *of* the *norms* *aims* at the *motivation*).

**The *significance*.** The *argument* *grounds* the *internalism* in the *practicality* of the *moral* *judgment*, and the *argument* is *the* *most* *influential* *case* for the *internalism*.

### 2.3 The amoralist and the psychopath

**The *amoralist*** (Brink, “Externalist Moral Realism,” 1986; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *agent* *who* *accepts* the *moral* *judgment* *sincerely* (the *slavery* *is* *wrong*) and *remains* *unmoved* (the *no* *motivation*), and the *agent* *is* *not* *irrational* (the *judgment* *and* the *action* *are* *consistent*, *given* the *no* *desire*), and the *possibility* *refutes* the *strong* *internalism*.

**The *argument*** (the *externalist* *reconstruction*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *amoralist* *is* *conceivable* (the *judgment* *without* the *motivation*).} \\
2. & \text{The *amoralist* *is* *actual* (the *psychopath*, the *sociopath*).} \\
3. & \text{The *MJI* *denies* the *possibility* (the *necessary* *connection*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *MJI* *is* *false* (the *externalism* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *evidence***.

**(i) The *psychopath*** (Blair, “A Cognitive Developmental Approach to Morality,” 1995; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *psychopath* *knows* the *moral* *rules* (the *verbal* *competence*) and *fails* to *feel* the *moral* *emotion* (the *remorse*, the *guilt*, the *empathy*), and the *dissociation* *shows* that the *judgment* and the *motivation* *are* *separable* (the *two*-*track* *picture*), and it *supports* the *externalism* (the *empirical* *insight*).

**(ii) The *moral*/*conventional* *distinction*** (Turiel, *The Development of Social Knowledge*, 1983). The *children* *distinguish* the *moral* *norms* (the *harm*, the *justice*) from the *conventional* *norms* (the *dress*, the *etiquette*), and the *psychopaths* *do* not (the *psychopath* *treats* the *moral* *rules* as *conventional*), and the *deficit* *supports* the *view* that the *moral* *knowledge* *is* *tied* to the *affect* (the *VIM*: the *violence* *inhibition* *mechanism*; Blair).

**(iii) The *acquired* *sociopathy*** (Damasio, *Descartes' Error*, 1994; the *Phineas* *Gage* *case*; the *vmPFC* *patients*). The *patients* *know* the *norms* and *fail* to *act* on them (the *no* *motivation*), and the *dissociation* *supports* the *externalism* (the *empirical* *insight*).

**The *internalist* *replies***.

**(i) The *restricted* *internalism*** (Smith 1994, Ch. 3; the *practicality* *requirement*). The *MJI* *applies* only to the *rational* *agents* (the *psychopath* is *not* *fully* *rational*; the *amoralist* is *not* a *genuine* *agent*), and the *reply* *restricts* the *thesis* and *saves* the *internalism*.

**The *objection***. The *restriction* is *ad hoc* (the *psychopath* *is* *instrumentally* *rational*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *conceptual* *reply***. The *psychopath* *uses* the *moral* *vocabulary* *without* the *moral* *concepts* (the *deference*, the *parroting*), and the *judgment* *is* *not* *genuine*, and the *reply* *saves* the *internalism*.

**The *objection***. The *conceptual* *reply* *begs* the *question* (the *diagnosis* *of* the *psychopath*'s *concepts* *is* *precisely* the *issue*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *two*-*track* *reply***. The *internalism* *is* *normative* (the *rational* *agent* *ought* to be *motivated*), and the *psychopath* *is* *defective* (the *motivational* *failure*), and the *reply* *separates* the *normative* from the *empirical*, and it *saves* the *internalism*.

**The *objection***. The *separation* *abandons* the *necessary* *connection* (the *normative* *internalism* *is* *compatible* with the *empirical* *externalism*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The two-track picture

**The *thesis*** (the *empirical* *insight*). The *judgment* and the *motivation* are *two* *tracks* (the *cognitive* and the *affective*), and they *normally* *run* *together* (the *development*, the *socialization*), and they *can* *come* *apart* (the *psychopath*, the *acquired* *sociopathy*, the *accidie*, the *depression*), and the *dissociation* *is* the *empirical* *fact*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Moral judgment} \ \xrightarrow{\text{the normal development}} \ \text{Moral motivation} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{the dissociation (the psychopath, the accidie)}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *picture* *reconciles* the *internalism* (the *normal* *connection*) with the *externalism* (the *possible* *dissociation*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *education* in the *two* *tracks* (the *cognitive* *teaching* + the *affective* *cultivation*), and it *connects* the *moral* *psychology* to the *virtue* *ethics* (the *character*; Ch. 45, §3).

**The *objection***. The *picture* is *empirically* *contested* (the *psychopath* *may* *lack* the *moral* *concepts*, not the *motivation*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.5 The state of the internalism debate

**The *assessment***. The *internalism* is *defensible* in the *weak* and the *conditional* forms, and the *externalism* is *defensible* in the *empirical* form, and the *two*-*track* *picture* is the *most* *promising* *reconciliation*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong internalism** | The *practicality* | The *amoralist*; the *psychopath* |
| **Weak internalism** | The *rational* *agent* | The *ad hoc* |
| **Externalism** | The *empirical* *dissociation* | The *practicality* *lost* |
| **Two-track** | The *reconciliation* | The *conceptual* *question* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *weak* *internalism* *combined* with the *two*-*track* *picture*: the *moral* *judgment* *normally* *motivates* the *rational* *agent* (the *internal* *insight*), and the *connection* *is* *real* but *defeasible* (the *psychopath*, the *accidie*), and the *defeasibility* *grounds* the *externalism* *insight*, *qualified* by the *admission* that the *conceptual* *question* (the *genuine* *judgment*) *remains* *contested*.

## 3. Reasons for Action

### 3.1 Motivating and normative reasons

**The *distinction*** (Smith 1994, Ch. 4; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| The *reason* | The *content* | The *question* |
|---|---|---|
| **Motivating** | The *reason* *why* the *agent* *acted* (the *explanation*) | “Why did you do it?” |
| **Normative** | The *reason* *that* *counts* in *favor* of the *action* (the *justification*) | “What is the reason to do it?” |

**The *significance*.** The *distinction* *separates* the *explanation* from the *justification*, and the *separation* *is* *central* (the *good* *agent* *acts* for the *normative* *reason*, and the *normative* *reason* *becomes* the *motivating* *reason*; the *virtuous* *agent*; Ch. 45, §3.2).

### 3.2 Internal reasons and external reasons

**The *thesis*** (Williams, “Internal and External Reasons,” 1981, in *Moral Luck*, Ch. 8). A *reason* for an *agent* to $\phi$ *must* be *grounded* in the *agent*'s *subjective* *motivational* *set* (the *S*: the *desires*, the *dispositions*, the *projects*, the *commitments*), and the *external* *reasons* (the *reasons* *independent* of the *S*) are *obscure* (the *“*bluff”* *objection*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Reason for } A \text{ to } \phi \ \iff \ \text{the *sound* *deliberative* *route* from } S_A \text{ *yields* the *motivation* to } \phi
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *reason* must be *able* to *explain* the *action* (the *explanatory* *constraint*).} \\
2. & \text{The *explanation* *requires* the *motivation* (the *action* *is* *explained* *by* the *motive*).} \\
3. & \text{The *motivation* *requires* the *S* (the *agent*'s *existing* *desires*, *dispositions*, *projects*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *reason* *must* be *internal* (the *Williams* *insight*), and the *external* *reasons* are *obscure* (the *bluff*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *reasons* in the *agent*'s *psychology* (the *naturalism*), and it *explains* the *motivational* *failure* (the *agent* *who* *does* *not* *share* the *S* *cannot* be *moved* *by* the *reason*), and it *fits* the *deliberation* (the *agent* *reasons* from the *S*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*obligation*’* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *obligation* *binds* *regardless* of the *S* (the *agent* *who* *does* *not* *care* *about* the *cruelty* *is* *still* *required* *not* to be *cruel*), and the *internal* *reasons* *cannot* *ground* the *obligation*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Williams 1981, §3; McDowell, “Might There Be External Reasons?” 1995). The *obligation* *is* *grounded* in the *S* *of* the *moral* *agent* (the *virtuous* *person*'s *S* *includes* the *concern* for the *others*; the *second* *nature*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *obligation* in the *virtuous* *S*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *virtuous* *S* *is* *not* *universal*; the *amoralist* *escapes*).

**(ii) The *‘*categorical*’* *objection*** (Korsgaard, “Skepticism about Practical Reason,” 1986; Enoch, *Taking Morality Seriously*, 2011, Ch. 4). The *moral* *reasons* *are* *categorical* (they *bind* *all* *agents*), and the *internal* *reasons* *are* *hypothetical* (they *bind* *only* the *agents* with the *S*), and the *categorical* *reasons* *require* the *externalism*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Korsgaard 1986). The *categorical* *reasons* *are* *grounded* in the *constitutive* *norms* of *agency* (the *reflective* *structure*; Ch. 44, §4.2), and the *reply* *grounds* the *categorical* in the *agency*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *empty* *formalism*; Ch. 44, §4.2).

**(iii) The *‘*reasoning* *from* *the outside*’* *objection*** (Enoch 2011, Ch. 4). The *agent* *can* *reason* *from* the *outside* (the *agent* *asks* “*why* be *moral*?”* and *the* *answer* *is* *the* *moral* *fact*), and the *reasoning* *does* not *presuppose* the *S*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *reasoning* *from* *the* *outside* *presupposes* the *minimal* *S* (the *concern* for the *truth*, the *coherence*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *minimal* *S* in the *agency*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.3 The instrumentalism and its critique

**The *thesis*** (the *Humean* *tradition*; the *instrumental* *conception*). The *only* *rational* *requirement* is the *instrumental* one (the *take* the *means* to the *ends*), and the *ends* *themselves* are *not* *rationally* *criticizable* (the *no* *substantive* *rationality*), and the *rationality* *is* *the* *efficiency*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Rational requirement}: \ \text{If } A \text{ intends } E \text{ and believes } M \text{ is necessary for } E, \ \text{then } A \text{ ought to intend } M
$$

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*bootstrapping*’* *objection*** (Bratman, “Intention and Means-End Reasoning,” 1981). The *instrumental* *principle* *validates* the *arbitrary* *ends* (the *agent* *intends* the *end* and the *principle* *requires* the *means*, and the *end* *is* *not* *justified*), and the *rationality* *is* *the* *slave* of the *arbitrary* *desire*.

**The *reply*** (Bratman 1987, Ch. 2). The *intention* *is* the *commitment* (the *planning* *agent* *must* be *consistent*), and the *consistency* *grounds* the *instrumental* *requirement*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *principle* in the *agency*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *arbitrary* *ends* *remain*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *substance*’* *objection*** (Parfit, *On What Matters*, 2011, Vol. I, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *substantive* *rationality* *exists* (the *agent* *can* be *irrational* *in* the *ends*: the *future* *discounting*, the *sour* *grapes*, the *self*-*harm*), and the *instrumental* *conception* *cannot* *criticize* the *ends*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Parfit 2011, Vol. I, Ch. 1). The *substantive* *rationality* *is* *grounded* in the *value* (the *reason* *is* *the* *fact*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *reason* in the *normative* *realism* (Ch. 44, §2). The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *will*’* *objection*** (Kavka, “The Toxin Puzzle,” 1983). The *agent* *cannot* *form* the *intention* *at* *will* (the *intention* *is* *the* *conclusion* of the *deliberation*, not the *object* of the *choice*), and the *instrumental* *reason* *cannot* *produce* the *intention*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intention* *is* *formed* *by* the *indirect* *strategy* (the *commitment*, the *self*-*manipulation*; the *two*-*level* *view*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *intention* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.4 The exclusionary reasons

**The *thesis*** (Raz, *Practical Reason and Norms*, 1975, Ch. 1–2). The *reasons* *come* in *two* *kinds* (the *first*-*order*: the *reasons* *to* *act*; the *second*-*order*: the *reasons* *to* *act* *for* the *reasons*, the *exclusionary* *reasons*), and the *exclusionary* *reasons* *exclude* the *first*-*order* *reasons* (the *promise*, the *authority*, the *law*), and the *structure* *explains* the *practical* *reasoning* (the *agent* *does* not *weigh* *all* the *reasons*; the *agent* *excludes*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Exclusionary reason} \ e \ \text{excludes} \ r \ \text{as a reason to } \phi \ (\text{the *second*-*order* role})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *practical* *reasoning* *excludes* the *reasons* (the *promise* *excludes* the *countervailing* *inclinations*).} \\
2. & \text{The *exclusion* *is* *not* the *weighing* (the *balance* *of* *strength*), but the *exclusion* (the *second*-*order* *role*).} \\
3. & \text{The *structure* *explains* the *authority* (the *law*, the *promise*, the *command*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *exclusionary* *reasons* are *real* (the *Raz* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *explains* the *authority* (the *second*-*order* *role*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *commitment*, the *pre*-*commitment*), and it *connects* the *practical* *reason* to the *political* *authority* (Ch. 47, §1).

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *exclusionary* *reasons* *collapse* into the *weighing* (the *exclusion* *is* *the* *weight* of the *second*-*order* *reason*, and the *structure* *adds* nothing), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Raz 1975, Ch. 2). The *exclusion* *is* *not* the *weight* (the *excluded* *reason* *does* not *count*, *not* even *negatively*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *exclusion* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 3.5 The state of the reasons debate

**The *assessment***. The *internal* *reasons* are *defensible* in the *broad* *form* (the *sound* *deliberative* *route*), and the *external* *reasons* are *defensible* in the *moral* *form* (the *categorical* *demand*), and the *instrumental* *conception* is *seriously* *contested*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal reasons** | The *naturalism*; the *explanation* | The *obligation*; the *categorical* |
| **External reasons** | The *categorical* | The *obscurity*; the *bluff* |
| **Instrumentalism** | The *simplicity* | The *bootstrapping*; the *no* *substance* |
| **Substantive rationality** | The *ends* criticized | The *measurement* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *internalism* *combined* with a *substantive* *rationality*: the *reasons* *are* *grounded* in the *S* (the *internal* *insight*), and the *S* *is* *not* *arbitrary* (the *substantive* *insight*: the *ends* *are* *rationally* *criticizable*), and the *critique* *of* the *S* *is* *grounded* in the *agency* (the *constitutive* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *categorical* *demand* *and* the *external* *reasons* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Moral Luck and the Architecture of Responsibility

### 4.1 The taxonomy and the control principle

**The *taxonomy*** (Williams 1976; Nagel 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 45, §6.3).

| The *kind* | The *content* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Resultant** | The *luck* in the *outcome* | The *drunk* *driver* *who* *hits* the *child* |
| **Circumstantial** | The *luck* in the *situation* | The *Nazi* *collaborator* vs. the *resister* |
| **Constitutive** | The *luck* in the *character* | The *temperament* (the *virtuous* by *nature*) |
| **Causal** | The *luck* in the *causes* of the *action* | The *upbringing* (the *determinism*) |

**The *control* *principle* (CP)**. If two *agents* *differ* *only* in the *factors* *beyond* *their* *control*, they *cannot* *differ* in the *moral* *responsibility* (the *no* *moral* *luck* *principle*; the *standard* *view*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{CP}: \ \big( A_1 \sim_{\text{control}} A_2 \big) \ \rightarrow \ \Big( R(A_1, \phi) \ \leftrightarrow \ R(A_2, \phi) \Big)
$$

**The *argument* (the *luck* *paradox***; Nagel 1979, §2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *responsibility* *requires* the *control*} \ (\text{the *CP*}). \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *judgment* *depends* on the *factors* *beyond* the *control* (the *outcome*, the *circumstance*, the *character*, the *causes*).} \\
3. & \text{The *two* *are* *incompatible* (the *paradox*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *responsibility* *collapses* (the *luck* *pessimism*), or the *CP* *is* *false* (the *luck* *realism*), or the *moral* *judgment* is *not* the *responsibility* *judgment* (the *dissolution*).}
\end{array}
$$

### 4.2 The responsibility as the attributability

**The *thesis*** (Strawson, “Freedom and Resentment,” 1962; Watson, “Two Faces of Responsibility,” 1996; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 27, §6). The *responsibility* *is* *two* *faces*: the *attributability* (the *action* *expresses* the *agent*'s *quality* of *will*; the *action* *is* *hers* *in* the *sense* *that* it *reveals* the *self*) and the *accountability* (the *action* *makes* the *agent* *liable* to the *reactive* *attitudes*, the *demands*, the *sanctions*).

**The *structure***.

| The *face* | The *content* | The *conditions* |
|---|---|---|
| **Attributability** | The *action* *expresses* the *quality* of *will* | The *epistemic* *condition* (the *agent* *knew*) |
| **Accountability** | The *action* *warrants* the *reactive* *attitudes* | The *control* *condition* (*or* the *quality* of *will*) |

**The *significance***. The *distinction* *dissolves* the *luck* *paradox* (the *resultant* *luck* *affects* the *accountability* — the *blame* — and *not* the *attributability* — the *agent*'s *quality* of *will*), and it *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *quality* of *will* (the *Strawsonian* *insight*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *excuse*: “*he* *did* not *mean* it”* *attaches* to the *attributability*, and the *justification*: “*it* *was* *necessary*”* *attaches* to the *accountability*).

**The *argument*** (Watson 1996, §1–3).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *responsibility* *has* *two* *faces* (the *attributability* and the *accountability*).} \\
2. & \text{The *luck* *affects* the *accountability* (the *resultant* *luck*: the *blame* *depends* on the *outcome*) and *not* the *attributability* (the *quality* of *will* *is* *the* *same*).} \\
3. & \text{The *two* *faces* *are* *distinct* (the *dissolution* of the *paradox*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *luck* is *real* (the *accountability*) and *not* *fatal* (the *attributability*), and the *responsibility* *survives* (the *Watson* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *dissolves* the *paradox* (the *two* *faces* *explain* the *intuitions*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *reactive* *attitudes*; the *excuses*), and it *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *quality* of *will*.

**The *objection***. The *account* *is* *revisionary* (the *ordinary* *responsibility* *is* the *accountability*, and the *attributability* *is* a *philosophical* *construct*), and the *reply* *departs* from the *practice*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.3 The epistemic condition and the tracing

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Zimmerman, “Moral Responsibility and Ignorance,” 1997; Rosen, “Culpability and Ignorance,” 2003; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *requires* the *epistemic* *condition* (the *agent* *knows*, or *should* *know*, the *relevant* *facts*), and the *ignorance* *excuses* *unless* it is *culpable*, and the *culpability* *is* *traced* to the *earlier* *acts* (the *benighting* *acts*: the *acts* *that* *produced* the *ignorance*).

**The *structure***.

$$
R(A, \phi) \ \text{via the ignorance} \ \iff \ \text{the *ignorance* is *traced* to the *earlier* *benighting* *act} \ \psi \ \text{for which} \ A \ \text{is responsible}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent* *who* *acts* *from* the *non*-*culpable* *ignorance* *is* *not* *responsible* (the *epistemic* *excuse*).} \\
2. & \text{The *agent* *who* *acted* *carelessly* *earlier* (the *benighting* *act*) *is* *responsible* *for* the *later* *ignorance* (the *tracing*).} \\
3. & \text{The *tracing* *preserves* the *responsibility* *without* the *occurrent* *knowledge* (the *trace* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *responsibility* *is* *grounded* in the *benighting* *acts* (the *tracing* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *preserves* the *responsibility* *for* the *ignorance* (the *negligence*, the *recklessness*), and it *explains* the *intuitions* (the *careless* *driver* *is* *responsible*), and it *fits* the *law* (the *criminal* *negligence*; Ch. 51).

**The *problems***.

**(i) The *tracing* *regress***. The *tracing* *requires* the *benighting* *act*, and the *benighting* *act* *requires* the *knowledge* (the *agent* *must* *know* that the *act* *might* *produce* the *ignorance*), and the *regress* *is* *vicious* (the *trace* *never* *grounds* the *responsibility*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *regress* *stops* at the *non*-*culpable* *ignorance* (the *agent* *is* *not* *responsible* *there*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *stop* in the *epistemic* *limit*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *epistemic* *responsibility*’* *objection***. The *agent* *cannot* be *responsible* *for* the *ignorance* (the *beliefs* are *not* *voluntary*; the *no* *doxastic* *voluntarism*; Ch. 27, §6), and the *tracing* *requires* the *voluntary* *act*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *for* the *ignorance* *is* *not* the *direct* *voluntary* *control* but the *omission*, the *carelessness*, the *character* (the *epistemic* *vice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 4.4 The moral ignorance and the affected ignorance

**The *thesis*** (Moody-Adams, “Culture, Responsibility, and Affected Ignorance,” 1994; Mills, “White Ignorance,” 2007; Medina, *The Epistemology of Resistance*, 2013; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *ignorance* *is* *often* *not* *innocent* (the *affective* *ignorance*: the *agent* *avoids* the *knowledge* *because* it *is* *uncomfortable*, *inconvenient*, or *threatening*), and the *ignorance* *is* *the* *accomplishment* (the *active* *ignorance*: the *structures* *produce* and *maintain* the *ignorance*), and the *responsibility* *extends* to the *ignorance* *that* the *agent* *sustains*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Affected ignorance} \ = \ \text{the *avoidance* of the knowledge that the *agent* *could* *have} \ (\text{the *motivated* *blindness*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *ignorance* *is* *often* *motivated* (the *agent* *benefits* from *not* *knowing*).} \\
2. & \text{The *motivated* *ignorance* *is* *not* *innocent* (the *agent* *is* *responsible* for the *avoidance*).} \\
3. & \text{The *structures* *produce* the *ignorance* (the *social* *epistemology*: the *hermeneutical* *injustice*; the *testimonial* *injustice*; Ch. 11, §4).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *responsibility* *extends* to the *ignorance* (the *affected* *ignorance* *insight*), and the *epistemic* *condition* *is* *social* (the *social* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *slavery* (the *slaveholders* *knew* and *did* not; the *affected* *ignorance*; the *contemporary* *literature*), the *climate* (the *citizens* *avoid* the *knowledge*; the *motivated* *ignorance*; Ch. 51, §6), the *whiteness* (the *white* *ignorance*; Mills 2007; the *structural* *ignorance*).

**The *significance***. The *thesis* *connects* the *moral* *psychology* to the *social* *epistemology* (Ch. 11) and to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §2), and it *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *social* *practice*, and it *challenges* the *individualist* *account* of the *ignorance*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *responsibility*’* *objection***. The *agent* *cannot* be *responsible* *for* the *structural* *ignorance* (the *structure* *is* *not* the *agent*), and the *thesis* *over*-*extends* the *responsibility*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Medina 2013, Ch. 3; Young, *Responsibility for Justice*, 2011, Ch. 2). The *responsibility* *is* *shared* (the *social* *connection* *model*), and the *agent* *is* *responsible* *for* the *contribution* (the *participation* in the *structure*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *participation*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *blame*’* *objection***. The *affected* *ignorance* *is* *not* *blameworthy* (the *agent* *is* *the* *product* of the *structure*), and the *thesis* *blames* the *victims* *of* the *structure*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *thesis* *distinguishes* the *responsibility* (the *forward*-*looking*: the *remediation*) from the *blame* (the *backward*-*looking*: the *sanction*), and the *forward*-*looking* *responsibility* *does* not *require* the *blame*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *remediation*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 4.5 The state of the responsibility debate

**The *assessment***. The *attributability*/*accountability* *distinction* is *defensible*, and the *tracing* is *contested*, and the *affected* *ignorance* is *the* *most* *promising* *account*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Control principle** | The *intuition* | The *moral* *luck* |
| **Attributability** | The *quality* of *will* | The *revisionary* |
| **Tracing** | The *responsibility* for the *ignorance* | The *regress* |
| **Affected ignorance** | The *social* *epistemic* | The *blame* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Strawsonian* *account*: the *responsibility* *is* *two* *faces* (the *attributability* *insight*), the *epistemic* *condition* *is* *traced* (the *tracing* *insight*), the *ignorance* *is* *often* *affected* (the *social* *insight*), and the *responsibility* *is* *forward*-*looking* (the *remediation* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *regress* and the *blame* *remain* *contested*.

## 5. The Moral Emotions, Empathy, and Development

### 5.1 The emotion as the value

**The *theories***.

| The *theory* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **James–Lange** | The *emotion* *is* the *bodily* *feeling* | James 1890; Lange |
| **Cognitive** | The *emotion* *is* the *judgment* *of* *value* | the *Stoics*; Nussbaum 2001 |
| **Perceptual** | The *emotion* *is* the *embodied* *appraisal* | Prinz 2004 |
| **Attitudinal** | The *emotion* *is* the *evaluative* *attitude* | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *thesis*** (Nussbaum, *Upheavals of Thought*, 2001, Ch. 1; the *Stoic* *tradition*). The *emotion* *is* a *judgment* *of* *value* (the *grief* *is* the *judgment* that the *loss* *matters*; the *anger* *is* the *judgment* that the *wrong* *was* *done*), and the *emotion* *is* *rational* (the *fittingness*: the *emotion* *is* *appropriate* *when* the *value* *is* *real*), and the *emotion* *is* *evaluable* (the *emotion* *can* be *justified*, *unjustified*, *inappropriate*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Emotion} \ E \ = \ \text{the *judgment* that} \ V \ \text{matters (the *value* *judgment*)} \ + \ \text{the *bodily* *arousal* (the *affect*)} + \ \text{the *action* *tendency*}
$$

**The *argument*** (Nussbaum 2001, Ch. 1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *emotions* *have* the *intentional* *objects* (the *grief* *is* *about* the *loss*; the *anger* *is* *about* the *wrong*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intentional* *objects* *are* *evaluated* (the *loss* *is* *seen* *as* *the* *loss*; the *wrong* *is* *seen* *as* the *wrong*).} \\
3. & \text{The *evaluation* *is* the *judgment* *of* *value* (the *propositional* *content*, the *appraisal*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *emotions* *are* *value* *judgments* (the *cognitive* *insight*), and they *can* be *rational* (the *rational* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *normative* *structure* of the *emotion* (the *fittingness*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *emotion* *can* be *criticized*, *educated*, *recalcitrant*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *perception* (the *emotion* *presents* the *value*; Ch. 44, §6.2).

**The *objection***. The *cognitive* *theory* *over*-*intellectualizes* the *emotion* (the *emotion* *is* *felt* *before* the *judgment*; the *infant*'s *fear*; the *animal*'s *fear*), and the *account* *confuses* the *emotion* with the *cognition* about it, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Prinz 2004, Ch. 1–2). The *emotion* *is* *the* *perception* of the *change* *in* the *body* (the *embodied* *appraisal*: the *perception* *represents* the *value* *without* the *explicit* *judgment*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *emotion* in the *perception*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 5.2 The fittingness of the emotion

**The *thesis*** (D’Arms & Jacobson, “The Moralistic Fallacy,” 2000; “The Significance of Recalcitrant Emotion,” 2003; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *emotions* *are* *evaluable* *by* two *standards* (the *fittingness*: the *emotion* *fits* the *value*; the *warrant*: the *evidence* *supports* the *emotion*), and the *two* *standards* *can* *diverge* (the *recalcitrant* *emotion*: the *fear* *of* the *harmless* *snake*, *known* *to* be *harmless*), and the *divergence* *shows* that the *emotion* *is* *not* *the* *belief* (it *can* *persist* *against* the *belief*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Fittingness} \ (\text{the *value* *fits* the *emotion}) \quad \neq \quad \text{Warrant} \ (\text{the *evidence* *supports* the *emotion})
$$

**The *significance***. The *distinction* *grounds* the *emotion* *critique* (the *emotion* *is* *unfitting*, *unreliable*, *misfocused*), and it *explains* the *recalcitrance* (the *phobia* *persists*), and it *connects* the *emotion* to the *rationality* (the *fittingness* *is* the *evaluative* *norm*; §5.1).

### 5.3 The empathy and its biases

**The *thesis*** (Batson, *The Altruism Question*, 1991; *Altruism in Humans*, 2011; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *empathy* (the *other*-*oriented* *emotional* *response*, the *feeling* *for* the *other*) *can* *motivate* the *altruistic* *action* (the *helping* *for* the *other*'s *sake*, not for the *self*'s), and the *empathy*-*altruism* *hypothesis* *challenges* the *egoism* (the *Humean* *tradition*; §1).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Empathy} \ \rightarrow \ \text{the *altruistic* *motivation*} \ (\text{the *ultimate* *goal*: the *welfare* of the *other*})
$$

**The *evidence***. The *Batson* *experiments*: the *high*-*empathy* *subjects* *help* *even* *when* the *helping* *does* *not* *relieve* *their* *own* *distress* (the *easy* *escape* *condition*), and the *helping* *persists* (the *altruistic* *insight*), and the *egoistic* *alternative* (the *Cialdini* *negative*-*state* *relief* *model*) *cannot* *explain* the *high*-*empathy* *helping* (the *reply* *is* *contested*).

**The *biases*** (Slovic, “If I Look at the Mass I Will Never Act,” 2007; Bloom, *Against Empathy*, 2016, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*).

| The *bias* | The *content* | The *consequence* |
|---|---|---|
| **Identifiable victim** | The *vivid* *individual* *moves* us; the *statistical* *mass* does not | The *donations* *skewed* to the *vivid* |
| **In-group** | The *empathy* *stronger* for the *own* *group* | The *parochialism* |
| **Salience** | The *emotional* *proximity* *matters* | The *moral* *myopia* |
| **Scope insensitivity** | The *empathy* *does* not *scale* with the *numbers* | The *psychic* *numbing* |

**The *argument* (the *empathy* *critique***; Bloom 2016, Ch. 2).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *empathy* *is* a *spotlight* (the *narrow*, *vivid*, *parochial* *illumination*).} \\
2. & \text{The *morality* *requires* the *impartial* *aggregation* (the *numbers*, the *distance*, the *strangers*).} \\
3. & \text{The *empathy* *misleads* the *moral* *judgment* (the *identifiable* *victim*, the *in-group*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *empathy* *is* *not* the *moral* *compass* (the *Bloom* *insight*), and the *reason* *must* *correct* the *empathy* (the *rationalist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply***. The *empathy* *is* *the* *spur*, not the *compass* (the *motivation* *plus* the *reason*), and the *empathy* *can* be *educated* (the *perspective*-*taking*, the *narrative*, the *exposure*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *empathy* in the *practice* (the *care* *ethics*; Noddings, *Caring*, 1984; Held, *The Ethics of Care*, 2006). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *empathy* *and* the *reason* *are* *complementary*).

**The *significance***. The *reply* is *defensible*.

## 6. Willpower, Self-Control, and Akrasia

### 6.1 The Socratic paradox

**The *thesis*** (Plato, *Protagoras* 358b–d). No one *goes* *willingly* *toward* the *bad* (the *akrasia* is *impossible*: the *agent* *who* *does* the *bad* *judges* *it* *good* *at* the *moment* of the *action*; the *ignorance*), and the *apparent* *incontinence* *is* *the* *ignorance* (the *mis*-*measurement* of the *pleasure*, the *hedonic* *calculus*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Akrasia} \ \text{is *impossible*}: \ \Box \neg \big( \text{the agent *knows* that } \phi \text{ is worse and *chooses* } \phi \text{ over } \psi \text{ (better)} \big)
$$

**The *significance*.** The *paradox* *defines* the *problem* (the *weakness* of *will*), and the *denial* is *counterintuitive* (the *akrasia* *is* *experienced*), and the *debate* *is* *the* *history* of the *reply*.

### 6.2 The Aristotelian reply

**The *thesis*** (Aristotle, *NE*, VII.3–4). The *akrasia* *is* *real*, and the *explanation* is the *practical* *syllogism* (the *universal* *premise*: the *dry* *food* *is* *good* *for* the *health*; the *particular* *premise*: the *sweet* *thing* *is* *dry*), and the *akratic* *has* the *knowledge* but *does* not *use* it (the *particular* *premise* *obscured* by the *appetite*), and the *incontinence* *is* *the* *defect* of the *character* (the *passion* *overcomes* the *judgment*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Akrasia} = \text{the *universal* knowledge *present* but the *particular* premise *obscured* (the *passion*)}
$$

**The *significance***. The *reply* *preserves* the *knowledge* (the *no* *ignorance* *of* the *universal*) and *explains* the *failure* (the *particular*), and it *grounds* the *continence* (the *enkrateia*: the *victory* of the *judgment* *over* the *appetite*), and it *fits* the *experience* (the *drunk* *recites* *Empedocles*).

### 6.3 The Davidsonian account

**The *thesis*** (Davidson, “How Is Weakness of Will Possible?” 1969, in *Essays on Actions and Events*, 1980). The *akratic* *action* *is* *intentional* (the *agent* *does* it *willingly*), and the *intentional* *action* *is* the *action* *that* is *better* *than* the *alternative* *in* the *light* of the *reasons*, and the *akratic* *judges* *both* (the *all*-*things*-*considered*: the *$\phi$* is *better*; the *sans* *phrase*: the *$\phi$* is *better* *here* *and* *now*), and the *two* *judgments* *are* *consistent* (the *conditional* *judgment* vs. the *unconditional*), and the *akrasia* *is* *the* *action* *against* the *all*-*things*-*considered* *judgment* *on* the *unconditional* *judgment*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
J_1: & (\text{all things considered}) \ \phi \ \text{is better than} \ \psi \\
J_2: & (\text{unconditional, here and now}) \ \psi \ \text{is better than} \ \phi \\
& \text{The *agent* *acts* *on*} \ J_2 \ (\text{the *akratic* *action*}), \ \text{and} \ J_1 \ \text{and} \ J_2 \ \text{are *logically* *consistent*} \ (\text{the *conditional*} \ vs. \ \text{the *unconditional*})
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *akratic* *action* *is* *intentional* (the *agent* *does* it *for* *reasons*).} \\
2. & \text{The *intentional* *action* *is* *better* *in* *the* *light* *of* *the* *agent*'s *reasons* (the *unconditional* *judgment*).} \\
3. & \text{The *unconditional* *judgment* *is* *consistent* *with* the *all*-*things*-*considered* *judgment* (the *logical* *point*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *akrasia* *is* *possible* *without* *the* *contradiction* (the *Davidson* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *preserves* the *intentionality* of the *akratic* *action* (the *no* *reduction* to the *compulsion*), and it *avoids* the *contradiction* (the *consistency* *of* the *judgments*), and it *fits* the *phenomenology* (the *agent* *knows* better and *does* it *anyway*).

**The *objection***. The *account* *misdescribes* the *akrasia* (the *akratic* *judges* the *$\phi$* as *better* *simpliciter*, and the *action* *against* the *judgment* *is* *the* *irrationality*; the *conditional* *vs.* the *unconditional* *distinction* *is* *ad* *hoc*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *distinction* *is* *real* (the *conditional* *judgment* “*if* *I* *consider* *only* *the* *health*, the *$\phi$* *is* better” *vs.* the *unconditional*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinction* in the *deliberation*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The willpower science

**The *themes***.

**(i) The ego depletion*** (Baumeister et al., “Ego Depletion: Is the Active Self a Limited Resource?” 1998). The *self*-*control* *is* a *limited* *resource* (the *muscle* *metaphor*), and the *exertion* *depletes* the *resource* (the *subsequent* *self*-*control* *fails*), and the *rest* *restores* it.

**The *replication*** (Hagger et al., “A Multilab Preregistered Replication of the Ego-Depletion Effect,” 2016). The *effect* *is* *near* *zero* (the *d* ≈ 0.04), and the *meta*-*analysis* (Carter et al., 2015) *finds* the *publication* *bias*, and the *ego* *depletion* *is* *a* *cautionary* *tale* (the *replication* *crisis*; the *humility*; Ch. 3, §6).

**(ii) The implementation intentions*** (Gollwitzer, “Implementation Intentions: Strong Effects of Simple Plans,” 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, “Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement,” 2006). The *if–then* *plans* (“*if* *situation* *X* *occurs*, *then* I *will* *do* *Y*”) *automate* the *self*-*control*, and the *meta*-*analysis* *finds* the *medium*-*to*-*large* *effects*, and the *plans* *are* *the* *robust* *finding*.

**(iii) The commitment devices*** (Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict*, 1960, Ch. 2; Ainslie, *Picoeconomics*, 1992; Thaler & Shefrin, “An Economic Theory of Self-Control,” 1981). The *agent* *binds* *the* *future* *self* (the *Ulysses* *contract*; the *savings* *account*; the *gym* *membership*), and the *binding* *compensates* for the *time* *inconsistency* (the *hyperbolic* *discounting*), and the *planner*-*doer* *model* *captures* the *conflict*.

**(iv) The habit formation*** (Lally et al., “How Are Habits Formed,” 2010; Wood & Neal, “A New Look at Habits and the Habit–Goal Interface,” 2007). The *habits* *are* *the* *context*-*cued* *automatic* *responses*, and the *formation* *takes* *the* *median* *of* *66* *days* (the *range* *18–254*), and the *habits* *are* the *architecture* *of* *the* *character* (the *Aristotelian* *habituation*; Ch. 45, §3.2).

**(v) The marshmallow test*** (Mischel, Ebbesen & Zeiss, 1972; the *replication*: Watts, Duncan & Quan, “Revisiting the Marshmallow Test,” 2018). The *original* *study* *found* the *delay* *of* *gratification* *predicts* the *later* *outcomes*, and the *replication* *finds* the *effect* *shrinks* *substantially* when the *SES* is *controlled*, and the *replication* *shows* the *confound* (the *poverty* *shapes* the *delay*), and the *lesson* *is* *the* *epistemic* *humility*.

**The *significance***. The *willpower* *science* *is* *a* *case* *study* of the *empirical* *turn* (the *robust* *findings*: the *implementation* *intentions*, the *habits*, the *commitment* *devices*; the *fragile* *findings*: the *ego* *depletion*, the *marshmallow* *prediction*), and the *case* *shows* the *methodological* *lesson* (the *replication*, the *preregistration*, the *calibration*).

### 6.5 The architecture of character

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *extended* *mind*; Clark & Chalmers, “The Extended Mind,” 1998). The *self*-*control* *is* *not* *the* *inner* *willpower* (the *muscle*) but the *architecture* (the *habit*, the *situation* *design*, the *commitment* *device*, the *social* *support*), and the *character* *is* *the* *stable* *pattern* of the *architecture*, and the *virtue* *is* the *well*-*designed* *architecture*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Self-control} \ = \ \text{the *inner* *effort*} \ + \ \text{the *external* *scaffolding*} \ (\text{the *plans*, the *habits*, the *situations*, the *commitments*, the *social* *support})
$$

**The *significance***. The *thesis* *answers* the *situationist* *challenge* (the *situation* *is* *not* *the* *enemy* *of* the *character*; it *is* the *material* of the *character*; Ch. 45, §3.4), and it *grounds* the *virtue* in the *practice* (the *habituation*, the *design*), and it *fits* the *empirical* *evidence* (the *implementation* *intentions*, the *habits*).

**The *objection***. The *thesis* *under*-*estimates* the *inner* *agency* (the *responsibility*, the *effort*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply***. The *inner* *effort* *is* *one* *component* (the *architecture* *includes* the *effort*), and the *reply* *integrates* the *two*, and the *reply* is *defensible*.

### 6.6 The state of the willpower debate

**The *assessment***. The *akrasia* *is* *real* (the *Socratic* *denial* *fails*), the *Davidsonian* *account* is *defensible*, and the *willpower* *science* *is* *fragile* (the *replication* *crisis*), and the *architecture* *is* *the* *robust* *finding*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Socratic** | The *no* *contradiction* | The *counterintuitive* |
| **Aristotelian** | The *practical* *syllogism* | The *no* *explanation* of the *akratic* *judgment* |
| **Davidsonian** | The *consistency* | The *conditional* *vs.* *unconditional* |
| **Willpower muscle** | The *metaphor* | The *replication* |
| **Architecture** | The *robust* *evidence* | The *inner* *agency* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Davidsonian* *account* *combined* with the *architecture* *insight*: the *akrasia* *is* *the* *action* *against* the *all*-*things*-*considered* *judgment* (the *Davidson* *insight*), *explained* by the *competing* *motivation* (the *particular* *premise*; the *Aristotelian* *insight*), *remedied* by the *architecture* (the *plans*, the *habits*, the *commitments*; the *architecture* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *ego* *depletion* and the *marshmallow* *prediction* *are* *not* *robust*.

## 7. Implicit Bias and Moral Ignorance

### 7.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Greenwald & Banaji, “Implicit Social Cognition,” 1995; Greenwald, McGhee & Schwartz, “Measuring Individual Differences in Implicit Cognition: The Implicit Association Test,” 1998; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *implicit* *biases* *are* the *unconscious* *associations* (the *stereotype*-*congruent* *links*) that *influence* the *judgment* and the *behavior* *without* the *agent*'s *awareness* or *endorsement*, and the *biases* *are* *measured* by the *Implicit* *Association* *Test* (the *IAT*), and the *biases* *are* *widespread* (the *even* *the* *agents* *who* *disavow* the *stereotypes* *show* the *bias*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Implicit bias} \ = \ \text{the automatic association} \ (\text{the *group*} \leftrightarrow \ \text{the *attribute*}) \ \text{that shapes the response without the awareness}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *bias* *challenges* the *account* of the *responsibility* (the *agent* *acts* *from* the *bias* *without* *knowing*; §4.3), and it *challenges* the *virtue* *ethics* (the *character* *is* *not* *transparent* to the *agent*), and it *connects* the *moral* *psychology* to the *social* *epistemology* (Ch. 11, §4) and to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §2).

### 7.2 The evidence and its limits

**The *themes***.

**(i) The *IAT*** (Greenwald et al. 1998). The *reaction* *time* *advantage* for the *congruent* *pairings* (the *white*/*good*; the *black*/*bad*), and the *score* *measures* the *associative* *strength*, and the *widespread* *results* (the *most* *people* *show* the *bias*).

**(ii) The *predictive* *validity*** (Oswald, Mitchell, Blanton, Jaccard & Tetlock, “Predicting Ethnic and Racial Discrimination: A Meta-Analysis of IAT Criterion Studies,” 2013). The *correlation* with the *behavior* *is* *small* (the *r* ≈ .15; the *aggregate* *prediction* *better* than the *individual*), and the *test*-*retest* *reliability* *is* *low* (the *r* ≈ .25–.6; Cunningham, Preacher & Banaji, 2001), and the *measure* *has* the *limitations*.

**(iii) The *reply*** (Greenwald, Banaji & Nosek, 2015). The *small* *effects* *can* *have* the *large* *societal* *consequences* (the *aggregate*), and the *bias* *is* *a* *group*-*level* *phenomenon* (the *aggregate* *prediction*), and the *reply* *defends* the *measure*.

**The *assessment***. The *IAT* *has* *some* *predictive* *validity* (the *contested* *magnitude*), and the *individual*-*level* *prediction* *is* *weak*, and the *measure* *is* *not* *a* *diagnosis* *of* the *individual*, and the *debate* *is* *the* *replication* *crisis* *in* the *social* *psychology* (the *lesson*: the *humility*; Ch. 3, §6).

### 7.3 The responsibility for the implicit bias

**The *theories***.

| The *theory* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Volitionism** | The *responsibility* *requires* the *control* (the *implicit* *bias* *is* *not* the *agent*'s) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Attributionism** | The *responsibility* *is* the *quality* of *will* (the *bias* *expresses* the *agent*'s *values*/*identity*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Social connection** | The *responsibility* *is* the *participation* in the *structure* | Young 2011 |

**The *attributionist* *argument*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *responsibility* *is* the *attributability* (the *action* *expresses* the *agent*'s *quality* of *will*; §4.2).} \\
2. & \text{The *implicit* *bias* *expresses* the *agent*'s *quality* of *will* (the *agent*'s *values*, the *agent*'s *identity*, the *agent*'s *history*).} \\
3. & \text{The *expression* *is* *real* (the *bias* *is* the *agent*'s, not the *invader*'s).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *agent* *is* *responsible* *for* the *implicit* *bias* (the *attributionist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *volitionist* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *requires* the *control* (the *volition*, the *choice*), and the *implicit* *bias* *is* *automatic* (the *agent* *cannot* *choose* the *response*), and the *attributionism* *over*-*extends* the *responsibility*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *attributionist* *tradition*). The *control* *is* *not* the *necessary* *condition* (the *quality* of *will* *is* *sufficient*; the *Strawsonian* *insight*), and the *agent* *can* *control* the *effects* (the *counter*-*measures*, the *situational* *design*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *attribution*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *attribution* *without* the *control* *is* *the* *majority* *view*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

**The *practical* *question***. The *remedy*: the *training* (the *short*-*term* *effects*), the *situational* *design* (the *blind* *review*, the *structured* *interviews*, the *diverse* *pools*; the *robust*), the *structural* *change* (the *policy*, the *institution*; the *robust*), and the *individual* *responsibility* *is* *one* *component*.

### 7.4 The moral ignorance and the epistemic vice

**The *thesis*** (§4.4; the *social* *epistemology*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *implicit* *bias* *is* *often* *supported* by the *moral* *ignorance* (the *agent* *does* not *know* the *bias*, the *agent* *does* not *know* the *harm*, the *agent* *avoids* the *knowledge*), and the *ignorance* *is* *the* *epistemic* *vice* (the *culpable* *ignorance*; the *affected* *ignorance*; the *active* *ignorance*), and the *responsibility* *connects* the *bias* to the *ignorance* (the *epistemic* *condition*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Implicit bias} \ \xrightarrow{\text{the epistemic vice}} \ \text{the moral ignorance} \ \xrightarrow{\text{the responsibility}} \ \text{the attribution}
$$

**The *significance***. The *thesis* *connects* the *implicit* *bias* to the *epistemic* *injustice* (the *hermeneutical*, the *testimonial*; Ch. 11, §4), and it *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *epistemic* *practice* (the *inquiry*, the *attention*), and it *challenges* the *individualist* *account* (the *structural* *insight*).

### 7.5 The state of the implicit bias debate

**The *assessment***. The *implicit* *bias* *exists* (the *associative* *effects* *are* *real*), and the *measurement* *is* *contested* (the *IAT*), and the *responsibility* is *contested* (the *attributionism* vs. the *volitionism*).

|---|---|---|

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *attributionism* *combined* with the *social* *connection*: the *agent* *is* *responsible* *for* the *implicit* *bias* (the *attribution* *insight*), *supported* by the *social* *connection* (the *participation* *insight*), *remedied* by the *situational* *design* and the *structural* *change* (the *practice* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *IAT* *is* *contested* and the *individual* *responsibility* *is* *limited*.

## 8. Moral Testimony and Moral Understanding

### 8.1 The debate

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimism** | The *moral* *knowledge* *is* *transmitted* by the *testimony* | Jones 1999; Sliwa 2012 |
| **Pessimism** | The *moral* *understanding* *is* *not* *transmitted* (the *knowledge* *is*) | Hills 2009 |
| **Impossibility** | The *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* | Wolff 1970; the *autonomy* *tradition* |

**The *question***. Can the *agent* *acquire* the *moral* *knowledge* (or the *moral* *understanding*) *from* the *testimony* (the *moral* *expert*, the *trusted* *friend*, the *teacher*), and the *answer* *determines* the *place* of the *testimony* in the *moral* *life*.

### 8.2 The pessimistic argument

**The *thesis*** (Hills, “Moral Testimony and Moral Epistemology,” 2009; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *testimony* *transmits* the *moral* *knowledge* (the *proposition*: the *slavery* *is* *wrong*), and it *does* not *transmit* the *moral* *understanding* (the *grasp* of *why* the *slavery* *is* *wrong*; the *reasons*, the *connections*), and the *agent* *who* *relies* *solely* on the *testimony* *is* *epistemically* *deficient* (the *deference*, the *no* *understanding*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Testimony} \ \rightarrow \ \text{the *knowledge*} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{the *understanding*} \ \not\rightarrow \ \text{the *testimony*} \ (\text{the *asymmetry*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *understanding* *requires* the *grasp* of the *reasons* (the *why*, the *connections*, the *justification*).} \\
2. & \text{The *grasp* *is* *achieved* by the *agent* (the *reflection*, the *inquiry*, the *engagement*), not *transmitted* by the *assertion*.} \\
3. & \text{The *testimony* *transmits* the *proposition* (the *knowledge*) and *not* the *grasp* (the *understanding*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *testimony* *does* *not* *transmit* the *understanding* (the *pessimistic* *insight*), and the *agent* *who* *relies* on the *testimony* *is* *epistemically* *deficient* (the *deference* *problem*).
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *captures* the *phenomenology* of the *understanding* (the *“*I* *see* *why*”* *is* *not* the *“*I* *read* *that* *why”*), and it *grounds* the *moral* *education* in the *engagement* (the *active* *learning*, the *critical* *thinking*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *moral* *disagreement* *requires* the *understanding*, not the *citation*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *asymmetry*’* *objection*** (Jones 1999; Sliwa 2012; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *testimony* *transmits* the *understanding* *in* the *moral* *domain* *as* *it* *does* *in* the *mathematical* *domain* (the *student* *grasps* the *theorem* *from* the *teacher*), and the *asymmetry* *is* *not* *defensible*, and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Hills 2009, §4). The *mathematical* *understanding* *also* *requires* the *grasp* (the *student* *who* *memorizes* *the* *proof* *does* *not* *understand* *the* *theorem*), and the *analogy* *supports* the *asymmetry*, and the *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *deference*’* *objection***. The *moral* *testimony* *is* *pervasive* (the *parents*, the *teachers*, the *culture*), and the *no*-*deference* *thesis* *is* *unrealistic* (the *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *testimony*), and the *reply* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *testimony* *is* *necessary* *for* the *moral* *knowledge* (the *initial* *acquisition*), and the *understanding* *is* *the* *goal* (the *reflection* *after* the *testimony*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *deference* in the *process*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 8.3 The autonomy and the trust

**The *autonomy* *argument*** (Wolff, *In Defense of Anarchism*, 1970, Ch. 1; the *Kantian* *tradition*; the *existentialist* *critique*; Sartre, *Being and Nothingness*, 1943, the *bad* *faith*). The *moral* *agent* *is* *autonomous* (the *self*-*legislation*; the *duty* *to* *think* *for* *oneself*), and the *deference* to the *moral* *testimony* *is* *heteronomy* (the *surrender* of the *autonomy*), and the *autonomy* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* (§6.3).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Autonomy} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *no* *deference*} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{the *deference*} \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the *heteronomy*}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *grounds* the *pessimism* in the *autonomy*, and it *connects* the *moral* *testimony* to the *practical* *reason* (§3) and to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §1).

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *care* *ethics*). The *autonomy* *is* *not* *the* *isolation* (the *moral* *life* *is* *relational*), and the *trust* *is* *a* *virtue* (the *epistemic* *reliance*, the *interdependence*), and the *deference* *is* *not* *the* *surrender* (the *critical* *endorsement*; the *trust* *with* the *reflection*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *deference* in the *relational* *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**The *trust***. The *trust* *is* *the* *epistemic* *attitude* (the *reliance* on the *other*'s *competence* and *honesty*; the *second*-*personal* *relation*; the *contemporary* *literature*), and the *trust* *requires* the *justification* (the *reliability*, the *goodwill*), and the *trust* *is* *the* *middle* *path* (between the *no* *deference* and the *credulity*).

### 8.4 The moral expertise

**The *question*** (Ch. 44, §6.5). Is there a *moral* *expertise*? (The *philosopher*, the *ethicist*, the *clinician*, the *elder*.)

**The *positions***.

| The *position* | The *claim* | The *evidence* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *expertise* *exists*** | The *moral* *expertise* *exists* (the *training*, the *virtue*, the *judgment*) | The *practice* (the *ethics* *committees*, the *courts*, the *clinicians*) |
| **The *expertise* *is* *limited*** | The *expertise* *exists* but *not* *transmissible* (the *understanding*; §8.2) | The *Hills* *asymmetry* |
| **The *expertise* *is* *impossible*** | The *moral* *judgment* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* (the *autonomy*) | The *disagreement* (the *experts* *disagree*) |

**The *argument* (the *no*-*expertise* *worry***; Ch. 44, §6.5).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *disagreement* *persists* among the *experts* (the *philosophers*, the *theologians*), and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *expertise* (the *peer* *disagreement*; Ch. 11, §3).} \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *knowledge* *requires* the *first*-*person* *engagement* (the *autonomy*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *expertise* is *not* *the* *genuine* *expertise* (the *no*-*expertise* *insight*), and the *moral* *testimony* *is* *not* *authoritative* (the *autonomy* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *epistemic* *authority*; Zagzebski, *Epistemic Authority*, 2012, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *expertise* *is* *the* *reliability* *in* the *moral* *domain* (the *training*, the *experience*, the *track* *record*; the *analogy* to the *medical* *expertise*), and the *deference* *to* the *expert* *is* *rational* (the *preemption*; the *Zagzebski* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *expertise* in the *reliability*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *expertise* *is* *domain*-*specific*, and the *trust* *is* *the* *practice*).

**The *checks***. The *deference* *is* *checked* by the *coherence* (the *expert*'s *advice* *fits* the *agent*'s *values*), the *track* *record* (the *expert*'s *performance*), and the *transparency* (the *reasons* the *expert* *offers*), and the *checks* *guard* against the *paternalism* (the *moral* *sycophancy*, the *licensing*; the *contemporary* *literature*).

### 8.5 The state of the testimony debate

**The *assessment***. The *optimism* (the *knowledge* *transmitted*) and the *pessimism* (the *understanding* *not*) are *defensible*, and the *autonomy* *argument* is *serious*, and the *trust* is the *middle* *path*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimism** | The *knowledge* | The *understanding* |
| **Pessimism** | The *autonomy* | The *no* *deference* |
| **Impossibility** | The *engagement* | The *unrealistic* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pessimism* *combined* with the *trust*: the *testimony* *transmits* the *knowledge* (the *optimistic* *insight*) and *not* the *full* *understanding* (the *pessimistic* *insight*), and the *deference* *is* *rational* *when* *supported* by the *trust*, the *coherence*, and the *checks* (the *relational* *insight*), and the *understanding* *is* *the* *goal* (the *educational* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *expertise* and the *autonomy* *remain* *contested*.

## Summary

- **The *practical* *reason*** *is* the *machinery* by which the *judgment* becomes the *motive*, the *action*, and the *responsibility*, and the *chapter* *examines* the *machinery* *mechanism* by *mechanism*.

- **The *akrasia*** is *real* (the *Socratic* *denial* *fails*), and the *Davidsonian* *account* is *defensible*, and the *willpower* *science* is *fragile* (the *ego* *depletion* *replication* *fails*; the *marshmallow* *effect* *shrinks*), and the *architecture* (the *implementation* *intentions*, the *habits*, the *commitments*) is the *robust* *finding*.
