# Chapter 43 — Mathematics: Objects, Knowledge, and Application

> “Die ganzen Zahlen hat der liebe Gott gemacht, alles andere ist Menschenwerk.”
> (“God made the integers, all the rest is the work of man.”)
> — Leopold Kronecker, quoted in Weber, “Leopold Kronecker” (1893)

> “If number is to be a property of things, then it is not the same property for all of them; and it is not clear what property of a heap of stones could be the number 5.”
> — a paraphrase of Benacerraf’s challenge, *What Numbers Could Not Be* (1965)

> “The miracle of the appropriateness of the language of mathematics for the formulation of the laws of physics is a wonderful gift which we neither understand nor deserve.”
> — Eugene Wigner, “The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences” (1960)

---

Chapter 42 examined *life* — the domain in which *naturalism* is *stretched* by the *teleology* and the *normativity*. This chapter examines *mathematics* — the domain in which *naturalism* is *stretched* to its *breaking point*.

The *stretching* has *three* *sources*, and they *structure* the chapter.

The *first* is *ontological*: mathematics *appears* to be *about* the *objects* — the *numbers*, the *sets*, the *functions*, the *structures* — that are *abstract* (they are *not* in *space* and *time*, and they have *no* *causal* *powers*), and the *abstractness* *confronts* the *naturalistic* *ontology* (Ch. 14, §1).

The *second* is *epistemological*: the *mathematical* *knowledge* is the *paradigm* of the *a* *priori* (Ch. 36, §5), and the *access* to the *abstract* *objects* is *mysterious* (Benacerraf’s *dilemma*), and the *mystery* *threatens* the *claim* to *knowledge*.

The *third* is *explanatory*: mathematics is *indispensable* to *science* (the *applicability*; Ch. 39, §4.1), and the *indispensability* *suggests* that the *mathematical* *objects* *exist* (the *Quine*–*Putnam* *argument*), and the *existence* *confronts* the *nominalism* that the *naturalism* *favours*.

The chapter’s *thesis* is that the *mathematical* *platonism* is *defensible* in a *qualified* form (the *structural* *platonism*; the *thin* *mathematical* *realism*), and that the *access* *problem* is *real* but *not* *fatal* (the *response* is the *abductive* *account* and the *structural* *epistemology*), and that the *applicability* is *real* but *not* *miraculous* (the *response* is the *structural* *account* of the *application*), and that the *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *structuralism* (the *ante* *rem* version is *promising* and *incomplete*; the *in* *rebus* and the *modal* versions are *the* *alternatives*).

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 examines *platonism* and *Benacerraf’s* *dilemma*. §2 examines the *three* *programmes* (the *logicism*, the *formalism*, the *intuitionism*). §3 examines *structuralism*. §4 examines *nominalism* and the *fictionalism*. §5 examines the *epistemology* (the *intuition*, the *proof*, the *computer*-*assisted*). §6 examines the *applicability*. §7 examines *set* *theory* and the *continuum* *problem*. §8 gives an *honest* *verdict*.

## 1. Platonism and Benacerraf’s Dilemma

### 1.1 The platonic picture

**The *thesis*** (Plato, *Republic* VI–VII; the *contemporary* *form*: Gödel, “Russell’s Mathematical Logic,” 1944; *What Is Cantor’s Continuum Problem?*, 1947; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematical* *objects* (the *numbers*, the *sets*, the *functions*) *exist* *independently* of the *mathematical* *practice*, and the *mathematical* *truths* are *discovered*, not *invented*.

**The *structure***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **Ontological** | The mathematical objects exist (abstractly) | The *realism* |
| **Semantic** | The mathematical statements are *literally true* (the *truth* is *about* the objects) | The *semantic* *realism* |
| **Epistemic** | The mathematical knowledge is *a priori* (the *grasp* of the objects) | The *epistemic* *realism* |

**The *virtue***. The *view* *grounds* the *objectivity* of *mathematics* (the *truths* are *not* *conventions*, the *disagreements* are *real*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *mathematicians* *discover* the *theorems*, and the *truths* *resist* the *wishful* *thinking*), and it *fits* the *semantics* of the *mathematical* *statements* (the *existential* *quantifiers* are *literally* *about* the *objects*).

**The *history***. The *view* *goes* *back* to Plato (the *“*mathematical* *objects”* *are* the *intermediate* *entities* between the *Forms* and the *sensible* *things*; the *Republic* 510d–511a), and it was *revived* by Frege (the *logicism*; §2.1), and by Gödel (the *“*mathematical* *intuition”*; §5.1).

### 1.2 Benacerraf's dilemma

**The *argument*** (Benacerraf, “Mathematical Truth,” 1973; the *most* *influential* *statement* of the *problem*).

**The *two* *premises***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
(\mathrm{S}) & \text{The *semantics*: the *mathematical* *statements* are *true* only if they *refer* to the *abstract* *objects* (the *semantic* *realism*).} \\
(\mathrm{E}) & \text{The *epistemology*: the *knowledge* *requires* a *causal* *connection* between the *knower* and the *known* (the *causal* *theory* of *knowledge*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{If (S), the *mathematical* *truths* are *about* the *abstract* *objects* (the *semantic* *thesis*).} \\
2. & \text{The *abstract* *objects* are *causally* *inert* (they *have* no *causal* *powers*).} \\
3. & \text{If (E), the *knowledge* of the *abstract* *objects* is *impossible* (the *no* *causal* *connection*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *mathematical* *knowledge* is *impossible* (the *dilemma*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *dilemma***. Either the *semantics* is *realist* (and the *epistemology* is *mysterious*) or the *epistemology* is *naturalistic* (and the *semantics* is *anti*-*realist*). The *two* *premises* are *individually* *plausible*, and *jointly* *incompatible*.

**The *significance***. The *dilemma* is the *central* *problem* in the *philosophy* of *mathematics* (Ch. 9, §7.2), and it is the *test* *case* for the *naturalisation* of *knowledge*, and it is the *source* of the *structuralism* (§3) and the *nominalism* (§4).

### 1.3 The responses

**The *responses***.

| The *response* | The *strategy* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Platonism with the a priori access** | The *a priori* (the *intuition*) is *not* the *causal* *knowledge* | Gödel; the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Structuralism** | The *objects* are the *positions* (the *access* is *structural*) | Shapiro; Resnik; Ch. 43, §3 |
| **Nominalism** | The *mathematics* is *reconstructed* *without* the *abstract* *objects* | Field; Ch. 43, §4 |
| **Fictionalism** | The *mathematics* is a *useful* *fiction* | Field; the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Indispensability** | The *mathematics* is *indispensable* (the *access* is *abductive*) | Quine; Putnam; Ch. 43, §4.3 |
| **Naturalism** | The *mathematics* is *continuous* with the *science* (the *access* is *empirical*) | Maddy; the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *significance***. The *responses* *divide* into the *two* *families* (the *realist* *responses* and the *anti*-*realist* *responses*), and the *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to the *semantics* and the *epistemology*, and the *weighting* is the *philosophical* *question*.

### 1.4 Benacerraf's other argument: what numbers could not be

**The *argument*** (Benacerraf, “What Numbers Could Not Be,” 1965; the *identity* *problem*).

**The *set*-*theoretic* *reductions***. The *natural* *numbers* can be *identified* with the *von* *Neumann* *ordinals* (the *successor* of $n$ is $n \cup \{n\}$) or with the *Zermelo* *ordinals* (the *successor* of $n$ is $\{n\}$).

**The *von* *Neumann* *ordinals***.

$$
0 = \varnothing, \quad 1 = \{\varnothing\}, \quad 2 = \{\varnothing, \{\varnothing\}\}, \quad 3 = \{\varnothing, \{\varnothing\}, \{\varnothing, \{\varnothing\}\}\}, \ldots
$$

**The *Zermelo* *ordinals***.

$$
0 = \varnothing, \quad 1 = \{\varnothing\}, \quad 2 = \{\{\varnothing\}\}, \quad 3 = \{\{\{\varnothing\}\}\}, \ldots
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Both the *reductions* *satisfy* the *axioms* of *arithmetic* (the *Peano* *axioms*).} \\
2. & \text{The *reductions* *disagree* on the *identity* *statements* (the *von* *Neumann* *2* has the *two* *members*, and the *Zermelo* *2* has the *one* *member*).} \\
3. & \text{The *choice* *between* the *reductions* is *arbitrary* (there is *no* *fact* of the *matter* about *which* is the *correct* *one*, and the *arithmetic* *does* not *distinguish* them).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *numbers* are *not* the *sets* (the *reduction* *fails*), and the *numbers* are the *positions* in the *structure* (the *structural* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequence***. The *argument* *supports* the *structuralism* (§3): the *numbers* are the *positions* in the *natural*-*number* *structure*, and the *identity* of the *position* is *exhausted* by the *relations* to the *other* *positions*, and the *question* “which *set* is 2?” is *misplaced* (the *set*-*theoretic* *reduction* is a *representation*, not an *identification*).

### 1.5 The state of the platonism debate

**The *assessment***. The *platonism* is the *most* *intuitive* *account*, and it *faces* the *access* *problem*, and the *responses* are *all* *contested*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Platonism (Gödel)** | The objectivity; the practice | The access |
| **Structuralism (Shapiro)** | The positions; the practice | The access (the structure) |
| **Nominalism (Field)** | The naturalism | The reconstruction; the mathematics |
| **Fictionalism** | The naturalism | The attitude; the application |
| **Indispensability** | The science | The access; the meta-induction |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *structural* *platonism*: the *mathematical* *objects* are *positions* in *structures* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *structures* are *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and the *access* is *abductive* (the *indispensability* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *access* *problem* is *not* *fully* *resolved* and the *Benacerraf* *dilemma* *remains* *the* *central* *challenge*.

## 2. The Three Programmes

### 2.1 Logicism

**The *thesis*** (Frege, *The Foundations of Arithmetic*, 1884; *The Basic Laws of Arithmetic*, 1893; Russell & Whitehead, *Principia Mathematica*, 1910–13; the *neo*-*logicist* *revival*: Wright, *Frege’s Conception of Numbers as Objects*, 1983; Hale & Wright, *The Reason’s Proper Study*, 2001). The *mathematics* is *reducible* to *logic*, and the *mathematical* *truths* are the *logical* *truths* (the *analytic*; Ch. 36, §2).

**The *structure***. The *Fregean* *programme*.

$$
\text{Arithmetic} \ \subseteq \ \text{Logic} \quad \text{(the *logicist* *reduction*)}
$$

**The *Fregean* *definitions***.

**(i) The *direction* *abstraction*** (the *paradigm*).

$$
\text{Dir}(a) = \text{Dir}(b) \ \leftrightarrow \ a \parallel b \quad \text{(the *parallelism*)}
$$

**(ii) The *Hume’s* *principle*** (the *numerical* *abstraction*).

$$
\#F = \#G \ \leftrightarrow \ F \approx G \quad \text{(the *equinumerosity*; the *one*-*to*-*one* *correspondence*)}
$$

where $F \approx G$ means that there is a *bijection* between the $F$s and the $G$s.

**The *Frege’s* *theorem*** (Frege 1884, §§68–83; the *modern* *reconstruction*: Wright 1983; Boolos, “The Consistency of Frege’s *Foundations of Arithmetic*,” 1987). The *second*-*order* *logic* *plus* the *Hume’s* *principle* *derives* the *second*-*order* *Peano* *arithmetic* (the *PA*$_2$), and the *derivation* is the *Frege’s* *theorem*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *Hume’s* *principle* is *analytic* (it is *constitutive* of the *concept* of *number*).} \\
2. & \text{The *second*-*order* *logic* *plus* the *Hume’s* *principle* *derives* the *PA*$_2$ (the *Frege’s* *theorem*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *arithmetic* is *analytic* (the *neo*-*logicist* *insight*), and the *logicism* is *vindicated* (the *revised* *form*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *collapse*** (Russell’s *paradox*; the *letter* to Frege, 1902; Ch. 33, §1.7). The *Fregean* *programme* *rested* on the *Basic* *Law* *V* (the *unrestricted* *comprehension*), and the *law* *generates* the *Russell’s* *paradox*, and the *system* *is* *inconsistent*.

**The *Basic* *Law* *V***.

$$
\{x : Fx\} = \{x : Gx\} \ \leftrightarrow \ \forall x\, (Fx \leftrightarrow Gx)
$$

**The *Russell’s* *paradox***. Let $R = \{x : x \notin x\}$, and then $R \in R \leftrightarrow R \notin R$.

**The *neo*-*logicist* *response***. The *Hume’s* *principle* is *consistent* (the *Frege’s* *theorem* *stands*), and the *programme* *rests* on the *HP*, not on the *Basic* *Law* *V*, and the *revival* *avoids* the *paradox*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *Caesar* *problem*** (Frege 1884, §56; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *HP* *determines* the *identity* *conditions* of the *numbers* (the *#F = #G*), and it *does* not *determine* *whether* *Julius* *Caesar* is a *number* (the *HP* is *silent* on the *cross*-*sortal* *identities*), and the *silence* *shows* that the *HP* is *not* *sufficient* for the *concept* of *number*.

**The *reply*** (Wright 1983, Ch. 4; Hale & Wright 2001, Ch. 12). The *HP* *fixes* the *sortal* (the *number* is *the* *sortal* whose *criterion* of *identity* is the *HP*), and the *cross*-*sortal* *question* is *answered* by the *sortal* *conventions* (the *number* is *not* the *person*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *sortal* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *Bad* *Company* *objection* (Boolos, “The Standard of Equality of Numbers,” 1990; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *abstraction* *principles* *come* in the *good* and the *bad* *company* (the *HP* is *good*, and the *Basic* *Law* *V* is *bad*, and the *“*N*”* *principle* (the *Nx* = *Ny* iff *x* = *y*) is *bad*), and the *neo*-*logicist* *criterion* (the *conservativeness*, the *stability*) *must* *distinguish* the *two*, and the *criterion* is *not* *available* (the *“*Bad* *Company*”* *problem*; Fine, *The Limits of Abstraction*, 2002).

**The *reply*** (Hale & Wright 2001, Ch. 13; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *criterion* is the *conservativeness* (the *principle* *must* *not* *entail* the *new* *arithmetical* *truths* beyond the *intended* ones) and the *stability* (the *principle* *must* *yield* the *same* *cardinal* *numbers* on the *different* *domains*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *criterion* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**The *significance***. The *logicism* is *not* *vindicated* in the *original* *form*, and it is *vindicated* in the *revised* *form* (the *Frege’s* *theorem* is *correct*, and the *derivation* *is* *valid*), and the *open* *problems* (the *Caesar*, the *Bad* *Company*) are *the* *frontier* of the *contemporary* *debate*.

### 2.2 Formalism

**The *thesis*** (Hilbert, “Über das Unendliche,” 1926; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *game* *formalism*: Curry, *Outlines of a Formalist Philosophy of Mathematics*, 1951; the *term* *formalism*). The *mathematics* is the *manipulation* of the *symbols* according to the *rules*, and the *mathematical* *truths* are the *theorems* of the *formal* *system*, and the *mathematical* *objects* are the *symbols* (or the *terms*), not the *independent* *entities*.

**The *Hilbert’s* *programme***. The *formal* *systems* *capture* the *mathematical* *content* (the *finitistic* *consistency* *proofs*), and the *consistency* is *proved* by the *finitary* *methods* (the *safe* *metamathematics*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Mathematics} = \text{the *formal* *system*} \ (\text{the *symbols*, the *rules*})
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematics* *uses* the *symbols* (the *formulas*, the *proofs*).} \\
2. & \text{The *symbols* *are* *manipulated* according to the *rules* (the *syntax*).} \\
3. & \text{The *manipulation* is *sufficient* for the *mathematical* *practice* (the *practice*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *mathematics* is the *formalism* (the *formalist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *collapse*** (Gödel, “On Formally Undecidable Propositions of *Principia Mathematica* and Related Systems,” 1931; Ch. 35, §7.1). The *incompleteness* *theorems* (*any* *consistent* *formal* *system* *containing* the *arithmetic* *is* *incomplete*, and *its* *consistency* *cannot* be *proved* *in* the *system*), and the *theorems* *defeat* the *Hilbert’s* *programme*.

**The *first* *incompleteness* *theorem***.

$$
T \ \text{recursively axiomatisable} \ \wedge \ T \ \text{consistent} \ \wedge \ T \ \supseteq \ \mathrm{Q} \ \Rightarrow \ \exists \varphi\, (T \nvdash \varphi \wedge T \nvdash \neg \varphi)
$$

**The *second* *incompleteness* *theorem***.

$$
T \ \text{consistent} \ \wedge \ T \ \supseteq \ \mathrm{Q} \ \Rightarrow \ T \nvdash \mathrm{Con}(T)
$$

**The *consequence***. The *consistency* of the *arithmetic* *cannot* be *proved* by the *methods* *formalised* *within* the *arithmetic*, and the *Hilbert’s* *programme* *fails* (the *finitary* *methods* are *formalised* *within* the *arithmetic*, and the *consistency* *escapes*).

**The *neo*-*formalist* *responses***.

| The *response* | The *claim* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *relative* *consistency*** | The *consistency* *proved* *relative* to the *stronger* *systems* (the *Gentzen* 1936; the *ZFC* ) | The *regress* (the *stronger* *system* *needs* the *proof*) |
| **The *game* *formalism*** (Curry) | The *mathematics* is the *game* (the *aesthetic*, the *interest*) | The *application* (the *game* *does* not *explain* the *applicability*) |
| **The *term* *formalism*** | The *mathematics* is the *manipulation* of the *terms* | The *content* (the *terms* *have* the *content*) |
| **The *deductivism*** | The *mathematics* is the *conditional* (the *if*-*then*; §4.4) | The *application* (the *conditional* *does* not *explain* the *assertion*) |

**The *significance***. The *formalism* is *refuted* as the *complete* *account* (the *Gödel* *theorems* *defeat* the *Hilbert’s* *programme*), and it *survives* as a *partial* *account* (the *syntactic* *manipulation* is *part* of the *practice*), and the *applicability* *and* the *content* *remain* *unexplained*.

### 2.3 Intuitionism

**The *thesis*** (Brouwer, “Intuitionism and Formalism,” 1913; *Collected Works*, 1975; Heyting, *Intuitionism: An Introduction*, 1956; Dummett, *Elements of Intuitionism*, 1977). The *mathematics* is the *construction* of the *mental* *objects* (the *intuition* of the *two*-*oneness*; the *temporal* *succession*), and the *existence* *requires* the *construction* (the *constructive* *insight*).

**The *BHK* *semantics*** (the *Brouwer*–*Heyting*–*Kolmogorov*).

| The *connective* | The *proof* | The *construction* |
|---|---|---|
| **Conjunction** | The *proof* of $A$ and the *proof* of $B$ | The *pair* $\langle p, q \rangle$ |
| **Disjunction** | The *proof* of $A$ or the *proof* of $B$ (the *specific* one) | The *injection* |
| **Implication** | The *construction* that *turns* the *proof* of $A$ into the *proof* of $B$ | The *function* |
| **Universal** | The *construction* that *turns* the *object* $a$ into the *proof* of $A(a)$ | The *function* |
| **Existential** | The *object* $a$ and the *proof* of $A(a)$ | The *witness* |

**The *rejection* of the *excluded* *middle***. The *law* $A \vee \neg A$ *requires* the *proof* of $A$ or the *proof* of $\neg A$, and the *proof* is *not* *available* for the *undecided* *statements* (the *Goldbach* *conjecture*), and the *law* *is* *rejected* (Ch. 35, §5.1).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematical* *truth* is the *constructibility* (the *BHK* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *constructibility* *requires* the *proof* (the *verification*).} \\
3. & \text{The *proof* is *not* *available* for the *undecided* *statements* (the *excluded* *middle*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *excluded* *middle* is *not* *valid* (the *intuitionist* *insight*), and the *classical* *mathematics* is *not* *constructive* (the *revisionist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *mathematics* in the *construction* (the *mental* *act*, the *verification*), and it *fits* the *constructive* *mathematics* (the *computer* *science*, the *proof* *assistants*), and it *avoids* the *mystery* of the *abstract* *access* (the *objects* are *constructed*, not *discovered*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *classical* *mathematics*’* *objection*. The *intuitionism* *rejects* the *classical* *proofs* (the *existence* *proofs* that *do* not *exhibit* the *object*; the *intermediate* *value* *theorem*; the *Bolzano*–*Weierstrass*), and the *rejection* *undermines* the *mathematical* *practice* (the *analysis*, the *topology*), and the *reconstruction* is *costly*.

**The *reply*** (Bishop, *Foundations of Constructive Analysis*, 1967; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *constructive* *analysis* *is* *viable* (the *Bishop* *reconstruction*), and the *reply* *reconstructs* the *analysis* *without* the *classical* *principles*, and the *reply* *is* *defensible* (the *cost* is the *loss* of the *classical* *results*, and the *gain* is the *constructive* *content*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *truth* *value*’* *objection*. The *intuitionism* *abandons* the *bivalence* (the *undecided* *statements* *have* the *no* *truth* *value*), and the *abandonment* *conflicts* with the *ordinary* *mathematical* *realism* (the *statements* *are* *true* or *false* *independently* of the *knowledge*).

**The *reply*** (Dummett 1977, Ch. 1). The *bivalence* *is* *not* *required* (the *truth* *is* the *verification*, and the *undecided* *statements* *are* *not* *verified*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *verification*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*psychologism*’* *objection* (Frege 1893, Introduction). The *intuitionism* *grounds* the *mathematics* in the *mental* *construction* (the *intuition*, the *consciousness*), and the *grounding* *makes* the *mathematics* *subjective* (the *construction* *depends* on the *subject*), and the *objectivity* *is* *lost*.

**The *reply*** (Brouwer 1913; Dummett 1977). The *construction* is *objective* (it is the *intersubjective* *verification*, the *proof* *that* the *community* *can* *check*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 2.4 The state of the programmes

**The *assessment***. The *three* *programmes* are *all* *defeated* in their *original* *forms*, and *all* *survive* in the *revised* *forms*.

| The *programme* | The *original* | The *revised* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Logicism** | The *Frege* (the *Basic Law V*) | The *neo*-*logicist* (the *HP*, the *Frege’s theorem*) | The *live* (the *Bad Company*) |
| **Formalism** | The *Hilbert* (the *finitary consistency*) | The *relative consistency*, the *deductivism* | The *partial* |
| **Intuitionism** | The *Brouwer* (the *excluded middle*) | The *constructive analysis* (the *Bishop*) | The *live* (the *cost*) |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism*: the *three* *programmes* *capture* the *different* *aspects* of the *mathematics* (the *logical* *structure*, the *syntactic* *manipulation*, the *constructive* *content*), and the *mathematics* is *not* *reducible* to a *single* *aspect*, and the *choice* *depends* on the *purpose* (the *foundations*, the *practice*, the *computer* *science*).

## 3. Structuralism

### 3.1 The thesis

**The *thesis*** (Benacerraf 1965; Shapiro, *Philosophy of Mathematics: Structure and Ontology*, 1997; Resnik, *Mathematics as a Science of Patterns*, 1997; Ch. 15, §7.1). The *mathematics* is *about* the *structures* (the *patterns*, the *positions*), not the *objects*, and the *numbers* are the *positions* in the *natural*-*number* *structure*, and the *structure* is *prior* to the *positions*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Mathematics} \ \text{studies the *structures*} \ (\text{the *relations* among the *positions*})
$$

**The *argument* (the *Benacerraf* *insight*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematical* *objects* can be *reduced* to the *different* *sets* (the *von* *Neumann*, the *Zermelo*), and the *reductions* *agree* on the *structural* *truths* (the *arithmetic*), and the *disagree* on the *identity* *statements*.} \\
2. & \text{The *structural* *truths* are the *mathematical* *content* (the *practice*), and the *identity* *statements* are *not* *determined* (the *arbitrariness*). \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *mathematics* is *about* the *structures* (the *structural* *insight*), not the *objects*.}
\end{array}
$$

### 3.2 The varieties

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *structure* *is* | The *status* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ante rem** | A *universal* (the *pattern* that *exists* independently) | Platonist | Shapiro; Parsons |
| **In rebus** | A *pattern* *instantiated* in the *concrete* *systems* | Naturalist | Resnik; the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Modal** | The *possible* *structures* (the *modal* *structuralism*) | Modal | Hellman, *Mathematics without Numbers*, 1989 |
| **Eliminative** | The *talk* of the *structures* is the *talk* of the *systems* (the *no* *abstract* *structures*) | Nominalist | the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *ante* *rem* *structuralism*** (Shapiro 1997, Ch. 5). The *structure* is a *universal* (the *ante* *rem*; the *prior* to the *instances*), and the *positions* are *defined* by the *relations* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *structure* *exists* *independently* of the *instances*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *structure* *determines* the *positions* (the *relations* are *constitutive*).} \\
2. & \text{The *structure* *exists* *independently* of the *particular* *instances* (the *pattern* is *repeatable*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *structure* is a *universal* (the *ante* *rem* *insight*), and the *mathematics* *studies* the *structures* (the *structural* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *explains* the *Benacerraf* *arbitrariness* (the *positions* are *not* the *sets*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *mathematicians* *study* the *structures*), and it *grounds* the *objectivity* in the *universals* (the *ante* *rem*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *access*’* *objection*. The *ante* *rem* *structures* are *abstract* (the *universals*; Ch. 15, §4.1), and the *access* is *mysterious* (the *Benacerraf* *dilemma* *returns*), and the *reply* is *contested* (§5.1).

**The *reply*** (Shapiro 1997, Ch. 6). The *access* is the *structural* *intuition* (the *grasp* of the *pattern*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *access* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *positions*’* *objection*. The *positions* are *not* *objects* (they are *defined* by the *relations*, and the *relations* *require* the *relata*), and the *structure* *without* the *objects* is *not* a *structure* (the *no*-*objects* *objection*; Ch. 19, §6.4).

**The *reply*** (Shapiro 1997, Ch. 5). The *positions* *are* the *objects* (the *objects* are *constituted* by the *relations*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *objects* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *uniqueness*’* *objection*. The *structures* are *not* *unique* (the *same* *mathematics* can be *instantiated* in the *different* *structures*; the *arithmetic* in the *von* *Neumann* and the *Zermelo*), and the *uniqueness* *is* *not* *required* (the *structure* is the *equivalence* *class*), and the *reply* is *defensible*.

### 3.3 Structuralism and the category theory

**The *thesis*** (the *category* *theoretic* *foundations*; Lawvere, “The Category of Categories as a Foundation for Mathematics,” 1966; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *structuralist* *insight*). The *mathematics* is *about* the *structures* (the *categories*), and the *category* *theory* *provides* the *language* (the *objects* are *defined* by the *morphisms*, and the *morphisms* by the *composition*, and the *composition* is the *primitive*).

**The *structure***. The *category* has the *objects*, the *morphisms*, the *composition*, and the *identities*, and the *objects* are *defined* *up* to *isomorphism* (the *structural* *insight*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *category* *theory* *captures* the *structures* (the *objects* *up* to the *isomorphism*).} \\
2. & \text{The *structures* are the *mathematical* *content* (the *structural* *insight*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *category* *theory* *is* the *structuralist* *foundations* (the *contemporary* *insight*), and the *objects* are the *structural* *positions* (the *no*-*intrinsic* *natures*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *unifies* the *structuralism* and the *foundations*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *category* *theory* is *used* in the *algebraic* *geometry*, the *homotopy* *type* *theory*), and it *avoids* the *set*-*theoretic* *reduction* (the *sets* are *one* *structure* among the *many*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *foundations*’* *objection*. The *category* *theory* *requires* the *metatheory* (the *set* *theory*), and the *foundations* *are* *not* *replaced* (the *category* *of* *categories* *is* *not* a *category*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *objects*’* *objection*. The *category* *theory* *defines* the *objects* *up* to the *isomorphism*, and the *objects* *are* *not* *individuals* (the *ancial* *question*; the *structural* *insight*), and the *reply* is *contested* (the *isomorphism* is *not* the *identity*; the *“*up* to* the* *isomorphism”* is the *structural* *answer*).

### 3.4 The state of the structuralism

**The *assessment***. The *structuralism* is the *most* *influential* *contemporary* *account*, and it *faces* the *access* and the *no*-*objects* *objections*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Ante rem** | The practice; the objectivity | The access |
| **In rebus** | The naturalism | The structures are instantiated |
| **Modal** | The naturalism; the structures | The modality |
| **Category** | The foundations | The metatheory |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *ante* *rem* *structuralism*: the *structures* are *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and the *positions* are *constituted* by the *relations* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *access* is the *structural* *intuition* (the *abductive* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *access* and the *no*-*objects* *objections* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Nominalism

### 4.1 The programme

**The *thesis*** (Field, *Science without Numbers*, 1980; *Realism, Mathematics and Modality*, 1989; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *abstract* *objects* *do* not *exist*, and the *mathematics* *can* be *reconstructed* *without* them (the *nominalist* *reconstruction*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Mathematics} \ \text{is *reconstructed*} \ (\text{the *nominalist* *paraphrase*})
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *abstract* *objects* are *epistemically* *inaccessible* (the *Benacerraf* *dilemma*).} \\
2. & \text{The *inaccessible* *entities* *should* be *dispensed* with (the *parsimony*; Ch. 14, §4).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *abstract* *objects* *should* be *dispensed* with (the *nominalist* *insight*), and the *mathematics* *should* be *reconstructed* (the *programme*).}
\end{array}
$$

### 4.2 Field's reconstruction

**The *thesis*** (Field 1980, Chs. 1–3). The *mathematics* *can* be *reconstructed* as a *conservative* *extension* of the *nominalistic* *physics*, and the *reconstruction* *shows* that the *mathematics* is *not* *indispensable*.

**The *structure***. Field's *strategy*.

**(i) The *nominalisation* of *physics***. The *physical* *theories* (the *Newtonian* *gravitation*) can be *formulated* *without* the *mathematical* *entities* (the *predicates* over the *spacetime* *points*, the *relations* of *betweenness* and *congruence*), and the *formulation* *captures* the *physical* *content*.

**(ii) The *representation* *theorems***. The *mathematical* *formulations* are *conservative* *extensions* (the *theorems* *that* *show* that the *mathematical* *statements* *add* *only* the *conservative* *content*), and the *conservativeness* *shows* that the *mathematics* is *not* *indispensable* (*if* the *nominalisation* *is* *adequate*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *physics* can be *formulated* *nominalistically* (the *reconstruction*).} \\
2. & \text{The *mathematics* is a *conservative* *extension* (the *representation* *theorem*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *mathematics* is *not* *indispensable* (the *nominalist* *insight*), and the *abstract* *objects* *can* be *dispensed* with (the *nominalist* *programme*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *programme* *avoids* the *access* *problem* (the *abstract* *objects* are *not* *posited*), and it *preserves* the *physical* *content* (the *nominalisation* *is* *adequate*), and it *fits* the *naturalism*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *nominalisation*’* *objection* (Malament, “Review of *Science without Numbers*,” 1982; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nominalisation* *requires* the *quantification* over the *spacetime* *points* (the *points* are *not* the *observable* *entities*, and they are *arguably* *abstract*), and the *reconstruction* *reintroduces* the *abstracta*, and the *programme* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Field 1989, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *spacetime* *points* *can* be *reconstructed* as the *equivalence* *classes* of the *regions* (the *nominalistic* *entities*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *points* in the *regions*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *equivalence* *classes* are *sets*, and the *sets* are *abstract*).

**(ii) The *‘*no* *conservativeness*’* *objection* (Shapiro, “Conservativeness and Incompleteness,” 1983; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *representation* *theorems* *presuppose* the *mathematical* *results* (the *theorems* are *proved* *mathematically*), and the *conservativeness* *is* *proved* *in* the *mathematics* (the *metatheory*), and the *programme* *is* *circular*.

**The *reply*** (Field 1989, Ch. 2). The *metatheory* can be *weakened* (the *conservativeness* *proved* in the *weaker* *systems*), and the *reply* *weakens* the *claim*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *adequacy*’* *objection*. The *nominalisation* is *not* *adequate* for the *contemporary* *physics* (the *quantum* *mechanics*, the *general* *relativity*), and the *programme* is *limited* to the *classical* *physics*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *nominalisation* *can* be *extended* (the *quantum* *mechanics* *nominalised*; the *contemporary* *literature*), and the *reply* is *promising* and *incomplete*.

### 4.3 The indispensability argument

**The *thesis*** (Quine, “Carnap and Logical Truth,” 1960; Putnam, “Philosophy of Mathematics: A Report,” 1964; the *contemporary* *literature*; Colyvan, *The Indispensability of Mathematics*, 2001). The *mathematical* *objects* *exist* because they are *indispensable* to the *best* *scientific* *theories*.

**The *argument*** (Ch. 14, §3.4; Ch. 39, §3.1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{We *ought* to have the *ontological* *commitment* to the *entities* that are *indispensable* to the *best* *scientific* *theories* (the *naturalistic* *premise*).} \\
2. & \text{The *mathematical* *entities* are *indispensable* to the *best* *scientific* *theories* (the *empirical* *premise*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{We *ought* to have the *ontological* *commitment* to the *mathematical* *entities* (the *indispensability* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *argument* *grounds* the *mathematical* *realism* in the *science* (the *naturalism*), and it *avoids* the *a* *priori* *access* (the *access* is *through* the *science*), and it *fits* the *confirmation* *holism* (Ch. 40, §1).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*Field*’* *objection*. The *nominalist* *reconstruction* *shows* that the *mathematics* is *not* *indispensable* (the *representation* *theorems*), and the *premise* 2 *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Colyvan 2001, Ch. 3). The *reconstruction* is *not* *adequate* (the *Malament* *objection*), and the *mathematics* *remains* *indispensable*, and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *confirmation*’* *objection* (Maddy, *Naturalism in Mathematics*, 1997; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematical* *entities* *play* *no* *confirmational* *role* (they are the *representational* *devices*, not the *empirical* *posits*), and the *ontological* *commitment* *requires* the *confirmational* *role* (the *“*naturalism”* *critique*), and the *argument* *fails*.

**The *reply*** (Colyvan 2001, Ch. 2; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematical* *entities* *do* *play* the *confirmational* *role* (the *mathematical* *explanations*; the *“*enhanced* *indispensability”* *argument*; Baker, “Mathematical Explanation in Science,” 2009), and the *reply* *grounds* the *commitment* in the *explanation*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *meta*-*induction*’* *objection* (Ch. 40, §5.3). The *history* of *science* *suggests* that the *current* *theories* *are* *false*, and the *mathematical* *entities* *are* *posited* by the *theories*, and the *meta*-*induction* *undermines* the *commitment*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematical* *entities* are *different* (they are *abstract*, *necessary*, *not* *causal*), and the *meta*-*induction* *applies* to the *empirical* *posits*, and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *two*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.4 The fictionalism

**The *thesis*** (Field 1980; Rosen, “Modal Fictionalism,” 1990; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 14, §5). The *mathematical* *statements* *are* *useful* *fictions* (they *are* *not* *literally* *true*), and the *mathematics* is a *useful* *instrument* (the *fictionalist* *insight*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Mathematics} = \text{a *useful* *fiction*} \ (\text{the *statements* are not *literally* *true*})
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematical* *objects* are *epistemically* *inaccessible* (the *Benacerraf* *dilemma*).} \\
2. & \text{The *inaccessible* *entities* *are* *not* *posited* (the *nominalism*).} \\
3. & \text{The *mathematics* is *useful* (the *practice*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *mathematics* is a *useful* *fiction* (the *fictionalism*), and the *statements* are *not* *literally* *true* (the *fictionalist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *avoids* the *access* *problem* (the *fictions* are *not* *posited*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *mathematicians* *use* the *fictions*), and it *grounds* the *utility* in the *fiction*.

**The *objections*** (Ch. 14, §5.2).

**(i) The *‘*no* *attitude*’* *objection*. The *fictionalism* *requires* the *mental* *state* of the *fictionalist* (the *pretence*, the *acceptance*), and the *state* is *not* *available* for the *mathematicians* (the *mathematicians* *believe* the *theorems*, not *pretend*), and the *account* *misdescribes* the *practice*.

**The *reply*** (Field 1980, Ch. 1; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *acceptance* is a *pragmatic* *attitude* (the *commitment* to the *theory*, not the *belief* in the *truth*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *acceptance* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *truth*’* *objection*. The *mathematical* *statements* *seem* *literally* *true* (the *“*2+2=4*”* is *true*), and the *fictionalism* *denies* the *truth*, and the *denial* *conflicts* with the *ordinary* *understanding*.

**The *reply*** (the *fictionalist* *tradition*; the *“*pretense”* *theory*; Walton, *Mimesis as Make-Believe*, 1990; Ch. 49, §4). The *truth* is *the* *truth* *in* the *fiction* (the *“*according* *to* the *story”*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *truth* in the *fiction*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *‘*no* *inference*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *fictions* *cannot* *support* the *inferences* (the *inference* from the *mathematics* to the *world* *requires* the *truth*), and the *application* *is* *not* *explained* (Ch. 39, §5.2).

**The *reply*** (Field 1980, Ch. 2; the *representation* *theorems*). The *inference* is *material* (the *conservativeness* *grounds* the *inference*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *application* in the *conservativeness*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 4.5 The state of the nominalism

**The *assessment***. The *nominalism* is *promising* and *incomplete*, and the *indispensability* *argument* is the *strongest* *argument* *for* the *platonism*, and the *fictionalism* is the *most* *developed* *nominalist* *account*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Field's reconstruction** | The naturalism | The nominalisation |
| **Indispensability** | The science | The access |
| **Fictionalism** | The naturalism | The attitude; the application |
| **Easy road (contemporary)** | The simplicity | The truth |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *anti*-*realism*: the *mathematics* is *not* *literally* *true* (the *fictionalist* *insight*), and the *utility* is *real* (the *representation* *theorem* *insight*), and the *application* is *grounded* in the *structure* (the *structuralist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *indispensability* *argument* is *not* *decisively* *refuted* and the *access* *problem* is *not* *decisively* *resolved*.

## 5. The Epistemology of Mathematics

### 5.1 Gödel and the mathematical intuition

**The *thesis*** (Gödel 1944, 1947; *Collected Works*, Vol. III; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 36, §7.2). The *mathematical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*), and the *intuition* is *analogous* to the *perception* (it *presents* the *mathematical* *objects*), and the *intuition* *grounds* the *axioms*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematical* *axioms* are *not* *derived* from the *logic* (the *Frege* *programme* *fails*).} \\
2. & \text{The *axioms* are *known* *a* *priori* (the *practice*: the *set*-*theoretic* *axioms*, the *large* *cardinals*).} \\
3. & \text{The *a* *priori* *knowledge* *requires* the *faculty* (the *intuition*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *mathematical* *intuition* *grounds* the *axioms* (the *Gödel* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *axioms* in the *intuition*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *mathematicians* *perceive* the *truth* of the *axioms*), and it *avoids* the *conventionalism* (the *axioms* are *not* *arbitrary*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *organ*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematical* *intuition* *has* *no* *sensory* *organ* (the *intuition* is *a* *mystery*), and the *faculty* is *not* *independently* *identified*, and the *account* *is* *obscure*.

**The *reply*** (Gödel 1947; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intuition* is *analogous* to the *perception* (it *is* the *faculty* of the *a* *priori*, and it is *identified* by its *role*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *intuition* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *verification*’* *objection*. The *intuition* *is* *not* *verifiable* (the *different* *mathematicians* *intuit* the *different* *axioms*; the *contemporary* *debate* on the *large* *cardinals*), and the *disagreement* *undermines* the *claim* to *knowledge* (Ch. 9, §6).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *intuition* is *fallible* (the *disagreement* *is* *real*, and the *calibration* is *required*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *fallibility* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

### 5.2 Proof and the surveyability

**The *thesis*** (Tymoczko, “The Four-Color Problem and Its Philosophical Significance,” 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *social* *epistemology* of *mathematics*; Ch. 11, §5). The *proof* is *not* *a* *private* *conviction* (the *Cartesian* *insight*) but a *public* *verification* (the *social* *insight*), and the *surveyability* (the *human* *checkability*) is the *criterion* of the *proof*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *proof* *is* the *public* *verification* (the *social* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *public* *verification* *requires* the *surveyability* (the *human* *checkability*).} \\
3. & \text{The *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* are *not* *surveyable* (the *human* *cannot* *check* the *computation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *are* *not* *proofs* (the *Tymoczko* *insight*), and the *mathematical* *knowledge* *is* *not* *purely* *a* *priori* (the *empirical* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *cases***.

| The *theorem* | The *proof* | The *status* |
|---|---|---|
| **The four-colour theorem** | The *Appel*–*Haken* (1976); the *computer* *check* of 1,936 *configurations* | The *contemporary* *debate* |
| **The Kepler conjecture** | The *Hales* (1998); the *formal* *verification* (2014) | The *formal* *proof* |
| **The classification of finite simple groups** | The *collective* *proof* (the *“*proof* *by* *committee”*), 10,000+ *pages* | The *social* *insight* |
| **The Fermat’s last theorem** | The *Wiles* (1994); the *modularity* *lifting* | The *human* *checkable* |

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *distinction*’* *objection* (Detlefsen & Luker, “The Four-Color Theorem and Mathematical Proof,” 1980; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *proof* is *not* *defined* by the *surveyability* (the *length* *is* a *matter* of *degree*, and the *long* *proofs* *are* *accepted*), and the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *are* *proofs* (the *verification* is *public*, and the *surveyability* is the *ideal*, not the *criterion*).

**The *reply*** (Tymoczko 1979; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *surveyability* is the *criterion* (the *apriority* *requires* the *human* *check*, and the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *introduce* the *empirical* *element*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *criterion* in the *apriority*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *apriority*’* *objection*. The *mathematical* *knowledge* *requires* the *computers* (the *empirical* *insight*), and the *a* *priori* *is* *false* (the *mathematical* *knowledge* is *empirical*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *computers* *do* *not* *verify* the *theorems* (they *perform* the *computations*, and the *computations* *are* *checked* by the *humans*), and the *a* *priori* *is* *compatible* with the *computers* (the *computation* is the *tool*, not the *source*). The *reply* is *defensible* (the *machine*-*checkable* *proof* *is* *a* *proof*, and the *apriority* *is* *the* *ideal*).

### 5.3 The formal verification

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *formal* *proof* *assistants*; the *homotopy* *type* *theory*; the *Lean* *theorem* *prover*). The *formal* *verification* (the *machine*-*checkable* *proof*) *is* the *most* *rigorous* *form* of the *proof*, and the *verification* *grounds* the *certainty*, and the *a* *priori* *is* *realised* in the *machine*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *formal* *verification* *is* the *machine*-*checkable* *proof* (the *Hales* 2014; the *four*-*colour* 2005; the *contemporary* *literature*).} \\
2. & \text{The *verification* *is* *more* *rigorous* than the *informal* *proof* (the *human* *error* *is* *eliminated*). \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *formal* *verification* *is* the *most* *rigorous* *proof* (the *formalist* *insight*), and the *apriority* *is* *realised* in the *machine* (the *contemporary* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *certainty* in the *machine*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *formal* *proofs* *are* *accepted*), and it *avoids* the *human* *error*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *understanding*’* *objection* (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *“*surveyability”* *insight*). The *formal* *verification* *does* not *provide* the *understanding* (the *machine* *checks* the *proof*, and the *human* *does* not *understand* the *why*), and the *understanding* is the *mathematical* *goal* (Ch. 38, §6).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *understanding* is *compatible* with the *formal* *verification* (the *human* *understands* the *statement*, and the *proof* *provides* the *certainty*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *understanding* in the *human* *check*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *apriority*’* *objection*. The *formal* *verification* *requires* the *machine* (the *empirical* *insight*), and the *apriority* *is* *lost*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *machine* is the *tool* (the *mathematical* *content* is *a* *priori*, and the *machine* *realises* the *verification*), and the *reply* *distinguishes* the *tool* from the *content*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *formal* *proof* *is* *a* *priori*, and the *machine* is the *medium*).

### 5.4 The state of the epistemology

**The *assessment***. The *mathematical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *proof*), and the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *introduce* the *empirical* *element*, and the *social* *epistemology* *is* *essential*.

| The *source* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Intuition (Gödel)** | The axioms | The no-organ |
| **Proof (surveyable)** | The apriority | The computer |
| **Formal verification** | The rigor | The understanding |
| **Social** | The practice | The no-apriority |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *a* *priori*: the *mathematical* *knowledge* is *a* *priori* *in* *the* *thin* *sense* (Ch. 36, §5), and the *intuition* is *fallible* (the *Gödel* *insight*), and the *proof* is *surveyable* *in* *principle* (the *Tymoczko* *insight*), and the *formal* *verification* *is* *a* *priori* (the *machine* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *apriority* is *contested* and the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *are* *a* *live* *problem*.

## 6. The Applicability of Mathematics

### 6.1 Wigner's puzzle

**The *problem*** (Wigner 1960; Ch. 9, §7.4; Ch. 39, §4.1). The *mathematics* *developed* *without* the *thought* of the *application* (the *number* *theory*, the *non*-*Euclidean* *geometry*, the *group* *theory*) *turns* *out* to *describe* the *physical* *world* with the *astonishing* *precision*, and the *effectiveness* *is* *unreasonable* (the *mystery*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *mathematics* is *developed* *a* *priori* (the *practice*).} \\
2. & \text{The *mathematics* *describes* the *physical* *world* with the *precision* (the *empirical* *fact*).} \\
3. & \text{The *precision* *requires* an *explanation* (the *why* *question*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *mysterious* (the *Wigner* *puzzle*).}
\end{array}
$$

### 6.2 The responses

**The *responses***.

| The *response* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *instrumentalist*** | The *mathematics* is the *tool* (the *selection* *effect*) | the *pragmatist* *tradition* |
| **The *realist*** | The *world* is *mathematical* (the *mathematical* *realism*) | the *Pythagorean* *tradition* |
| **The *structural*** | The *mathematics* *describes* the *structure* (the *structural* *insight*) | the *structural* *realism* (Ch. 19, §6.3) |
| **The *mapping*** | The *mathematics* is the *representational* *device* (the *mapping* *insight*) | Pincock; the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *instrumentalist* *response***. The *mathematics* is the *tool* (it is *selected* *because* it *works*), and the *effectiveness* is the *selection* *effect* (the *mathematics* *that* does *not* *work* is *discarded*), and the *mystery* *dissolves*.

**The *objection***. The *selection* *presupposes* the *tool* (the *mathematics* is *available* *before* the *selection*), and the *unreasonable* *precision* *remains* (the *a* *priori* *mathematics* *turns* *out* to *fit* *without* the *selection*).

**The *realist* *response***. The *world* is *mathematical* (the *structure* is *real*), and the *mathematics* *describes* the *structure*, and the *fit* is the *structural* *correspondence* (the *structural* *insight*).

**The *objection***. The *world* is *not* the *mathematics* (the *physical* *objects* are *causal*, and the *mathematical* *objects* are *abstract*), and the *correspondence* is *not* the *identity*.

**The *structural* *response*** (Ch. 39, §4.4). The *mathematics* *describes* the *structure* (the *relations* among the *entities*; Ch. 19, §6.3), and the *structure* is *real*, and the *fit* is the *partial* *structural* *correspondence* (the *structure* is *the* *invariant* *content*; the *content* is *multiple*).

**The *mapping* *response*** (Pincock, “A Revealing Exception,” 2004; “A New Perspective on the Problem of Applying Mathematics,” 2004; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *application* is the *mapping* between the *mathematical* *structure* and the *physical* *structure*, and the *mapping* *grounds* the *inference* (the *structure*-*preserving* *mapping*; the *representation* *theorem*).

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *application* in the *structure* *preserving* *mapping*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *models* are the *structures*; Ch. 39, §5), and it *avoids* the *mystery* (the *fit* is the *structural* *correspondence*, not the *coincidence*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *mapping*’* *objection*. The *mapping* is *not* *unique* (the *different* *mathematical* *structures* *can* *map* the *same* *physical* *structure*), and the *choice* is *pragmatic*, and the *mapping* *does* not *explain* the *effectiveness*.

**The *reply*** (Pincock 2004; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mapping* is *constrained* by the *physical* *content* (the *structure* is *the* *invariant*, and the *choice* is *guided* by the *content*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *mapping* in the *structure*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *explanation*’* *objection*. The *mapping* *describes* the *application*, and it *does* not *explain* *why* the *mathematics* is *available* (the *a* *priori* *mathematics* *turns* *out* to *fit*), and the *mystery* *remains*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *mathematics* is *developed* *from* the *structures* (the *mathematicians* *study* the *structures*, and the *structures* *are* the *general* *patterns*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *availability* in the *structural* *generality*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The structural explanation

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 19, §6.3; the *structural* *realism*). The *applicability* is *explained* by the *structural* *realism*: the *mathematics* *describes* the *structure* (the *relations* among the *entities*), and the *structure* is *real*, and the *fit* is the *structural* *correspondence*.

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *physics* *describes* the *structure* (the *relations*; Ch. 19, §1.5).} \\
2. & \text{The *mathematics* *describes* the *structure* (the *patterns*; Ch. 43, §3).} \\
3. & \text{The *structure* is the *common* *content* (the *structural* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *applicability* is *explained* by the *structural* *correspondence* (the *structural* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *explains* the *fit* (the *structure* is *the* *common* *content*), and it *fits* the *structuralism* (§3) and the *structural* *realism* (Ch. 19, §6.3), and it *avoids* the *mystery* (the *fit* is *the* *structural* *correspondence*, not the *coincidence*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *structure*’* *objection*. The *structure* is *not* a *real* *feature* (it is *a* *projection* of the *interests*; Ch. 22, §3), and the *explanation* *collapses*.

**The *reply*** (the *structural* *realism*; Ch. 22, §4.2; Sider 2011). The *structure* is *real* (it is the *fundamental* *description*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *structure* in the *fundamentality*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *uniqueness*’* *objection*. The *structure* *varies* (the *different* *levels*, the *different* *descriptions*; Ch. 21, §8.2), and the *choice* is *interest*-*relative*, and the *explanation* *is* *partial*.

**The *reply*** (the *fundamental* *structure*; Ch. 22, §4.2). The *fundamental* *structure* is *unique* (the *privileged* *description*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *uniqueness* in the *fundamentality*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The state of the applicability debate

**The *assessment***. The *applicability* is *real*, and the *structural* *explanation* is the *most* *defensible*, and the *mystery* *remains* *partial*.

| The *response* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrumentalist** | The selection | The availability |
| **Realist** | The world is mathematical | The abstract/concrete |
| **Structural** | The structural correspondence | The structure is contested |
| **Mapping** | The inference | The no-explanation |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *structural* *realism*: the *applicability* is *explained* by the *structural* *correspondence* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *structure* is *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and the *mapping* *grounds* the *inference* (the *mapping* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *structure* is *not* *unique* and the *mystery* is *not* *fully* *resolved*.

## 7. Set Theory and the Continuum Problem

### 7.1 The independence of CH

**The *continuum* *hypothesis*** (Cantor, 1878).

$$
\mathrm{CH}: \quad 2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1
$$

**The *independence***.

| The *result* | The *content* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **Gödel (1938)** | CH is *consistent* with ZFC (the *constructible* *universe* $L$) | The *no* *refutation* |
| **Cohen (1963)** | CH is *independent* of ZFC (the *forcing*) | The *no* *proof* |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The CH is *independent* of ZFC (the *Gödel*–*Cohen* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *independence* *shows* that the ZFC *does* not *decide* the CH (the *formal* *insight*).} \\
3. & \text{The *decision* *requires* the *new* *axioms* (the *axiomatic* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The CH *requires* the *new* *axioms* (the *Gödel* *insight*; Ch. 43, §7.2), and the *choice* *is* the *philosophical* *question* (the *foundational* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

### 7.2 The new axioms

**The *thesis*** (Gödel, “What Is Cantor’s Continuum Problem?,” 1947; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *set*-*theoretic* *practice*). The *new* *axioms* (the *large* *cardinals*, the *forcing* *axioms*, the *determinacy* *axioms*) *decide* the CH, and the *choice* *is* *justified* by the *intrinsic* and the *extrinsic* *criteria*.

**The *criteria*** (Gödel 1947; the *contemporary* *literature*; Maddy, *Defending the Axioms*, 2011; Koellner, “The Search for New Axioms,” 2013).

| The *criterion* | The *definition* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsic** | The *axiom* is *self*-*evident* (the *intuition*; §5.1) | The *reflection* *principles* |
| **Extrinsic** | The *axiom* *yields* the *fruitful* *consequences* (the *unification*, the *problem*-*solving*) | The *determinacy* axioms |
| **Consequentialist** | The *axiom* *settles* the *questions* (the CH, the *descriptive* *set* *theory*) | The *Martin’s* *maximum* |

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *new* *axioms* *decide* the CH (the *practice*; the *determinacy*, the *forcing* axioms).} \\
2. & \text{The *decision* is *justified* by the *intrinsic* and the *extrinsic* *criteria* (the *Gödel* *insight*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The CH is *decidable* (the *axiomatic* *insight*), and the *decision* *depends* on the *choice* of the *axioms* (the *foundational* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *grounds* the *decision* in the *criteria*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *set* *theorists* *choose* the *axioms*), and it *avoids* the *formalism* (the *axioms* are *not* *arbitrary*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *‘*no* *neutrality*’* *objection*. The *criteria* are *not* *neutral* (the *intrinsic* *criteria* *presuppose* the *intuition*, and the *extrinsic* *criteria* *presuppose* the *values*), and the *choice* is *contested*.

**The *reply*** (Maddy 2011, Ch. 3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *criteria* are *defensible* (the *practice* *provides* the *standards*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *criteria* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *‘*no* *determinacy*’* *objection*. The *new* *axioms* are *not* *unique* (the *different* *axioms* *decide* the CH *differently*; the *forcing* *axioms* *and* the *determinacy* *are* *incompatible*), and the *choice* is *not* *determined*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Woodin, “The Continuum Hypothesis, the Generic-Multiverse of Sets, and the $\Omega$ Conjecture,” 2011; Hamkins, “The Set-Theoretic Multiverse,” 2012). The *choice* is the *choice* of the *universe* (the *multiverse*; the *universe*), and the *reply* *accepts* the *pluralism* (the *multiverse* *insight*) or the *uniqueness* (the *universe* *insight*). The *reply* is *contested*.

### 7.3 The multiverse and the universe

**The *thesis*** (Hamkins 2012; the *multiverse* *view*; the *contemporary* *literature*; the *universe* *view*: Woodin; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *set* *theory* is *not* *about* a *single* *universe* (the *multiverse* *view*), or it *is* *about* a *single* *universe* (the *universe* *view*), and the *choice* *is* the *philosophical* *question*.

**The *multiverse* *view*** (Hamkins 2012, §1). The *set* *theory* *studies* the *multiverse* (the *collection* of the *set*-*theoretic* *universes*), and the *universes* *are* *related* by the *forcing* (the *extensions*), and the *truth* is *relative* to the *universe* (the *relativism*).

**The *universe* *view*** (Woodin; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *set* *theory* *studies* the *single* *universe* (the *V*), and the *new* *axioms* *decide* the *truths* (the *universe* *is* *determined*), and the *truth* is *absolute* (the *realism*).

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *set* *theory* has the *multiverse* (the *forcing* *extensions*) or the *universe* (the *V*).} \\
2. & \text{The *multiverse* *supports* the *relativism* (the *truth* *relative* to the *universe*), and the *universe* *supports* the *realism* (the *truth* *absolute*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *choice* is the *philosophical* *question* (the *foundational* *insight*), and the *realism* and the *relativism* are *both* *defensible* (the *pluralist* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *connects* the *set* *theory* to the *realism* (Ch. 40, §5) and to the *relativism* (Ch. 13, §3), and it *shows* that the *foundational* *questions* are *not* *settled* by the *formalism*.

### 7.4 Determinacy and the large cardinals

**The *thesis*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Martin & Steel, “A Proof of Projective Determinacy,” 1989; the *set*-*theoretic* *practice*). The *determinacy* *axioms* (the *PD*, the *AD* in the *inner* *models*) *settle* the *questions* (the *projective* *sets*, the *regularity* *properties*), and the *large* *cardinals* *provide* the *consistency* *strength*, and the *hierarchy* *grounds* the *justification*.

**The *structure***. The *consistency* *strength* *hierarchy*.

$$
\mathrm{ZFC} \ < \ \mathrm{ZFC} + \text{the *inaccessible*} \ < \ \cdots \ < \ \mathrm{ZFC} + \mathrm{PD} \ < \ \cdots
$$

**The *argument*.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *determinacy* *axioms* *settle* the *questions* (the *projective* *sets*, the *regularity*).} \\
2. & \text{The *large* *cardinals* *provide* the *consistency* *strength* (the *hierarchy*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *determinacy* *and* the *large* *cardinals* *ground* the *justification* (the *structural* *insight*), and the *hierarchy* is the *criterion* of the *acceptability* (the *consistency* *strength* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance***. The *argument* *grounds* the *new* *axioms* in the *structural* *hierarchy*, and it *fits* the *set*-*theoretic* *practice*, and it *supports* the *realism* (the *axioms* are *discovered*, not *invented*).

### 7.5 The state of the set theory

**The *assessment***. The *set* *theory* is the *deepest* *mathematics*, and the *foundational* *questions* are *unresolved*, and the *realism* and the *relativism* are *both* *defensible*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Universe (Woodin)** | The realism | The no-uniqueness |
| **Multiverse (Hamkins)** | The pluralism | The relativism |
| **Determinacy** | The new axioms | The consistency |
| **Formalism** | The practice | The applicability |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *structural* *realism*: the *set* *theory* *studies* the *structures* (the *universes*, the *multiverse*), and the *structures* are *real* (the *realist* *insight*), and the *choice* *depends* on the *criteria* (the *intrinsic*, the *extrinsic*), and the *pluralism* is *defensible* (the *multiverse* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *foundational* *questions* are *unresolved*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *Benacerraf* *dilemma* is *real* and *central*.** The *access* *problem* is *not* *decisively* *resolved*, and the *dilemma* is the *test* *case* for the *naturalisation* of *knowledge*.
- **The *logicism* is *revived* in the *neo*-*logicist* form.** The *Frege’s* *theorem* is *correct*, and the *programme* *rests* on the *HP*, and the *Bad Company* and the *Caesar* are *open*.
- **The *formalism* is *refuted* as the *complete* *account*.** The *Gödel* *theorems* *defeat* the *Hilbert’s* *programme*, and the *formalism* *survives* as a *partial* *account*.
- **The *intuitionism* is *live*.** The *constructive* *mathematics* *is* *viable*, and the *intuitionist* *logic* *is* *rigorous*, and the *cost* is the *loss* of the *classical* *results*.
- **The *structuralism* is the *most* *influential* *contemporary* *account*.** The *Benacerraf* *arbitrariness* and the *category* *theory* *support* the *view*, and the *access* and the *no*-*objects* *objections* *remain*.
- **The *indispensability* *argument* is the *strongest* *argument* *for* the *platonism*.** The *quine*–*putnam* *argument* is *not* *decisively* *refuted*, and the *nominalist* *reconstruction* is *contested*.
- **The *applicability* is *real* but *not* *miraculous*.** The *structural* *realism* *explains* the *fit*, and the *mystery* *remains* *partial*.
- **The *set* *theory* *is* *independent* of the ZFC.** The CH *is* *independent*, and the *new* *axioms* *decide* it, and the *multiverse* and the *universe* *views* are *contested*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *access* to the *abstract* *objects*.** The *Gödel* *intuition*, the *structural* *access*, the *abduction*, the *a* *priori*.
- **The *Nature* of the *mathematical* *objects*.** The *platonism*, the *structuralism*, the *nominalism*, the *fictionalism*.
- **The *indispensability*.** The *Field* *reconstruction*, the *enhanced* *indispensability*, the *naturalism*.
- **The *epistemology* of the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs*.** The *surveyability*, the *apriority*, the *formal* *verification*.
- **The *applicability*.** The *Wigner* *puzzle*, the *structural* *explanation*, the *mapping*.
- **The *foundations*.** The *CH*, the *new* *axioms*, the *multiverse*.

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a case study in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinctions* between the *platonism*, the *nominalism*, the *structuralism*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — the *Benacerraf* *sets*, the *Field* *reconstruction*, the *grue* *emeralds*, the *four*-*colour* *theorem* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *second*-*order* *logic*, the *BHK* *semantics*, the *forcing*, the *category* *theory* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the *method* by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are *adjusted*.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to *hold* the *question* *open*.

The honest verdict: the philosophy of mathematics is a field in transition. The classical positions are untenable in their pure forms; the contemporary positions are promising but incomplete; the debate is unresolved. The unresolved character is instructive: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a settled answer, but a sharper understanding of what the question is.

## Summary

- **The *platonism*** (the *abstract* *objects* *exist*) is the *most* *intuitive* *account*, and it *faces* the *Benacerraf* *dilemma* (the *semantics* *requires* the *abstract* *objects*, and the *epistemology* *requires* the *causal* *connection*).
- **The *Benacerraf* *identity* *argument*** (the *von* *Neumann*/*Zermelo* *arbitrariness*) *supports* the *structuralism*: the *numbers* are the *positions* in the *structures*, not the *sets*.
- **The *logicism*** (Frege, Russell) is *defeated* by the *Russell’s* *paradox* and *revived* in the *neo*-*logicist* form (the *Frege’s* *theorem*, the *HP*), and the *Bad Company* and the *Caesar* are *open*.
- **The *formalism*** (Hilbert) is *defeated* by the *Gödel* *incompleteness*, and it *survives* as a *partial* *account* (the *relative* *consistency*, the *deductivism*).
- **The *intuitionism*** (Brouwer) *rejects* the *excluded* *middle* (the *existence* *requires* the *construction*), and it *faces* the *cost* of the *classical* *loss*.
- **The *structuralism*** (Shapiro, Resnik, Hellman) is the *most* *influential* *account*, and it *faces* the *access* and the *no*-*objects* *objections*.
- **The *nominalism*** (Field) *reconstructs* the *physics* *without* the *mathematics*, and the *indispensability* *argument* is the *strongest* *argument* *for* the *platonism*, and the *fictionalism* is the *most* *developed* *nominalist* *account*.
- **The *epistemology*** is *a* *priori* (the *intuition*, the *proof*), and the *computer*-*assisted* *proofs* *introduce* the *empirical* *element* (the *surveyability*), and the *formal* *verification* *is* the *most* *rigorous* *form*.
- **The *applicability*** (Wigner) is *mysterious*, and the *structural* *realism* is the *most* *defensible* *explanation* (the *structural* *correspondence*).
- **The *set* *theory*** is the *deepest* *mathematics*, and the CH *is* *independent* of the ZFC, and the *new* *axioms* *decide* it, and the *multiverse* and the *universe* *views* are *contested*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *structural* *platonism*: the *structures* are *real* (the *realist* *insight*), the *positions* are *constituted* by the *relations* (the *structural* *insight*), the *access* is *abductive* (the *indispensability* *insight*), and the *applicability* is *structural* (the *structural* *realism* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *access* *problem*, the *indispensability*, and the *applicability* *remain* *unresolved*.
