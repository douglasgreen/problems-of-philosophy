# Chapter 45 — Normative Ethics: The Good, the Right, the Virtuous

> “The creed which accepts as the foundation of morals, Utility, or the Greatest Happiness Principle, holds that actions are right in proportion as they tend to promote happiness, wrong as they tend to produce the reverse of happiness.”
> — J. S. Mill, *Utilitarianism* (1861), Ch. 2

> “Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end.”
> — Immanuel Kant, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785), 4:429

> “Virtue, then, is a state of character concerned with choice, lying in a mean, i.e. the mean relative to us, this being determined by a rational principle, and by that principle by which the person of practical wisdom would determine it.”
> — Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* (c. 340 BCE), II.6, 1106b36–1107a2

> “Utilitarianism does not take seriously the distinction between persons.”
> — John Rawls, *A Theory of Justice* (1971), §5

---

Chapter 44 examined the *nature* of the moral judgment — its *semantics*, its *metaphysics*, its *epistemology*. This chapter examines its *content*. The *metaethical* question is *what it is* to *judge* that *something* is *right*; the *normative* question is *what* is *right*, and *why*.

The *distinction* is *real* but *permeable*. The *metaethical* position *constrains* the *normative* theory: the *expressivist* cannot *offer* the *realist foundations* (Ch. 44, §3.2); the *non-naturalist* must *defend* the *moral facts* (Ch. 44, §2.4); the *constructivist* must *specify* the *procedure* (Ch. 44, §4). And the *normative* theory *tests* the *metaethical* position: a *theory* that *yields* the *determinate* *norms* for the *hard cases* is *evidence* for the *moral* *facts* (the *reflective equilibrium*; §5.3); a *theory* that *collapses* into the *intuition* is *evidence* against them.

The *chapter* proceeds in *seven* *movements*. §1 examines the *consequentialism*. §2 examines the *deontology*. §3 examines the *virtue ethics*. §4 examines the *contractualism*. §5 examines the *particularism*, the *pluralism*, and the *reflective equilibrium*. §6 examines the *hard cases* (the *trolley*, the *demandingness*, the *integrity*, the *moral luck*). §7 asks *what* a *moral theory* is *for*. §8 gives the *verdict*.

## 1. Consequentialism

### 1.1 The structure

**The *thesis***. The *rightness* of an *action* is *determined* by the *goodness* of its *consequences*, and the *goodness* is *agent*-*neutral* (the *good* is *the same* from *every* *perspective*), and the *right* action is the one that *maximizes* the *good* (the *optimizing* structure; the *satisficing* and the *scalar* variants are *qualifications*; §1.3).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Act } A \text{ is right} \iff A \text{ maximizes the *impersonal* good (actual or expected)}
$$

**The *three* *components***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *contested* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *value* theory** | What is *good* (the *hedonism*, the *desire*, the *objective* *list*) | The *good* (§1.2; Ch. 48, §1) |
| **The *maximizing* *structure*** | The *right* action *maximizes* the *good* | The *optimizing* vs. the *satisficing* (§1.3) |
| **The *agent*-*neutrality* | The *good* is *impersonal* (the *equal* *weight*) | The *separateness* of the *persons* (§1.6) |

**The *varieties***.

| The *variety* | The *focus* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Act-consequentialism** | The *individual* *act* | Bentham; Sidgwick; Singer |
| **Rule-consequentialism** | The *rule* (the *ideal* *code*) | Hooker; Brandt |
| **Motive-consequentialism** | The *motive* (the *character*) | the *contemporary* *literature* |
| **Scalar consequentialism** | The *degree* of the *good* (the *no* *dichotomy*) | Norcross; the *contemporary* *literature* |

**The *significance*.** The *varieties* *divide* on the *object* of the *evaluation* (the *act*, the *rule*, the *motive*, the *degree*), and the *choice* *depends* on the *theory* of the *action* (Ch. 27, §1) and on the *theory* of the *value* (Ch. 48, §1).

### 1.2 Utilitarianism and the value theory

**The *thesis*** (Bentham, *An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation*, 1789, Ch. 1; Mill 1861; Sidgwick, *The Methods of Ethics*, 1874, Bk. IV). The *good* is *happiness* (the *pleasure*, the *absence* of *pain*; the *hedonism*), and the *right* action *maximizes* the *happiness* of the *greatest* *number* (the *utilitarian* *insight*).

**The *structure***.

$$
U(A) = \sum_{i \in \text{persons}} \big( \text{pleasure}_i(A) - \text{pain}_i(A) \big), \qquad A \text{ is right} \iff U(A) \text{ is maximal}
$$

**The *arguments*** (Mill 1861, Ch. 4; Sidgwick 1874, Bk. IV, Ch. 1–2).

**(i) The *argument* from the *impartiality***. The *moral* *point* of *view* is *impersonal* (the *equal* *concern*), and the *impartial* *concern* *aggregates* the *interests*, and the *aggregation* *requires* the *utilitarian* *calculus*.

**(ii) The *argument* from the *intuition***. The *fundamental* *moral* *intention* is the *reduction* of the *suffering* (the *humanitarian* *insight*), and the *reduction* *requires* the *aggregation*, and the *aggregation* *yields* the *utilitarianism*.

**(iii) The *argument* from the *simplicity***. The *utilitarianism* *offers* a *single* *principle* (the *simplicity*; Ch. 4, §3), and the *single* *principle* *explains* the *moral* *judgments* (the *explanatory* *power*), and the *simplicity* is *evidence* for the *theory*.

**The *problem* of the *value* *theory***. The *hedonism* *faces* the *experience* *machine* (Nozick, *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, 1974, pp. 42–45; Ch. 48, §2), the *desire* *theory* *faces* the *adaptive* *preferences* (the *sour* *grapes*; Ch. 48, §1), and the *objective* *list* *faces* the *paternalism* *objection* (Ch. 48, §1). The *utilitarian* *calculus* *presupposes* the *cardinal* *comparability* of the *interests* (the *interpersonal* *comparison*), and the *comparison* *is* *contested* (Ch. 10, §6).

### 1.3 The demandingness objection

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Singer, “Famine, Affluence, and Morality,” 1972; the *contemporary* *literature*; Williams, “A Critique of Utilitarianism,” 1973).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *utilitarianism* *requires* the *maximization* of the *impersonal* *good*} \ (\S 1.1). \\
2. & \text{The *maximization* *requires* the *sacrifice* of the *agent*'s *projects*, *interests*, and *relationships* *whenever* the *sacrifice* *increases* the *aggregate*. \\
3. & \text{The *universal* *sacrifice* is *psychologically* *impossible* and *morally* *repugnant* (the *alienation*; the *integrity*; the *supererogation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *utilitarianism* *misdescribes* the *moral* *life* (the *demandingness* *objection*), and the *unrestricted* *maximization* is *false*}.
\end{array}
$$

**The *phenomenon***. Singer’s *pond* (*op. cit.*): the *child* *drowning* *nearby*, and the *shallow* *pond*, and the *ruined* *shoes*. The *principle*: if it is in *our* *power* to *prevent* *something* *bad* *without* *sacrificing* *anything* *of* *comparable* *moral* *importance*, *we* *ought* to *do* it. The *principle* *applies* *globally* (the *famine*, the *disease*, the *poverty*), and the *conclusion* is the *radical* *demandingness* (the *duty* to *give* *until* *the* *marginal* *utility* *equalizes*).

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *two-level* *view*** (Hare, *Moral Thinking*, 1981, Ch. 2; Railton, “Alienation, Consequentialism, and the Demands of Morality,” 1984). The *morality* *operates* at *two* *levels* (the *critical* level: the *utilitarian* *justification*; the *intuitive* level: the *ordinary* *norms*, the *dispositions*, the *relationships*), and the *critical* *level* *justifies* the *intuitive* *level*, and the *agent* *acts* on the *intuitive* *level* (the *sophisticated* *consequentialism*; the *divided* *self*; the *alienation* *avoided*).

**The *objection***. The *two-level* *view* is *unstable* (the *critical* *level* *can* *override* the *intuitive* *level* at *any* *time*, and the *agent* *who* *knows* the *critical* *level* *cannot* *avoid* the *alienation*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *satisficing* *view*** (Slote, *Common-Sense Morality and Consequentialism*, 1985, Ch. 3). The *right* action *achieves* *enough* (the *satisficing*), not the *maximum*, and the *demandingness* *is* *reduced* (the *good* *enough* *threshold*).

**The *objection***. The *satisficing* *threshold* is *arbitrary* (why *this* *level*?), and the *view* *collapses* into the *maximizing* (the *indifference* *to* the *good* *above* the *threshold*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *prioritarian* *view*** (Parfit, “Equality and Priority,” 1997; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *priority* *to* the *worse* *off* *restricts* the *demandingness* (the *gains* to the *worse* *off* *matter* *more*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *restriction* in the *distribution*.

**The *objection***. The *prioritarianism* *is* *not* *enough* (the *demandingness* *remains*), and the *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.4 Aggregation

**The *themes***.

| The *principle* | The *formula* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Total** | $\sum_i u_i$ | The *repugnant* *conclusion* (Parfit) |
| **Average** | $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i u_i$ | The *mere* *addition* *paradox* (Parfit) |
| **Prioritarian** | $\sum_i f(u_i)$, $f$ concave | The *measurement* of the *priority* |
| **Sufficientarian** | The *threshold* *guaranteed* | The *threshold* is *arbitrary* |

**The *repugnant* *conclusion*** (Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, 1984, Ch. 17, §3). For *any* *population* living at a *high* *welfare* *level*, there is a *much* *larger* *population* living *barely* *above* the *zero* *level* *whose* *total* *welfare* is *greater* (the *same* *total*, *spread* *thinly*). The *total* *view* *concludes* that the *second* *population* is *better*, and the *conclusion* is *repugnant* (the *lives* *are* *barely* *worth* *living*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{For any population } P \text{ at the high level, there is a larger } P' \text{ at the barely-above-zero level with a greater total.} \\
2. & \text{The total view ranks } P' \text{ above } P. \\
3. & \text{The life in } P' \text{ is barely worth living (the repugnance).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The total view is false (the repugnance), and the aggregation requires a constraint (the variable value).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***. The *variable* *value* *theories* (the *life* *improves* the *welfare* *more* when the *welfare* is *higher*; the *perfectionism*), the *average* *view* (the *mere* *addition* *paradox*), the *critical*-*level* *view* (the *lives* *below* the *critical* *level* *count* *negatively*), the *intuitionist* *refusal* (the *threshold*; the *refusal* *to* *aggregate* *across* the *persons*; the *Rawlsian* *separateness*). The *debate* is *active* (Ch. 48, *optional* *threads*).

**The *significance*.** The *aggregation* *is* the *hard* *problem* of the *consequentialism*, and it *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *population* *ethics* (Ch. 48) and to the *public* *policy* (Ch. 47, §3).

### 1.5 Rule-consequentialism

**The *thesis*** (Brandt, “Toward a Credible Form of Utilitarianism,” 1963; Hooker, *Ideal Code, Real World*, 2000). The *right* action is the one *conforming* to the *rules* (the *ideal* *code*) *whose* *general* *acceptance* *maximizes* the *good*, and the *rules* *are* *justified* by their *consequences*, not by their *intrinsic* *rightness*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Act } A \text{ is right} \iff A \text{ conforms to the code } C \text{ whose *universal* *acceptance* maximizes the good}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *act*-*consequentialism* *demands* the *sacrifice* *that* *no* *one* *can* *bear*} \ (\S 1.3). \\
2. & \text{The *general* *acceptance* of the *rules* (the *promise*-*keeping*, the *property*, the *care*) *maximizes* the *good* (the *coordination*; the *trust*). \\
3. & \text{The *rules* *secure* the *coordination* and *restrict* the *demandingness* (the *two-level* *insight*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *right* action is the *rule*-*conforming* *action* (the *rule*-*consequentialism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *account* *answers* the *demandingness* *objection* (the *rules* *permit* the *personal* *projects*), and it *explains* the *ordinary* *morality* (the *promises*, the *property*, the *care*), and it *preserves* the *consequentialist* *foundation* (the *maximization*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *collapse* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *classic* *critique*). The *rule*-*consequentialism* *collapses* into the *act*-*consequentialism* (when the *rule*-*breaking* *maximizes* the *good*, the *theory* *must* *permit* the *breaking*, and the *rule* *is* *abandoned*), or into the *rule*-*worship* (when the *rule*-*breaking* *is* *forbidden* *despite* the *maximization*, the *rule* is the *intrinsic* *value*, and the *consequentialism* is *abandoned*).

**The *reply*** (Hooker 2000, Ch. 4). The *rules* are *justified* by their *general* *acceptance*, and the *exceptions* are *internal* to the *rule* (the *rule* *forbids* the *breaking*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *stability* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *rule* *is* *either* *exceptionless* *or* *exception*-*ridden*; the *dilemma* *remains*).

**(ii) The *no*-*determinacy* *objection*. The *ideal* *code* *cannot* be *specified* (the *complexity* of the *society*, the *uncertainty*, the *different* *codes*), and the *theory* *does* not *deliver* the *determinate* *rules*.

**The *reply*** (Hooker 2000, Ch. 3). The *ideal* *code* is the *approximation* (the *empirical* *investigation*, the *fallible* *estimate*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *determinacy* in the *science*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 1.6 The state of the consequentialism

**The *assessment***. The *consequentialism* is *defensible* in the *two-level*, *rule*, and *prioritarian* forms, and the *demandingness*, the *aggregation*, and the *separateness* *objections* are *serious*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Act** | The *simplicity*; the *clarity* | The *demandingness*; the *separateness* |
| **Two-level** | The *alienation* avoided | The *instability* |
| **Rule** | The *coordination* | The *collapse* |
| **Satisficing** | The *good enough* | The *arbitrary threshold* |
| **Prioritarian** | The *worse off* | The *demandingness remains* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *two-level* *prioritarianism*: the *maximization* of the *prioritarian* *good* (the *weight* to the *worse* *off*), *combined* with the *intuitive* *level* of the *rules* and the *dispositions* (the *two-level* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *demandingness* and the *aggregation* *remain* *contested*.

## 2. Deontology

### 2.1 The structure

**The *thesis*** (Kant, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*, 1785; *Critique of Practical Reason*, 1788; *The Metaphysics of Morals*, 1797; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *rightness* of an *action* is *not* *determined* *solely* by the *consequences*, and the *moral* *law* *imposes* *constraints* (the *prohibitions*) that *cannot* be *overridden* by the *aggregate* *good*, and the *moral* *worth* is *grounded* in the *duty* and the *motive*, not in the *outcome*.

**The *three* *components***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Constraints** | The *prohibitions* (the *killing*, the *lying*, the *coercion*) | The *negative* *duties* |
| **Options** | The *permissions* (the *personal* *projects*, the *partiality*) | The *supererogation* |
| **Special obligations** | The *duties* *grounded* in the *relationships* | The *promises*, the *care* |

**The *significance*.** The *components* *define* the *deontological* *structure*, and the *tension* with the *consequentialism* is the *tension* between the *agent*-*relative* *constraints* and the *impersonal* *maximization* (the *separateness* *of* the *persons*; Rawls 1971, §5).

### 2.2 The categorical imperative

**The *formulae*** (Kant 1785, 4:421–4:436).

**(i) The *Formula* of *Universal* *Law* (FUL)**.

$$
\text{Act only on that maxim through which you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law}
$$

**(ii) The *Formula* of *Humanity* (FH)**.

$$
\text{Act so as to use humanity, in your own person or in the person of another,} \\
\text{always at the same time as an end, never merely as a means}
$$

**(iii) The *Formula* of the *Kingdom* of *Ends* (FKE)**.

$$
\text{Act according to the maxims of a member giving universal law in a merely possible kingdom of ends}
$$

**(iv) The *Formula* of *Autonomy* (FA)**.

$$
\text{Act so that the will can regard itself as at the same time giving universal law through its maxims}
$$

**The *procedure***. The *maxim* (the *subjective* *principle* of the *action*: the *act*, the *circumstance*, the *end*) is *universalized*, and the *universalization* *yields* a *contradiction* (the *contradiction* *in* the *conception*: the *maxim* *cannot* be *conceived* as a *universal* *law*; the *contradiction* *in* the *will*: the *maxim* *cannot* be *willed* as a *universal* *law*), and the *contradiction* *marks* the *impermissibility*.

**The *examples*** (Kant 1785, 4:421–4:423). The *false* *promise* (the *contradiction* in the *conception*: the *universal* *practice* *destroys* the *institution* of the *promise*, and the *maxim* is *self*-*defeating*), the *suicide* (the *contradiction* in the *will*), the *indifference* to the *others* (the *contradiction* in the *will*), the *beneficence* (the *universalizable*).

**The *significance*.** The *examples* *show* the *procedure* *at* *work*, and the *interpretation* *shows* the *controversy* (the *universalization* *tests* the *consistency*, the *kinds* of *contradiction*, the *relation* between the *formulae*).

**The *derivation* *question***. Do the *formulae* *derive* from *each* *other* (the *equivalence* *thesis*), or are they *distinct* *tests* (the *plurality* *thesis*)? The *equivalence* *thesis* *faces* the *empty* *formalism* (§2.3); the *plurality* *thesis* *faces* the *inconsistency* (the *formulae* *can* *divergence*).

### 2.3 The defects

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *empty* *formalism*** (Hegel, *Philosophy of Right*, 1821, §135; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *FUL* is *empty* (it *does* not *yield* the *determinate* *norms*; the *different* *maxims* *can* be *universalized*; the *content* *comes* from the *empirical* *facts*, not from the *form*).

**The *reply*** (Korsgaard, *Creating the Kingdom of Ends*, 1996, Ch. 3; O’Neill, *Constructions of Reason*, 1989, Ch. 5). The *FUL* *yields* the *norms* *when* *combined* with the *empirical* *premises* (the *contradiction* in the *conception*, the *social* *practice*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *norms* in the *procedure* + the *facts*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *rigorism* (the *conflict* of the *duties)*** (Constant, “Des réactions politiques,” 1797; Kant, “On a Supposed Right to Lie from Philanthropy,” 1797). The *duty* *forbids* the *lying* *to* the *murderer* *at* the *door*, and the *rigorism* *is* *morally* *repugnant* (the *lying* *saves* the *life*; the *refusal* is the *monstrous* *consequence*).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *priority* *of the *duties*; the *specification* *view*; the *threshold* *deontology*). The *duty* *not* *to* *lie* is *specified* (the *assertion* *to* the *murderer* is *not* the *lie*; the *self*-*defense*; the *threshold*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *exception* in the *specification*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *specification* *is* *ad hoc*; the *threshold* *collapses* into the *consequentialism*).

**(iii) The *conflict* of the *duties***. The *moral* *norms* *conflict* (the *promise* *vs.* the *rescue*), and the *deontology* *cannot* *resolve* the *conflict* (the *no* *priority*), and the *dilemmas* *are* *real* (the *tragic* *choices*; §5.2).

**The *reply*** (Ross 1930, Ch. 2; the *prima facie* *duties*; §2.4). The *duties* are *prima facie* (they *bind* *unless* *overridden*), and the *conflict* *resolves* *by* the *weight* (the *judgment*, the *phronesis*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *resolution* in the *practical* *wisdom*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *residue*: the *regret* *for* the *overridden* *duty*; the *tragic* *choice*).

### 2.4 The contemporary deontology

**The *varieties***.

**(i) The *Rossian* *prima facie* *duties*** (Ross, *The Right and the Good*, 1930, Ch. 2). The *duties* (the *fidelity*, the *gratitude*, the *justice*, the *beneficence*, the *self*-*improvement*, the *non*-*maleficence*) are *prima facie* (the *binding* *unless* *overridden*), and the *actual* *duty* is the *result* of the *weighing* (the *judgment*), and there is *no* *single* *principle* (the *pluralism*).

**(ii) The *threshold* *deontology*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *constraints* are *absolute* *below* the *threshold* (the *catastrophic* *consequences*), and *overridden* *above* the *threshold*, and the *threshold* *avoids* the *rigorism*.

**(iii) The *specificationist* *view*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Richardson, “Specifying Norms as a Way to Resolve Concrete Ethical Problems,” 1990). The *norms* are *specified* (the *more* *determinate* *content*), and the *specification* *resolves* the *conflict* (the *lying* *to* the *murderer*), and the *norms* *remain* *deontological* (the *constraints*).

**(iv) The *non*-*consequentialist* *principles*** (Kamm, *Morality, Mortality*, 1993–96; Scanlon 1998, Ch. 2). The *principles* (the *harm*, the *intention*, the *causal* *structure*) *explain* the *moral* *judgments* (the *trolley*; §6.1), and the *principles* *are* *not* *reduced* to the *aggregation*.

**(v) The *doctrine* of *double* *effect*** (Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, II-II, q. 64, a. 7; the *contemporary* *literature*; Ch. 51, §1). The *action* *with* a *good* *effect* and a *bad* *effect* is *permissible* *if* (i) the *action* is *good* or *indifferent*, (ii) the *bad* *effect* *is* *not* *intended* (the *foreseen* *only*), (iii) the *bad* *effect* *is* *not* the *means* to the *good*, (iv) the *good* *is* *proportionate* to the *bad*.

**The *significance*.** The *varieties* *show* the *deontology* *adaptive* (the *prima facie*, the *threshold*, the *specification*), and the *doctrine* of the *double* *effect* *connects* the *deontology* to the *hard* *cases* (§6).

### 2.5 The state of the deontology

**The *assessment***. The *deontology* is *defensible* in the *Rossian*, *threshold*, and *specification* forms, and the *empty* *formalism* and the *rigorism* *objections* are *serious*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Kantian** | The *autonomy*; the *dignity* | The *empty* *formalism*; the *rigorism* |
| **Rossian** | The *pluralism*; the *residue* | The *no* *priority* |
| **Threshold** | The *catastrophe* avoided | The *arbitrary* *threshold* |
| **Specification** | The *determinacy* | The *ad hoc* |
| **Double effect** | The *intention* | The *foresight* *problem* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Rossian* *deontology*: the *plural* *prima facie* *duties* (the *constraints*, the *options*, the *special* *obligations*), *combined* with the *threshold* *exception* (the *catastrophe*), *specified* by the *judgment* (the *phronesis*), and *grounded* in the *dignity* (the *Kantian* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *priority* of the *duties* and the *threshold* *remain* *contested*.

## 3. Virtue Ethics

### 3.1 The structure

**The *thesis*** (Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, c. 340 BCE, Bks. I–II, VI; Anscombe, “Modern Moral Philosophy,” 1958; Foot, *Natural Goodness*, 2001; MacIntyre, *After Virtue*, 1981; Hursthouse, *On Virtue Ethics*, 1999). The *primary* *moral* *concept* is the *virtue* (the *character*), not the *act* or the *rule*, and the *good* *life* is the *eudaimonia* (the *flourishing*), and the *virtuous* *person* *perceives* the *situation* *correctly* and *acts* *from* the *established* *character*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Virtue} \ v \ \text{is a *stable* *state* of *character*} \ (\text{a *hexis*}) \ \text{that *disposes* the agent to *feel*, *choose*, and *act* *well*}
$$

**The *components***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Eudaimonia** | The *flourishing* is the *highest* *good* | Aristotle |
| **Aretē** | The *virtues* are the *excellences* of the *character* | Aristotle; the *contemporary* |
| **Phronesis** | The *practical* *wisdom* *perceives* the *particular* | Aristotle; McDowell |
| **The *mean*** | The *virtue* *lies* between the *excess* and the *defect* | Aristotle |

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *morality* *is* *primarily* *about* the *agent* (the *character*, the *life*), not the *isolated* *act*} \ (\text{the *aretaic* *insight}). \\
2. & \text{The *act* *is* *right* *if* it is *what* the *virtuous* *person* would *do* (the *v*-*rules*). \\
3. & \text{The *virtue* *is* *prior* to the *act* (the *aretaic* *primacy*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *virtue* *ethics* *grounds* the *morality* in the *character* (the *aretaic* *insight}).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *virtue* *ethics* *captures* the *character* (the *integrity*, the *emotion*, the *perception*), and it *answers* the *demandingness* *objection* (the *good* *life* *is* the *virtuous* *life*), and it *avoids* the *rule*-*worship* (the *particular* *judgment*).

### 3.2 The neo-Aristotelianism

**The *thesis*** (Anscombe 1958; Foot 2001; MacIntyre 1981; Hursthouse 1999; Annas, *The Morality of Happiness*, 1993; *Intelligent Virtue*, 2011).

**Anscombe’s *argument*** (1958, §§1–3). The *modern* *moral* *philosophy* *presupposes* the *law* *conception* (the *obligation*, the *duty*, the *guilt*), and the *conception* *derives* from the *divine* *legislator*, and the *loss* of the *legislator* *leaves* the *law* *conception* *without* a *foundation* (the *“*the* *moral* *ought*”* *is* a *survival*), and the *return* to the *virtues* *restores* the *foundation*.

**Foot’s *argument*** (2001, Ch. 1–3). The *natural* *goodness* *is* the *functional* *excellence* (the *eye* *is* *good* if it *sees* well; the *wolf* is *good* if it *hunts* well), and the *human* *goodness* *is* the *excellence* of the *human* *life* (the *practical* *rationality*), and the *moral* *evaluation* *is* the *natural* *evaluation* (the *naturalism* *insight*).

**Hursthouse’s *argument*** (1999, Ch. 2). The *v*-*rules* (“*do* what the *virtuous* *would* *do*”; “*do* not *lie*, *because* the *honest* *would* *not*”) *ground* the *right* *action* in the *virtue*, and the *rules* *are* *derived* from the *virtue*, and the *derivation* *answers* the *action*-*guidance* *objection*.

**The *significance*.** The *neo*-*Aristotelianism* *connects* the *virtue* *ethics* to the *naturalism* (Ch. 39, §3.2) and to the *normative* *epistemology* (the *perception*; Ch. 44, §6.2), and it *grounds* the *character* in the *human* *nature*.

### 3.3 The problem of action-guidance

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *classic* *objection*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *virtue* *ethics* *does* not *guide* the *action* (the *v*-*rules* are *circular*; the *“*do* what the *virtuous* *would* *do*”* *presupposes* the *independent* *specification* of the *virtue*, and the *specification* *requires* the *principles* that the *virtue* *ethics* *rejects*), and the *theory* *is* *empty* for the *deliberation*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *v*-*rules* *require* the *independent* *specification* of the *virtuous* *person* (the *criterion*). \\
2. & \text{The *criterion* *requires* the *principles* (the *determinate* *norms*). \\
3. & \text{The *virtue* *ethics* *denies* the *principles* (the *particularism*; §5.1).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *virtue* *ethics* *cannot* *guide* the *action* (the *guidance* *objection*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *reply*** (Hursthouse 1999, Ch. 2–3; Annas 2011, Ch. 1). The *virtue* *ethics* *guides* *through* the *perception* (the *phronesis*; the *virtuous* *person* *sees* *what* *is* *required*), and the *guidance* *is* *not* the *rule*-*application* but the *judgment* (the *practical* *wisdom*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *guidance* in the *perception*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *perception* *requires* the *trained* *agent*, and the *training* *presupposes* the *norms*, and the *circularity* *remains*).

### 3.4 The situationist challenge

**The *thesis*** (Doris, *Lack of Character*, 2002; Harman, “Moral Philosophy Meets Social Psychology,” 1999; the *empirical* *literature*; Milgram, *Obedience to Authority*, 1974; Darley & Batson, “From Jerusalem to Jericho,” 1973).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *virtue* *ethics* *presupposes* the *robust* *character* *traits* (the *cross*-*situational* *consistency* of the *behavior*).} \\
2. & \text{The *empirical* *psychology* *shows* that the *situation* *predicts* the *behavior* *far* *better* than the *character* (the *Milgram* *obedience*, the *Good* *Samaritan* *study*).} \\
3. & \text{The *robust* *traits* *do* not *exist* (the *situationist* *conclusion*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *virtue* *ethics* *is* *empirically* *unsupported* (the *situationist* *objection*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *evidence***.

| The *study* | The *finding* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **Milgram (1974)** | The *65%* *obedience* to the *authority* | The *situation* *overrides* the *character* |
| **Darley & Batson (1973)** | The *hurried* *seminarians* *do* not *help* (the *10%* vs. the *63%*) | The *time* *pressure* *overrides* the *character* |
| **Isen & Levin (1972)** | The *small* *gift* (the *dime*) *increases* the *helping* | The *trivial* *situation* *overrides* the *character* |

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *robust* vs. the *local* *traits*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *virtue* *ethics* *requires* only the *local* *traits* (the *domain*-*specific* *dispositions*), not the *global* *traits*, and the *situationism* *refutes* the *global* *traits* (the *straw* *man*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *local* *traits*.

**The *reply*** is *defensible* (the *local* *traits* *are* *empirically* *supported*; the *virtue* *is* *the* *skill*; Annas 2011, Ch. 3).

**(ii) The *skill* *model*** (Annas 2011; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *virtue* *is* *the* *expertise* (the *practice*, the *training*, the *habituation*), and the *expertise* *is* *robust* (the *expert* *transcends* the *situation*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *virtue* in the *expertise*.

**The *reply*** is *defensible* (the *expert* *is* *less* *situation*-*sensitive*; the *empirical* *literature* *supports* the *expertise*).

**(iii) The *methodological* *reply***. The *situationism* is a *methodological* *challenge* (the *empirical* *evidence* *should* *inform* the *virtue* *theory*), not a *refutation*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *virtue* in the *science* (the *naturalism*).

**The *reply*** is *defensible* (the *virtue* *theory* *is* *revised* by the *evidence*).

### 3.5 The state of the virtue ethics

**The *assessment***. The *virtue* *ethics* is *defensible* in the *neo*-*Aristotelian* form, and the *action*-*guidance* and the *situationist* *objections* are *serious*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristotelian** | The *flourishing* | The *action* *guidance* |
| **Foot** | The *naturalism* | The *no* *normativity* |
| **Hursthouse** | The *v*-*rules* | The *circularity* |
| **Skill** | The *expertise* | The *elitism* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *neo*-*Aristotelianism*: the *virtue* *is* the *stable* *state* of the *character* (the *aretaic* *insight*), *grounded* in the *human* *flourishing* (the *eudaimonistic* *insight*), *perceived* by the *practical* *wisdom* (the *phronesis* *insight*), *informed* by the *empirical* *psychology* (the *situationist* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *action*-*guidance* and the *situationism* *remain* *contested*.

## 4. Contractualism

### 4.1 The structure

**The *thesis*** (Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, 1998, Ch. 5; Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, 1971, §§3–4; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *facts* are *constituted* by the *agreement* among the *reasonable* (the *justification* *to* the *others*), and the *action* *is* *wrong* if it *violates* the *principles* that *no one* could *reasonably* *reject* (the *contractualist* *formula*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Act } A \text{ is wrong} \iff A \text{ is forbidden by the *principles* that *no one* could *reasonably* *reject*}
$$

**The *components***.

| The *component* | The *claim* | The *significance* |
|---|---|---|
| **The *justification*** | The *morality* *requires* the *justification* to the *others* | The *relational* *insight* |
| **The *reasonable*** | The *rejection* *grounded* in the *reasons* (not the *mere* *preference*) | The *impartiality* |
| **The *principles*** | The *general* *rules* (the *no* *arbitrary* *exclusion*) | The *determinacy* |

**The *significance*.** The *contractualism* *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47) and to the *practical* *reason* (Ch. 46), and it *grounds* the *morality* in the *justification* (the *relational* *insight*).

### 4.2 The Scanlonian formula

**The *argument*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5, §1).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *morality* *is* the *system* of the *mutual* *justification* (the *contractualist* *insight*).} \\
2. & \text{The *justification* *requires* the *principles* *no one* could *reasonably* *reject* (the *reasonable* *rejection*).} \\
3. & \text{The *principles* *ground* the *right* and the *wrong* (the *moral* *facts*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *contractualism* *constitutes* the *morality* (the *Scanlonian* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *problems***.

**(i) The *aggregation* *problem*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Parfit, “Justifiability to Each Person,” 2003; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *Scanlonian* *formula* *forbids* the *aggregation* (the *principles* *must* be *justifiable* *to* *each* *person*, and the *aggregation* *sacrifices* the *individual* to the *sum*), and the *formula* *cannot* *resolve* the *rescue* *case* (the *one* *vs.* the *many*: *whom* *to* *save*?), and the *aggregation* *is* *intuitive* (the *numbers* *matter*).

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5, §3; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *contractualism* *accommodates* the *numbers* *through* the *tie*-*breaking* *principles* (the *rescue* *case*: the *principle* *that* *saves* the *more* *is* *justifiable* *to* *each* *person*; the *tie*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *aggregation* in the *justifiability*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *aggregation* *is* *ad hoc*; the *numbers* *remain* *a* *problem*).

**(ii) The *no*-*agreement* *objection***. The *agreement* *requires* the *parties* (the *contractors*), and the *parties* *have* the *interests*, and the *interests* *shape* the *agreement*, and the *agreement* *is* *not* *neutral* (the *power* *relations*; the *feminist* *critique*; Ch. 47, §2).

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5; Rawls 1971, §24). The *parties* are *idealized* (the *veil* *of* *ignorance*, the *reasonable*), and the *idealization* *removes* the *power* *relations*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *neutrality* in the *idealization*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(iii) The *inclusion* *problem*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *contractualism* *includes* only the *parties* *capable* of the *agreement* (the *rational* *agents*), and the *moral* *status* of the *animals*, the *infants*, the *dementia* *patients*, and the *future* *persons* is *not* *grounded* (the *inclusion* *problem*; Ch. 51, §5).

**The *reply*** (Scanlon 1998, Ch. 5; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *contractualism* *includes* the *non*-*contractors* *through* the *principles* *that* *protect* them (the *trustees*, the *representatives*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *inclusion* in the *principles*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *inclusion* *is* *derivative*; the *intrinsic* *status* *is* *lost*).

### 4.3 The contractualism and the contractarianism

**The *distinction***.

| The *theory* | The *ground* | The *proponent* |
|---|---|---|
| **Contractualism** | The *justification* to the *others* (the *reasonable* *rejection*) | Scanlon; Rawls |
| **Contractarianism** | The *mutual* *advantage* (the *rational* *choice*) | Hobbes; Gauthier, *Morals by Agreement*, 1986 |

**The *significance*.** The *contractualism* *grounds* the *morality* in the *justification* (the *moral* *relation*), and the *contractarianism* *grounds* it in the *advantage* (the *rational* *choice*), and the *distinction* *marks* the *difference* between the *moral* *and* the *prudential*.

**The *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *contractarianism* *cannot* *explain* the *moral* *obligation* (the *advantage* *is* *prudential*, and the *obligation* *is* *categorical*), and the *contractualism* *answers* the *objection* (the *justification* *is* *moral*).

### 4.4 The state of the contractualism

**The *assessment***. The *contractualism* is *defensible*, and the *aggregation*, the *no*-*agreement*, and the *inclusion* *objections* are *serious*.

| The *variety* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Scanlon** | The *justification* | The *aggregation* |
| **Rawls** | The *political* *stability* | The *idealization* |
| **Contractarianism** | The *rational* *choice* | The *no* *obligation* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *Scanlonian* *contractualism*: the *justification* to the *others* (the *relational* *insight*), the *reasonable* *rejection* (the *impartiality*), the *tie*-*breaking* *principles* (the *numbers*), *extended* to the *non*-*contractors* (the *inclusion*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *aggregation* and the *idealization* *remain* *contested*.

## 5. Particularism, Pluralism, and Reflective Equilibrium

### 5.1 Moral particularism

**The *thesis*** (Dancy, *Ethics Without Principles*, 2004; *Moral Reasons*, 1993; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *reasons* are *holistic* (the *same* *feature* *contributes* the *opposite* *reason* in the *different* *contexts*), and there are *no* *moral* *principles* (the *general* *norms* that *determine* the *right* *action*), and the *moral* *judgment* *requires* the *perception* of the *particular* *situation*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Feature } f \ \text{contributes a *pro tanto* reason} \ r \ \text{in context} \ C_1, \ \text{and the *opposite* reason in context} \ C_2
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *reasons* are *context*-*sensitive* (the *holism*: the *same* *feature* *counts* *differently* in the *different* *contexts*).} \\
2. & \text{The *principles* *require* the *context*-*invariant* *reasons* (the *atomism*).} \\
3. & \text{The *atomism* *is* *false* (the *holism*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There are *no* *moral* *principles* (the *particularism*), and the *judgment* *requires* the *perception* (the *perceptual* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *examples***. The *pleasure* *is* *good* (the *enjoyment*) and *bad* (the *sadistic* *pleasure*); the *promise* *binds* and *does* *not* (the *promise* *to* the *murderer*); the *truth* *helps* and *harms* (the *truth* *to* the *dying* *patient*).

**The *virtue***. The *particularism* *captures* the *context*-*sensitivity* of the *moral* *judgment*, and it *fits* the *practice* (the *hard* *cases*, the *judgment*, the *residue*), and it *avoids* the *rule*-*worship* (the *rigorism*).

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no*-*guidance* *objection***. The *particularism* *cannot* *guide* the *action* (the *judgment* *requires* the *virtue*, the *perception*, the *training*), and the *moral* *education* *requires* the *principles*.

**The *reply*** (Dancy 2004, Ch. 1). The *particularism* *guides* *through* the *perception* (the *trained* *judgment*), and the *guidance* *is* *not* the *rule*-*application*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *guidance* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *default*-*reason* *objection*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *holism* *does* not *entail* the *no* *principles* (the *features* *can* be *default* *reasons* *with* the *exceptions*, and the *default* *reasons* *are* the *principles*), and the *particularism* *overstates* the *holism*.

**The *reply*** (Dancy 2004, Ch. 2). The *default* *reasons* *are* *not* *principles* (the *default* *is* *defeasible*, and the *defeasibility* *is* *the* *particularism*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *particularism* in the *defeasibility*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.2 Moral pluralism

**The *thesis*** (Ross 1930, Ch. 2; Berlin, “The Pursuit of the Ideal,” 1988; Nagel, “The Fragmentation of Value,” 1979). The *values* are *plural* and *irreducible* (the *liberty*, the *equality*, the *welfare*, the *fidelity*, the *knowledge*), and the *values* *conflict* (the *tragic* *choices*), and there is *no* *single* *scale* (the *incommensurability*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\text{Values} \ V_1, \ldots, V_n \ \text{are *irreducible* (no *common* *measure*), and the conflicts are *real* (the *tragic* *choices*)}
$$

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *values* are *qualitatively* *distinct* (the *liberty* *is* *not* the *welfare*; the *fidelity* *is* *not* the *justice*).} \\
2. & \text{The *distinct* *values* *cannot* be *reduced* to a *common* *measure* (the *incommensurability*).} \\
3. & \text{The *conflicts* *are* *real* (the *tragic* *choices*) and *irresolvable* by a *single* *scale* (the *residue*: the *regret*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *pluralism* is *true* (the *pluralist* *insight*), and the *monism* (the *single* *principle*) is *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *pluralism* *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *metaethics* (the *moral* *realism*, the *non*-*naturalism*; Ch. 44, §2.4) and to the *political* *philosophy* (the *value* *pluralism*; Berlin; Ch. 47, §2), and it *grounds* the *tragic* *choices* (the *residue*).

### 5.3 The reflective equilibrium

**The *thesis*** (Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, 1971, §9; Daniels, “Wide Reflective Equilibrium and Theory Acceptance in Ethics,” 1979; Ch. 3, §4). The *method* of the *moral* *theory* is the *reflective* *equilibrium* (the *adjustment* of the *particular* *judgments*, the *principles*, and the *background* *theories* until they are *coherent*), and the *equilibrium* is *wide* (the *background* *theories* *included*) and *narrow* (the *judgments* *only*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *considered* *judgments* (the *particular* *moral* *intuitions*).} \\
2. & \text{The *principles* (the *general* *norms*).} \\
3. & \text{The *background* *theories* (the *psychology*, the *metaphysics*, the *semantics*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *equilibrium* (the *coherence* of the *three* *levels*), *revised* by the *mutual* *adjustment* (the *method*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue***. The *method* *avoids* the *intuitionism* (the *unreflective* *intuitions*) and the *foundationalism* (the *self*-*evident* *principles*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *theory* *construction*), and it *grounds* the *normative* *theory* in the *coherence*.

**The *objections***.

**(i) The *no*-*independent* *check* *objection***. The *equilibrium* *is* *circular* (the *judgments* *justify* the *principles*, and the *principles* *justify* the *judgments*), and the *circularity* *undermines* the *justification*.

**The *reply*** (Daniels 1979, §3; the *wide* *equilibrium*; the *coherentism*; Ch. 8, §3). The *coherence* *is* *the* *justification* (the *support* *is* *mutual*, and the *mutual* *support* *raises* the *probability*; the *coherentist* *insight*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *equilibrium* in the *coherence*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *equilibrium* *is* *the* *best* *available* *method*).

**(ii) The *no*-*determinacy* *objection***. The *equilibrium* *under*-*determines* the *theory* (the *different* *adjustments* *yield* the *different* *equilibria*), and the *choice* between the *equilibria* is *arbitrary*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *equilibrium* *is* *constrained* by the *background* *theories* (the *wide* *equilibrium*), and the *constraint* *reduces* the *indeterminacy*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *determinacy* in the *background*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 5.4 The state of the particularism and the pluralism

**The *assessment***. The *particularism* and the *pluralism* are *defensible*, and the *no*-*guidance* and the *default*-*reason* *objections* are *serious*, and the *reflective* *equilibrium* is the *method*.

| The *position* | The *strength* | The *weakness* |
|---|---|---|
| **Particularism** | The *context*-*sensitivity* | The *no* *guidance* |
| **Pluralism** | The *tragic choices* | The *no* *priority* |
| **Equilibrium** | The *coherence* | The *circularity* |
| **Monism** | The *determinacy* | The *no* *context* |

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. A *qualified* *pluralism* *combined* with the *reflective* *equilibrium*: the *values* are *irreducible* (the *pluralist* *insight*), the *judgments* *are* *context*-*sensitive* (the *particularist* *insight*), and the *theory* *is* *constructed* by the *wide* *reflective* *equilibrium* (the *equilibrium* *insight*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *indeterminacy* and the *tragic* *choices* *remain*.

## 6. Hard Cases

### 6.1 The trolley problems

**The *cases*** (Foot, “The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect,” 1967; Thomson, “Killing, Letting Die, and the Trolley Problem,” 1976; “The Trolley Problem,” 1985).

| The *case* | The *situation* | The *intuition* |
|---|---|---|
| **Switch** | The *five* on the *track*, the *one* on the *side* *track*, the *bystander* *pulls* the *switch* | The *permissible* (the *save* the *five*) |
| **Bridge** | The *fat* *man* *pushed* onto the *track* | The *impermissible* (the *use* as *means*) |
| **Loop** | The *switch* *sends* the *trolley* *round* to the *one* | The *contested* |
| **Transplant** | The *surgeon* *kills* the *one* for the *five* *organs* | The *impermissible* |

**The *significance***. The *cases* *isolate* the *moral* *structure* (the *doing*/*allowing*, the *intention*/*foresight*, the *means*/*side*-*effect*), and the *intuitions* *test* the *theories* (Ch. 3, §2), and the *pattern* (the *switch* *permissible*, the *bridge* *impermissible*) *constrains* the *deontology* (§2.4) and the *consequentialism* (§1).

**The *arguments***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *switch* *and* the *bridge* *differ* *morally* (the *intuition*).} \\
2. & \text{The *consequentialism* *cannot* *distinguish* the *cases* (the *same* *aggregate*, the *one* *dies* for the *five*).} \\
3. & \text{The *deontology* *distinguishes* the *cases* (the *bridge* *uses* the *fat* *man* *as* a *means*; the *doing*/*allowing*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *deontology* *fits* the *intuitions* *better* (the *trolley* *insight*), and the *consequentialism* *misdescribes* the *deontic* *structure*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *consequentialist* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *consequentialism* *accommodates* the *cases* *through* the *rules* (the *rule*-*consequentialism*: the *universal* *practice* of the *pushing* *undermines* the *trust*, and the *rule* *forbids* the *pushing*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *distinction* in the *rules*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *rule* *is* *ad hoc*).

**(ii) The *experimental* *response*** (the *x-phi*; the *contemporary* *literature*; Greene et al., “An fMRI Investigation of Emotional Engagement in Moral Judgment,” 2001; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *trolley* *intuitions* *vary* across the *demographics* (the *culture*, the *gender*, the *personality*), and the *variation* *undermines* the *evidential* *value* of the *intuitions* (the *calibration* *problem*; Ch. 3, §2).

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *variation* *is* *real* but *limited* (the *switch* *permissible* *is* *robust* across the *cultures*), and the *robust* *intuitions* *remain* *evidence*, and the *reply* *grounds* the *evidence* in the *robust* *pattern*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *no*-*theory* *response*** (the *particularism*; §5.1). The *trolley* *cases* *are* *artificial* (the *fantasy* *scenarios*), and the *intuitions* *are* *unreliable* (the *no* *real* *context*), and the *cases* *do* not *test* the *real* *morality*.

**The *reply*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *cases* *isolate* the *variables* (the *doing*/*allowing*), and the *isolation* *is* the *method* (Ch. 3, §2), and the *reply* *grounds* the *method* in the *experimental* *control*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *artificiality* *is* *the* *point*).

### 6.2 The demandingness and the integrity

**The *integrity* *objection*** (Williams, “A Critique of Utilitarianism,” 1973, §5; the *Jim* *and* the *Indians* *case*). The *utilitarianism* *requires* the *agent* to *sacrifice* the *projects* that *constitute* the *self*, and the *sacrifice* *alienates* the *agent* from the *own* *life* (the *integrity*), and the *morality* *demands* the *alienation*.

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *agent*'s *projects* *constitute* the *self* (the *identity*). \\
2. & \text{The *utilitarianism* *requires* the *sacrifice* of the *projects* *whenever* the *aggregate* *requires* it. \\
3. & \text{The *sacrifice* *destroys* the *self* (the *alienation*; the *one thought too many*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *utilitarianism* *is* *incompatible* with the *integrity* (the *integrity* *objection*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *two-level* *response*** (§1.3). The *sophisticated* *consequentialism* *permits* the *projects* (the *critical* *level* *justifies* the *intuitive* *level*), and the *reply* *answers* the *objection*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *instability*).

**(ii) The *agent*-*relative* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; Scheffler, *The Rejection of Consequentialism*, 1982). The *agent*-*relative* *prerogatives* *permit* the *personal* *projects* (the *options*; §2.1), and the *reply* *grounds* the *permission* in the *integrity*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *hybrid* *theory*).

**(iii) The *no*-*integrity* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *integrity* *is* *not* a *moral* *value* (the *self*-*indulgence*), and the *objection* *is* *rhetorical*, and the *reply* *contests* the *premise*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.3 The moral luck

**The *thesis*** (Williams, “Moral Luck,” 1976; Nagel, “Moral Luck,” 1979; Ch. 46, §2).

**The *kinds***.

| The *kind* | The *content* | The *example* |
|---|---|---|
| **Resultant** | The *luck* in the *outcome* | The *drunk* *driver* who *hits* the *child* |
| **Circumstantial** | The *luck* in the *situation* | The *Nazi* *collaborator* vs. the *resister* |
| **Constitutive** | The *luck* in the *character* | The *temperament* (the *virtuous* *by* *nature*) |
| **Causal** | The *luck* in the *causes* of the *action* | The *upbringing* (the *determinism*; Ch. 27) |

**The *problem***. The *moral* *judgment* *depends* on the *factors* *beyond* the *agent*'s *control* (the *outcome*, the *circumstance*, the *character*), and the *moral* *responsibility* *requires* the *control* (the *no*-*control* *principle*), and the *two* *are* *incompatible* (the *paradox* of the *moral* *luck*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *moral* *responsibility* *requires* the *control* (the *no*-*control* *principle*).} \\
2. & \text{The *moral* *judgment* *depends* on the *factors* *beyond* the *control* (the *luck*). \\
3. & \text{The *two* *are* *incompatible* (the *paradox*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *responsibility* *collapses* (the *luck* *pessimism*; Nagel 1979), or the *control* *principle* *is* *false* (the *luck* *realism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses***.

**(i) The *control* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *responsibility* *requires* only the *control* *of* the *action* (the *attempt*, the *intention*), not the *outcome*, and the *resultant* *luck* *is* *not* *moral* (the *legal* *luck* *is* *separate*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *control*. The *reply* is *contested* (the *outcome* *matters* *intuitively*).

**(ii) The *luck* *egalitarian* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*; the *moral* *luck* *and* the *desert*). The *constitutive* *luck* *undermines* the *desert* (the *Rawlsian* *insight*; Ch. 47, §3), and the *moral* *responsibility* *is* *reconceived* (the *attributability*, the *accountability*; Ch. 27, §6), and the *reply* *grounds* the *responsibility* in the *practice*. The *reply* is *defensible*.

**(iii) The *skeptical* *response*** (the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *luck* *shows* that the *morality* *is* *not* *the* *autonomous* *realm* that the *Kantian* *claims* (the *purity* *is* *lost*), and the *skepticism* *is* *the* *honest* *response*. The *reply* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The diagnostic value of the hard cases

**The *thesis***. The *hard* *cases* *are* the *lens* through which the *structure* of the *theory* is *revealed* (Ch. 8, §6.1). The *cases* *isolate* the *variables* (the *doing*/*allowing*, the *intention*/*foresight*, the *aggregation*), and the *pattern* of the *intuitions* *constrains* the *theory*, and the *theory* that *cannot* *accommodate* the *pattern* is *refuted* (or *revised*).

**The *lesson***. The *normative* *ethics* is *not* the *application* of the *principles* to the *cases*; it is the *equilibrium* between the *principles* and the *cases* (§5.3), and the *hard* *cases* *are* the *testing* *ground*.

## 7. What Moral Theory Is For

### 7.1 The functions

**The *thesis***. The *moral* *theory* has *four* *functions* (the *descriptive*, the *normative*, the *critical*, the *self*-*understanding*), and the *functions* *are* *distinct*, and the *theory* *that* *serves* one *may* *fail* the *others*.

| The *function* | The *content* | The *criterion* |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | The *explanation* of the *moral* *judgments* | The *explanatory* *power* |
| **Normative** | The *guidance* of the *action* | The *determinacy* |
| **Critical** | The *debunking* of the *ideology* | The *normative* *distance* |
| **Self-understanding** | The *articulation* of the *values* | The *articulacy* |

**The *significance*.** The *functions* *are* *the* *criteria* (Ch. 4, §3), and the *criteria* *are* *the* *basis* of the *choice* between the *theories*.

### 7.2 The descriptive function

**The *thesis***. The *moral* *theory* *explains* the *moral* *judgments* (the *ordinary* *intuitions*, the *hard* *cases*, the *pattern*), and the *explanation* *is* the *evidence* for the *theory* (the *IBE*; Ch. 10, §6).

**The *criteria***. The *explanatory* *power* (the *range* of the *cases* *explained*), the *simplicity* (the *number* of the *principles*), the *fertility* (the *new* *predictions*, the *implications*), the *conservatism* (the *fit* with the *background* *theories*).

**The *problem***. The *moral* *intuitions* *are* *contested* (the *disagreement*; Ch. 44, §5), and the *data* *are* *unstable* (the *x-phi*; §6.1), and the *explanation* *is* *not* *the* *prediction* (the *moral* *facts* *are* *not* *observable*), and the *descriptive* *function* *is* *limited*.

### 7.3 The normative function

**The *thesis***. The *moral* *theory* *guides* the *action* (the *deliberation*, the *decision*), and the *guidance* *requires* the *determinate* *norms* (the *principles*, the *rules*), and the *guidance* *is* the *practical* *function*.

**The *criteria***. The *determinacy* (the *action*-*guiding* *content*), the *applicability* (the *real* *cases*), the *efficacy* (the *compliance*).

**The *problem***. The *particularism* *denies* the *determinacy* (§5.1), and the *virtue* *ethics* *locates* the *guidance* in the *judgment* (§3.3), and the *theory* *may* *be* *too* *abstract* to *guide* (the *demandingness*, the *hard* *cases*).

### 7.4 The critical function

**The *thesis***. The *moral* *theory* *debunks* the *ideology* (the *norms* that *serve* the *power*), and the *critique* *requires* the *normative* *distance* (the *theory* *stands* *apart* from the *practice*), and the *critique* *is* the *emancipatory* *function* (the *Marxist*, the *feminist*, the *critical* *race* *insights*; Ch. 47, §2).

**The *criteria***. The *normative* *distance* (the *independence* of the *theory* from the *practice*), the *explanatory* *power* (the *explanation* of the *ideology*), the *emancipatory* *efficacy*.

**The *problem***. The *critique* *presupposes* the *normative* *standpoint* (the *theory*), and the *standpoint* *is* *contested* (the *metaethical* *debate*; Ch. 44), and the *critique* *can* *be* *self*-*undermining* (the *genealogy* *applies* to the *theory* *itself*; Ch. 3, §5).

### 7.5 The self-understanding function

**The *thesis***. The *moral* *theory* *articulates* the *values* (the *meaning* of the *moral* *life*), and the *articulation* *is* the *expressive* *function*, and it *connects* the *theory* to the *good* *life* (Ch. 48, §3).

**The *criteria***. The *articulacy* (the *clarity* of the *values*), the *resonance* (the *fit* with the *lived* *experience*), the *transformative* *power* (the *change* of the *self*-*understanding*).

**The *problem***. The *self*-*understanding* *is* *not* the *justification* (the *resonance* *is* *not* the *truth*), and the *articulation* *can* *be* *ideological*.

### 7.6 The anti-theory challenge

**The *thesis*** (Williams, *Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy*, 1985, Ch. 10; Clarke, *Moral Theory*, 1987; Baier, “Theory and Reflective Practices,” 1985; the *contemporary* *literature*). The *moral* *theory* *is* *not* *necessary* (the *ethical* *life* *can* *be* *lived* *without* the *theory*), and the *theory* *is* *harmful* (the *abstraction* *alienates* the *practice*; the *theory* *colonizes* the *moral* *life*), and the *theory* *is* *impossible* (the *systematic* *account* *fails*; the *disagreement*, the *pluralism*).

**The *argument***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *ethical* *life* *is* *the* *practice* (the *tradition*, the *perception*, the *judgment*).} \\
2. & \text{The *theory* *is* *not* *required* for the *practice* (the *practice* *precedes* the *theory*).} \\
3. & \text{The *theory* *is* *harmful* (the *abstraction*, the *rigorism*, the *alienation*). \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *moral* *theory* *is* *not* *necessary* (the *anti*-*theory* *insight*), and the *practice* *is* *primary* (the *practice* *insight*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *anti*-*theory* *challenge* *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *Wittgensteinian* *tradition* (the *practice*, the *form* of *life*; Ch. 52, §2) and to the *Confucian* *ethics* (the *ritual*, the *practice*; the *optional* *threads*), and it *challenges* the *systematic* *ambition* of the *chapter*.

**The *reply***. The *theory* *is* *not* *the* *replacement* of the *practice* (it is the *clarification*), and the *practice* *requires* the *reflection* (the *crises*, the *conflicts*, the *reform*), and the *reply* *grounds* the *theory* in the *reflection*. The *reply* is *defensible* (the *theory* *and* the *practice* *are* *complementary*).

### 7.7 The state

**The *assessment***. The *theory* is *necessary* for the *reflection* (the *crises*, the *critique*), and it is *not* the *replacement* of the *practice* (the *anti*-*theory* *insight*), and the *criteria* *are* the *basis* of the *choice* (§7.1).

**The *most* *defensible* *position***. The *theory* *serves* the *reflection* (the *critical* *function*), *informs* the *practice* (the *normative* *function*), and *articulates* the *values* (the *self*-*understanding* *function*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *theory* *is* *not* the *practice* (the *anti*-*theory* *insight*).

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The *three* *families*** (the *consequentialism*, the *deontology*, the *virtue* *ethics*) *are* *not* *rivals* in the *simple* *sense* (they *answer* the *different* *questions*: the *value*, the *duty*, the *character*), and the *most* *defensible* *theories* *are* *hybrids* (§8.3).
- **The *demandingness*** is the *central* *objection* to the *consequentialism*, and the *two*-*level* and the *satisficing* *replies* *reduce* the *force* of the *objection* (the *contested*).
- **The *empty* *formalism*** and the *rigorism* are the *central* *objections* to the *Kantian* *deontology*, and the *Rossian* and the *threshold* *forms* *reduce* the *force* of the *objections* (the *contested*).
- **The *action*-*guidance*** and the *situationism* are the *central* *objections* to the *virtue* *ethics*, and the *skill* *model* and the *local* *traits* *reduce* the *force* of the *objections* (the *contested*).
- **The *aggregation*** is the *central* *objection* to the *contractualism*, and the *tie*-*breaking* *principles* *reduce* the *force* of the *objection* (the *contested*).
- **The *hard* *cases*** (the *trolley*, the *integrity*, the *luck*) *are* the *lens* through which the *structure* of the *theory* is *revealed* (§6.4), and the *intuitions* *are* *evidence*, *defeasible* by the *theory* (the *reflective* *equilibrium*; §5.3).
- **The *reflective* *equilibrium*** is the *method* of the *normative* *theory* (the *wide* *equilibrium*, the *coherence*), and the *method* *is* *contested* (the *circularity*, the *indeterminacy*).
- **The *theory* *serves* the *reflection*** (the *critical* *function*), and the *practice* *precedes* the *theory* (the *anti*-*theory* *insight*).

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *value* *theory*.** The *hedonism*, the *desire*, the *objective* *list* (Ch. 48, §1).
- **The *aggregation*.** The *total*, the *average*, the *prioritarian*, the *repugnant* *conclusion* (§1.4).
- **The *demandingness*.** The *two*-*level*, the *satisficing*, the *prioritarian*, the *effective* *altruism* (§1.3).
- **The *deontic* *structure*.** The *constraints*, the *options*, the *double* *effect*, the *threshold* (§2.4).
- **The *virtue*.** The *skill* *model*, the *situationism*, the *naturalism* (§3.4).
- **The *contractualism*.** The *aggregation*, the *inclusion*, the *idealization* (§4.4).
- **The *particularism*.** The *holism*, the *default* *reasons*, the *guidance* (§5.1).
- **The *hard* *cases*.** The *trolley* *intuitions*, the *x-phi* *data*, the *moral* *luck* (§6).

### 8.4 The most defensible synthesis

**The *thesis***. The *most* *defensible* *position* is a *qualified* *hybrid*: a *two*-*level* *prioritarian* *consequentialism* (the *impersonal* *aggregation* for the *political* *and* the *impersonal* *choices*) *combined* with the *Rossian* *deontic* *constraints* (the *side* *constraints*, the *options*, the *special* *obligations*) and the *virtue*-*ethical* *character* (the *phronesis*, the *perception*, the *flourishing*), *integrated* by the *wide* *reflective* *equilibrium*, and *qualified* by the *pluralism* (the *irreducible* *values*, the *tragic* *choices*, the *residue*).

**The *structure***.

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *impersonal* *aggregation*} \ (\text{the *prioritarian* *insight*}) \ \text{for the *political* *choices*.} \\
2. & \text{The *deontic* *constraints*} \ (\text{the *Rossian* *insight*}) \ \text{for the *personal* *choices*.} \\
3. & \text{The *virtue*-*ethical* *character*} \ (\text{the *phronesis* *insight*}) \ \text{for the *perception* *and* the *life*. \\
4. & \text{The *wide* *reflective* *equilibrium*} \ (\text{the *coherence* *insight*}) \ \text{for the *integration*. \\
5. & \text{The *pluralism*} \ (\text{the *irreducibility* *insight*}) \ \text{for the *tragic* *choices* *and* the *residue*. \\
\hline
6. & \therefore \text{The *hybrid* *theory} \ (\text{the *synthesis* *insight*}), \ *qualified* \text{by the *admission* that the *aggregation*, the *demandingness*, \text{ and the *priority* *remain* *contested*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *significance*.** The *synthesis* *is* *not* an *eclecticism* (the *arbitrary* *mix*); it is a *principled* *division* of the *normative* *labor* (the *impersonal* *aggregation* for the *public*, the *constraints* for the *personal*, the *virtue* for the *life*), and it *fits* the *practice* (the *different* *contexts* *require* the *different* *norms*), and it *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47) and to the *applied* *ethics* (Ch. 51).

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *particularism* *debate* and the *default*-*reason* *view*.** The *particularism* (Dancy) *denies* the *principles*, and the *default*-*reason* *view* (the *contemporary* *literature*) *defends* the *principles* *as* *defeasible* *defaults*, and the *debate* *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *moral* *reasons* (Ch. 28) and to the *holism* (the *semantics*, the *epistemology*). The *debate* is *active*.

**2. The *situationism* and the *empirical* *psychology*.** The *situationist* *challenge* (Doris, Harman) is a *model* of the *empirical* *philosophy* (Ch. 3, §6), and the *response* (the *skill* *model*, the *local* *traits*) *integrates* the *psychology* into the *virtue* *theory* (the *naturalism*). The *connection* to the *character* (Ch. 27, §6) and to the *self* (Ch. 26) is *central*.

**3. The *trolley* *problems* and the *experimental* *ethics*.** The *trolleyology* is the *most* *developed* *case* *study* of the *experimental* *ethics* (Ch. 3, §6), and the *data* (the *fMRI* *studies*, the *cross*-*cultural* *variation*) *test* the *deontological* *and* the *consequentialist* *intuitions*, and the *debate* *connects* the *normative* *ethics* to the *cognitive* *science* (Ch. 30) and to the *empirical* *method*.

**4. Non-Western normative ethics.** The *Confucian* *ethics* (the *ren*, the *li*, the *roles*; the *Analects*) *grounds* the *normativity* in the *relational* *practice*, and the *Buddhist* *ethics* (the *śīla*, the *karuṇā*, the *interdependence*) *grounds* it in the *compassion* and the *non*-*harm*, and the *African* *ubuntu* *grounds* it in the *community* (the *“*I am because we are”*). The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* to the *individualist* *ontology* of the *Western* *ethics* (Ch. 51, *optional* *threads*), and they *challenge* the *abstraction* *and* the *impartiality* of the *mainstream* *theories*.

**5. The *demandingness* and the *effective* *altruism*.** The *effective* *altruism* (Singer; MacAskill, *Doing Good Better*, 2015) *applies* the *consequentialist* *demandingness* to the *global* *poverty* and to the *existential* *risk* (Ch. 51, §3), and the *movement* *raises* the *questions* of the *partiality* (the *care* *ethics*), the *institutions* (the *political* *change* vs. the *individual* *donation*), and the *measurement* (the *cost*-*effectiveness*). The *connection* to the *applied* *ethics* (Ch. 51) and to the *political* *philosophy* (Ch. 47, §7) is *direct*.

**6. The *normative* *ethics* of the *AI*.** The *alignment* *problem* (Ch. 51, §3) *requires* the *normative* *theory* (the *value* *specification*, the *aggregation*, the *constraints*), and the *choice* of the *theory* *has* the *practical* *consequences* (the *different* *theories* *yield* the *different* *policies*), and the *connection* *is* *urgent* and *underdeveloped*.

## Summary

- **The *normative* *ethics*** *asks* what is *right*, and the *three* *families* (the *consequentialism*, the *deontology*, the *virtue* *ethics*) *answer* the *different* *questions* (the *value*, the *duty*, the *character*), and the *most* *defensible* *theories* *are* *hybrids*.
- **The *consequentialism*** (the *act*, the *rule*, the *two*-*level*, the *scalar*) *grounds* the *rightness* in the *good* *consequences*, and the *utilitarianism* *maximizes* the *happiness*, and the *demandingness* *objection* (the *sacrifice*, the *alienation*) *is* the *central* *challenge*, and the *two*-*level* and the *satisficing* *replies* *reduce* the *force*.
- **The *aggregation*** (the *total*, the *average*, the *prioritarian*, the *sufficientarian*) is the *hard* *problem*, and the *repugnant* *conclusion* (Parfit) *challenges* the *total* *view*, and the *variable* *value* and the *critical*-*level* *views* *respond*.
- **The *deontology*** (the *Kantian*, the *Rossian*, the *threshold*, the *specification*) *grounds* the *rightness* in the *duty*, and the *categorical* *imperative* (the *FUL*, the *FH*, the *FKE*, the *FA*) is the *procedure*, and the *empty* *formalism* and the *rigorism* *are* the *central* *objections*, and the *Rossian* and the *threshold* *forms* *reduce* the *force*.
- **The *virtue* *ethics*** (the *Aristotelian*, the *Foot*, the *Hursthouse*, the *skill*) *grounds* the *morality* in the *character* and the *flourishing*, and the *action*-*guidance* and the *situationism* *are* the *central* *objections*, and the *skill* *model* and the *local* *traits* *reduce* the *force*.
- **The *contractualism*** (the *Scanlonian*, the *Rawlsian*, the *contractarian*) *grounds* the *morality* in the *justification* (the *reasonable* *rejection*), and the *aggregation*, the *no*-*agreement*, and the *inclusion* *are* the *central* *objections*.
- **The *particularism*** (the *holism*, the *no* *principles*) and the *pluralism* (the *irreducible* *values*) *challenge* the *systematic* *ethics*, and the *reflective* *equilibrium* (the *wide*, the *narrow*) is the *method*, and the *circularity* and the *indeterminacy* *are* the *objections*.
- **The *hard* *cases*** (the *trolley*, the *demandingness*, the *integrity*, the *moral* *luck*) *reveal* the *structure* of the *theory* (the *doing*/*allowing*, the *intention*/*foresight*, the *aggregation*, the *control*), and the *intuitions* *are* *evidence*, *defeasible* by the *theory*.
- **The *moral* *theory*** *serves* the *four* *functions* (the *descriptive*, the *normative*, the *critical*, the *self*-*understanding*), and the *anti*-*theory* *challenge* (the *practice* *primacy*) *is* *serious*, and the *theory* *and* the *practice* *are* *complementary*.
- **The *most* *defensible* *position*** is a *qualified* *hybrid*: a *two*-*level* *prioritarian* *consequentialism* (the *impersonal* *aggregation* for the *public*), *combined* with the *Rossian* *constraints* (the *personal*), the *virtue*-*ethical* *character* (the *perception*, the *life*), the *wide* *reflective* *equilibrium* (the *method*), and the *pluralism* (the *tragic* *choices*), *qualified* by the *admission* that the *aggregation*, the *demandingness*, and the *priority* *remain* *contested*.
