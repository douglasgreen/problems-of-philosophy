# Chapter 19 — Substance, Matter, and the Physical World

> “It is not the table of common sense that physics describes, but a world of fields and probabilities; and the table of common sense is a construction out of the effects of those fields on observers like us.”
> — the structural realist’s conclusion, in a sentence

> “I have two tables. One is the familiar table of everyday life; the other is the scientific table, which is mostly emptiness and a great many rapidly moving electrons. … Which is the real table?”
> — Arthur Eddington, *The Nature of the Physical World* (1928), Introduction

> “The physical world is not a world of things; it is a world of relations.”
> — a structuralist slogan, variously attributed

---

Chapter 18 examined the *cement* of the universe — causation. This chapter examines the *bricks*: what are the *fundamental* *entities* that the causal relations connect, and what does *physics* tell us about them?

The question is the *metaphysical* form of Russell's table. In Chapter 5, the table was the occasion for a theory of *perception*; in Chapter 6, it was the occasion for a theory of *knowledge*; here it is the occasion for a theory of *matter*. Russell argued, in *The Problems of Philosophy*, Chapter III, that physics tells us only about the *structure* of the physical world — the *relations* among its parts — and *nothing* about the *intrinsic* *nature* of those parts. The argument was made in 1912, and it has proved remarkably *durable*. It is the *ancestor* of the most important *contemporary* position in the philosophy of science: **structural realism**.

The chapter has three layers.

- **The *historical* layer** (§1): the *transformation* of the concept of *matter* from *classical* *corpuscles* to *quantum* *fields*, and the *consequences* for the metaphysics of substance.
- **The *metaphysical* layer** (§§2–4): the *theories* of *substance* (substratum, bundle, sortal), the *formulations* of *physicalism*, and the *causal* *exclusion* *problem*.
- **The *interpretive* layer** (§§5–7): the *interpretations* of *quantum* *mechanics*, the *structural* *realist* *response* to the *pessimistic* *meta-induction*, and the *panpsychist* *alternative*.

The *connecting* *question* is the one Russell posed: **what is the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical*?** The *chapter's* thesis is that the *question* is *not* *answered* by *physics*, and that the *philosophical* *options* — *structural* *realism*, *panpsychism*, *primitivism* — are *genuine* *options*, *not* *curiosities*.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 restates Russell's problem for the *quantum* age. §2 examines the theories of *substance*. §3 examines the *formulations* of *physicalism*. §4 examines the *causal* *exclusion* *problem*. §5 examines *quantum* *mechanics* and its *metaphysical* *implications*. §6 examines *structural* *realism*. §7 examines *panpsychism*. §8 gives an honest verdict.

## 1. Russell's Problem Restated: From Corpuscles to Fields

### 1.1 Eddington's two tables

The *problem* is stated most *vividly* by Arthur Eddington, in the *introduction* to *The Nature of the Physical World* (1928).

> “I have settled down to the task of writing these lectures and have drawn up my chairs to my two tables. Two tables! Yes; there are duplicates of every object about me — two tables, two chairs, two pens. … One of them has been familiar to me from earliest years. It is a commonplace object of that environment which I call the world. … It has extension; it is comparatively permanent; it is coloured; above all it is substantial. … My second table is a scientific table. It is my scientific table that is the only one which can claim the name of reality. … It is composed of electrical charges rushing about with great speed. … It is a shadow-show of electrons and empty space.”

**The two tables.**

| The familiar table | The scientific table |
|---|---|
| Solid, brown, extended, permanent | Mostly empty space; a cloud of electrons and nuclei |
| Perceived directly | Known by inference and theory |
| The object of common sense | The object of physics |
| Substantial | A “shadow-show” |

**Eddington's *conclusion***. The *scientific* table is the *real* one; the *familiar* table is a *construction* of the *mind*. The *conclusion* is *contested*, and the *contest* is the *subject* of the chapter.

### 1.2 Corpuscularianism

**The *view*.** Matter is composed of *tiny*, *indivisible*, *solid* *particles* — *atoms* or *corpuscles* — that *move* in *empty* *space* and *interact* by *contact* (or by *forces* at a distance).

**The *statement*.** Boyle (*The Sceptical Chymist*, 1661); Locke (*Essay*, 1690, II.viii); Newton (*Opticks*, 1704, Query 31).

**The *structure*.**

| Property | Location | The *corpuscularian* *account* |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | The *particles* | Primary |
| **Size** | The *particles* | Primary |
| **Motion** | The *particles* | Primary |
| **Colour** | The *observer* | Secondary |
| **Solidity** | The *particles* | Primary (but *mysterious*) |

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *reduces* the *qualitative* *richness* of the *world* to a *small* *set* of *quantitative* *properties*, and it *explains* the *qualitative* *richness* as an *effect* on *observers* (Ch. 5, §7.1).

**The *problems*.**

**(i) The *mystery* of *solidity***. What *is* the *solidity* of the *corpuscles*? If it is *impenetrability*, what *is* *impenetrability*? The *answer* (a *force* that *resists* *penetration*) *reintroduces* the *forces* that the *corpuscularian* *wanted* to *avoid*.

**(ii) The *problem* of *contact***. If the *corpuscles* *interact* by *contact*, what *happens* at the *point* of *contact*? The *question* *raises* the *problem* of *action* at a *distance* and the *problem* of the *nature* of *contact* itself.

**(iii) The *empirical* *failure*. Newtonian *mechanics* *requires* *forces* (gravity) that are *not* *contact* *forces*, and *electromagnetism* *requires* *fields* that are *not* *corpuscular*. The *corpuscularian* *picture* is *superseded* by the *field* *picture*.

### 1.3 The field revolution

**The *view*.** The *fundamental* *entities* are *fields* — *continuous* *distributions* of *properties* through *space* and *time* — and *particles* are *excitations* of the fields.

**The *statement*.** Faraday (*Experimental Researches in Electricity*, 1839–1855); Maxwell (*A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism*, 1873); Einstein (*The Special and General Theory*, 1916).

**The *structure*.**

| Concept | The *field* *account* |
|---|---|
| **Field** | A *continuous* *assignment* of *values* to *every* *point* in *space* |
| **Particle** | A *localized* *excitation* of the field (a *quantum*) |
| **Interaction** | The *field* *mediates* the *interaction* (no *action* at a *distance*) |
| **Vacuum** | The *ground* *state* of the field (not *empty* space) |

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *explains* *action* at a *distance* (the *field* *mediates* the *interaction*), and it *unifies* *electricity*, *magnetism*, and *light* (the *electromagnetic* *field*).

**The *consequences*.**

1. **The *particle* *concept* is *derivative***. The *fundamental* *entity* is the *field*; the *particle* is a *mode* of the *field*.
2. **The *vacuum* is *not* *empty***. The *vacuum* has *structure* (the *vacuum* *expectation* *values*, the *zero-point* *energy*), and the *structure* is *physically* *significant* (the *Casimir* *effect*).
3. **The *properties* *are* *relational***. The *field* *values* are *defined* *relative* to *observers*, *coordinates*, and *frames*; the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *field* is *not* *given* by the *mathematics*.

### 1.4 Quantum field theory and the dissolution of the particle

**The *view*.** Quantum field theory (QFT) is the *most* *successful* *physical* *theory* ever *formulated*. It *combines* *quantum* *mechanics* and *special* *relativity*, and it *describes* the *fundamental* *entities* as *quantum* *fields*.

**The *structure*.**

| Concept | The *QFT* *account* |
|---|---|
| **Field** | An *operator-valued* *field* $\hat{\phi}(x)$ defined at *every* *spacetime* *point* |
| **Particle** | An *excitation* of the field — a *quantum* — created by the *creation* *operator* $a^\dagger$ |
| **Vacuum** | The *state* $|0\rangle$ with *no* *quanta* |
| **Interaction** | The *field* *self-interaction* (the *interaction* *terms* in the *Lagrangian*) |

**The *dissolution* of the *particle*.** In QFT, the *particle* is *not* a *fundamental* *entity*; it is a *convenient* *description* of a *field* *excitation* in the *weak-coupling* *limit*. In *strongly* *interacting* *regimes*, the *particle* *concept* *breaks* *down* (confinement, the *parton* *model*, the *unruh* *effect*).

**The *consequences*.**

**(i) The *particle* *concept* is *approximate***. The *fundamental* *ontology* is *fields*, not *particles*.

**(ii) The *fields* are *not* *localized***. The *entangled* *states* of QFT *violate* *Bell* *inequalities*, and the *non-separability* is *fundamental* (unless one *adopts* a *nonlocal* *hidden-variable* *theory*; §5).

**(iii) The *vacuum* is *rich***. The *vacuum* *contains* *fluctuations*, *condensates*, and *topological* *structure* (the *Higgs* *condensate*).

### 1.5 What physics tells us: structure, not intrinsic nature

**Russell's *conclusion*** (*Problems*, Ch. III).

> “Physics … tells us nothing about the intrinsic nature of the physical world. It tells us only about the structure of the world — the relations among its parts.”

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Physics *describes* the world *in* *terms* of *equations* that *relate* *quantities* to one another.} \\
2. & \text{The *equations* *preserve* their *form* under *transformations* (coordinate changes, gauge changes, duality).} \\
3. & \text{What is *preserved* is the *structure*, not the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *entities* that *occupy* the *structure*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Physics tells us about the *structure*, not about the *intrinsic* *nature*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequences*.**

**(i) The *structural* *realist* *conclusion***. The *best* *interpretation* of *physics* is *structural* *realism*: we *know* the *structure* of the *world*, but *not* its *intrinsic* *nature*.

**(ii) The *epistemic* *gap***. The *intrinsic* *nature* is *epistemically* *inaccessible* — the *“Ramseyan* *humility”* (Lewis 2009).

**(iii) The *metaphysical* *question***. If physics *does* not *tell* us the *intrinsic* *nature*, what *does*? The *answers* — *panpsychism*, *primitivism*, *quietism* — are the *subject* of §7.

**The *connection* to Ch. 5**. Russell's *distinction* between *knowledge* *by* *acquaintance* and *knowledge* *by* *description* is the *ancestor* of the *structuralist* *conclusion*: we are *acquainted* with *sense-data* (the *intrinsic* *nature* of *experience*), and we *describe* the *physical* *world* (the *structure*). The *dualism* is *unstable*, but the *distinction* is *real*.

## 2. Substance, Substratum, and Bundle

### 2.1 The concept of substance

**The *question*.** What *is* a *substance*?

**The *history*.**

| Thinker | The *concept* | The *paradigm* |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristotle** | A *primary* *substance* is a *this* (a *particular* *thing*), a *unity* of *form* and *matter* | The *individual* *man*, *horse*, *tree* |
| **Descartes** | A *substance* is a *thing* that *exists* *in* *such* a *way* as to *depend* on *nothing* *else* for its *existence* | *Mind* (*res* *cogitans*) and *body* (*res* *extensa*) |
| **Locke** | A *substance* is a *support* of *qualities*; the *substratum* is “something, I know not what” | The *substratum* *underlying* the *qualities* |
| **Hume** | There is *no* *impression* of *substance*; the *idea* is a *bundle* of *perceptions* | The *bundle* *theory* |
| **Kant** | Substance is a *category* of the *understanding*; the *permanent* in *experience* | The *category* of *substance* |

**The *core* *notion*.** A *substance* is (i) a *particular* (it is *this* *thing*), (ii) a *bearer* *of* *properties* (it *has* the *properties*, but it is *not* *identical* with them), and (iii) a *unity* (it is *one* thing, *not* a *mere* *collection*).

### 2.2 The substratum theory

**The *thesis*.** A substance is a **bare particular** — a *substratum* that *supports* the *properties* but has *no* *properties* *of* *its* *own* (or, more precisely, has *only* the *properties* that *are* *borne* by it).

**The *statement*.** Locke (*Essay*, II.xxiii.2); the *contemporary* *defenders*: Sider (“Bare Particulars,” 2006), and the *four-category* *ontology* of Lowe (*The Possibility of Metaphysics*, 1998).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *particular* is *distinct* from *every* *other* *particular*, even one with *exactly* the same *properties*.} \\
2. & \text{The *distinctness* *requires* a *principle* of *individuation*.} \\
3. & \text{The *principle* is the *bare* *particular* (the *substratum*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{There are *bare* *particulars*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *individuation* *of* *indiscernible* *particulars* (the *identity* of *indiscernibles* is *false* if there are *bare* *particulars*), and it *grounds* the *unity* of the *object*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *“something* *I* *know* *not* *what”* *objection*** (Berkeley 1710; Hume 1739). The *bare* *particular* is *not* *empirically* *accessible*: we *never* *perceive* a *bare* *substratum*, and the *notion* is a *mystery*.

**The *reply*.** The *bare* *particular* is *not* *perceived*; it is *inferred* from the *need* for *individuation*. The *inference* is *an* *IBE* (Ch. 10, §6), and the *reply* *depends* on the *strength* of the *inference*.

**(ii) The *“no* *properties”* *objection***. A *bare* *particular* has *no* *properties*, but *something* with *no* *properties* is *nothing*. (The *reply*: the *bare* *particular* has *properties* — the *properties* it *bears* — but its *nature* is *exhausted* by *bareness*. The *reply* is *contested*.)

**(iii) The *invisibility* *objection***. The *bare* *particular* *does* *no* *explanatory* *work* beyond *individuation*, and *individuation* can be *explained* by *other* *means* (the *bundle* *theory*, the *haecceity* *theory*; Ch. 16, §4.4).

### 2.3 The bundle theory

**The *thesis*.** A substance is a **bundle** of *properties* (or *tropes*; Ch. 15, §3.2). The *object* is *nothing* *over* *and* *above* its *properties*, *bound* *together* by a *relation* of *compresence*.

**The *statement*.** Hume (*Treatise*, I.iv.6); Russell (*Problems*, Ch. V, the *bundle* *of* *sense-data*); the *contemporary* *defenders*: Williams (“On the Elements of Being,” 1953); Campbell (*Abstract Particulars*, 1990); Paul (*Category Mistakes*, 2017).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *only* *entities* *we* *observe* are *properties* (or *tropes*).} \\
2. & \text{The *postulation* of a *substratum* is *unobservable* and *unmotivated*.} \\
3. & \text{The *bundle* of *properties* *explains* everything the *substratum* *explains* (except *individuation*, which can be *explained* by *compresence*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *bundle* *theory* is *preferable*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *avoids* the *mystery* of the *substratum*, and it *fits* the *empiricist* *tradition*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *compresence* *regress*** (Ch. 15, §3.2). The *relation* of *compresence* *requires* a *further* *relation* to *bind* it to the *tropes*, and the *regress* is *infinite*.

**(ii) The *identity* *of* *indiscernibles*. The *bundle* *theory* *implies* that *two* *objects* with *exactly* the *same* *properties* are *the* *same* *object*. The *implication* is *contested* (the *principle* is *false* if *haecceities* are *possible*; Ch. 16, §4.4).

**(iii) The *modal* *properties*. The *bundle* *theory* *must* *include* the *modal* *properties* (the *dispositions*, the *powers*) in the *bundle*, and the *inclusion* *raises* the *problem* of *whether* the *modal* *properties* are *grounded* in the *categorical* *ones* (Ch. 18, §6).

### 2.4 Sortal-dependent substance

**The *thesis*.** A substance is *not* a *bare* *particular* or a *mere* *bundle*; it is a *thing* of a *sortal* *kind* — a *person*, a *tree*, a *statue* — and the *kind* *determines* the *persistence* *conditions* and the *identity* *conditions*.

**The *statement*.** Wiggins (*Sameness and Substance*, 1980; *Sameness and Substance Renewed*, 2001); Lowe (*Kinds of Being*, 1989; *The Four-Category Ontology*, 2006).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *substance* *persists* through *change*, and the *persistence* *requires* *criteria* *of* *identity*.} \\
2. & \text{The *criteria* *are* *supplied* by the *sortal* *kind*.} \\
3. & \text{The *kind* is *not* a *mere* *cluster* of *properties*; it is a *principle* of *unity* and *persistence*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Substance is *sortal-dependent*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* *explains* the *persistence* *conditions* (the *sortal* *determines* what *counts* as *the* *same* *thing*), and it *fits* the *Aristotelian* *tradition*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *sortal* *relativity* *objection***. The *same* *portion* of *matter* can *fall* under *different* *sortals* (a *statue*, a *lump* of *clay*, a *collection* of *molecules*), and the *question* of *which* *sortal* is *the* *substance* is *underdetermined*. (The *reply*: the *sortal* is *determined* by the *context* and the *interest*; the *reply* *reintroduces* the *relativity*.)

**(ii) The *sortal* *essentialism* *objection***. The *theory* *requires* *essentialism* about *kinds* (Ch. 16, §3), and the *essentialism* is *contested* (Ch. 42, §3).

### 2.5 The mystery of intrinsicality

**The *question*.** What *is* the *intrinsic* *nature* of a *substance*?

**The *problem*.** The *substratum* *theory* *says*: a *bare* *particular*, which has *no* *intrinsic* *nature* (it is *bare*). The *bundle* *theory* *says*: the *tropes*, which *have* *intrinsic* *natures* (the *qualities*), but the *natures* are *relational* if the *properties* are *dispositional* (Ch. 18, §6). The *sortal* *theory* *says*: the *kind*, which is *not* an *intrinsic* *nature* in the *ordinary* *sense*.

**The *consequence*.** The *intrinsic* *nature* of *substance* is *mysterious*. The *mystery* is *the* *motivation* for *structural* *realism* (§6) and *panpsychism* (§7).

## 3. Physicalism and Its Formulations

### 3.1 The formulations

**The *thesis*.** Everything is *physical* — or, more *precisely*, the *physical* *facts* *exhaust* the *facts*.

**The *formulations*.**

| Formulation | The *claim* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Type identity** | Every *mental* *type* is a *physical* *type* | Multiple realizability (Ch. 23, §3) |
| **Token identity** | Every *mental* *token* is a *physical* *token* | Trivial; does not *capture* the *dependence* |
| **Supervenience** | The *mental* *supervenes* on the *physical* | Does not *capture* the *direction* or the *ground* |
| **Realization** | The *mental* is *realized* by the *physical* | The *nature* of *realization* is *contested* |
| **Grounding** | The *mental* is *grounded* in the *physical* | The *nature* of *grounding* (Ch. 16, §3.3) |
| **Fundamentality** | The *physical* is *fundamental*; the *rest* is *derived* | The *definition* of *fundamental* |
| **Ontic structural realism** | The *structure* is *fundamental*; the *objects* are *derived* | The *status* of *objects* |

**The *standard* *formulation*** (Lewis 1983; Jackson 1998; Chalmers & Jackson 2001). Physicalism is the *thesis* that *every* *truth* is *entailed* by the *complete* *physical* *truth*:

$$
(\mathrm{Phys}) \quad \forall p\, (\text{if } p \text{ is true, then } \Box(\text{Phys} \to p))
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *formulation* *captures* the *dependence* *without* *committing* to *type* *identity*, and it *fits* the *supervenience* *intuition*.

**The *problem*.** The *formulation* *presupposes* a *notion* of *“the* *complete* *physical* *truth”*, and the *notion* is *problematic*: what *counts* as *physical*?

### 3.2 Hempel's dilemma

**The *dilemma* (Hempel 1969; Chomsky 1972).**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{"Physical" is *defined* either by *current* *physics* or by an *ideal* *future* *physics*.} \\
2. & \text{If *defined* by *current* *physics*, physicalism is *false* (current physics is *incomplete*).} \\
3. & \text{If *defined* by *ideal* *future* *physics*, physicalism is *unintelligible* (we do not know what the ideal physics will contain).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{Physicalism is either *false* or *unintelligible*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *ostensive* *response*** (Lewis 1983). “Physical” is *defined* *ostensively*: the *physical* is the *kind* of *thing* that *current* *physics* *describes* (the *entities*, *properties*, and *relations* that *physics* *posits*). The *definition* is *not* *circular*; it *fixes* the *reference* by *ostension*.

**(ii) The *naturalistic* *response*** (Papineau 2001; the *“via* *negativa”*). “Physical” is *defined* *negatively*: the *physical* is the *non-mental*, the *non-* *supernatural*, the *non-* *abstract*. The *definition* is *broad*, and it *avoids* the *dilemma* by *refusing* to *specify* the *content* of *physics*.

**(iii) The *empirical* *response***. Physicalism is a *hypothesis* about the *actual* *world*, and it is *confirmed* by the *success* of *physics*. The *confirmation* is *inductive*, and it is *fallible* (the *pessimistic* *meta-induction*; §6.2).

### 3.3 Non-reductive physicalism

**The *thesis*.** The *mental* is *not* *reducible* to the *physical* — there are *no* *type* *identities*, and the *special* *sciences* are *autonomous* — but the *mental* *supervenes* on the *physical*, and the *physical* is *fundamental*.

**The *statement*.** Fodor (“Special Sciences,” 1974); Putnam (“The Nature of Mental States,” 1967); Davidson (“Mental Events,” 1970); Yablo (“Mental Causation,” 1992).

**The *structure*.**

| Level | The *entities* | The *relations* |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Particles, fields | Causal laws |
| **Chemical** | Molecules, reactions | Causal laws |
| **Biological** | Cells, organisms | Causal laws |
| **Psychological** | Beliefs, desires | Causal laws |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *special* *sciences* have *autonomous* *laws*, *irreducible* to *physics*.} \\
2. & \text{The *autonomy* *does* not *imply* a *new* *substance*; the *levels* *supervene* on the *physical*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *world* is *hierarchically* *organized*, *with* the *physical* as the *base*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *causal* *exclusion* *problem* (§4). If the *physical* *level* is *causally* *closed*, how can the *higher* *levels* have *causal* *efficacy*?

**(ii) The *supervenience* *problem*. Supervenience *does* not *explain* the *dependence*; it *states* it. The *grounding* *account* (Ch. 16, §3.3) *attempts* to *explain* it, but the *explanation* is *contested*.

### 3.4 Ontic structural realism as physicalism

**The *thesis*.** The *fundamental* *level* of *reality* is a *structure*, and the *objects* are *nodes* in the *structure*. The *view* is a *form* of *physicalism* (everything is *physical* *structure*), and it is a *form* of *structuralism* (Ch. 15, §7.2).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Physics *describes* the *world* *in* *terms* of *structure* (§1.5).} \\
2. & \text{The *best* *interpretation* of *physics* *treats* the *structure* as *fundamental*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Physicalism is *structuralism*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequences*.** The *view* *avoids* the *Hempel* *dilemma* (the *physical* is the *structural*, not the *particulate*), and it *fits* the *structural* *realist* *account* of *science* (§6).

**The *objection*.** The *view* *is* *unstable* (Ch. 15, §7.2): a *structure* without *objects* is *not* a *structure*; it is a *form* with *no* *content*. (The *reply*: the *form* is the *content*; the *distinction* is a *legacy* of the *substance* *ontology* that *structuralism* *rejects*.)

## 4. The Causal Exclusion Problem

### 4.1 Kim's argument

The *most* *influential* *objection* to *non-reductive* *physicalism* is the **causal* *exclusion* *argument***, due to Jaegwon Kim (“Mechanism, Purpose, and Explanatory Exclusion,” 1989; *Mind in a Physical World*, 1998; *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough*, 2005).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *physical* *domain* is *causally* *closed*: every *physical* *event* has a *sufficient* *physical* *cause*.} \\
2. & \text{The *mental* *event* } M \text{ *causes* the *physical* *event* } P. \\
3. & \text{The *physical* *event* } P \text{ has a *sufficient* *physical* *cause* } P^*. \\
4. & \text{If } M \text{ *causes* } P, \text{ and } P^* \text{ *causes* } P, \text{ the *cause* is *overdetermined*.} \\
5. & \text{Overdetermination is *implausible* (the *mental* *cause* is *excluded* by the *physical* *cause*).} \\
\hline
6. & \therefore \text{The *mental* *event* } M \text{ does *not* *cause* } P \text{ (or *epiphenomenalism* is *true*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *structure*.** The *argument* *assumes* (i) *causal* *closure*, (ii) *the* *reality* of *mental* *causation*, and (iii) the *exclusion* *principle* (if an *event* has a *sufficient* *cause*, no *other* *cause* is *needed*). The *three* *assumptions* are *individually* *plausible*, and *together* they *generate* the *exclusion*.

**The *consequences*.** The *argument* *threatens* *non-reductive* *physicalism*: the *mental* is *not* *reducible*, and it is *not* *causally* *efficacious*. The *dilemma* is *between* *reductionism* (the *mental* is *identical* to the *physical*) and *epiphenomenalism* (the *mental* is *causally* *idle*).

### 4.2 The responses

**(i) The *reductionist* *response*** (Kim 1998, 2005). The *mental* *is* *reducible* to the *physical*, and the *reduction* *solves* the *exclusion* *problem*. The *cost* is the *loss* of the *autonomy* of the *special* *sciences*.

**(ii) The *emergentist* *response***. The *mental* *emerges* from the *physical*, and the *emergent* *properties* have *causal* *powers* *not* *reducible* to the *physical*. The *cost* is the *mystery* of *emergence* (Ch. 21, §2).

**(iii) The *interventionist* *response*** (Woodward 2008; List & Menzies 2009). The *exclusion* *argument* *assumes* a *counterfactual* *account* of *causation*, and the *interventionist* *account* *allows* *multiple* *causes* at *different* *levels*. The *cost* is the *pluralism* of Ch. 18, §5.

**(iv) The *determinable* *response*** (Yablo 1992). The *mental* *properties* are *determinables* of *physical* *determinates* (the *mental* *property* *being* *in* *pain* is a *determinable*; the *physical* *property* *C-fiber* *firing* is a *determinate*). The *determinable* *is* *not* *excluded* by the *determinate*; it is *realized* by it. The *cost* is the *mystery* of *determinables*.

**(v) The *grounding* *response*** (Ch. 16, §3.3). The *mental* is *grounded* in the *physical*, and the *grounding* *relation* *explains* the *dependence* *without* *requiring* *causal* *efficacy* at the *mental* *level*. The *cost* is the *mystery* of *grounding*.

### 4.3 The autonomy of the special sciences

**The *thesis*** (Fodor 1974). The *special* *sciences* are *autonomous*: their *laws* are *not* *reducible* to *physics*, and their *explanations* are *irreducible*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *special* *sciences* *classify* *events* by *properties* that *are* *multiply* *realizable*.} \\
2. & \text{The *multiple* *realizability* *blocks* *type* *reduction*.} \\
3. & \text{The *explanations* *of* the *special* *sciences* *are* *causal* *explanations* *at* their *own* *level*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *special* *sciences* are *autonomous*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objections*.** (i) The *autonomy* is *epistemic*, not *metaphysical* (the *properties* *supervene* on the *physical*). (ii) The *causal* *exclusion* *argument* *threatens* the *autonomy*. (iii) The *multiple* *realizability* *argument* *is* *contested* (some *properties* *are* *reducible*; Ch. 21, §5).

### 4.4 The connection to Ch. 21

The *causal* *exclusion* *problem* is the *central* *problem* of Ch. 21 (reduction and emergence), and it is the *bridge* between the *philosophy* of *mind* (Part IV) and the *metaphysics* of *science* (Part VI). The *chapter's* *position* is *pluralist*: the *levels* are *real*, the *causation* is *real* at *each* *level*, and the *exclusion* *argument* *fails* *because* it *assumes* a *monist* *account* of *causation* (Ch. 18, §5).

## 5. Quantum Mechanics and the Metaphysics of Physics

### 5.1 The measurement problem

**The *problem*.** Quantum mechanics *describes* the *state* of a *system* by a *wavefunction* $|\psi\rangle$ that *evolves* according to the *Schrödinger* *equation*:

$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{H} |\psi(t)\rangle
$$

The *equation* is *linear* and *deterministic*: the *wavefunction* *evolves* into a *superposition* of *states*. But *measurement* *yields* a *definite* *outcome* — a *single* *eigenvalue* — and the *transition* from *superposition* to *definite* *outcome* is *not* *described* by the *equation*.

**The *problem* *stated*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *wavefunction* *evolves* *linearly* (Schrödinger).} \\
2. & \text{The *wavefunction* *after* *measurement* is a *superposition*.} \\
3. & \text{The *measurement* *outcome* is *definite*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *theory* is *incomplete* or the *evolution* is *not* *linear* (or *both*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *interpretations* are *responses* to the *problem*.

### 5.2 The interpretations

| Interpretation | The *solution* | The *metaphysics* |
|---|---|---|
| **Copenhagen** | The *wavefunction* is *epistemic*; the *collapse* is *instrumental* | Anti-realist; the *classical* *apparatus* is *fundamental* |
| **Many-worlds** | The *wavefunction* is *ontic*; the *collapse* is an *illusion* (branching) | Realist; *all* *branches* are *real* |
| **Bohmian mechanics** | The *wavefunction* *guides* *particles* with *definite* *positions* | Realist; *deterministic*; *nonlocal* |
| **GRW (spontaneous collapse)** | The *wavefunction* *collapses* *randomly* (a *stochastic* *modification*) | Realist; *indeterministic*; *nonlinear* |
| **QBism** | The *wavefunction* is a *subjective* *degree* of *belief* | Anti-realist; *personalist* |
| **Relational QM** | The *wavefunction* is *relative* to an *observer* | Realist; *relational* |

**The *summary*.**

- **Copenhagen** *denies* that the *wavefunction* is a *description* of *reality*; it is a *tool* for *prediction*. The *metaphysics* is *anti-realist* (or *instrumentalist*), and the *consequence* is that *quantum* *mechanics* *does* not *describe* a *world* *independent* of *observation*.
- **Many-worlds** *accepts* the *wavefunction* as *real* and *denies* the *collapse*; the *apparent* *collapse* is *branching* into *many* *worlds*. The *metaphysics* is *realist*, and the *consequence* is a *radical* *pluralism* of *worlds* (Ch. 16, §2.2).
- **Bohmian mechanics** *adds* *hidden* *variables* (the *positions* of the *particles*) that *are* *definite* *at* *all* *times*, and the *wavefunction* *guides* them. The *metaphysics* is *realist* and *deterministic*, and the *consequence* is a *nonlocal* *causal* *structure* (the *pilot* *wave*).
- **GRW** *modifies* the *Schrödinger* *equation* with a *stochastic* *collapse* *term*. The *metaphysics* is *realist* and *indeterministic*, and the *consequence* is a *testable* *deviation* from *quantum* *mechanics* (the *collapse* *rate*).
- **QBism** *treats* the *wavefunction* as a *subjective* *credence* (a *Bayesian* *degree* of *belief*; Ch. 10, §4). The *metaphysics* is *anti-realist*, and the *consequence* is that *quantum* *mechanics* *does* not *describe* a *world* at all; it *guides* *action*.
- **Relational QM** (Rovelli 1996) *treats* the *wavefunction* as *relative* to an *observer*, and the *consequence* is that *there* is *no* *absolute* *state* *of* the *world*; the *state* is *always* *relative*.

### 5.3 The wavefunction: ontic versus epistemic

**The *question*.** Is the *wavefunction* a *representation* of *reality* (ontic) or a *representation* of *knowledge* (epistemic)?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *wavefunction* is | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **ψ-ontic** | A *real* *physical* *entity* | Everett; Bohm; GRW; the *wavefunction* *realists* |
| **ψ-epistemic** | A *representation* of *knowledge* (a *probability* *distribution*) | QBism; the *epistemic* *interpreters* |
| **ψ-nomological** | A *law* of *nature* | the *nomological* *interpreters* (Albert; Goldstein & Zanghì) |

**The *PBR* *theorem*** (Pusey, Barrett & Rudolph, “On the Reality of the Quantum State,” 2012). Under *weak* *assumptions* (independence of the *systems*, no *superluminal* *signalling*), the *wavefunction* *must* be *ontic*: two *systems* with the *same* *wavefunction* *cannot* be *distinguished* by any *measurement*, and the *epistemic* *interpretation* *requires* *distinguishability*.

**The *consequence*.** The *theorem* *rules* out a *broad* *class* of *ψ-epistemic* *interpretations*, and it *supports* the *ψ-ontic* *view*. The *interpretation* of the *theorem* is *contested*, and the *ψ-epistemic* *interpretations* *survive* only in *restricted* *forms* (the *QBist* *view* *denies* the *assumptions* of the *theorem*).

**The *significance*.** The *debate* *bears* on the *metaphysics* of *quantum* *mechanics*: if the *wavefunction* is *ontic*, the *world* *contains* a *fundamental* *entity* (the *wavefunction*) that is *not* *a* *particle* or a *field* in the *classical* *sense*. The *entity* *is* a *challenge* to the *substance* *ontology* of §2.

### 5.4 Entanglement and non-separability

**The *phenomenon*.** Two *particles* in an *entangled* *state* (e.g., the *singlet* *state* of two *spin-1/2* *particles*) have *correlated* *properties*: the *measurement* of one *instantaneously* *determines* the *outcome* of the *other*, *regardless* of the *distance*.

**The *Bell* *theorem*** (Bell 1964). *No* *local* *hidden-variable* *theory* can *reproduce* the *quantum* *correlations*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *quantum* *correlations* *violate* the *Bell* *inequalities*.} \\
2. & \text{The *violation* is *experimentally* *confirmed* (Aspect et al. 1982; the *loophole-free* *tests*, 2015).} \\
3. & \text{No *local* *hidden-variable* *theory* can *reproduce* the *violations*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *world* is *nonlocal* (or *non-separable*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *metaphysical* *implications*.**

**(i) Nonlocal causation.** The *correlations* *suggest* that *distant* *events* are *causally* *connected* (a *nonlocal* *causal* *structure*). The *view* *conflicts* with *special* *relativity* (which *forbids* *superluminal* *causation*).

**(ii) Non-separability.** The *correlations* *suggest* that the *relata* are *not* *independent* *entities*; they are *aspects* of a *single* *entangled* *whole* (the *holistic* *view*). The *view* *supports* *ontic* *structuralism* (Ch. 15, §7.2): the *structure* (the *entangled* *state*) is *prior* to the *objects*.

**(iii) The *“peaceful* *coexistence”***. The *nonlocality* *does* not *allow* *signalling*, and the *no-signalling* *theorem* *preserves* *relativity* *at* the *level* of *observable* *statistics*. The *metaphysical* *nonlocality* *remains*, but the *operational* *relativity* is *intact*.

### 5.5 What each interpretation implies

**The *table*.**

| Interpretation | Determinism | Locality | Realism | The *metaphysics* of *substance* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Copenhagen** | Indeterministic | Nonlocal (or *instrumental*) | Anti-realist | Classical *apparatus* is *fundamental* |
| **Many-worlds** | Deterministic | Nonlocal | Realist | *Branching* *worlds*; *structures* |
| **Bohmian** | Deterministic | Nonlocal | Realist | *Particles* with *definite* *positions* |
| **GRW** | Indeterministic | Nonlocal | Realist | *Wavefunction* *collapse* |
| **QBism** | Indeterministic | Local (subjective) | Anti-realist | No *world* *described* |
| **Relational** | Indeterministic | Relational | Realist | *Relational* *states* |

**The *consequences*.**
- **Determinism** is *interpretation-dependent*: Bohmian and many-worlds are *deterministic*; Copenhagen, GRW, and QBism are *indeterministic*.
- **Locality** is *violated* by *all* *realist* *interpretations* (the *Bell* *theorem*). The *anti-realist* *interpretations* *evade* the *conclusion* by *denying* that the *theory* *describes* a *world*.
- **Realism** is *contested*: the *anti-realist* *interpretations* (Copenhagen, QBism) *deny* that the *theory* *describes* an *observer-independent* *reality*.
- **Substance** is *transformed*: the *classical* *substance* *ontology* (particles with *definite* *properties*) *survives* in *Bohmian* *mechanics*; it is *abandoned* in *many-worlds*, *relational* QM, and *ontic* *structuralism*.

## 6. Structural Realism and the Pessimistic Meta-Induction

### 6.1 The no-miracles argument

**The *argument*** (Putnam 1975; Boyd 1983; Psillos 1999). The *success* of *science* is *miraculous* unless *scientific* *theories* *approximately* *describe* the *world*. The *best* *explanation* of the *success* is *realism*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Scientific *theories* are *empirically* *successful*.} \\
2. & \text{The *success* would be a *miracle* if the *theories* were *not* *approximately* *true*.} \\
3. & \text{We should *not* *accept* *miracles*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *theories* are *approximately* *true* (scientific *realism*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *explains* the *success* *without* *positing* a *miracle*, and it *supports* the *realist* *interpretation* of *science*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* is an *IBE* (Ch. 10, §6), and the *IBE* is *contested* (the *circularity* *problem*; the *van* *Fraassen* *challenge*).

### 6.2 The pessimistic meta-induction

**The *argument*** (Laudan 1981; the *historical* *induction*).

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *history* of *science* is a *history* of *successful* *theories* that *turned* *out* to be *false* (phlogiston, caloric, the *luminiferous* *ether*, *Newtonian* *mechanics*).} \\
2. & \text{Our *current* *theories* are *probably* *false* (by *induction*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Scientific *realism* is *probably* *false*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *challenge*.** The *argument* *undermines* the *no-miracles* *argument*: the *success* of *science* *does* not *imply* *truth*, because *false* *theories* *have* been *successful*.

### 6.3 Epistemic and ontic structural realism

**The *structural* *realist* *response*** (Worrall 1989). The *history* of *science* *shows* that the *content* of *theories* *changes*, but the *structure* *is* *preserved*. The *Fresnel* *theory* of *light* was *false* (there is *no* *ether*), but its *equations* *survive* in *Maxwell’s* *theory*. The *realism* should be *about* the *structure*, not the *entities*.

**The *two* *forms*.**

| Form | The *claim* | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Epistemic structural realism (ESR)** | We *know* the *structure* of the *world*, but *not* the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *entities* | Worrall; Psillos (in a *qualified* *form*) |
| **Ontic structural realism (OSR)** | The *structure* *is* *all* *there* *is*; there are *no* *objects* *over* *and* *above* the *structure* | Ladyman; French; Wallace |

**The *ESR* *argument*.** The *structure* *is* *preserved* *across* *theory* *change*, and the *intrinsic* *nature* is *not*. The *realism* *should* *be* *about* the *preserved* *part*.

**The *OSR* *argument*.** The *ESR* *is* *unstable*: if we *know* only the *structure*, and the *intrinsic* *nature* is *unknowable*, the *intrinsic* *nature* is *an* *idle* *posit*. The *OSR* *eliminates* the *intrinsic* *nature*: the *structure* *is* the *reality*.

### 6.4 Newman's problem

**The *problem*** (Newman 1928; Ketland 2004). The *ESR* *claims* that the *structure* *is* *preserved*, but the *structure* is *defined* *only* *up* to *isomorphism*. The *claim* that the *structure* is *preserved* is *trivial*: *any* *set* of the *right* *cardinality* *can* *realize* *any* *structure*, and the *claim* *does* *not* *distinguish* the *actual* *structure* from *arbitrary* *relabellings*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *structure* is *specified* *only* *up* to *isomorphism* (the *Ramsey* *sentence*).} \\
2. & \text{The *isomorphism* *does* not *pick* *out* a *unique* *structure*.} \\
3. & \text{The *claim* that the *structure* *is* *preserved* is *vacuous* (any *structure* *can* be *preserved* by *some* *interpretation*).} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *ESR* *is* *uninformative*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.** (i) The *OSR* *avoids* the *problem* by *identifying* the *structure* with the *world* (there is *no* *underlying* *nature* to be *relabelled*). (ii) The *ESR* *can* *appeal* to *natural* *properties* (Ch. 15, §5.1) to *distinguish* the *structures*; the *appeal* *is* *contested*.

### 6.5 The current state

**The *debate*.** The *structural* *realist* *position* is *the* *most* *influential* *response* to the *pessimistic* *meta-induction*, and it is *contested*.

**The *positions***.

| Position | The *claim* | The *problem* |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific realism (entity)** | The *theories* *approximately* *describe* the *entities* | The *pessimistic* *meta-induction* |
| **ESR** | The *structure* is *preserved*; the *nature* is *unknowable* | Newman's problem |
| **OSR** | The *structure* is *fundamental* | The *status* of *objects* |
| **Constructive empiricism** | The *theories* are *empirically* *adequate* (van Fraassen) | The *no-miracles* argument |
| **Instrumentalism** | The *theories* are *tools* for *prediction* | The *success* is *unexplained* |

**The *honest* *position*.** The *structural* *realist* *account* is the *most* *defensible* *response* to the *pessimistic* *meta-induction*, and it is *not* *decisive*. The *choice* *depends* on the *weight* given to *realism*, *parsimony*, and *explanatory* power (Ch. 4, §3).

## 7. Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism

### 7.1 Russellian monism

**The *view*.** The *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *mental* (or *proto-mental*). The *view* *combines* the *structural* *realist* *conclusion* (physics *describes* only *structure*) with the *panpsychist* *thesis* (the *intrinsic* *nature* is *experience*).

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Physics *describes* the *relational* *structure* of the *world* (§1.5).} \\
2. & \text{The *structure* *requires* an *intrinsic* *nature* to be *instantiated*.} \\
3. & \text{The *intrinsic* *nature* is *not* *described* by *physics*.} \\
4. & \text{The *only* *intrinsic* *nature* we *know* is *experience* (Ch. 12, §1).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{The *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *experience* (or *proto-experience*).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *statement*.** Russell (*The Analysis of Mind*, 1921; *The Analysis of Matter*, 1927); Chalmers (“Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism,” 2015); Goff (*Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 2017); Strawson (“Realistic Monism,” 2006); the *contemporary* *defenders*: Alter & Nagasawa (2015), the *essays* in *Consciousness in the Physical World* (2015).

**The *argument*.**

**(i) The *intrinsic* *nature* *argument***. The *physical* *structure* *requires* an *intrinsic* *nature*; the *only* *intrinsic* *nature* *known* is *experience*; therefore the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *experience*.

**(ii) The *hard* *problem* *argument***. The *hard* *problem* of *consciousness* (Ch. 24, §2) *requires* a *fundamental* *place* for *experience*; the *panpsychist* *provides* it.

**(iii) The *combination* *argument***. The *panpsychist* *avoids* the *emergence* *mystery*: *experience* is *not* *emergent*; it is *fundamental*.

### 7.2 The combination problem

**The *problem*** (James 1890; the *contemporary* *formulation*: Chalmers 2015, Ch. 6; Goff 2017, Ch. 5). If *fundamental* *entities* have *experiences* (protoconsciousness), how do *micro-experiences* *combine* into a *unified* *macro-experience* (a *human* *mind*)?

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Panpsychism: *fundamental* *entities* *have* *experiences*.} \\
2. & \text{Human *minds* *have* *unified* *experiences*.} \\
3. & \text{The *unity* *requires* a *combination* *relation* (the *micro-experiences* *combine* into the *macro-experience*).} \\
4. & \text{The *combination* *relation* is *mysterious* (it is *as* *mysterious* as *emergence*).} \\
\hline
5. & \therefore \text{Panpsychism *does* not *solve* the *hard* *problem*; it *relocates* it.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *cosmopsychism* *response*** (Goff 2017; Shani 2015). The *fundamental* *entity* is the *cosmos*, and the *micro-experiences* are *derived* (the *whole* is *prior* to the *parts*). The *view* *reverses* the *combination* *problem*: the *question* *becomes* how the *cosmic* *experience* *differentiates* into *individual* *minds*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *is* *unstable* (the *differentiation* *is* as *mysterious* as the *combination*).

**(ii) The *panprotopsychism* *response***. The *fundamental* *entities* have *proto-experiential* *properties* (not *full* *experience*), and the *proto-properties* *combine* into *experience*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *is* *unstable*: the *proto-properties* are *not* *experience*, and the *emergence* of *experience* is *as* *mysterious* as *before*.

**(iii) The *phenomenal* *bonding* *response*** (Seager 2016). The *combination* is *not* a *relation* *between* *experiences*; it is a *form* of *unity* *that* *constitutes* the *macro-experience*. The *reply* *is* *contested*, and it *reintroduces* the *combination* *mystery*.

### 7.3 Panpsychism as a response to structuralism

**The *argument*.** The *structural* *realist* *conclusion* — that physics *describes* only the *structure*, not the *intrinsic* *nature* — *leaves* the *intrinsic* *nature* *unexplained*. The *panpsychist* *fills* the *gap*.

**The *structure*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Structural *realism*: physics *describes* the *structure*, not the *intrinsic* *nature*.} \\
2. & \text{The *intrinsic* *nature* *must* be *something* (the *structure* *requires* *instantiation*).} \\
3. & \text{The *only* *intrinsic* *nature* *known* is *experience*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *intrinsic* *nature* is *experience* (panpsychism) or *proto-experience* (panprotopsychism).}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *argument* *connects* the *structural* *realist* *conclusion* to the *panpsychist* *thesis*, and it *explains* why the *two* are *natural* *allies*.

**The *objection*.** The *argument* *assumes* that the *intrinsic* *nature* *must* be *knowable* (by *analogy* with *experience*), and the *assumption* is *contested*. (The *alternative*: the *intrinsic* *nature* is *unknowable* — the *“Ramseyan* *humility”* — and the *panpsychist* *inference* is a *form* of *anthropomorphism*.)

### 7.4 Not a mere curiosity

**The *status*.** Panpsychism is a *minority* *position*, but it is *taken* *seriously*, and it is *one* of the *most* *active* *areas* of *contemporary* *metaphysics*.

**The *reasons*.**

1. **The *hard* *problem* is *unsolved*.** The *physicalist* *accounts* (functionalism, higher-order theories, illusionism) *have* *not* *solved* the *hard* *problem* (Ch. 24, §5), and the *panpsychist* *offers* a *fundamental* *place* for *experience*.
2. **The *structural* *realist* *conclusion* is *widely* *accepted*.** The *conclusion* *leaves* the *intrinsic* *nature* *unexplained*, and the *panpsychist* *fills* the *gap*.
3. **The *argument* is *a* *priori* *and* *empirically* *neutral*.** The *panpsychist* *argument* *does* not *depend* on *empirical* *discoveries*; it *depends* on the *nature* of *physical* *description*.
4. **The *combination* *problem* is *recognised*.** The *panpsychists* *acknowledge* the *problem*, and the *debate* about the *solution* is *productive*.

**The *prospects*.** The *panpsychist* *programme* is *promising* and *incomplete*. The *combination* *problem* is the *central* *challenge*, and it is *unresolved*.

## 8. The State of the Debate

### 8.1 What is settled

- **The corpuscularian view is untenable.** The *field* *revolution* and *QFT* *replace* the *particle* *ontology* with a *field* *ontology*.
- **Physics describes structure, not intrinsic nature.** The *structural* *realist* *conclusion* is *widely* *accepted*, and it is *the* *most* *influential* *interpretation* of *physics*.
- **The substratum theory is on the defensive.** The *bare* *particular* is *mysterious*, and the *bundle* and *sortal* *theories* are *the* *main* *alternatives*.
- **The causal exclusion problem is serious.** The *argument* *threatens* *non-reductive* *physicalism*, and the *responses* are *contested*.
- **The quantum interpretations are empirically equivalent.** The *choice* *among* them *depends* on *metaphysical* *criteria*, not on *experiment* (except for the *GRW* *deviations*).
- **Structural realism is the most influential response to the pessimistic meta-induction.** The *ESR/OSR* *distinction* is *central*, and *Newman's* *problem* is *serious*.
- **Panpsychism is a live option.** The *view* *fills* the *gap* left by *structural* *realism*, and it is *taken* *seriously*.

### 8.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *substance*.** Is it a *substratum*, a *bundle*, or a *sortal-dependent* *unity*?
- **The *formulation* of *physicalism*.** Is it *supervenience*, *grounding*, or *fundamentality*? How is the *Hempel* *dilemma* *answered*?
- **The *causal* *exclusion* *problem*.** Is the *mental* *causally* *efficacious*?
- **The *interpretation* of *quantum* *mechanics*.** Is the *wavefunction* *ontic* or *epistemic*? Is the *world* *deterministic* and *nonlocal*?
- **The *structural* *realist* *position*.** Is the *ESR* or the *OSR* *correct*? How is *Newman's* *problem* *answered*?
- **The *panpsychist* *combination* *problem*.** How do *micro-experiences* *combine* into a *macro-experience*?

### 8.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a *case* *study* in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between *substance* and *property*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — Eddington's *two* *tables*, the *quantum* *measurement* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *wavefunction*, the *Bell* *inequalities*, the *structural* *realist* *formalism* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *matter* is a *field* in *transition*. The *corpuscularian* *view* is *dead*; the *structural* *realist* *view* is *dominant* but *incomplete*; the *panpsychist* *alternative* is *live* but *contested*; the *quantum* *interpretations* are *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled answer*, but a *sharper understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 8.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Substratum theory** | Substance is a bare particular | Individuation | Mystery of bareness |
| **Bundle theory** | Substance is a bundle of properties | Empiricist; parsimonious | Compresence regress |
| **Sortal theory** | Substance is a thing of a kind | Persistence conditions | Sortal relativity |
| **Type identity** | Mental types are physical types | Simple; reductive | Multiple realizability |
| **Non-reductive physicalism** | Mental supervenes on physical | Autonomy of special sciences | Causal exclusion |
| **Ontic structural realism** | Structure is fundamental | Fits physics; avoids Hempel | Status of objects |
| **Copenhagen** | Wavefunction is epistemic | Fits practice | Anti-realist |
| **Many-worlds** | Wavefunction is ontic; branching | Realist; deterministic | Profligate |
| **Bohmian** | Hidden variables with definite positions | Realist; deterministic | Nonlocal; awkward |
| **GRW** | Spontaneous collapse | Realist; testable | The collapse mechanism is ad hoc |
| **QBism** | Wavefunction is subjective | Fits Bayesianism | Anti-realist |
| **ESR** | Structure is preserved; nature is unknowable | Fits theory change | Newman's problem |
| **OSR** | Structure is all there is | Avoids Newman; fits entanglement | Status of objects |
| **Panpsychism** | Intrinsic nature is experience | Fills the gap; fundamental | Combination problem |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *wavefunction* and the *substance* ontology.** If the *wavefunction* is *ontic*, the *fundamental* *entity* is not a *particle* or a *field* in the *classical* sense; it is a *state* in a *high-dimensional* *configuration* *space*. The *view* *challenges* the *substance* *ontology* of §2, and it *supports* *ontic* *structuralism*. The *debate* is *connected* to the *interpretation* of *quantum* *mechanics* (§5) and to the *theory* of *properties* (Ch. 15, §5).

**2. The *measurement* *problem* and *consciousness*.** Some *interpretations* (Wigner; the *consciousness-causes-collapse* *view*) *invoke* *consciousness* to *solve* the *measurement* *problem*. The *view* is *contested* (it *makes* *consciousness* *fundamental* and *causally* *efficacious*), and it is *connected* to *panpsychism* (§7) and to the *hard* *problem* (Ch. 24, §2).

**3. The *quantum* *gravity* *problem*.** The *attempt* to *reconcile* *quantum* *mechanics* and *general* *relativity* *raises* *metaphysical* *questions* about *spacetime*, *causation*, and *substance* (the *problem* of *time*; the *problem* of *background* *independence*). The *debate* is *technical* and *speculative* (Ch. 41, §4).

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *matter*.** The *Buddhist* *Abhidharma* *tradition* *developed* a *radical* *process* *ontology* (the *dharmas* are *momentary* *events*, not *substances*), and the *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* *tradition* *developed* a *realist* *substance* *ontology* (the *nine* *substances*, including *earth*, *water*, *fire*, *air*, *ether*, *time*, *space*, *self*, *mind*). The *traditions* *anticipate* the *contemporary* *debate* *between* *substance* and *process*, and they are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *ethics* of *matter*.** The *choice* of *ontology* *has* *ethical* *consequences*: the *view* that *matter* is *passive* (the *mechanistic* *picture*) *supports* the *domination* of *nature*; the *view* that *matter* is *active* (the *dispositionalist* *picture*) *supports* a *different* *relation* to the *world* (the *ecological* *view*; Ch. 51, §6). The *ethics* of *ontology* is *underdeveloped*, and it is *urgent*.

## Summary

- **Russell's problem** — that physics *describes* only the *structure* of the *world*, not its *intrinsic* *nature* — is the *central* *problem* of the chapter. It is *restated* for the *quantum* age (Eddington's *two* *tables*), and it is the *ancestor* of *structural* *realism*.
- **The *corpuscularian* *ontology** is *superseded* by the *field* *ontology*. In *QFT*, the *fundamental* *entity* is the *field*, and the *particle* is an *excitation* (a *quantum*). The *vacuum* is *rich*, and the *fields* are *entangled*.
- **The *substance* *theories** — *substratum*, *bundle*, *sortal* — are *all* *live*, and *all* *costly*. The *substratum* is *mysterious*; the *bundle* *faces* the *compresence* *regress*; the *sortal* *faces* the *relativity* *problem*.
- **The *formulations* of *physicalism** — *type* *identity*, *token* *identity*, *supervenience*, *grounding*, *fundamentality* — are *all* *contested*. **Hempel's dilemma** is the *central* *challenge*, and the *responses* are *available* but *costly*.
- **The *causal* *exclusion* *problem** (Kim) *threatens* *non-reductive* *physicalism*. The *responses* (reductionism, emergentism, interventionism, determinables, grounding) are *all* *contested*.
- **The *quantum* *interpretations** — Copenhagen, many-worlds, Bohmian, GRW, QBism, relational — are *empirically* *equivalent* and *metaphysically* *divergent*. The *PBR* *theorem* *supports* the *ψ-ontic* *view*, and the *Bell* *theorem* *establishes* the *nonlocality* of the *world* (on *realist* *interpretations*).
- **Structural realism** is the *most* *influential* *response* to the *pessimistic* *meta-induction*. The *ESR* *faces* *Newman's* *problem*; the *OSR* *eliminates* the *intrinsic* *nature*.
- **Panpsychism** is a *live* *option*: the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *physical* is *experience* (or *proto-experience*). The *combination* *problem* is the *central* *challenge*, and it is *unresolved*.
- **The *state* of the debate**: the *corpuscularian* *view* is *dead*; the *structural* *realist* *view* is *dominant* but *incomplete*; the *panpsychist* *alternative* is *live*; the *quantum* *interpretations* are *unresolved*.
