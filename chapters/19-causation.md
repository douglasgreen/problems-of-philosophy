# Chapter 18 — Causation, Powers, and Laws of Nature

> “The word ‘cause’ is so inextricably bound up with misleading associations as to make its complete extrusion from the philosophical vocabulary desirable.”
> — Bertrand Russell, “On the Notion of Cause” (1912)

> “Causation is the cement of the universe.”
> — J. L. Mackie, *The Cement of the Universe* (1974), title

> “To ask for the cause of an event is to ask for the thing that made the difference, and to ask for the thing that made the difference is to ask what would have happened without it.”
> — the counterfactual tradition, in a sentence

---

Chapter 17 ended with the *arrow* of time. This chapter asks what *fills* the arrow: **causation** — the relation that makes the past *fix* the future, the reason that explains *why* things happen, and the concept on which science, law, medicine, and ordinary life all depend.

The chapter opens with a *challenge* that has shaped the debate for a century. Russell argued, in 1912, that the concept of cause is a *relic*: physics has no use for it, and philosophy should *extrude* it. The challenge was *half* right. Fundamental physics does not *need* the concept — the laws of physics are *symmetric* equations, not causal *relations* — but the *special sciences* (biology, medicine, economics, psychology), the *law*, and *ordinary* *reasoning* cannot do without it. The chapter's task is to *reconcile* the two facts: causation is *real* in the *domains* where it is used, and it is *not* a *fundamental* *feature* of the *physical* *world*.

The reconciliation takes the form of a **pluralism**: there are (at least) *two* concepts of causation — **difference-making** and **production** — and the two are *distinct*, *irreducible*, and *both* *legitimate*. The *difference-making* concept is the concept of the *counterfactual* and *interventionist* tradition; the *production* concept is the concept of the *process* and *mechanism* tradition. The *pluralism* is the *most* *defensible* *position* in the *contemporary* *debate*, and it is the *position* this chapter defends.

The chapter proceeds in eight movements. §1 examines Hume's legacy and the regularity theory. §2 examines the counterfactual theory. §3 examines interventionism. §4 examines process and mechanism theories. §5 examines pluralism, omissions, and the semantics of causal claims. §6 examines dispositions and powers. §7 examines laws of nature. §8 examines the practical stakes in the special sciences and the law. §9 gives an honest verdict.

---

## 1. Hume's Legacy and the Regularity Theory

### 1.1 Hume's two definitions

Hume’s treatment of causation is the *foundation* of the modern debate, and it is *more* *subtle* than the *slogan* suggests.

**The *two* *definitions*** (*Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, 1748, Section VII).

**(D1) The *regularity* *definition***:

> “An object precedent and contiguous to another, and where all the objects resembling the former are plac’d in a like relation of priority and contiguity to those objects, that resemble the latter.”

**(D2) The *psychological* *definition***:

> “An object precedent and contiguous to another, and so united with it in the imagination, that the idea of the one determines the mind to form the idea of the other, and the impression of the one to form a more lively idea of the other.”

**The *distinction*.** (D1) is *objective*: it *analyzes* causation into *regularity*, *priority*, and *contiguity*. (D2) is *subjective*: it *analyzes* the *idea* of *necessary* *connection* into the *feeling* of *determination* that *arises* when we *observe* the *regularity*.

**The *skeptical* *conclusion***. Hume *argues* that *we* *never* *observe* a *necessary* *connection* *between* *events*; we *observe* only *constant* *conjunction*. The *idea* of *necessity* is a *projection* of the *mind*, not a *feature* of the *world* (Ch. 10, §1.3).

**The *legacy*.** The *debate* *between* the *regularity* *theory* and its *rivals* is a *debate* *about* whether (D1) is *adequate* — whether the *objective* *content* of *causation* is *exhausted* by *regularity*, *priority*, and *contiguity*.

### 1.2 The regularity theory

**The *thesis*.** $c$ *causes* $e$ iff (i) $c$ *precedes* $e$; (ii) $c$ is *contiguous* with $e$; (iii) *all* *events* *of* *type* $C$ are *followed* by *events* *of* *type* $E$.

**The *statement*.** Hume; Mill (*A System of Logic*, 1843, III.v); and, in the *contemporary* *literature*, Mackie (*The Cement of the Universe*, 1974), and — in a *sophisticated* *form* — Lewis (“A Subjectivist’s Guide to Objective Chance,” 1980) and Psillos (*Causation and Explanation*, 2002).

**The *virtue*.** The *theory* is *naturalistic*: it *reduces* causation to *non-causal* *notions* (regularity, priority, contiguity), and it *fits* the *empiricist* *program*. The *theory* is *parsimonious*: it *does* not *posit* a *mysterious* *necessary* *connection*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *accidental* *regularity*.** Not every *constant* *conjunction* is *causal*. The *classic* *example*: *day* *follows* *night*, but *night* does not *cause* *day*; both are *caused* by the *rotation* of the *earth*. The *regularity* *theory* *identifies* the *conjunction* with the *causal* *relation*, and the *identification* is *false*.

**(ii) The *asymmetry* *problem*.** The *regularity* *theory* is *symmetric*: if *all* $C$s are *followed* by $E$s, then *all* $E$s are *preceded* by $C$s. But *causation* is *asymmetric*: the *flagpole*’s *height* *causes* the *shadow*’s *length*, but the *shadow*’s *length* does not *cause* the *flagpole*’s *height*. The *regularity* *theory* *cannot* *distinguish* the *direction*.

**(iii) The *problem* *of* *singular* *causation*.** The *regularity* *theory* *analyzes* *singular* *causation* in *terms* of *general* *regularities*. But we *say* that *this* *bomb* *caused* *this* *explosion* even if *no* *other* *bomb* *ever* *exploded* (the *first* *atomic* *bomb*). The *theory* *cannot* *handle* the *singular* *case* *without* *positing* a *regularity* that *may* *not* *exist*.

**(iv) The *problem* *of* *complex* *causation*.** Most *causes* are *not* *sufficient* *conditions*. A *spark* does *not* *cause* *a* *fire* *by* *itself*; it *causes* the *fire* *in* *conjunction* with *oxygen*, *fuel*, and *dryness*. The *simple* *regularity* *theory* *cannot* *handle* the *conjunction*, and Mill’s *methods* (Ch. 10, §2.1) *presuppose* the *causal* *structure* they *are* *meant* to *discover*.

### 1.3 Mackie's INUS conditions

The *most* *influential* *refinement* of the *regularity* *theory* is J. L. Mackie’s *INUS* *account* (“Causes and Conditions,” 1965; *The Cement of the Universe*, 1974).

**The *definition*.** A *cause* is an **Insufficient but Non-redundant part of an Unnecessary but Sufficient condition**.

**The *structure*.**

- The *cause* is *not* *sufficient*: the *spark* does *not* *cause* the *fire* *by* *itself*.
- The *cause* is *non-redundant*: the *spark* *makes* a *difference* — *without* it, the *fire* would *not* have *occurred* (given the *other* *conditions*).
- The *cause* is part of a *sufficient* *condition*: the *conjunction* of the *spark*, *oxygen*, *fuel*, and *dryness* is *sufficient* for the *fire*.
- The *sufficient* *condition* is *unnecessary*: the *fire* *could* have *been* *caused* *by* *other* *means* (a *match*, *lightning*, *spontaneous* *combustion*).

**The *formula*.**

$$
\text{Cause} = \text{INUS condition} = (A \wedge B \wedge C) \vee (D \wedge E) \vee \cdots
$$

where the *cause* is *one* of the *conjuncts* ($A$) in *one* of the *sufficient* *sets*.

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *handles* *complex* *causation*, and it *fits* the *legal* *notion* of *cause* (the *but-for* *test*; §8). The *account* is the *ancestor* of the *contemporary* *“actual* *causation”* *literature* (Wright 1985; Halpern & Pearl 2005).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *selection* *problem*.** The *INUS* *account* *tells* us *which* *condition* is *part* of the *sufficient* *set*, but it *does* not *tell* us *which* *condition* is *the* *cause*. The *spark*, the *oxygen*, the *fuel*, and the *dryness* are *all* *INUS* *conditions*; the *account* *cannot* *distinguish* the *cause* from the *conditions*.

**The *reply*.** The *selection* is *pragmatic*: the *cause* is the *condition* that is *abnormal*, *unexpected*, or *controllable* (Hart & Honoré 1959; §8.2). The *reply* *makes* *causation* *interest-relative*, and the *relativization* is *contested*.

**(ii) The *asymmetry* *problem*.** The *INUS* *account* is *still* *symmetric*: the *flagpole’s* *height* and the *shadow’s* *length* are *both* *INUS* *conditions* for the *other*. The *account* *cannot* *explain* the *asymmetry*.

**The *reply*.** The *asymmetry* is *grounded* in the *counterfactual* *asymmetry* (§2.4), not in the *regularity* *itself*.

### 1.4 Sophisticated regularity

The *most* *defensible* *version* of the *regularity* *theory* is *sophisticated*: the *regularities* are *not* *naive* *conjunctions*; they are the *laws* *of* the *best* *system* (Lewis 1973, 1986, 1994; §7.1).

**The *thesis*.** $c$ *causes* $e$ iff (i) $c$ *precedes* $e$; (ii) there is a *law* *of* *nature* *connecting* *events* *of* *type* $C$ to *events* *of* *type* $E$; (iii) the *law* is part of the *best* *system* of *the* *world*.

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *avoids* the *accidental* *regularity* *objection*: the *day-night* *conjunction* is *not* a *law* of the *best* *system* (it is a *consequence* of the *laws*, but it is *not* an *axiom*); the *flagpole* *conjunction* is *not* a *law*.

**The *objection*.** The *account* *inherits* the *problems* of the *best-system* *analysis* of *laws* (§7.1): the *choice* of the *best* *system* is *underdetermined*, and the *account* *may* *not* *be* *unique*.

### 1.5 The state of the regularity theory

The *regularity* *theory* is *not* *dead*, but it is *on* the *defensive*. The *naive* *version* is *untenable*; the *sophisticated* *version* is *live*, but it *faces* the *problems* of §7. The *most* *important* *legacy* of the *theory* is *methodological*: it *forced* the *debate* to *specify* the *difference* between *causation* and *mere* *regularity*, and the *specification* is the *subject* of the *rest* of the *chapter*.

## 2. The Counterfactual Theory

### 2.1 Lewis's analysis

The *most* *influential* *alternative* to the *regularity* *theory* is the **counterfactual* *analysis***, due to David Lewis (“Causation,” 1973; *Philosophical Papers*, Vol. II, 1986).

**The *thesis*.** $c$ *causes* $e$ iff, *had* $c$ *not* *occurred*, $e$ *would* *not* *have* *occurred*.

**The *formal* *statement*** (using the counterfactual conditional $\Box\!\to$):

$$
c \ \text{causes} \ e \quad \text{iff} \quad \neg c \ \Box\!\to\ \neg e
$$

**The *structure*.**

1. **The *relata*.** The *relata* are *events* (or *event* *types*), *not* *properties* or *facts*.
2. **The *comparison*.** The *counterfactual* *compares* the *actual* *world* with the *nearest* *possible* *world* in which $c$ does *not* *occur*.
3. **The *asymmetry*.** The *asymmetry* of *causation* is *grounded* in the *asymmetry* of *counterfactuals* (§2.4).
4. **The *non-redundancy*.** The *counterfactual* *captures* the *difference-making* *intuition*: the *cause* is the *thing* *without* *which* the *effect* would *not* have *occurred*.

**The *virtue*.** The *analysis* *captures* the *difference-making* *intuition*, and it *handles* the *singular* *cases*. The *analysis* is *simple*, *elegant*, and *fits* the *ordinary* *language* of *counterfactual* *reasoning*.

**The *problems*.** The *analysis* *fails* for *the* *cases* that *are* the *engine* of the *contemporary* *debate*: *pre-emption*, *overdetermination*, and *trumping*.

### 2.2 Pre-emption

**The *case* (early* *pre-emption*).** *Suzy* and *Billy* *throw* *rocks* at a *bottle*. *Suzy*’s *rock* *arrives* *first* and *shatters* the *bottle*. *Billy*’s *rock* *would* have *shattered* it if *Suzy*’s had *not*.

**The *analysis*.**

- $c$ = *Suzy*’s *throw*; $e$ = the *shattering*.
- The *counterfactual*: *had* *Suzy* *not* *thrown*, *Billy*’s *rock* *would* have *shattered* the *bottle*.
- So $\neg c \ \Box\!\to\ \neg e$ is *false* (the *bottle* would *still* have *shattered*).
- The *analysis* *implies* that *Suzy*’s *throw* is *not* a *cause* — *which* is *false*.

**The *problem*.** The *counterfactual* *analysis* *fails* for *pre-emption*: the *cause* is *not* a *counterfactual* *cause*, because the *backup* *would* have *produced* the *effect* anyway.

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *quasi-dependence* *response*** (Lewis 1986, 2000). The *cause* is *connected* to the *effect* by a *chain* of *counterfactual* *dependencies*: the *manner* of the *bottle*’s *shattering* (the *direction* of the *shards*, the *time* of the *shattering*) *depends* *counterfactually* on *Suzy*’s *throw*, even if the *occurrence* does not.

**(ii) The *influence* *response*** (Lewis 2000, 2004). $c$ *causes* $e$ iff there is a *substantial* *range* of *alterations* to $c$ that *produce* *alterations* to $e$. The *influence* *account* *handles* *pre-emption*: *Suzy*’s *throw* *influences* the *shattering* (the *faster* the *throw*, the *more* *fragments*), even though the *occurrence* is *overdetermined*.

**(iii) The *causal* *modeling* *response*** (Pearl 2000; Halpern & Pearl 2005). The *structural* *equations* *distinguish* the *actual* *cause* from the *backup*: the *equations* *encode* the *mechanism*, and the *mechanism* *selects* *Suzy*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *influence* *account* is the *most* *influential* *response*, and it is *contested*. The *counterfactual* *analysis* is *not* *dead*, but it is *refined*.

### 2.3 Overdetermination

**The *case*.** *Suzy* and *Billy* *throw* *rocks* that *arrive* *simultaneously* and *each* *shatters* the *bottle*. *Either* *rock* *alone* *would* have *shattered* it.

**The *problem*.** *Neither* *throw* is a *counterfactual* *cause*: *had* *Suzy* *not* *thrown*, *Billy*’s *rock* *would* have *shattered* the *bottle*; *had* *Billy* *not* *thrown*, *Suzy*’s would have. But *both* are *causes*.

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *denial* *of* *overdetermination***. Some *philosophers* (e.g., Bunzl 1979) *argue* that *genuine* *overdetermination* is *rare* or *impossible*; the *apparent* *cases* are *cases* of *joint* *causation*, not *independent* *causation*.

**(ii) The *influence* *account***. Both *throws* *influence* the *shattering* (they *both* *determine* the *manner*), even though *neither* is *necessary* for the *occurrence*.

**(iii) The *disjunctive* *account*** (Lewis). The *cause* is the *disjunction* of the *two* *throws*; the *disjunction* is *counterfactually* *necessary* for the *effect*.

**The *state* of the debate.** Overdetermination is *a* *serious* *problem* for the *simple* *counterfactual* *analysis*, and the *responses* are *contested*.

### 2.4 The asymmetry of causation

**The *problem*.** The *flagpole* *case*. The *height* of the *pole* *causes* the *length* of the *shadow*; the *length* of the *shadow* does *not* *cause* the *height* of the *pole*. The *counterfactuals* are *symmetric*: *had* the *pole* been *taller*, the *shadow* would have been *longer*; *had* the *shadow* been *longer*, the *pole* would have been *taller*? (The *second* *counterfactual* is *false*, but *why*?)

**Lewis’s *answer*** (“Counterfactual Dependence and Time’s Arrow,” 1979). The *counterfactuals* are *asymmetric* because of the *asymmetry* of *overdetermination*. In the *nearest* *world* where the *shadow* is *longer*, the *pole* is *not* *taller*; the *shadow* is *longer* because the *sun* is *lower*. The *asymmetry* of *counterfactuals* *grounds* the *asymmetry* of *causation*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The nearest world where the shadow is longer is a world where the sun is lower (not where the pole is taller).} \\
2. & \text{So "had the shadow been longer, the pole would have been taller" is false.} \\
3. & \text{The asymmetry of counterfactuals is grounded in the asymmetry of overdetermination.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The asymmetry of causation is grounded in the asymmetry of counterfactuals.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *answer* *presupposes* the *asymmetry* of *counterfactuals*, and the *asymmetry* is *itself* *grounded* in the *arrow* of *time* (Ch. 17, §6) — which is *statistical* and *cosmological*, not *causal*. The *circularity* is *real*, and the *counterfactual* *theory* *owes* an *account* of the *asymmetry* that *does* not *presuppose* the *causation* it is *meant* to *explain*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *asymmetry* *problem* is *unresolved*, and it is *one* of the *deepest* *problems* in the *field*. The *counterfactual* *theory* *captures* the *difference-making* *intuition*, but it *does* not *explain* the *direction*.

### 2.5 The semantics of causal claims

**The *question*.** What *are* we *doing* when we *say* “$c$ *caused* $e$”?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | What a causal claim does | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | Describes a *relation* in the world | the *realists* |
| **Counterfactual** | Asserts a *counterfactual* *dependence* | Lewis; the *difference-making* *tradition* |
| **Interventionist** | Asserts a *manipulability* *relation* | Woodward; Pearl |
| **Pragmatic** | Selects the *cause* from the *conditions* | Hart & Honoré; Hitchcock; Knobe |
| **Expressivist** | Expresses a *stance* or *attitude* | the *expressivists* (minority) |

**The *pragmatic* *dimension*.** The *selection* of *the* *cause* from the *conditions* is *interest-relative*: the *cause* is the *abnormal*, *unexpected*, or *controllable* *condition* (Hart & Honoré 1959; Hilton & Slugoski 1986; Hitchcock & Knobe 2009). The *selection* is *not* *arbitrary*, but it is *not* *determined* by the *world* alone.

**The *implication*.** Causal *claims* are *not* *purely* *descriptive*: they *encode* *norms*, *interests*, and *practices*. The *implication* is *central* to the *legal* and *political* *uses* of *causation* (§8).

## 3. Interventionism

### 3.1 Woodward's account

The *most* *influential* *contemporary* *account* is **interventionism**, due to James Woodward (*Making Things Happen*, 2003; “Interventionism,” 2008).

**The *thesis*.** $X$ *causes* $Y$ iff there is a *possible* *intervention* on $X$ that *changes* $Y$, *holding* *other* *variables* *fixed*.

**The *definition*** (simplified).

$$
X \ \text{causes} \ Y \quad \text{iff} \quad \text{there is an intervention } I \text{ on } X \text{ such that } P(Y \mid do(X = x_1)) \neq P(Y \mid do(X = x_2))
$$

where $do(X = x)$ is the *intervention* *operator* (Pearl 2000): the *operation* of *setting* $X$ to $x$ *by* a *mechanism* that is *independent* of the *other* *causes* of $Y$.

**The *intuition*.** Causation is the *relation* that *makes* a *difference*: the *cause* is the *thing* that, *if* you *manipulate* it, *changes* the *effect*. The *intervention* is the *idealized* *experiment* — the *experimenter*’s *manipulation* — and the *relation* is the *relation* the *experiment* *tests*.

**The *formal* *apparatus*** (Pearl 2000; Spirtes, Glymour & Scheines 1993). The *structural* *causal* *model* (SCM) is a *system* of *structural* *equations*:

$$
Y = f_Y(X, U_Y), \qquad X = f_X(U_X)
$$

where $U$ are the *exogenous* *variables*. The *intervention* is the *operation* of *replacing* the *equation* for $X$ with a *constant*: $X = x$. The *causal* *effect* is the *change* in $Y$ *under* the *intervention*.

**The *virtues*.**

**(i) The *fit* with *experimental* *science*.** The *account* *fits* the *practice* of *experimental* *science*: the *experiment* is the *intervention*, and the *causal* *claim* is the *claim* that the *intervention* *changes* the *outcome*.

**(ii) The *fit* with *causal* *modeling*.** The *account* *fits* the *contemporary* *causal* *inference* *literature* (Pearl, Spirtes et al., the *causal* *Bayesian* *networks*), and it *provides* a *formal* *semantics* for *causal* *claims*.

**(iii) The *handling* of *pre-emption*.** The *account* *handles* *pre-emption* *better* than the *simple* *counterfactual* *analysis*: the *intervention* on the *cause* *changes* the *effect* even when a *backup* is *present* (the *structural* *equations* *distinguish* the *actual* *mechanism* from the *backup*).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *circularity* *objection*.** The *notion* of an *intervention* *presupposes* the *notion* of *causation*: an *intervention* is a *causal* *process* that *changes* $X$ *without* *changing* the *other* *causes* of $Y$. The *circularity* is *real*, and Woodward’s *reply* is that the *circularity* is *virtuous* (the *intervention* *relates* the *epistemic* *practice* to the *metaphysical* *relation*; Ch. 2, §3.4).

**(ii) The *fundamental* *physics* *objection*.** In *fundamental* *physics*, *interventions* are *not* *possible*: the *experimenter* cannot *manipulate* the *fundamental* *particles*, and the *laws* are *symmetric*. The *objection* is Russell’s *objection* (§0) *applied* to *interventionism*: the *account* is *not* *applicable* to the *fundamental* *level*.

**The *reply*.** The *account* is *not* *meant* to *apply* to *fundamental* *physics*; it is *meant* to *apply* to the *special* *sciences* and to *ordinary* *reasoning*. The *reply* *accepts* the *pluralism* of §5.

**(iii) The *single-case* *objection*.** The *account* is *probabilistic*: it *requires* a *population* of *cases* in which the *intervention* *varies*. The *single* *case* (the *first* *atomic* *bomb*) *does* not *fit* the *probabilistic* *framework*, and the *account* *needs* to *handle* the *singular* *causation*.

**The *reply*.** The *single* *case* can be *handled* by the *counterfactual* *version* of the *account* (the *intervention* is *possible*, not *actual*), and the *counterfactual* *version* is *compatible* with the *structural* *equations*.

**(iv) The *normativity* *objection*.** The *account* *presupposes* an *ideal* *experimenter* — a *being* who *can* *manipulate* *any* *variable* *without* *side* *effects* — and the *idealization* is *not* *realized* in *practice*. The *objection* is *serious*, and the *reply* (the *idealization* is a *regulative* *ideal*) is *contested*.

### 3.2 The causal modeling framework

The *interventionist* *account* is *developed* in the *causal* *modeling* *framework*, which is *one* of the *most* *important* *developments* in *the* *philosophy* *of* *science* in the *last* *thirty* *years*.

**The *framework*** (Pearl 2000; Spirtes, Glymour & Scheines 1993; Peters, Janzing & Schölkopf 2017).

| Element | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal graph** | A *directed* *acyclic* *graph* (DAG) with *nodes* (variables) and *edges* (direct causal links) | $X \to Z \to Y$, $X \to Y$ |
| **Structural equations** | The *functions* that *determine* each variable from its *parents* | $Y = f(X, Z, U)$ |
| **Intervention** | The *operation* of *fixing* a variable and *removing* its *incoming* edges | $do(X = x)$ |
| **Confounder** | A *common* *cause* of two variables | $Z$ causes both $X$ and $Y$ |
| **Backdoor criterion** | A *graphical* *criterion* for *identifying* causal effects from *observational* data | Adjust for $Z$ |

**The *virtue*.** The *framework* *provides* a *formal*, *testable*, and *algorithmic* *account* of *causal* *inference*, and it has *transformed* *epidemiology*, *economics*, *computer* *science*, and *social* *science*.

**The *objections*.** (i) The *framework* *presupposes* the *causal* *graph*, and the *graph* is *not* *given* by the *data*; the *choice* of *graph* is *underdetermined* (the *Markov* *equivalence* *class*). (ii) The *framework* is *probabilistic*, and the *single* *case* is *not* *handled*. (iii) The *framework* *assumes* *acyclicity* (no *feedback* *loops*), and the *assumption* is *false* in *many* *domains* (economics, biology, climate). The *extensions* (the *cyclic* *models*, the *dynamic* *models*) *handle* some *cases*, but the *problems* *remain*.

### 3.3 The connection to manipulationism

The *interventionist* *account* is the *descendant* of the **manipulationist** *account*, which *goes* *back* to *Gasking* (1955), *Collingwood* (1940), and *von Wright* (*Explanation and Understanding*, 1971).

**The *thesis*.** To *say* that $c$ *causes* $e$ is to *say* that *manipulating* $c$ is a *way* of *bringing* *about* $e$.

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *practical* *dimension* of *causal* *knowledge*: we *know* causes in order to *control* the world.

**The *objection*.** The *account* *makes* causation *anthropocentric*: the *causal* *relation* *depends* on the *existence* of *agents* who *manipulate*. (The *reply*: the *manipulation* is an *idealization*; the *causal* *relation* is *real* *independently* of *actual* *agents*, and the *manipulation* is a *test* of the *relation*, not its *constitution*.)

## 4. Process and Mechanism Theories

### 4.1 Salmon's process theory

The *most* *influential* *alternative* to the *difference-making* *tradition* is the **process* *theory***, due to Wesley Salmon (*Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World*, 1984; “Causality without Counterfactuals,” 1994).

**The *thesis*.** Causation is the *transmission* of a *mark* (a *change* in a *property*) *along* a *causal* *process*. A *causal* *process* is a *spatiotemporally* *continuous* *world-line*; a *causal* *interaction* is an *intersection* of *two* *processes* in which a *mark* is *transmitted*.

**The *structure*.**

- **Causal process**: a *world-line* that *can* *transmit* a *mark*.
- **Causal interaction**: an *intersection* of *two* *processes* in which a *mark* is *transmitted* from one to the other.
- **Pseudo-process**: a *world-line* that *cannot* *transmit* a *mark* (a *shadow*, a *spot* of *light*).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Causation is a *connection* between *events*, not a *pattern* of *regularities*.} \\
2. & \text{The *connection* is *physical*: it is the *transmission* of a *mark* along a *spacetime* *path*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Causation is *reducible* to the *transmission* of *marks* along *causal* *processes*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *production* *intuition*: the *cause* *produces* the *effect*, and the *production* is a *physical* *process*. The *account* *handles* the *flagpole* *case*: the *shadow* is a *pseudo-process* (it cannot *transmit* a *mark*), while the *light* *is* a *causal* *process*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *conserved* *quantity* *problem* (Dowe 1992; *Physical Causation*, 2000). Salmon’s *mark* *transmission* *criterion* is *vague*: what *counts* as a *mark*? Is *any* *change* a *mark*? The *conserved* *quantity* *theory* *replaces* the *mark* with a *conserved* *quantity* (energy, momentum, charge), and the *causal* *process* is one that *possesses* a *conserved* *quantity* *and* *transmits* it.

**(ii) The *omission* *problem*. The *process* *theory* *cannot* *handle* *causation* *by* *omission* (§5.2): the *gardener*’s *failure* to *water* the *plant* *caused* its *death*, but there is *no* *causal* *process* *connecting* the *omission* to the *death*.

**(iii) The *fundamental* *physics* *problem*. The *process* *theory* *describes* causation *at* the *level* of *classical* *physics* (particles, waves, fields), and it *is* *not* *clear* that the *account* *applies* to *quantum* *mechanics* (where the *processes* are *not* *continuous*).

### 4.2 Mechanism theory

The *most* *important* *contemporary* *development* of the *process* *tradition* is the **new* *mechanist*** *philosophy* *of* *science* (Machamer, Darden & Craver, “Thinking about Mechanisms,” 2000; Bechtel & Abrahamsen 2005; Craver, *Explaining the Brain*, 2007).

**The *thesis*.** A *mechanism* is a *system* of *entities* and *activities* whose *organization* *produces* a *phenomenon*. To *explain* a *phenomenon* is to *describe* the *mechanism* that *produces* it.

**The *structure*.**

| Element | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Entities** | The *components* of the mechanism | Neurons, ions, channels |
| **Activities** | What the entities *do* | Firing, diffusing, binding |
| **Organization** | How the components are *arranged* | The *circuit* *structure* |
| **Phenomenon** | What the mechanism *produces* | Memory, consciousness |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *special* *sciences* *explain* *phenomena* by *describing* *mechanisms*.} \\
2. & \text{The *description* *of* a *mechanism* is a *description* of a *causal* *structure*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{Causation *in* the *special* *sciences* is *mechanism* *production*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *captures* the *explanatory* *practice* of *biology*, *neuroscience*, and *cognitive* *science*. The *account* *handles* *complex* *causation*: the *mechanism* *integrates* the *components*, and the *integration* is *not* a *simple* *sum*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *levels* *problem*. The *mechanism* *is* *nested*: the *mechanism* *for* *memory* *is* *composed* of *mechanisms* (synaptic *plasticity*, protein *synthesis*, gene *expression*) *that* are *themselves* *composed* of *mechanisms*. The *nesting* *raises* the *problem* of *which* *level* is *the* *causal* *level*, and the *problem* is *connected* to the *exclusion* *argument* (Ch. 21, §3).

**(ii) The *normativity* *problem*. The *mechanism* *description* *presupposes* a *distinction* between the *normal* *operation* and the *pathological* *deviation*, and the *distinction* is *normative* (Ch. 42, §2). The *mechanist* *owes* an *account* of the *norm*.

**(iii) The *fundamental* *physics* *problem*. The *mechanism* *account* is *not* *applicable* to *fundamental* *physics*, where there are *no* *mechanisms* (only *equations*).

### 4.3 The production versus difference-making distinction

The *most* *important* *conceptual* *distinction* in the *contemporary* *debate* is between **production** and **difference-making** (Hall 2004; Godfrey-Smith 2009; Hitchcock 2012).

**The *distinction*.**

| Concept | What it is | Paradigm |
|---|---|---|
| **Production** | The *physical* *connection* between cause and effect | The process/mechanism tradition |
| **Difference-making** | The *counterfactual* or *interventionist* *dependence* of the effect on the cause | The counterfactual/interventionist tradition |

**The *argument*.** The *two* *concepts* are *distinct*: the *production* *concept* *requires* a *physical* *connection*, and the *difference-making* *concept* does *not* (omissions can *make* a *difference* without *producing* anything). The *two* *concepts* *diverge* in the *hard* *cases* (pre-emption, overdetermination, omissions), and *neither* *captures* *all* the *data*.

**The *consequence*.** The *monist* *accounts* (the *counterfactual* *theory*, the *process* *theory*) *fail* because they *try* to *capture* *both* *concepts* with a *single* *analysis*. The *pluralist* *accounts* (§5) *accept* the *distinction*, and *treat* the *two* *concepts* as *equally* *legitimate*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *distinction* is *widely* *accepted*, and the *pluralism* is *the* *most* *defensible* *position*. The *debate* is *about* the *relation* between the *two* *concepts* (are they *two* *sides* of a *single* *relation*, or *two* *distinct* *relations?).

## 5. Pluralism, Omissions, and the Varieties of Causation

### 5.1 Causal pluralism

**The *thesis*.** There are *several* *concepts* of *causation*, and *none* is *reducible* to the *others*. The *choice* *among* them *depends* on the *context*, the *purpose*, and the *level* of *description*.

**The *forms*.**

| Form | Claim | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Conceptual pluralism** | *Different* *concepts* of cause for *different* *purposes* | Cartwright 2007; Godfrey-Smith 2009 |
| **Evidential pluralism** | *Different* *methods* for *different* *causal* *claims* | Russo & Williamson 2007 |
| **Explanatory pluralism** | *Different* *explanations* for *different* *interests* | the *mechanist* *tradition* |
| **Metaphysical pluralism** | *Different* *relations* in the world | Hall 2004; Hitchcock 2012 |

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *data* of *causation* include *production*, *difference-making*, *omission*, *pre-emption*, *overdetermination*, and *trumping*.} \\
2. & \text{No *single* *analysis* *captures* *all* the *data*.} \\
3. & \text{*Different* *analyses* *capture* *different* *subsets*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *best* *account* is *pluralist*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *pluralism* is *uninformative*: it *tells* us *that* there are *several* *concepts*, but it *does* not *tell* us *which* *concept* *applies* in *which* *context*. (The *reply*: the *pluralism* is *supplemented* by a *theory* of *causal* *selection* (Hart & Honoré; Hitchcock & Knobe), and the *theory* *tells* us *which* *concept* *applies*.)

### 5.2 Causal selection

**The *problem*.** Given a *sufficient* *set* of *conditions*, *which* *one* is *the* *cause*?

**The *classic* *example*** (Hart & Honoré 1959). A *fire* *breaks* *out* in a *factory*. The *conditions* include the *presence* of *oxygen*, the *presence* of *fuel*, the *defective* *wiring*, and the *absence* of a *sprinkler*. Which is *the* *cause*?

**The *answers*.**

| Answer | The cause is | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **The *abnormal* *condition*** | The condition that *deviates* from the *norm* | Hart & Honoré; Hilton & Slugoski |
| **The *controllable* *condition*** | The condition that *can* be *manipulated* | Woodward; the *interventionists* |
| **The *explanatory* *condition*** | The condition that *answers* the *why-question* | the *pragmatists* (van Fraassen 1980) |
| **The *legal* *condition*** | The condition that *satisfies* the *legal* *test* | the *law* (§8) |

**The *normality* *account* (Hitchcock & Knobe 2009).** The *cause* is the *event* that *deviates* from the *norm*. The *account* *explains* the *Knobe* *effect* (Ch. 3, §6.3): *bad* *side* *effects* are *more* *likely* to be *called* *intentional* (and *causal*) because they *deviate* from the *norm*.

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *explains* the *selection* *data*, and it *fits* the *legal* *practice*. The *account* *makes* *causation* *interest-relative*, and the *relativization* is *contested*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *selection* *problem* is *unresolved*, and the *accounts* are *contested*. The *most* *defensible* *position* is *pluralist*: the *selection* *depends* on the *context*, and the *context* *determines* *which* *concept* *applies*.

### 5.3 Causation by omission

**The *problem*.** The *gardener*’s *failure* to *water* the *plant* *caused* its *death*. The *absence* of *rain* *caused* the *drought*. The *failure* to *act* is a *cause*.

**The *challenge* *for* the *process* *theory*.** The *omission* is *not* a *process*; it is *nothing* — an *absence*. The *process* *theory* *cannot* *handle* the *case*.

**The *challenge* *for* the *counterfactual* *theory*.** The *counterfactual* *theory* *handles* the *case*: *had* the *gardener* *watered*, the *plant* *would* *not* *have* *died*. But the *theory* *makes* *omissions* *indistinguishable* from *positive* *causes*, and the *distinction* is *significant* (the *law* *treats* them *differently*; §8.3).

**The *challenge* *for* *interventionism*.** The *interventionist* *account* *handles* the *case* *if* the *intervention* *can* *set* the *variable* (the *watering*) to a *value*. But the *intervention* *presupposes* a *variable*, and the *omission* is *not* a *variable* in the *ordinary* *sense*.

**The *responses*.**

**(i) The *causal* *pluralism* *response*.** Omissions are *causes* in the *difference-making* *sense*, not in the *production* *sense*. The *pluralism* *accommodates* the *case*.

**(ii) The *modal* *response*** (Lewis 2004; Kment 2014). Omissions *cause* *in* *virtue* *of* the *modal* *facts* — the *counterfactuals* — and the *modal* *facts* are *real*.

**(iii) The *denial* *response***. Some *philosophers* (e.g., Beebee 2004) *argue* that *omissions* are *not* *causes* in the *strict* *sense*; they are *explanations* that *invoke* *causes* (the *absence* of a *cause* *explains* the *effect*, but it is *not* *itself* a *cause*). The *response* *is* *contested*.

### 5.4 The varieties of causal claims

**The *taxonomy*.**

| Kind | Example | The concept |
|---|---|---|
| **Event causation** | The *spark* caused the *fire* | Production / difference-making |
| **Omission** | The *failure* to water caused the death | Difference-making |
| **Prevention** | The *sprinkler* prevented the fire | Difference-making |
| **Disposition** | The *glass* is *fragile* | Production (powers) |
| **Agency** | *She* *broke* the window | Production + intention |
| **Explanation** | The *cause* of the war was the assassination | Pragmatic / explanatory |

**The *implication*.** The *taxonomy* *supports* the *pluralism*: the *varieties* of *causal* *claims* *require* *different* *analyses*, and *no* *single* *analysis* *captures* them *all*.

## 6. Dispositions and Powers

### 6.1 The dispositional/categorical distinction

The *distinction* was *introduced* in Ch. 15, §5.3, and it is *developed* here.

**The *distinction*.**

| Kind | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dispositional** | A *power* to *behave* in a *certain* *way* in *certain* *circumstances* | Solubility, fragility, charge |
| **Categorical** | A *qualitative* *feature* that is *not* *defined* by its *effects* | Shape, mass (on the *categorical* *view*) |

**The *question*.** Are the *fundamental* *properties* *dispositional* or *categorical*? Or are they *both*?

### 6.2 Dispositionalism

**The *thesis*.** All *fundamental* *properties* are *dispositional*: their *nature* is *exhausted* by their *causal* *role*.

**The *statement*.** Bird, *Nature’s Metaphysics* (2007); Mumford, *Dispositions* (1998); Ellis, *Scientific Essentialism* (2001); Molnar, *Powers* (2003).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Every *property* is *known* *through* its *causal* *role*.} \\
2. & \text{The *causal* *role* is *constitutive* of the *property*: there is *nothing* *more* to *know*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{All *properties* are *dispositional*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *consequences*.**

- **The *identity* of *properties* is *structural*: a *property* is *the* *property* *that* *plays* a *certain* *causal* *role*, and *two* *properties* *that* *play* the *same* *role* are *identical*.
- **The *laws* are *necessary*: the *laws* are *consequences* of the *essences* of the *properties*, and the *necessity* is *metaphysical*, not *nomological*.
- **The *modality* is *grounded* in the *properties* (Ch. 16, §3.4).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *“always* *packing, never* *traveling”* *objection*** (Blackburn 1990). If *all* *properties* are *dispositional*, the *world* is a *world* of *pure* *powers* with *no* *categorical* *basis* — a *world* of *“always* *packing, never* *traveling.”* The *world* *needs* a *categorical* *ground*; otherwise the *powers* *are* *powers* *to* *do* *nothing.

**The *reply*** (Bird 2007, Ch. 5). The *“packing”* *metaphor* *misdescribes* the *view*. The *powers* are *not* *pure* *potentialities*; they are *real* *properties* *that* *manifest* in *certain* *circumstances*. The *manifestation* is *real*, and the *world* is *not* *less* *real* for *being* *dispositional*.

**(ii) The *circularity* *objection*.** The *dispositionalist* *defines* a *property* by its *effects*, and the *effects* by their *causes*. The *definition* is *circular*, and the *circle* is *not* *broken*.

**The *reply*** (Bird 2007, Ch. 3). The *circle* is *not* *vicious*: the *properties* *form* a *structure* (the *“power* *structure”*), and the *structure* *is* the *explanation*. The *reply* *is* *structuralist* (Ch. 15, §7), and it is *contested*.

**(iii) The *conceivability* *objection*** (Chalmers 1996; Lewis 2009). It is *conceivable* that a *world* has the *same* *categorical* *properties* as the *actual* *world* but *different* *laws* (a *world* where *charge* does *not* *repel*). If it is *conceivable*, it is *possible*. If it is *possible*, the *laws* are *not* *supervenient* on the *categorical* *properties*. The *argument* *supports* the *categoricalist* *view*.

**The *reply***. The *conceivability* *is* *not* *a* *reliable* *guide* to *possibility* (Ch. 16, §6.2); the *apparent* *possibility* *is* *an* *artifact* of our *ignorance* of the *essences*.

### 6.3 Categoricalism

**The *thesis*.** The *fundamental* *properties* are *categorical*; the *dispositions* are *grounded* in the *categorical* *basis*, but the *basis* is *not* *exhausted* by the *dispositions*.

**The *statement*.** Armstrong, *A World of States of Affairs* (1997), Ch. 5; Lewis, “Ramseyan Humility” (2009); Prior, Pasnau & others.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{A *disposition* is *grounded* in a *categorical* *basis*.} \\
2. & \text{The *basis* is *not* *itself* a *disposition* (otherwise the *regress* is *infinite*).} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *fundamental* *properties* are *categorical*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *provides* a *ground* for the *dispositions*, and it *avoids* the *“always* *packing”* *objection*.

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *nature* of the *categorical* *is* *mysterious*. What *is* the *categorical* *basis* of *charge*? It is a *property* *that* *is* *not* *defined* by its *effects*, and the *property* *is* *not* *empirically* *accessible*. (This is *Lewis’s* *“Ramseyan* *humility”*: the *intrinsic* *nature* of the *fundamental* *properties* is *unknowable*.)

**(ii) The *quidditism* *problem*. If the *categorical* *properties* have *intrinsic* *natures* that are *independent* of their *causal* *roles*, then *two* *worlds* can *have* the *same* *categorical* *properties* *arranged* *differently* in the *causal* *structure* (the *“quiddity”* *problem*; Ch. 16, §3.4). The *view* *makes* the *laws* *contingent*, and the *contingency* is *contested*.

### 6.4 The powers and laws

**The *connection*.** The *dispositionalist* *view* *grounds* the *laws* in the *essences* of the *properties*: the *law* “all *charged* *particles* *repel”* is *necessary* *because* *charge* is *essentially* a *power* *to* *repel. The *view* *is* the *most* *influential* *alternative* to the *Humean* *best-system* *account* (§7.2).

**The *virtue*.** The *view* *explains* the *necessity* of the *laws*, and it *connects* the *laws* to the *properties*.

**The *objection*.** The *view* *makes* the *laws* *dependent* on the *properties*, and the *properties* are *dependent* on the *laws* — the *circularity* *is* *real*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *dispositionalist* *and* *categoricalist* *views* are *both* *live*, and the *debate* is *connected* to the *theory* of *laws* (§7) and to the *theory* of *modality* (Ch. 16).

## 7. Laws of Nature

### 7.1 The Humean best-system account

**The *thesis*.** The *laws* of *nature* are the *generalizations* that are *part* of the *best* *system* of *the* *world* — the *system* that *balances* *simplicity* and *strength* (Lewis 1973, 1986, 1994; Ramsey 1928; Mill 1843).

**The *definition*** (Lewis 1986, p. xi).

> The *laws* are the *axioms* and *theorems* of the *deductive* *system* that *achieves* the *best* *combination* of *simplicity* and *strength* — where *strength* is *informational* *content* (the *number* of *facts* *entailed*) and *simplicity* is *syntactic* *economy*.

**The *structure*.**

1. **The *system*.** A *deductive* *system* of *true* *generalizations*.
2. **The *tradeoff*.** The *system* *balances* *simplicity* (few *axioms*) and *strength* (many *consequences*).
3. **The *laws*.** The *axioms* (and *theorems*) of the *best* *system*.
4. **The *chance*.** The *probabilistic* *laws* are the *chances*; they are *determined* by the *best* *system* (Lewis 1980).

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *avoids* the *mystery* of *necessity*: the *laws* are *not* *necessary*; they are *patterns* *in* the *world*. The *account* *fits* the *Humean* *supervenience* *thesis*: the *laws* *supervene* on the *occurrent* *facts* (Lewis 1986, “Humean Supervenience”).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *simplicity* *objection*.** The *notion* of *simplicity* is *not* *well-defined* (Ch. 4, §3.3): the *choice* of *language* *determines* the *simplest* *system*, and the *choice* *is* *not* *neutral*.

**(ii) The *uniqueness* *objection*.** The *best* *system* *may* *not* *be* *unique*: there *may* be *two* *systems* *that* *are* *equally* *simple* *and* *strong*, and the *laws* *would* be *underdetermined*.

**(iii) The *necessity* *objection*.** The *account* *makes* the *laws* *contingent*: the *laws* *could* have *been* *different* (if the *best* *system* had *been* *different*). But *intuitively*, the *laws* are *necessary*: it is *not* *possible* for a *charged* *particle* *not* to *repel*. The *account* *cannot* *explain* the *necessity*.

**(iv) The *“governing”* *objection*. The *account* *makes* the *laws* *descriptive*, not *governing*: the *laws* *describe* the *regularities*, but they do *not* *explain* *why* the *regularities *obtain*. The *scientific* *practice* *treats* the *laws* as *explanatory*, and the *account* *cannot* *underwrite* the *practice*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *Humean* *account* is the *most* *influential* *contemporary* *view*, and it is *contested* by the *necessitarian* and *dispositionalist* *accounts*.

### 7.2 The necessitarian account

**The *thesis*.** The *laws* are *relations* of *necessitation* between *universals* (Armstrong 1983; Dretske 1977; Tooley 1977).

**The *structure*.** The *law* “all $F$s are $G$” is *analyzed* as:

$$
N(F, G)
$$

where $N$ is the *relation* of *necessitation* between the *universals* $F$ and $G$.

**The *statement*. Armstrong, *What Is a Law of Nature?* (1983); Dretske, “Laws of Nature” (1977); Tooley, “The Nature of Laws” (1977).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* are *necessary*: they *support* *counterfactuals*, and they *explain* the *regularities*.} \\
2. & \text{The *necessity* *requires* a *relation* of *necessitation* between *universals*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *laws* are *relations* of *necessitation*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *explains* the *necessity* of the *laws* and their *counterfactual* *support*. The *account* *fits* the *realist* *view* of *universals* (Ch. 15, §4).

**The *objections*.**

**(i) The *inference* *problem*** (van Fraassen 1989, *Laws and Symmetry*, Ch. 5). The *relation* $N(F, G)$ *does* not *explain* *why* all $F$s are $G$: the *relation* is a *primitive*, and the *regularity* *follows* from it only if we *add* a *further* *principle* (that $N$ *necessitates* the *regularity*). The *principle* *is* *not* *explained*, and the *account* *is* *circular*.

**(ii) The *identification* *problem***. How do we *know* that $N(F, G)$ rather than $N(G, F)$? The *relation* *has* a *direction*, and the *direction* *is* *not* *given* by the *regularity*. The *problem* is the *asymmetry* *problem* again.

**(iii) The *“governing”* *problem***. The *relation* $N$ is a *“quasi-relation”* (van Fraassen): it is *not* a *causal* *relation*, and it is *not* a *logical* *relation*. What *is* it? The *account* *owes* an *explanation*, and the *explanation* is *not* *available*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *necessitarian* *account* is *influential*, and it is *contested*. The *debate* is *unresolved*, and it is *one* of the *central* *issues* in the *philosophy* of *science*.

### 7.3 The dispositionalist account

**The *thesis*.** The *laws* are *grounded* in the *essences* of the *dispositional* *properties*: the *law* is *necessary* because the *property* is *essentially* a *power* to *behave* in a *certain* *way*.

**The *statement*.** Bird, *Nature’s Metaphysics* (2007); Ellis, *Scientific Essentialism* (2001); Mumford, *Laws in Nature* (2004).

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* are *necessary*.} \\
2. & \text{The *necessity* is *grounded* in the *essences* of the *properties*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *laws* are *consequences* of the *essences*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *explains* the *necessity* *without* *positing* a *mysterious* *relation* $N$: the *necessity* *is* the *necessity* of *essence* (Ch. 16, §3.4).

**The *objections*.** (i) The *circularity* *problem* (the *properties* are *defined* by the *laws*, and the *laws* by the *properties*). (ii) The *conceivability* *problem* (the *categoricalist* *argument* of §6.2).

### 7.4 The primitivist account

**The *thesis*.** The *laws* are *primitive*: they are *not* *reducible* to *regularities*, *universals*, or *essences*. The *governing* *role* of the *laws* is *a* *fundamental* *feature* of the *world*.

**The *statement*.** Maudlin, *The Metaphysics within Physics* (2007), Ch. 1; the *account* is *close* to the *necessitarian* *view*, but it *rejects* the *analysis* in *terms* of *universals*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{The *laws* *govern*: they *determine* what *happens*.} \\
2. & \text{The *governing* is *not* *reducible* to *regularity*, *universals*, or *essence*.} \\
\hline
3. & \therefore \text{The *laws* are *primitive*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *virtue*.** The *account* *takes* the *scientific* *practice* *seriously*: the *laws* *are* the *fundamental* *structure* of the *world*.

**The *objection*.** The *account* *is* *unexplanatory*: it *posits* a *primitive* and *does* not *explain* *why* the *laws* *hold*.

**The *state* of the debate.** The *primitivist* *account* is *a* *minority* *position*, and it is *taken* *seriously*.

### 7.5 Ceteris paribus laws and the special sciences

**The *problem*.** The *special* *sciences* (biology, psychology, economics) have *laws* that are *not* *exceptionless*: “*ceteris paribus*, *if* an *organism* is *deprived* of *food*, it *dies*.” The *laws* are *qualified* by *ceteris* *paribus* *clauses*, and the *clauses* are *not* *precisely* *specifiable* (Ch. 39, §2).

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *status* of *ceteris* *paribus* *laws* | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Eliminativism** | They are *not* *laws*; they are *rules* of *thumb* | the *Humean* *orthodoxy* |
| **Anti-eliminativism** | They are *laws*, but *different* in *kind* from *fundamental* *laws* | the *special-science* *realists* (Cartwright, Mitchell) |
| **Mechanism** | The *laws* are *descriptions* of *mechanisms* | the *mechanist* *tradition* |

**The *significance*.** The *debate* *bears* on the *unity* of *science*: if the *special* *sciences* have *laws*, and the *laws* are *not* *reducible* to *fundamental* *laws*, then the *unity* *is* *not* *reductionist* (Ch. 21, §4).

## 8. Causation in the Special Sciences and the Law

### 8.1 The special sciences

**The *claim*.** Causation in the *special* *sciences* is *not* *reducible* to *fundamental* *physics*.

**The *argument*.**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{Each *special* *science* *identifies* *causes* at its *own* *level* of *description*.} \\
2. & \text{The *causes* *are* *multiply* *realizable*: the *same* *effect* *can* be *produced* by *different* *physical* *mechanisms*.} \\
3. & \text{The *multiple* *realizability* *blocks* the *reduction* to *fundamental* *physics*.} \\
\hline
4. & \therefore \text{The *special* *sciences* have *autonomous* *causal* *claims*.}
\end{array}
$$

**The *objection*.** The *autonomy* is *epistemic*, not *metaphysical*: the *special-science* *causes* *supervene* on the *physical* *causes*, and the *autonomy* is a *matter* of *description*. (The *reply*: the *supervenience* does *not* *reduce* the *causal* *claims*; the *causal* *claims* are *real* at their *level*; the *reply* is *connected* to the *exclusion* *argument*; Ch. 21, §3.)

### 8.2 The law

**The *legal* *notion* of *cause*.** The *law* *distinguishes* the *cause* from the *conditions* (Hart & Honoré 1959), and it *uses* several *tests*.

| Test | What it asks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **But-for** | Would the *harm* have *occurred* *without* the *act*? | The *driver*’s *negligence* |
| **NESS** | Is the *act* a *necessary* *element* of a *sufficient* *set*? | The *pollution* |
| **Proximate cause** | Is the *act* *close* *enough* in the *causal* *chain*? | The *manufacturer* |
| **Foreseeability** | Was the *harm* *foreseeable*? | The *practical* *test* |

**The *significance*.** The *legal* *tests* *encode* a *pluralism*: the *but-for* *test* is the *difference-making* *concept*; the *proximate* *cause* is the *pragmatic* *selection*; the *foreseeability* is the *normative* *dimension*. The *law* *uses* *all* *three*, and *the* *use* is *not* *reducible* to a *single* *concept*.

### 8.3 The ethics of causal attribution

**The *question*.** Who *is* *responsible* for a *harm*?

**The *positions*.**

| Position | The *criterion* | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal** | The *person* *who* *caused* the *harm* | the *classical* *view* |
| **Moral** | The *person* *who* *is* *at* *fault* | the *moral* *view* |
| **Structural** | The *system* *that* *produced* the *harm* | the *critical* *view* (Ch. 47, §7) |

**The *argument*.** The *causal* *attribution* is *not* *neutral*: the *selection* of the *cause* *reflects* *interests* and *norms*. The *selection* of the *individual* as the *cause* (rather than the *system*) *serves* *particular* *interests*, and the *critical* *view* *challenges* the *selection*.

**The *implication*.** Causation is *entangled* with *value* (Ch. 13, §5.4): the *causal* *claims* are *not* *purely* *descriptive*, and the *ethics* of *causal* *attribution* is *central* to the *law*, *medicine*, and *politics*.

### 8.4 Causation and climate

**The *problem*.** The *climate* *crisis* *raises* *causal* *questions*: did *climate* *change* *cause* *this* *storm*? Is the *harm* *attributable* to *this* *country*?

**The *science*.** The *attribution* *science* *uses* the *counterfactual* *method*: it *compares* the *actual* *world* with a *counterfactual* *world* *without* *anthropogenic* *emissions*. The *method* is *the* *interventionist* *account* *applied* to a *global* *system*.

**The *philosophical* *questions*.** (i) The *counterfactual* *world* is *not* *accessible*, and the *model* *is* *uncertain*. (ii) The *causal* *claim* is *statistical*, and the *individual* *event* is *not* *attributable* in the *simple* *but-for* *sense*. (iii) The *moral* and *political* *questions* — who *is* *responsible*? — are *not* *settled* by the *causal* *science* (Ch. 51, §6).

**The *significance*.** The *climate* *case* *shows* that the *causal* *concepts* are *not* *academic*: they *shape* *policy*, *law*, and *justice*.

## 9. The State of the Debate

### 9.1 What is settled

- **The regularity theory is untenable in its naive form.** The *accidental* *regularity*, *asymmetry*, and *singular* *causation* *objections* are *decisive*.
- **The counterfactual theory captures the difference-making intuition.** The *analysis* is *elegant*, and it is *refined* by the *influence* *account*.
- **The process theory captures the production intuition.** The *analysis* *fits* the *physical* *sciences*, and it is *refined* by the *mechanism* *theory*.
- **The pluralism is the most defensible position.** The *two* *concepts* — *difference-making* and *production* — are *distinct*, *irreducible*, and *both* *legitimate*.
- **The interventionist framework is the most influential.** The *causal* *modeling* *literature* has *transformed* the *philosophy* of *science* and the *social* *sciences*.
- **The laws debate is unresolved.** The *Humean*, *necessitarian*, *dispositionalist*, and *primitivist* *accounts* are *all* *live*.

### 9.2 What is open

- **The *nature* of *the* *causal* *relation*.** Is it *difference-making*, *production*, or *both*?
- **The *asymmetry* of *causation*.** Is it *grounded* in the *counterfactuals*, the *arrow* of *time*, or *agency*?
- **The *status* of *omissions*.** Are they *causes* in the *strict* *sense*?
- **The *causal* *selection*.** Is it *pragmatic*, *normative*, or *objective*?
- **The *nature* of *laws*.** Is the *Humean* *account* *adequate*, or are the *laws* *necessary*?
- **The *dispositional/categorical* *distinction*.** Are the *fundamental* *properties* *powers* or *qualities*?
- **The *autonomy* of *the* *special* *sciences*.** Is the *causal* *autonomy* *epistemic* or *metaphysical*?

### 9.3 The methodological lesson

The chapter is a *case* *study* in the method of Part I.

- **Conceptual analysis** (§1 of Ch. 3) generated the *distinction* between *cause* and *condition*.
- **Thought experiments** (§2 of Ch. 3) — *pre-emption*, *overdetermination*, *omission* — are the *engine* of the debate.
- **Formalization** (§3 of Ch. 3) — the *structural* *equations*, the *do-calculus*, the *causal* *graphs* — is *essential* for the *field*.
- **Reflective equilibrium** (§4 of Ch. 3) is the method by which the *intuitions* and the *theories* are adjusted.
- **Negative capability** (§7 of Ch. 3) is the *response* to the *residue*: the *humility* to hold the *question* open.

The *honest* verdict: the *theory* of *causation* is a *field* in *transition*. The *monist* *accounts* are *untenable*; the *pluralist* *account* is *promising* but *under-specified*; the *laws* *debate* is *unresolved*. The *unresolved* character is *instructive*: it shows what philosophical progress looks like — not a *settled answer*, but a *sharper understanding* of what the *question* is.

### 9.4 The dialectical map

| Position | Core claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regularity** | Causation is constant conjunction | Naturalistic; parsimonious | Accidental regularities; asymmetry |
| **Counterfactual** | Causation is counterfactual dependence | Captures difference-making | Pre-emption; asymmetry |
| **Interventionist** | Causation is manipulability | Formal; fits science | Circularity; single case |
| **Process** | Causation is mark transmission | Captures production | Omissions; quantum |
| **Mechanism** | Causation is mechanism production | Fits special sciences | Levels; normativity |
| **Pluralism** | Different concepts for different purposes | Accommodates all data | Under-specified |
| **Humean laws** | Laws are best-system regularities | Parsimonious | Simplicity; necessity |
| **Necessitarian laws** | Laws are relations of universals | Explains necessity | Inference; identification |
| **Dispositionalist laws** | Laws are grounded in essences | Explains necessity | Circularity; conceivability |
| **Primitivist laws** | Laws are primitive | Takes science seriously | Unexplanatory |

The *table* is a *map*, not a *verdict*. The *reader* who wants to *choose* a *position* must decide which *cost* is *acceptable* — and the *decision* is a *philosophical* *argument*.

## Optional threads

Cross-cutting material, for the reader who wants to go deeper.

**1. The *asymmetry* of *causation* and the *arrow* of *time*.** The *most* *difficult* *problem* in the field is the *asymmetry*: why does *causation* *align* with the *temporal* *arrow*? The *counterfactual* *answer* (Lewis 1979) *grounds* the *asymmetry* in the *asymmetry* of *overdetermination*; the *process* *answer* *grounds* it in the *direction* of *physical* *processes* (the *retarded* *potentials*); the *agency* *answer* (Price 1996; Woodward 2003) *grounds* it in the *practical* *intervention*. The *debate* is *connected* to Ch. 17, §6, and it is *unresolved*.

**2. The *normativity* of *causation*.** The *causal* *claims* *encode* *norms* (the *abnormal* *condition* is the *cause*; the *foreseeable* *harm* is *attributable*). The *normative* *dimension* is *central* to the *law* (§8.2) and to the *ethics* of *attribution* (§8.3). The *philosophical* *question* — is the *normativity* *constitutive* of *causation*, or *parasitic* on it? — is *unresolved*.

**3. The *causal* *exclusion* *argument*.** If *mental* *events* *cause* *physical* *events*, and *physical* *events* *cause* *physical* *events*, the *mental* *causes* *are* *excluded* (Kim 1998; Ch. 21, §3; Ch. 23, §5). The *argument* is *one* of the *most* *important* *applications* of the *theory* of *causation*, and it is *unresolved*.

**4. Non-Western *theories* of *causation*.** The *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* *tradition* *developed* a *theory* of *causation* (the *satkāryavāda* and *asatkāryavāda* *debates*, the *kāraṇa* *theory*) that *anticipates* the *contemporary* *debate* *between* *production* and *difference-making*. The *Buddhist* *tradition* *developed* a *theory* of *dependent* *origination* (*pratītyasamutpāda*) that *is* a *form* of *causal* *pluralism* *avant* *la* *lettre*. The *traditions* are *systematic* *alternatives* (Matilal 1986; Ganeri 2001; the *essays* in *The Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017).

**5. The *causation* *and* *artificial* *intelligence*.** The *causal* *inference* *literature* (Pearl, Spirtes et al.) is the *foundation* of *AI* *causal* *reasoning* (the *causal* *Bayesian* *networks*, the *reinforcement* *learning* *agents*). The *philosophical* *questions* — *what* *is* the *causal* *knowledge* of an *AI*? *is* it *grounded* in *intervention* or in *correlation*? — are *new* and *urgent* (Ch. 29, §3).

## Summary

- **Hume’s legacy** is the *regularity* *theory*: causation is *constant* *conjunction* + *priority* + *contiguity*. The *theory* is *naturalistic* and *parsimonious*, but it *fails* for *accidental* *regularities*, *asymmetry*, *singular* *causation*, and *complex* *causation*.
- **Mackie’s INUS** *account* *refines* the *regularity* *theory*: the *cause* is an *insufficient* but *non-redundant* part of an *unnecessary* but *sufficient* *condition*. The *account* *handles* *complex* *causation*, but it *does* not *solve* the *selection* *problem*.
- **The counterfactual theory** (Lewis) *analyzes* causation as *counterfactual* *dependence*. It *captures* the *difference-making* *intuition*, but it *fails* for *pre-emption*, *overdetermination*, and the *asymmetry*. The *influence* *account* (Lewis 2000, 2004) *refines* the *analysis*.
- **Interventionism** (Woodward, Pearl) *analyzes* causation as *manipulability*: $X$ *causes* $Y$ iff an *intervention* on $X$ *changes* $Y$. The *account* *fits* *experimental* *science* and *causal* *modeling*, but it *faces* the *circularity* and *single-case* *objections*.
- **Process and mechanism theories** (Salmon, Dowe, Machamer et al.) *analyze* causation as *production*: the *transmission* of a *mark* or a *conserved* *quantity* along a *causal* *process*. The *account* *handles* the *production* *intuition*, but it *fails* for *omissions* and *quantum* *mechanics*.
- **Causal pluralism** is the *most* *defensible* *position*: the *difference-making* and *production* *concepts* are *distinct*, *irreducible*, and *both* *legitimate*. The *selection* of the *cause* from the *conditions* is *interest-relative* (Hart & Honoré; Hitchcock & Knobe).
- **Dispositions and powers**: the *dispositionalist* *holds* that *fundamental* *properties* are *powers*; the *categoricalist* *holds* that *they* are *qualitative* *features* *grounding* the *powers*. The *debate* is *connected* to the *theory* of *laws*.
- **Laws of nature**: the *Humean* *best-system* *account* *reduces* the *laws* to *regularities*; the *necessitarian* *account* *posits* a *relation* of *necessitation* *between* *universals*; the *dispositionalist* *account* *grounds* the *laws* in *essences*; the *primitivist* *account* *treats* the *laws* as *primitive*.
- **The practical stakes**: causation is *central* to the *special* *sciences* (multiple *realizability*, *autonomy*), to the *law* (*but-for*, *NESS*, *proximate* *cause*), and to *ethics* (attribution, responsibility, climate).
- **The state of the debate**: the *monist* *accounts* are *untenable*; the *pluralist* *account* is *promising*; the *laws* *debate* is *unresolved*.
